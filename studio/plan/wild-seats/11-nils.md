# Nils — Wild, paper

**Thesis for this surface, one line:** the Tasks screen is not a list of cards on a background. It is *one sheet of paper, edge to edge*, and a task is a slip on that sheet — a slip has no outline, no corner radius, no fill of its own. A slip exists only because a perforation runs above it, a rule runs under its writing, and its grain moves with it when it moves. Remove the containers and the paper has to do all the work: separation, order, state, consequence. It can.

**Shared material — the sheet (built once, used by everything below):**

- **Ground = the sheet.** The whole Tasks surface, top edge to bottom edge, gutter to gutter, is one `PaperSurface`: night ground `#171412` raised one staircase step, overlaid with fibre grain — ~400 pre-generated 1px strokes, 4–9 px long, angle ±20° from horizontal, alpha 0.03–0.06 in ink tone. No margins showing a darker "app background" behind it, no rounded sheet corners. The sheet *is* the screen; that is what "no containers" means at the top level.
- **Grain belongs to the paper, not the screen.** Each slip's grain is seeded from the slip's own id and drawn in slip-local coordinates. When a slip is dragged, fed, or settles, its grain travels with it exactly 1:1. A pattern that stays fixed to the glass while content moves underneath reads as a screen effect; a pattern that moves with the slip reads as a *thing*. This one decision sells every behaviour below.
- **Depth is tone and edge only, never shadow.** Resting slips: flush, zero tone difference from the sheet — a slip at rest is just a region of the sheet between two perforations. Head slip (next up): +1 surface step (≥1.35:1 against the ground — it is physically the top slip of the stack, so it catches the light). Lifted slip (being dragged / reordered): one further step, still under the fills cap `#4A423C`. Torn edges get no catch-light — torn paper doesn't catch light cleanly; they are drawn in raw fibre tone, ink-step lighter at alpha 0.5. The one catch-light in the system is a 1 px line, one step lighter, on the *top* edge of the head slip only.
- **Typography is the print on the paper.** Order numbers in Anonymous Pro, titles in Atkinson Hyperlegible, the head slip's START in Sora. Text tiers hold at ≈11 / 5.6 / 3.4:1. Orange appears exactly once on this surface: the START commit fill on the head slip, per the locked foundations. Paper, ink, perforation and crease spend zero accent budget anywhere else.
- **Two hairline vocabularies, never mixed.** A *rule* is solid, alpha 0.14 — it is printed, it belongs to writing. A *perforation* is dashed (3 dp dash / 3 dp gap), alpha 0.18 — it is a tear line, it belongs to separation. The eye learns the difference in one visit: solid = write here, dashed = this can come away.

## Tasks — composer

### Chosen form — THE BLANK SLIP: the next line of the sheet is the composer

**The moment.** There is no input box, no "+" button, no composer card, no modal. At the bottom of the queue, after the last queued slip, sits one more slip — blank. It is distinguished from a queued slip by exactly three printed things: a solid rule under its writing line (the only solid rule on the screen), a pencil-tier placeholder *"Write the next task"* at text tier 3 (≈3.4:1 — deliberately the faintest legal tier; it is a suggestion printed in pencil, not ink), and a perforation *below* it that is drawn incomplete — its dashes fade out left to right over the last 40% of the width, because this slip is still attached to the pad. Tap anywhere on the blank slip and the caret lands: a 2 px vertical ink bar, blinking at a paper-calm 1060 ms period (530 on / 530 off, hard switch, no fade — print doesn't fade). Type, and you are writing on the sheet, in line, in order. The new task will execute after everything above it because it is physically below everything above it. Order is not metadata here; it is position.

**Commit — the slip sets, and a fresh one feeds in.** Commit is the keyboard's Done key, or a tap on the perforation zone under the slip. On commit, in sequence:

1. The incomplete perforation *completes itself*: the faded dashes ink in left to right, 8 dashes × 40 ms stagger, 240 ms total, linear — the slip is now a real, separable slip.
2. The placeholder does not return. The slip's rule greys from writing-rule to nothing — a written slip carries no rule; rules are only for the blank line.
3. A fresh blank slip **feeds in from the bottom edge**: it rises one slip-height (56 dp) from below the sheet's bottom edge over 260 ms `EaseOutCubic`, pushing nothing — it arrives in the space the sheet makes by scrolling the stack up exactly one slip-height in the same motion, same curve, so the whole sheet breathes up by one line and the new blank line lands where the old one sat. The composer is always in the same place on the glass. His thumb learns one address.

**Mechanism / data source.** Composer state is a single local draft string bound to the blank slip; nothing is written to the task store until commit. Commit appends one task (title text only — no duration picker, no priority, no tags, no date: fields he didn't ask for are decisions he didn't make) to the end of the persisted queue. The feed-in slip is a new instance of the same composable, keyed fresh. Draft survives leaving and returning to Tasks (it is ink already on paper — leaving the sheet doesn't erase pencil), but it is never rendered anywhere else: one fact, one home.

**Typing feel — ink soak.** Each character lands at 103% scale and alpha 0.65 and settles to 100% / full alpha in 90 ms `EaseOutQuad`, transform origin at the glyph's baseline-left. It is subliminal at typing speed (2–3 glyphs mid-settle at once) and reads as ink taking to fibre rather than pixels appearing. Backspace is the honest opposite: the glyph lifts — alpha to 0 in 60 ms with a 1 dp rise — no smear, no eraser crumbs; paper forgives digitally here because a fake erasure animation would be decoration, not truth.

**Motion summary.** Caret: hard blink 1060 ms. Per-character settle: 90 ms `EaseOutQuad`. Perforation completion: 240 ms linear, staggered. Feed-in + stack scroll: 260 ms `EaseOutCubic`, locked together.

**Haptics.** Typing: **silence** — the system keyboard owns keystroke feedback; doubling it is noise. Perforation completing: one **tick, 5 ms @ 0.3**, at the moment the last dash inks. Feed-in landing: **soft knock, 10 ms @ 0.45**, as the new blank slip's top edge meets the slip above it. Commit is deliberately the quietest consequential act on the surface — writing a line should feel like writing a line.

**Sound.** **Explicit silence throughout the composer.** No key clicks, no feed whirr. Paper at writing distance is silent; the tear sounds in this system are reserved for separation events in the queue, and spending one on mere entry would devalue them.

**Standards check.**
- *ARM cost:* zero. The composer lives below the queue, off the arm path entirely; arming from Deck never passes through it. Adding a task is rested-user work (evening, planning) and takes exactly: tap slip, type, Done — 3 acts, no dialogs.
- *Mid-session decisions:* zero introduced. The composer exists only while unsealed. While a session is sealed, the whole Tasks surface is behind HOLD — the blank slip is not reachable, not greyed-out-and-tappable, simply not presented as an actor (the sheet can be *looked* at as the session's printed order, nothing on it responds). No edit affordance competes with a running session.
- *Truth risk:* none. The draft is visibly a draft (pencil placeholder, incomplete perforation, faintest text tier) and becomes a task only at commit, at which point its rendering changes completely (perforation completes, rule vanishes). There is no state in which a draft is counted in the queue total, because there is no queue total rendered anywhere — the number of slips is the number; nothing else states it.

### Runner-up — THE PULLED TAB: a slip you draw up from the sheet's bottom edge

A 28 dp tab — a deckled paper tongue — sits proud of the bottom edge. Pull it up and a full blank slip unfolds upward over the queue (rotationX from −72° to 0° around its bottom hinge, 340 ms `EaseOutCubic`, **tick 7 ms @ 0.45** at flat), write on it, flick it down and it folds back, feeding the written slip into the queue's end as it goes.

**Why it lost.** It is a lovely gesture spent on the wrong problem. It costs one extra gesture (the pull) before every single entry, it *covers the queue while you write* — so you compose blind, unable to see the line above yours that gives the new line its context — and its fold hinge duplicates the fold language the arm gesture family already speaks elsewhere, diluting it. The blank slip is already at the correct address, already in context, already open. A door on a room you're already standing in is theatre.

**Guard fork.** Not relevant to the composer — entry behaves identically under both Guard outcomes; no blocked-app state is ever written, read, or implied here.

## Tasks — queue

### Chosen form — THE STACK OF SLIPS: rows without containers, order you can settle

**Row anatomy — what a slip is, with nothing around it.** Each queued task occupies a 56 dp band of the sheet. There is no background fill, no border, no corner. A slip is bounded above by the perforation of the slip above it and below by its own perforation (dashed hairline, full width, gutter to gutter — perforations run edge to edge, because a tear line that stops short of the edge is a lie about where the paper separates). Inside the band, three printed elements and nothing else:

- **Order numeral** — Anonymous Pro, tier 2, in a 28 dp left gutter, baseline-aligned with the title. The numeral is real data (execution position) rendered once, here, and nowhere else. When order changes, numerals re-print (see settle, below) — they never animate or slide; print is print, it is simply *re-struck*.
- **Title** — Atkinson Hyperlegible, tier 1, one line, ellipsis if it must (the full title lives in the composer only if he re-opens that slip unsealed; the row never wraps to two lines, because a slip is one line tall and a wrapped title would silently change slip heights and break the stack's rhythm).
- **State, only when real.** The head slip alone carries START — Sora, on the orange commit fill, right-aligned, the screen's single orange event. Non-head slips carry **nothing** on the right: no status word, no chevron, no duration they don't have. A slip that is merely waiting looks like paper waiting. Nothing on this surface performs readiness.

**The head slip sits on top — literally one step up.** The head slip and only the head slip renders at +1 tone step with the 1 px catch-light on its top edge. This is not a highlight colour and it encodes no progress: it is the physical fact that one slip lies on top of a stack. When the head slip tears away (below), the next slip *becomes* the top of the stack: its tone steps up over 180 ms `EaseOutCubic` and the catch-light fades in with it. The promotion is a material event, not a badge change.

**Completion — tear-off, with the stack settling behind it.** Outside a sealed session, completing a task is a tear. Drag a slip rightward: its right end lifts first (the slip skews up to 1.8° around its left perforation origin — paper peels from one side), the perforation dashes under it extinguish one by one as the drag crosses them, and 8–12 fibre strands draw as 1 px quadratic curves bridging slip and sheet, stretching and snapping as their length passes 10 dp. Haptics during the drag: **tick 6 ms @ 0.35** per perforation dash crossed, spaced by dash pitch (~every 11 dp of travel). Release past 55% width and the tear completes: **240 ms, stepped ease** — five visible advances of ~48 ms, because real tears stutter as fibres catch — the slip exits right with a single 120 ms flutter (rotation 1.8° → −0.8° → 0). Release before 55% and the slip settles back flat in 190 ms `EaseOutCubic` with a soft **8 ms @ 0.4** slap; nothing is recorded, nothing is half-done. Sound at the completed tear only: a filtered noise rip, ~260 ms, bright attack with a fibrous crackle tail, quiet — paper, not cardboard — plus a **settle knock 16 ms @ 0.9** haptic as the stack lands. A tap-target alternative exists for accessibility: long-press a slip 400 ms and a printed "TEAR OFF" word appears in the gutter; tapping it runs the identical 240 ms tear with identical sound and haptics. Same event, two doors.

What a completed tear leaves behind: **nothing.** No stub archive, no receipt list, no tower — the Ledger is dead and this surface does not resurrect it in miniature. The one truthful receipt is the session-end receipt the settled law already allows, elsewhere. On the sheet, a torn-off task is gone the way torn paper is gone; the stack closing up *is* the record the eye needs.

**The settle — the behaviour the whole surface is named for.** Whenever the stack changes — a tear removes a slip, a commit feeds one in at the bottom, a reorder lands — the affected slips do not teleport, crossfade, or bounce. They **settle**, in two phases:

1. *Travel:* each displaced slip translates to its new band over 200–280 ms (scaled by distance: 200 ms for one band, +40 ms per additional band, capped 280 ms) `EaseOutCubic`. Stagger is by physical adjacency, 24 ms per slip from the disturbance point outward — the slip nearest the gap moves first, the wave runs down the stack like weight running down paper.
2. *Land:* each slip overshoots its band by 1 dp and returns over 60 ms `EaseOutQuad`, and as it lands its perforation re-draws — dashes re-ink left to right in 90 ms — because a slip that has moved has a new tear line against new neighbours.

The entire settle is announced by **one** haptic only: a **knock 12 ms @ 0.6** when the *last* displaced slip lands. One event, one knock — a per-row buzz chorus would turn a paper sound into a phone sound. Order numerals re-print at the end of travel, struck all at once with a 40 ms alpha step (0 → 1, no fade curve — print appears). Sound during settle: **one soft paper tap** (low-pass thock, 50 ms) synced to the final landing, nothing during travel.

**Reorder — lift a slip out of the stack.** Long-press (280 ms) lifts the slip: tone steps up one further step (still ≤ `#4A423C` cap), grain travelling with it, a **tick 8 ms @ 0.5** at lift. Neighbouring slips part in real time, opening a gap of exactly one band height wherever the lifted slip hovers past a boundary — the stack shows a hole where the slip *would* land, so the drop target is a physical absence, not a highlight bar. **Tick 5 ms @ 0.3** each time the hover crosses a band boundary. Drop: the slip lands (travel-and-land phases as in the settle, single-slip case), neighbours close, numerals re-print. Cancel (drag to the sheet's top edge and hold 300 ms): the slip settles home to its original band; no state was ever written during the drag — reorder commits only on drop, so a cancelled reorder has literally nothing to undo. Reorder, like all queue editing, is unsealed-only.

**Delete — the same tear, with no completion meaning.** A queued task he no longer wants is removed by the identical tear gesture. Paper does not distinguish "done" from "discarded" and neither does the sheet — both are a slip torn out before its session ran. This is truthful: the app never records *why* a slip left the queue, and inventing a gentler delete animation (a fade, a trash icon) would imply a softer, recoverable kind of removal that doesn't exist. Tear is tear. (The session-end receipt reports deflections and completions from *sessions*; an un-run slip was never in a session and appears nowhere — one fact, one home, and this fact has none.)

**Scrolling — the sheet moves as one object.** The stack scrolls vertically; perforations, grain and print all move locked together (grain is slip-local; the exposed sheet above the first slip and below the composer carries the base sheet grain seeded to the sheet itself). Overscroll at the top: the sheet's top edge — straight-cut, with a 1 px catch-light — pulls down over the darker app ground behind it, 1:1 with finger, max 48 dp, and springs back in 320 ms `EaseOutCubic` with a single **tick 6 ms @ 0.35**. That glimpse of edge is the only time the sheet admits it is a sheet lying on something — rationed to a gesture he makes twice a day.

**Empty queue — print the truth.** Zero queued slips: the sheet shows the blank composer slip and, above it where the stack would be, one line in Atkinson tier 2: *"Nothing queued."* No illustration, no empty-state art, no suggestion list. It is a fact, stated once, in the place the fact concerns.

**Mechanism / data source.** Queue = the persisted ordered task list; position is index, completion is removal, reorder is index change on drop. Every visual state above is a pure function of (list, head position, drag state, sealed state). No derived counters, no percentages, no "x of y" rendering anywhere on the surface.

**Standards check.**
- *ARM cost:* zero on the arm path. START on the head slip is one tap on an already-rendered element — and it is a *commit fill*, not a detour: it hands the head task to the Deck's arm flow, it does not add a screen. Editing gestures (tear, lift, reorder) are all unreachable while sealed, so none of them can ever stand between cold open and sealed.
- *Mid-session decisions:* zero. During a sealed session the queue is a read-only printed order: no tear, no lift, no composer, no prompts. The tear-to-complete gesture is available only between sessions — which is when the fact it records (he did this task outside a block, or is clearing the sheet) is a rested-user fact.
- *Truth risk:* two, both closed. (1) The head slip's tone step could be misread as "progress" — it isn't and can't be: it is binary (top of stack or not), it never varies with time or completion fraction, and nothing else on the surface uses tone to mean state. (2) Numerals re-printing only at settle-end means a mid-settle glance could pair a slip with a stale numeral for ≤280 ms — accepted and bounded: numerals strike only when every slip is at rest, so a numeral is never *wrong at rest*, which is the only state in which he reads them.

### Runner-up — THE FOLDED SHEET: the queue concertina-folded, only the working fold open

The queue is one long sheet folded into three panels — *Now* (head 3 slips), *Today* (next ~7), *Later* (the rest) — stacked concertina-style at rest, showing only the Now panel. Pull a fold's crease down and that panel unfolds (rotationX −84° → 0°, 280 ms per panel, `EaseOutCubic` with 6° overshoot, **tick 7 ms @ 0.45** per hinge landing, soft paper *thwip* ~70 ms per panel), revealing its slips; the composer rides on the last panel's foot.

**Why it lost.** It hides facts behind a gesture. The queue's length, its Later contents, even whether Later exists at all, become things he must unfold to know — a second, effortful rendering of facts the flat stack states at a glance, for free. Worse, fold boundaries force a classification decision (which panel does a new task join? when does a Today slip become a Now slip?) that the flat stack never asks: position answers everything there. The fold is beautiful machinery solving a density problem this queue — ten blocks a day — does not have. Density he doesn't suffer from must not be paid for with truth he can currently see.

**Guard fork.** None for placement, one for content: under either Guard outcome the queue itself is unchanged — slips, composer, settle, all identical. The fork only touches what a slip may *reference*: if Guard survives as its own surface, a slip stays pure text and never grows a per-task blocked-app affordance (that fact's home is Guard's); if Guard is dissolved into Shield/chrome, a slip still stays pure text — per-task blocking shortcuts on rows would put a settings decision on the execution surface, against HOLD's spirit even between sessions. His call on Guard; the sheet argues, from its lens, that a slip should carry a task and nothing else.

## Too good to lose

**1. THE STUB — a torn task leaves its stub on the sheet until the next arm.** When a slip tears off, a 14 dp stub strip remains at the tear position for the rest of the unsealed period: task title in Anonymous Pro micro-caps, jagged torn top edge (24-point seeded polygon matching the tear), one tone step darker than the sheet (it is the part that was underneath). It is *not* an archive: at the next arm, all stubs clear in one 200 ms collapse, and they exist nowhere else — no screen, no list, no history. It buys one thing the chosen form deliberately gave up: between tearing a task off and arming the next block, the sheet still shows *that something was finished here*, in place, in order — consequence you can see while it is still today's consequence. It lost the chosen slot only because at rest a stub is a second rendering of a completion the session receipt will state once, truthfully, at session end — and TELL THE TRUTH once outranks my sentiment. Specced so it can be revived whole if Sarthak ever wants in-place evidence: stub height 14 dp, collapse 180 ms `EaseOutCubic`, **settle knock 12 ms @ 0.7** on tear, stub landing tap 50 ms.

**2. THE DOG-EAR — fold a slip's corner to send it to the bottom.** Drag a slip's bottom-right corner diagonally inward: a triangular fold (side ~36 dp) peels across the slip's corner, paper-back tone (−1 step, grain mirrored) on the fold's underside, driven 1:1 by the drag over a 44 dp path, `EaseOutCubic` 160 ms snap if released past half, **tick 6 ms @ 0.4** at snap, soft paper *flick* 60 ms. Release committed: the slip sinks to the queue's foot (full settle behaviour, numerals re-print) and — the detail that matters — it keeps a faint 1 px crease diagonal where the fold was, alpha 0.12, for the rest of the day. Deferring is the one queue decision that is currently menu-shaped everywhere; the dog-ear makes it one flick, and the persisting crease tells the truth that this slip was put off once, without a counter, a colour, or a "deferred" label. It stays here rather than in the chosen form for one honest reason: it adds a second reorder gesture alongside lift-and-drag, and two ways to move a slip is one more than the surface needs until he asks for a defer verb specifically.
