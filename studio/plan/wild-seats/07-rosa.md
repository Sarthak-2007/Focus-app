## Deck — Start-up self-check and arming ergonomics

The problem in the current renders: arming is one slide and three separate count-pills (`HELD 02`, `GUARD 1/4`, `SHUT 07 APPS`) that can disagree with each other and with reality — three renderings of related facts, none of them checked at the moment of commitment. A car solves this at the key: the bulb check proves the warning lamps work, the lamps then report queried systems, and an interlock (gear in Park, clutch in) physically refuses a start that cannot be real. Monolith's arm should do exactly that much, in parallel with the slide, adding zero seconds.

### Chosen form — Parallel bulb-check detent slide

**Mechanism and state/data source.**
The existing slide-to-arm stays the only arming gesture. It gains two felt detents and one hard interlock, and a single check strip — three flush cells directly above the slide track, labelled in letterpressed Anonymous Pro 9sp: `BLOCK`, `APPS`, `TIME`. This strip is the *one home* for arming readiness; the `HELD`/`GUARD` pills leave the Deck top bar (counts that matter mid-session live in Shield/chrome, per settled law — the Deck keeps only what the arm needs). **⚑ LEDGER-OPEN:** the `HELD` count those pills carried is a Monolith-native fact (deflections absorbed this session/day) whose long-term home depends on Sarthak's Ledger call — the rebuilt tab under option (a), a Shield section or chrome corner under (b), or no home beyond the live session count under (c). My strip decision does not depend on the answer: removing the pills from the Deck only kills a duplicate, possibly-stale rendering. It must not be read as voting the record itself out of existence.

Checks are dispatched on slide touch-down (and re-polled at the first detent) and run **in parallel with the drag** — never as a serial sequence before or after it. Each cell reports a live query, nothing stored, nothing assumed:

- **BLOCK** — the blocking stack is actually alive: accessibility service enabled *and* the shield/blocking service running, queried at that moment.
- **APPS** — enforced count vs. loadout count, from the blocking service's own report: renders `SHUT 6 OF 7` if that is the truth. The stored label in a database is not a source.
- **TIME** — finish time recomputed from the wheel's current total (block × count, the Deck's one big number): if the finish crosses a routine boundary, the cell says so in words (`CROSSES SLEEP 20:00`) — pre-arm information for the rested user, not a mid-session interruption later.

Lamp behaviour is the car's bulb check, honestly scoped: on touch-down all three cells light together for one frame — that proves the *rendering* works. Each cell then flips to its true state the moment its query returns: pass = fill drops back out, word stays ink (quiet); fail = fill stays, word becomes the fault (`BLOCK OFF`, `APPS 6/7`, the crossing line). There is **no fixed stagger** — my wave-1 spec extinguished lamps at choreographed 90ms intervals regardless of when data arrived; that is theatre and it is cut. If the data is instant, the check is instant and invisible, which is the correct outcome on a healthy morning. A query that times out (1500ms) renders the word `UNKNOWN` — never a pass.

**The detents (the ergonomics).**
Slide travel is full-width, commit threshold at 0.65 of travel, as already settled.

- **35% — poll detent.** A light tick in the thumb. Crossing it re-polls any cell still `UNKNOWN`. No resistance change; the thumb learns this position in two days and never looks again.
- **65% — commit detent, and the interlock point.** If BLOCK has failed, the cap meets a hard stop here: it cannot enter the final zone, and one Atkinson 13sp line appears under the track — `BLOCKING IS OFF — NOTHING WILL BE HELD.` Release returns the cap. This is the gear-not-in-Park refusal: a session that would block nothing must not be sealable, because sealing it would print a lie for 50 minutes. APPS and TIME failures are **advisory only** — the cap passes, the session arms, the failed cell stays lit in the strip, which dims but remains on the running Deck as the fault's one home. A car starts with a check-engine light on; it does not start with no engine.
- **Final ~14dp — the crank zone.** Drag gain drops to 0.6× (the thumb travels further than the cap: spring-load felt as travel, since a phone cannot measure force). Releasing the cap anywhere past 82% lets it complete the last of its travel itself in 110ms — the latch comes to the thumb, he does not push it home. Release between 65% and 82% springs the cap back to rest; nothing half-arms, ever.

The session clock starts at the crank-release frame, not after the seal animation — what little ceremony remains costs zero focus time.

**Timing budget (the standards arithmetic).** Touch-down → queries dispatched: 0ms. Typical local query return: <150ms, hidden inside a 500–900ms human drag. Seal settle: 190ms, overlapped with the already-running clock. Typical cold-open-to-sealed: **~1.2s**; worst case with one cell at its 1500ms timeout, cap waiting at the detent: **~2.5s**. Both far inside the 10-second arm law, with no serial wait inserted anywhere.

**Motion.** Lamp fills flip in one frame, no easing — a bulb does not fade. Cap travel is 1:1 finger tracking outside the crank zone (easing under a held finger reads as lag). Crank-zone auto-complete: 110ms, cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.9, 0.25, 1.0). Interlock return and sub-65% return: spring stiffness 380, damping ratio 0.62, ~260ms. Seal (carried from my wave-1 door thunk, because it was already honest): the Deck composition drops 2dp and stops dead over 190ms, cubic-bezier(0.7, 0.0, 0.85, 0.3), no overshoot, no bounce — then stillness.

**Haptics.** Poll detent: tick **5ms @ 0.4**. Crank-zone entry: **7ms @ 0.6**. Pushed against the interlock stop: knock **16ms @ 1.0**, and continued pushing gives a rough scrape **4ms @ 0.25 every 30ms**, capped at 200ms — refusal feels cheap and gritty, success feels expensive. Advisory pass at commit (fault lit, session arming anyway): nothing extra — the fault is visual, the hand is not nagged. Seal: pre-tick **6ms @ 0.4**, 28ms silence, thunk **28ms @ 1.0**, then **12ms @ 0.5** and **8ms @ 0.25** at 20ms intervals — mass settling, three descending strikes.

**Sound.** Explicit silence. The first arm of his day happens around 05:00 in a sleeping house, and a self-check that chirps is a self-check he will mute and then distrust. Haptics carry the whole event.

**Depth/material.** The check strip is one recess, one tone step below the Deck ground, closed by a 1px lighter hairline on its bottom edge only. Cells are flush inlays inside it, separated by hairlines, never boxes. Night: ground `#171412`, cell fills capped at `#4A423C`, lit-fault text at the cream tiers (≈11 / 5.6 / 3.4:1) — a lit cell reads by fill-flip and word, never by glow, halo, or colour. Orange appears exactly once on this surface: the slide cap's commit fill, as settled. The check cells are never orange, in any state — orange here would advertise faults.

### Runner-up — Full rev-sweep self-check (my wave-1 Cold Start, cut down)

The readout and wheel sweep full-scale and back (680ms), lamps extinguish at a fixed 90ms stagger, the needle hunts with two decaying wobbles (350ms) before the timer starts; total ≈1,090ms serial, gated before the clock. It survives as runner-up because the *lamp test* instinct inside it was right. It loses on truth and on tax: the sweep moves identically whether or not anything was queried (a needle sweeping proves nothing), the fixed stagger fakes sequential checking that did not happen, the hunt wobble perturbs the readout — the app's one big number — for effect, and 1,090ms inserted in series on every arm of every day is ceremony taxing the daily path, which the settled law forbids. Its one sound bone — a lamp that stays lit only for a real fault — is absorbed into the chosen form.

### Guard fork

- **If Guard is dissolved:** nothing about the check changes. BLOCK and APPS read the blocking service and the Shield list directly; their detailed state already lives where he looks (Shield/chrome). The strip never needed a Guard screen to point at.
- **If Guard stays a destination:** the strip is still readout-only on the Deck — no cell is a button during the arm, and pre-arm a lit cell may be tapped to open Guard at the relevant row (a rested-user action, before any session exists). The check itself, its data sources, and the interlock are identical under both outcomes. His call; the mechanism does not care.

### Standards check

- **Arm-path cost:** 0ms added serial time; ~1.2s typical, ~2.5s worst case, against a 10s ceiling. One gesture, as before.
- **Mid-session decisions introduced:** none. Every branch (interlock refusal, advisory fault, routine crossing) resolves before the seal, where the rested user is. Mid-session the lit strip is a readout with no actions on it.
- **Truth risk:** counts come from the blocking service's live report, not stored strings; timeout renders `UNKNOWN`, never pass; the one rendering of readiness is this strip, and the top-bar pills it replaces are removed, not duplicated. Residual risk: a service that reports itself healthy while failing to block an individual app — mitigated by APPS comparing enforced-vs-loadout per package, not trusting a single service-level boolean.

## Deck (running) — The banked 60-second emergency valve as a physical control

The automotive facts that govern this control: the hazard switch is the largest, most central control in the cabin, findable without looking, shaped unlike anything near it — because it is operated by frightened people. A dead-man's switch needs no confirmation dialog: holding *is* the intent, releasing *is* the withdrawal. And every control a panicked hand touches is debounced, because tremor is not intent. The settled valve (60 seconds total, checkpoints at 15/30/45, slip drains rather than zeroes, one use per week) is exactly a dead-man's switch with a memory. Here it is as one physical object.

### Chosen form — Full-width hold bar with banked detents and a draining tail

**Where it lives, and its two sizes.** On the running Deck, the bottom edge carries a quiet entry strip: full screen width, 56dp tall, one tone step recessed, one line of Atkinson 12sp — `EMERGENCY — PRESS AND HOLD`. No orange, no icon, no pulsing; a control he must ignore for 50 minutes at a time has to be ignorable. The moment contact lands on it, the strip expands upward to the active hold surface: **full width × 112dp** (well above the 88dp minimum the banked-hold decision demands), expansion 160ms, cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.9, 0.25, 1.0), and — critically — **hold time accrues from the first contact frame, not from the end of the expansion**. The animation never eats his seconds. Held time is measured on a monotonic clock, immune to wall-clock changes.

Any contact holds it: thumb, finger, palm edge, two fingers, a hand gone shaky and sliding — the contact centroid is ignored, multi-touch counts as contact, and there is no sub-target inside the bar to aim at or miss. The whole surface is the pedal.

**Checkpoints — the banked detents.** The bar's surface carries its scale printed on itself: three notch marks at the 15 / 30 / 45 positions and the word `BANKED` beside each notch once earned. State is two numbers, kept separate because they behave differently:

- `banked` ∈ {0, 15, 30, 45} — the floor. Earned the frame held time crosses a checkpoint.
- `tail` — held seconds accrued above the floor in the current grip.

Displayed progress = `banked + tail`, rendered as fill travelling the bar left-to-right plus the one hero numeral above the bar (Anonymous Pro, counting **up**: `HELD 0:37`). The numeral is the fact's one rendering; the fill is position, not a second total, and the notches are the scale it travels against. At each checkpoint crossing, the earned segment's fill locks solid (it will not drain — see below), the bar gives a visible 2dp step-down under the thumb like a pedal passing a detent, and the word for that notch flips to `BANKED`. Banking alone **never consumes the weekly break** — only completion does. A bank earned and abandoned is intent demonstrated, not a break taken, and the record must say exactly that.

**Drain behaviour — the slip, specified exactly.** Contact lost, for any reason (lift, slide-off, tremor break):

1. **800ms grace — no drain at all.** This is the debounce. A bounce, a readjustment, a knuckle shifting: none of it costs anything, and re-grip inside the grace resumes accrual from the exact displayed value.
2. **Then the tail drains at 2 banked-seconds per real second** — displayed fill visibly retreats toward the floor, and a draining tick (**every 250ms @ 0.2**, descending) lets him *feel* the loss without looking, so re-grip is a hand decision, not an eye decision. Re-grip at any moment halts the drain on that frame and resumes accrual from wherever the value has fallen to.
3. **The floor never drains.** Drain stops dead at `banked`. If the tail reaches the floor and no re-grip comes within 10s, the attempt closes: the bar collapses to the entry strip, which now truthfully reads `BANKED 30 — PRESS AND HOLD TO CONTINUE` for the rest of that session. Banked progress persists for the remainder of the session only; it resets at session end (completion, abandonment, or the valve completing) and never carries into another session, never mints anything, and is rendered nowhere else — this strip is its one home.

**Feedback — fully eyes-off operable.** Counting on a clock you must stare at fails the exact user this control exists for, so every milestone is in the hand: a soft tick **4ms @ 0.3 every 5 held seconds** (the metronome — he can count without a screen); at each checkpoint a double detent knock, **10ms @ 0.8**, 30ms gap, **14ms @ 1.0** (the bank landing is the second-heaviest event in the control); from 50 held seconds, one tick per second, amplitude climbing **0.5 → 0.9** across the final ten. Visual feedback runs in parallel for a steady hand: fill, hero numeral, notch words — all driven by the same progress value, updated per frame with **no easing anywhere** (an eased timer is a lying timer; fill position is direct-drive 1:1 with measured time, and drain movement is linear at the drain rate).

**Completion.** At 60.0 held seconds the bar unlatches — my wave-1 door thunk run in reverse, because opening a sealed cabin is heavier than closing one: the running Deck's seal line draws itself backwards and the composition rises 2dp over **420ms**, cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.6, 0.3, 1.0), with the unlatch haptic **18ms @ 0.6 then 10ms @ 0.9** (release is sharper than closure). Blocking lifts at the completion frame, the weekly use is written at that frame and no earlier, and the session's one truthful receipt states actual elapsed time and `BREAK USED`. **⚑ LEDGER-OPEN — this is the dependency that matters most in my file:** the spent hold is a Monolith-native fact (YPT will never know it happened) and the *record* — timestamp, session it ended, week it belongs to — must be written to the app's own store at the completion frame under **all three** Ledger options; what the Ledger decision changes is only where that record is *displayed* afterwards. Under (a) it is a line in the rebuilt Ledger tab; under (b) a line in the demoted sheet/Shield section; under (c) it is displayed nowhere beyond the receipt at the moment of use and the bar's current-week state word — the data still exists (the weekly cap is unenforceable without it), it simply has no archive surface. My valve spec is identical in all three worlds; only the receipt's afterlife is his call. If the week already holds a used break, the entry strip reads `BREAK USED — BACK [reset day]` in word-state and contact on it does nothing but re-render that line: a spent control that still invites a 60-second hold would be a trap and a lie.

**Sound.** Explicit silence, in every state, regardless of the app's sound setting elsewhere. This control is used at his worst moment, possibly at 3 AM, possibly in a quiet house; its entire feedback budget is spent on haptics and one numeral.

**Depth/material.** Both strip and bar are recesses below the running ground (night ground `#171412`, recess one step under it), hairline on the top edge where the thumb's light would catch. Held fill caps at `#4A423C` by night; banked segments render as solid cream fill at text tier — the visual difference between *banked* (solid, permanent) and *tail* (capped-tone, drainable) is density, readable at a glance and learnable in one use. Checkpoint notches are printed scale on the moving surface's track, square-cornered, no pills. Zero orange anywhere on this control: orange is a commit fill with exactly two sanctioned homes, and colouring the emergency exit like a commit button would both break the ration and advertise the exit all session.

### Runner-up — The hazard pad

One large pad, 96 × 96dp, centred low on the running Deck, distinct rounded-square form, hold logic identical (same checkpoints, grace, drain, banking). It is the honest automotive citation in shape — cars really do use one big button for this — and it loses on the body, not the idea: a pad must be *found* (aim costs 200–400ms and one look, at the moment he can least spare either), the pressing thumb then occludes the pad's own surface where its feedback would live, and a single contact point rolls off an edge far more easily than a full-width bar slips — every roll-off is a slip event the drain then taxes. The bar converts the entire bottom of the phone into the target and lets any part of the hand pay the 60 seconds. The pad's one virtue — a form unlike anything else on screen — the bar keeps by scale and recess instead of outline.

### Guard fork

The valve itself never moves: it lives on the running Deck under both outcomes, because an emergency control filed inside a settings destination is not an emergency control. What forks is only its bookkeeping. **If Guard stays:** the weekly rule (`1 PER WEEK`, reset day) is stated as a word row in Guard, and the valve's used/available state is rendered there too — outside a session. **If Guard is dissolved:** the same word row sits in the settings drawer; during a session the bar's own state word remains the state's one home. Under neither Guard outcome does the valve gain settings or a reason picker. **⚑ LEDGER-OPEN:** whether the valve gains a *history* is precisely the reopened question and I no longer presume it closed — if Sarthak keeps or demotes the Ledger, the hold record belongs there (see the completion flag above); if he kills it, the valve carries no history and the current-week state word is the whole visible truth. What I do presume, under every option: the history, if it exists, lives in that one place — never duplicated onto the valve itself.

### Standards check

- **Arm-path cost:** none — this is an exit control; it adds nothing to arming and is inert (a flat strip) for the whole session.
- **Mid-session decisions introduced:** exactly one — the single sanctioned decision the settled law already made (use the break or don't). Inside the control there are no branches at all: no confirm, no reason, no cancel button. Holding is the decision, releasing is the withdrawal, completing is the outcome. AOD shows nothing of it (true black, no interaction surface on a locked clock).
- **Truth risk:** held time from a monotonic clock, checkpoints written only at true crossings, weekly consumption written only at the completion frame, banking rendered in exactly one place and expiring truthfully at session end. The receipt reports actual elapsed session time — no rounding up to the planned block, no ghost minutes from the abandoned remainder. **⚑ LEDGER-OPEN:** the one truth risk this element cannot close on its own — if the Ledger is killed *and* no receipt surface survives, the spent-hold fact becomes invisible the moment the session ends while still being enforced (bar reads `BREAK USED` next session with no visible provenance). Enforcement without a readable record is the one dishonest configuration available here; options (a) and (b) both avoid it, and option (c) avoids it only if the session-end receipt is kept as a surface.

## Too good to lose

**1. The H-gate as Shield's loadout selector (pre-arm only).** My wave-1 gated shifter, kept off the Deck deliberately: on Shield, changing loadout tier requires a two-axis gesture — the cap pops *up* out of its slot (spring stiffness 900, damping 28, 110ms; tick **6ms @ 0.5**), travels a neutral channel (tick **5ms @ 0.4** per gate tooth crossed), and drops *down* into the next tier (clack **10ms @ 0.8**, 18ms gap, thunk **22ms @ 1.0**); a diagonal push scrapes (4ms @ 0.25 every 30ms) and selects nothing. Parked, not chosen, for one reason: it is a rested-user control. On Shield it makes a consequential change physically hard to do by accident at zero cost to anyone's day; anywhere near the arm path it would tax the 10-second law. Silence for sound; gate plate as flush inlay, slots as recesses, orange only as the engaged slot's marker line for 600ms after seating.

**2. Steering weight on the wheel.** Progressive resistance as information: past the 45-minute region of the wheel's arc, drag gain falls from 1.0 toward 0.4 at the 90-minute end-stop, detent ticks fatten from **5ms @ 0.4** to **7ms @ 0.7**, and the end-stop itself answers with a knock **16ms @ 1.0** — the thumb learns that 90 minutes is a serious place to go before the eyes read the number. No animation (physics lives in the drag mapping, rendering stays 1:1 with the resisted value), no sound, rim teeth bunching ×0.92 on the loaded side drawn per-frame. Parked because wheel physics is already owned by the flywheel work; if that slips, weight alone — one mapping function — delivers most of its honesty.
