# Vega — Wild, physics

Assignment: the Deck duration wheel, the hub / block-count behaviour, and the total readout dynamics. One integrator runs all three — the wheel is a flywheel, the hub is a lighter second body coupled to it, and the readout is the still witness that only ever prints committed values. Nothing here carries a story. Everything here carries mass.

Ground rules applied throughout: the wheel's scale is stepped and printed honestly (a value that cannot be set is never displayed); block length lives **only** as rim position against the printed scale, count lives **only** as hub pips, the total lives **only** as the hero readout; orange appears nowhere on any of these three elements — Deck's single orange COMMIT fill stays on the slide, untouched; depth is one tone step of recess plus a 1px catch-light, never shadow; night ground `#171412`, control fills capped at `#4A423C`, text at the settled tiers; type is Anonymous Pro for every numeral and rim figure, Atkinson Hyperlegible for words, Sora nowhere on the instrument face.

---

## Deck — duration wheel

**Chosen form — the stepped flywheel: 36 stops, one true scale, a spin that ends the same way every time**

- **Mechanism / data source.** One state variable, `blockMinutes`, owned by the Deck's session state; wheel angle is *derived* from it (`angle = f(stopIndex)`), never stored separately, so the wheel can never desync from the value it claims to show. The scale has **36 stops over a 315° sweep (9° per stop)**, with a 45° dead sector at 6 o'clock where the thumb parks and the slide lives below. Stop 0 is **FLOW** — a mode, not a minute value. Stops 1–35 are **10, 15, 20 … 180 minutes** in 5-minute steps. Between FLOW and 10 sits a printed **axis-break** (two short parallel hairlines across the rim): the one place where angular distance does *not* mean minutes, marked as a break instead of lying about it. Everywhere else the scale is strictly linear — 9° always means 5 minutes. Tick hierarchy on the rim: 5-minute stops short, 15-minute stops medium, 30-minute stops long with the numeral printed on the rim itself (30 / 60 / 90 / 120 / 150 / 180, Anonymous Pro). The current render's orange thumb dot dies; the contact point is an ink pip with a single marker line, and the fixed index mark sits at 12 o'clock on the panel — the scale moves, the datum doesn't.
- **Input.** Edge-drag on the rim annulus only (the hub disc is a separate hit target), 1:1 with the finger, zero easing under a held finger — any easing there reads as lag and kills the mass. Velocity is genuinely tracked and genuinely used. Tapping a printed rim numeral commands the same physics with an initial velocity toward that stop, so tap and drag share one identical ending. Accessibility actions step ±5 minutes; no hidden gestures, no long-press secrets.
- **Motion.** One physics loop, state (θ, ω). While dragging: θ follows the finger. On release: viscous decay `ω *= exp(−dt/τ)`, τ ≈ 0.38 s, plus Coulomb friction so the wheel truly stops instead of asymptoting. Detents are magnetic wells effective within ±4° of a stop: below a capture velocity of ~540°/s the well catches; above it the wheel blows through with a tick — one rule producing both the ratchet and the seat. Final capture is an underdamped spring, stiffness ≈ 210, ζ ≈ 0.38: exactly two decaying overshoots (+1 stop, −½ stop), settled in ≤ 520 ms. Total coast is clamped — a hardest-possible fling from stop 0 to stop 35 lands and seats inside **1,400 ms**. Grabbing the rim mid-coast is a brake: τ drops to 0.12 s under the finger and the wheel seats at the nearest stop on release. End stops are walls with sag: 8° of rubber-band overshoot past FLOW or 180, spring return in ~300 ms, one low knock, then nothing — further outward drags are silent and move nothing, so a wall never feels like a dead touchscreen.
- **Haptics.** Detent crossing under drag or coast: **5 ms @ 0.35**, rate-limited to one per 12 ms so a fast spin is texture, not a buzz-saw; above ~720°/s crossings collapse to **12 ms @ 0.4** per 45° of travel. 15-minute majors: **8 ms @ 0.55**. Final seat: knock **20 ms @ 0.95** plus a trailing **6 ms @ 0.3** at +40 ms — the pawl dropping in. End-stop wall: **18 ms @ 0.9**, once. Hub overshoot peaks: silence, deliberately — the seat knock lands because nothing competed with it.
- **Sound.** None. Shipped silent at any hour; his first set of the day happens in a sleeping house at ~03:40, and the seat knock already exists in the hand. No ratchet samples, no seat thock.
- **Depth / material.** The wheel sits in a one-tone-step recess below the Deck ground (below `#171412` at night — recess goes down, never up), closed by a 1px catch-light on the top/left edge. The knurl is 120 radial teeth whose highlight is computed from each tooth's **absolute screen angle against one fixed light direction**, not from wheel angle — teeth flare entering the light zone and die leaving it, so during a fast spin the rim aliases into a shimmer band that *is* the speed readout, and at rest the wheel looks lit by the room rather than painted. No orange, no glow, no shadow at any speed.
- **Guard fork.** None, in both outcomes. The wheel never displays, tints by, or gates on Guard state — no readiness colour, no permission count, no poll. If Guard is dissolved into chrome + Shield, nothing here changes; if Guard survives as a destination, nothing here changes. A session arms from this wheel regardless; blocking state is reported where blocking lives.
- **Standards check.** *Arm-path cost:* setting a block is one drag or one fling — 0.3–1.4 s worst case, inside the ≤10 s arm budget with the slide still to come. *Mid-session decisions:* zero — on seal the wheel's input dies entirely and the rim rests at its stop for the session's duration; it cannot be edited, nudged, or renegotiated mid-session. *Truth risk:* the stepped scale plus the axis-break are the whole defence — the wheel physically cannot point at a value that doesn't exist, and FLOW is printed as a word, never as a fake "5".

**Runner-up — the ratchet wheel (drag only, no coast)**

Same 36-stop scale, same wells, but release velocity is discarded: the wheel only moves while the finger moves, with heavier detent ticks (**7 ms @ 0.5**) and a 180 ms spring seat over the final half-stop. *Why it lost:* crossing the scale (10 → 150 for a long block) takes three or four drag strokes instead of one fling, and those strokes are paid every single session, at 03:40, forever. The flywheel's coast isn't decoration — it's the fastest honest route across a 36-stop instrument, and its two-overshoot ending is learnable in a way a dead stop never is. The ratchet is the fallback if fling tuning ever feels unstable on his handset; it is not the ambition.

## Deck — hub / block-count behaviour

**Chosen form — the counterweight hub: pips only, press-and-release, and a visible lag behind the rim**

- **Mechanism / data source.** One state variable, `blockCount`, range **1–4** (the existing cycle, kept). The hub (Ø ~108 dp, concentric with the wheel) renders count **only as pips**: four pip wells arranged on a short arc across the hub face, filled pips = count, empty wells = the remaining headroom. No numeral, no "×N", no arithmetic anywhere on the hub. A single static micro-label `BLOCKS` (Anonymous Pro caps, ~8–9 sp, letterpress — set in the surface's own darkened tone) is printed once on the hub face so a first-time user knows what the pips count; the label never changes, animates, or carries a value. Four is inside the subitising range — the count is *perceived*, not read, which is why the cap of 4 is a feature and not a limit to apologise for.
- **The second body.** The hub is a separate physical body coupled to the rim by a weak torsional spring with an upright rest position. When the rim spins, the hub **lags**: up to ~6° behind at peak rim velocity, overshooting into a few degrees of counter-rotation as the rim seats, then settling upright within ~400 ms on its own spring (ζ ≈ 0.45). Pips ride the hub, so during a spin the count is briefly unreadable and at rest it is exact — which is honest, because during a spin the count is not what you're setting. The hub always returns to upright; a count you have to tilt your head to read would be a lie of orientation.
- **The press.** Tap = press-and-release, committed **on release**, like a real pushbutton with a cancel: on DOWN the hub depresses — its recess deepens one tone step and the catch-light flips to the bottom edge over 90 ms, haptic **4 ms @ 0.5** at the bottom of the travel. On UP within the hub's bounds, count cycles 1→2→3→4→1 and the newly filled (or emptied) pip prints with the same ink-settle as the readout (88% → 100% ink over 120 ms). Drag off the hub before release: the press cancels, no count change, hub springs back over 160 ms (ζ ≈ 0.6), silence. The wrap 4→1 gets **no** special sound, animation, or warning — it is the identical press, and the hero readout instantly showing the smaller total is all the feedback the truth requires. Long-press does nothing. While the rim is being dragged or coasting, hub input is dead — rotation and count never fight over one touch.
- **FLOW interaction.** Under FLOW, count is meaningless, so the hub says so: pips dim to ~40% ink, the press state is disabled (DOWN produces no dip, no haptic, no change). A control that silently does nothing while looking alive is a small lie; a visibly resting control is a fact.
- **Haptics.** Press bottom: **4 ms @ 0.5**. Commit on release: **6 ms @ 0.6**. Cancel: silence. Coupled lag/overshoot: silence — the hub's motion is seen, never buzzed.
- **Sound.** None.
- **Depth / material.** Hub face sits one tone step *up* from the wheel's recess (it is the part the finger lands on), edge defined by a hairline, not a shadow. Pip wells are 1-step recesses; a filled pip is ink at full strength — fill is the state, position is the meaning.
- **Guard fork.** None, either outcome — the hub carries no Guard data and no Guard consequence.
- **Standards check.** *Arm-path cost:* one tap per added block, ~250 ms each; his common cases (×2, ×3 for the fasted block) cost under a second. *Mid-session decisions:* zero — hub input dies on seal with the wheel's. *Truth risk:* pips are the count's only rendering anywhere in the app; the instant any other surface prints the count as digits, this element's truth case collapses — that includes the slide, which under this spec carries **no count and no total**.

**Runner-up — the numeral hub, finished properly**

Keep `2×`-style digits but do them honestly: real-weight Anonymous Pro, a proper press dip (1 dp tonal, 90 ms in / 160 ms out), the same torsional lag, count committed on release. *Why it lost:* it restates a factor whose product is already the hero readout — `25 × 2` printed at the dial's centre is arithmetic the total has already closed over, and a digit at the centre competes with the one number that matters. Pips are position-state, readable in peripheral vision without a fixation; digits demand a read. The numeral hub is better than the current sticker hub and still worse than saying the count in geometry.

## Deck — total readout dynamics

**Chosen form — the still witness: a stepped readout with ink-settle, provisional FINISHES, and no rolling digits, ever**

- **Mechanism / data source.** The hero readout renders exactly one fact: `totalMinutes = blockMinutes × blockCount`, computed from the two state variables above — no cached total, no second copy. Format is human language, not stopwatch costume: under 60 minutes it prints `50 MIN`; at and over 60 it prints `2H 30M` (hours in Sora SemiBold, minutes in Anonymous Pro, units as small caps on the same baseline). It never prints `:00` — before the arm there are no seconds to claim, and fake seconds are fake precision. (On seal, the running countdown takes over this same position as a *different state with its own honest seconds*; that hand-over is a state change, not a second rendering.) Beneath it, one derived line: `FINISHES 7:40 AM`, computed from the real clock at the moment of the last commit — that is a different fact (a clock time, not a duration) with its own single home on that line.
- **Dynamics — the core call.** The readout changes **only at detent crossings and hub commits**, in the same frame as the tick or press haptic that caused them. Between detents it does not move, roll, crossfade, or preview — during a drag the rim travels continuously, but the instrument is stepped, so values between stops *do not exist* and the readout refuses to print them. A change is a hard cut with one physical trace: the new glyphs land at 88% ink and settle to 100% over **120 ms** — letterpress ink taking to paper, a print event, not an animation. Legibility is instant (the cut), the settle is felt more than seen. During any wheel motion (drag or coast) the `FINISHES` line dims to ~55% ink: it is provisional while the hand is on the instrument, and printing it at full strength mid-spin would claim a finish time for a total that hasn't seated. On the seat frame, FINISHES recomputes from the seat-time clock and returns to full ink. On a hub commit, the total cuts and ink-settles identically, and FINISHES recomputes in the same frame.
- **FLOW.** The readout prints the word `FLOW` at hero size (Anonymous Pro), and the FINISHES line is replaced by `UNTIL YOU STOP` — the only true sentence available for an open-ended session. No total, no finish, no estimate is shown, because none exists.
- **Haptics.** None of its own — every readout change is already spoken by the wheel tick or hub press that caused it, in the same frame. A readout that buzzes on its own behalf would double-report one event as two.
- **Sound.** None.
- **Depth / material.** The readout lives in the Deck panel's existing recess (one tone step, 1px catch-light). Numerals stay flat at full ink at rest; the only rendering event in its life is the 120 ms ink-settle. No orange — the total is not the commit; the slide below it is.
- **Guard fork.** None, either outcome. The readout shows duration arithmetic only; it never borrows Guard's permission fraction or app count (the current header chips doing exactly that are chrome's problem, not this element's — but nothing here depends on them).
- **Standards check.** *Arm-path cost:* zero — the readout is read, not operated; its glance cost is one fixation because there is exactly one big number on the screen. *Mid-session decisions:* zero — it introduces no branch, prompt, or comparison. *Truth risk:* this element *is* the truth surface, and its risks are named: (1) rolling digits — refused, they print non-existent values; (2) a slide badge repeating the total — refused, the slide carries no number under this spec; (3) preset caps below the wheel highlighting a block length — acceptable only as *control state following the wheel's value*, never as an independent readout; the moment a cap and the rim disagree, the rim is right and the cap is a bug.

**Runner-up — the mechanical rolling counter**

Digits on vertical reels, each column rolling through intermediate values slaved to rim angle, FINISHES sliding with hub lag. It is the most physically romantic option and it lost on truth, not taste: a reel rolling from 50 to 90 passes through 63, 71, 84 — minute values this stepped instrument cannot set — so for ~600 ms per hard spin the hero readout's main job is printing lies beautifully. It also delays legibility (a rolling digit is unreadable until it lands) on the screen he reads at 03:40 with one eye open. The ink-settle cut gives the same "printed by a machine" feeling with zero false values and zero read-time.

## Too good to lose

1. **The pitch ladder (sound-on only).** If a mechanical sound set is ever enabled globally, detent ticks take a pitch mapped across the scale — low at FLOW, rising stop by stop to 180 — so position on the scale is audible with eyes off the wheel, and the seat lands one low thock below the ladder. Fixed pitches per stop (a printed scale of notes), never a continuous glide. Cut from the chosen form only because the shipped default is silence; the mapping costs nothing to keep specced.
2. **The hub as parking brake.** Pressing and holding the hub *during a coast* brakes the wheel hard (τ → 0.08 s) and seats it at the nearest stop — stopping a spin with the thumb that's already at the centre, instead of catching the rim. Fully credible physics and genuinely useful after an over-hard fling. Cut because it overloads the hub's one meaning (count) with a second verb, and the rim-grab brake already covers the case; it stays here in case real use proves the grab awkward one-handed.
