## 2. The wheel — pre-set to 50, by rested hands, announced by position alone

- **Pre-set to 50 minutes** — his standing block length (the Station
  Dock's default, the day's canonical block: ~10 blocks of 50 across his
  day). On the filed Uhr geometry (Ø288, R144; radial stack hub 0–64 ·
  gap 64–72 · face 72–114 · tick band 114–130 · clearance 130–136 ·
  knurl 136–144), the wheel sits rotated **90° clockwise**, bringing the
  50-minute detent under the one fixed reader: a Ø4.0dp dot at 0°, R133,
  glyph `#EDE3D8`. Scale 5–180 in 5-minute steps, 10° per detent; rim
  numerals majors only (`30 60 90 120 150 180`, Anonymous Pro 700 15sp,
  centred R100) — the rim orients the hand, the hero states the total,
  neither restates the other. Ticks: minor `#776A60`, medium `#9C8E84`,
  major `#D2C6BB`. Knurl: the filed A/B pair (`#4A423C / #241E1A`), lit
  only by the fixed specular rig, behind Old Tom's gate — fail one test
  and the rim ships clean. Disc at recess `#100E0C` with its catch
  hairline. **Zero orange on the wheel, in every state, including this
  one.**
- **By whose action:** rested-him's. He authored the routine on the
  Prep Bench and pressed Save (knock 14ms @ 0.7); the summary line he
  verified at save time — generated from the same draft fields Save
  committed — is the rested rendering of exactly this setting. The
  machine's prime only *carries* his rested decision to this hour. The
  man at 04:58 has set nothing, and the frame never pretends he has.
- **Does it announce its pre-set state? No mark. None.** Position is
  the statement: the wheel AT 50, under the reader, *is* the fact,
  rendered once, in the wheel's own grammar. I considered and killed
  every candidate announcement — a filled reader dot in accent (spends
  the COMMIT fill on information; dead on the accent law alone), a
  plate word under the wheel (`SET BY ROUTINE` — a second rendering of
  primed-ness, and a legend a half-awake man must read to learn what
  his eyes already took in), a tone lift on the disc (primed-ness
  rendered a third time — wheel position, loadout line, and now light
  all saying "a routine did this"). One fact, one rendering: the
  *setting* renders in the wheel, the *source* renders once, as a time,
  in chrome (§6), and the two never meet on the Deck body. If the wheel
  is ever found at 50 on an unprimed morning, it reads identically —
  and that is correct, because at 50 the two mornings genuinely offer
  the same block; what differs (a window governs) is chrome's fact, not
  the wheel's.
- **Arrival:** on this frame the wheel is already seated — dead still,
  detent caught, no spin-in, no settle animation on first paint. A
  setting that performs its own arrival is claiming an event that did
  not happen at 04:58; it happened at his rested Save. The one
  exception: if the Deck is *already open* when the prime horizon is
  crossed, the wheel seats itself in one 320ms `EaseOutCubic` glide to
  the primed detent, detent ticks silent, **no haptic** — a haptic for

  holding the rim (Teo's rule for unseen drawings, applied to touch).
- The wheel is live, not locked: priming is a preset, not a sentence.
  He may turn it (relative drag, ±3° tremor dead zone per HPA-23) and
  the hero re-strikes at detent granularity, hard cut, never animated.
  But the frame does not invite the turn: at 04:58 the wheel is seated
  work and this is not a seated hour. It asks for nothing.

## 4. The loadout — mise en place: five things laid, each once, no schedule word

"Prepared" on this Deck is a geography, not a sentence. The window has

2. **The count** — in the hub: three filled wells (§3).
3. **The total** — in the hero: `170` MIN, derived live as

## 9. The beauty case, in plain words

A kitchen before service. The rested cook has laid the station in the
dark: knife where the hand falls, pans in firing order, everything
labelled by position and nothing by announcement. The tired cook
walks in, and the room does the remembering.

That is this frame, and it is beautiful the way a laid station is
beautiful — not decorated, *ready*. One big number in the brightest
ink the night palette owns, read without waking fully. One word under
it. A wheel sitting at its detent like a dial on a safe that was set
last night by a man who knew what he was doing. Three filled wells.
And at the bottom, under the thumb, the only orange thing in the
world: a verb with nothing written on it, asking for one gesture and
offering, in exchange, three hours in which he will not be asked for
anything else.

The beauty is inseparable from the sameness. He will meet this frame
186 mornings in a row. A frame that performed dawn — special light,
special words, a greeting — would be charming on day three and
unbearable on day forty, and worse, it would be lying: the app does
not know it is dawn for *him*; it knows a window is in approach, and
it renders exactly that, in state, and shuts up. What makes the frame
worth framing is restraint under maximum temptation: at the most
emotional minute of his day, the screen spends nothing — no colour
but the verb's, no motion at all, no sentence longer than a word —
and the one thing it spends, the glyph-bright hero, it spends on the
single fact he came for. Liv's test holds: wound, waiting, beautiful
on arrival, at zero cost. Nacht's eye order holds: total → slide by
thumb → task. If this frame is beautiful, he comes back tomorrow.
That is the whole retention mechanism, and it is built out of
geometry and silence.

## 11. The strongest case against this frame — argued against myself

**1. A primed Deck is a promise, and I have not said loudly enough
what the app owes when it breaks it.** The frame asserts, in state:
*this loadout, this length, this count, this task — slide and it
seals.* Every element of that assertion can be false in a way the
frame cannot see. The routine was **edited** rested but carelessly
(the Prep Bench summary line is the only check, and a man can
misread his own line). It was **paused** for a family day and the
family day became a week (then the frame owes him a free Deck and a
chrome line naming the *next live* firing — my spec delivers both,
but only if pause-state is read at prime time, every time, from the
store and never from a cached prime: I state that here as a build
requirement, because a cached prime is a ghost with good manners).
It is simply **wrong** — test Saturday, illness, a shifted exam —
and here the frame's honesty reaches its limit: it will faithfully
lay out a wrong morning, beautifully. What it owes him then is
exactly two things, and I claim both are in the drawing: one-glance
falsifiability (the loadout word and the hero are the two checkable
facts, both at sizes a half-awake man can actually read — if STUDY
should be SLEEP, he can catch it), and a **free refusal**: he does
not slide, the window closes at 08:00, nothing is recorded, no miss
is minted at window-open, no debt follows him to breakfast (HPA-02).
Walking away is the reject gesture, and it costs nothing. A primed
Deck that charged for its own refusal — a miss, a streak-break, a
word in the evening — would be a trap wearing my station's clothes.
Date exceptions (HPA-07, test days) must suppress the prime entirely
at the source; if this frame ever primes on a test morning, that is
the app's lie, not his error, and no falsifiability argument excuses
it.

**2. Tap-can-commit + a primed frame is an accident waiting for a
fumble.** He reaches for the phone half-asleep, palm-squeezes the
track, and seals 170 minutes he did not choose. My defences are real
but thin: the 40ms brush floor, the track's position in the thumb arc
rather than the grip edge — and the actual load-bearing one, the
**accidental-start grace cancel** (free abort, no token, no record),
which lives outside this frame entirely. If the grace cancel is ever
weakened, the tap path on a primed frame is the first thing that
must be re-argued. I register that dependency in print so it cannot
be lost quietly.

**3. My hero fights Ruth's by exactly one break, and I might be
wrong.** Ruth prints `3H 00M` — the set's total, the window filled.
I print `170` — blocks plus the breaks *between* them, per Critics
T-11's own arithmetic (two blocks: 110, not 120). If the shipped
session model in fact runs a break after the final block *inside*
the session (the 50/10 preset's trailing 10), her number is the true
span and mine under-claims the morning by ten minutes — a truth
failure in the hero, the worst place for one. The fight reduces to
one checkable fact about the session engine: does the third block's
break belong to the session or to the walk to the gym? I have priced
my side from the Critics' text; the merge must price it from the
code, and if the code says trailing, I lose the hero and keep the
frame — every other element is indifferent to the number. Named here
so nobody "resolves" it by averaging: 175 is not a compromise, it is
a lie with a smaller error bar.

**4. The loadout word may not survive the scope test.** My defence
(§4.4) rests on primed-state ≠ stored-config. If the merge finds no
path by which the two can diverge at 04:58 — no override, no
precedence case that reaches dawn — then the word is a second
rendering wearing a scope costume, and it dies under TRUTH. I have
pre-registered the fallback (empty strip, geometry intact) so the
kill is cheap. What I will not concede in advance: that the Deck may
show *nothing* of the loadout and still call itself prepared. If the
word dies, the loadout's dawn presence becomes the slide's check
alone — proof during the gesture, silence before it — and the frame
is poorer in exactly the way a station is poorer when one item is
still in the fridge.

**5. Is identical geometry at 04:58 and 19:30 art, or abdication?**
Sana's composition seats will argue — are already arguing, in her
D-half's dawn light — that the most important frame in the app
deserves a light of its own: the hero already steps up to glyph in
my drawing (her D1, adopted); the next step is a dawn-only
treatment of the wheel or the reserve, and the step after that is a
screen that knows what morning it is. My answer is the frame
itself: every dawn-specific treatment is a second rendering of
primed-ness or a concept trying to re-enter through the light rig.
But I name the risk honestly: restraint and emptiness are separated
by one bad build. If the reserve reads as *unfinished* rather than
*held*, if the stillness reads as *dead* rather than *laid*, this
frame is not a station — it is a settings screen at a poetic hour,
and the art criterion fails on my own terms. The defence cannot be
written; it can only be built and looked at, at 04:58, by the one
judge whose eye the law names.
