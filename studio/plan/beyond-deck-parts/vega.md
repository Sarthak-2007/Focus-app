# Vega — Wild, physics — Phase 3: Beyond the Deck (wheel, hub, total)

*The filed spec was an honest instrument. These three beyonds make it a
physical one: the count gains chunk-structure the eye can actually hold,
the scale's one lie-risk becomes the rim's one event, and the load you
are about to seal gains mass you can feel before you seal it. Nothing
here adds a gesture, a word, a numeral, or a colour. Orange stays on
Fen's slide, alone. Silence stays the shipped sound.*

---

## Beyond 1 — The Closed Hand: tetrad closure for counts 5–8, and a count ceiling the day itself sets

### The beauty case, in plain words

Four stones in the palm you know without counting. Eight loose stones
you count, twice, at 03:40, and still aren't sure. But a closed fist
next to three stones is not eight things — it is two things, a fist
and a three, and you know it the way you know four. So the hub never
shows loose pips past four. When the fourth block lands, the four pips
*close* — a solid arc bar joins them into one object, a tetrad, a hand
shut around its load. The fifth block starts a second hand, loose.
The eighth closes it. At every count from 1 to 8 the hub holds at
most four objects, and most counts hold fewer. The count stops being
read and goes back to being *seen* — and a hub with one shut hand and
three stones on it is a better picture of a morning than eight dots
in a ring will ever be.

### The fight, settled

- **My filed cap of 4 was perception dressed as a limit.** Subitising
  (≈4 objects perceived without counting) is a fact about *objects*,
  not about *blocks*. It forbids eight loose pips. It does not forbid
  eight blocks grouped into subitisable chunks. I concede the cap;
  the Designers' range 1–8 (DESIGNERS §3.2 / ruling R20) stands.
- **The Designers' 8 loose pips on an R40 ring fail the same fact.**
  At counts 5–8 the user must serially count Ø10dp dots on a Ø128dp
  hub — under sleep inertia, in peripheral vision, at the exact
  moment the arm path should be thoughtless. 7 vs 8 is the worst
  discrimination in the set and it prices the session's total.
  Grouping is not decoration on their rendering; it is the only way
  their range survives my lens.
- **The ceiling of 8 is set by the day, not the ring.** His day is
  ~10 blocks of 50 min across separated anchors (fasted study, gym,
  meals, sadhana). A single seal is a chain *inside* one anchor-gap,
  under HOLD — no mid-session decisions, so no chain may honestly
  claim the anchors it would have to cross. At the canonical block,
  8 blocks span 8×50 + 7×10 breaks = **470 min** — already past any
  single anchor-gap in his day, an outer edge, not a habit. A third
  tetrad (9–12) at 50 min spans 590–710 min: that is not a session,
  it is the whole day wearing one seal, and it would be a lie under
  HOLD the moment it crossed gym or a meal. Two tetrads fill the
  hub; the day needs at least two seals anyway. **Cap = 8 = two
  closed hands.** Counts at shorter blocks that could physically
  chain further are still capped: the hub is a physical object with
  two hands, and a control's geometry is its honest limit, printed
  in its fullness — at 8 there is no empty well left to want.

### Technique spec

- **Geometry (adopts Designers' hub, regroups it).** Hub Ø128dp,
  raised tone, recess well — unchanged. Eight pip wells Ø10dp on the
  R40 ring at 45° spacing, grouped as two tetrads: wells 1–4 on the
  upper arc (centred 12 o'clock, spanning 135°), wells 5–8 on the
  lower arc, with a **12° extra gap between well 4 and well 5**
  (tetrad boundary — grouping is printed in the spacing before any
  fill exists, so an empty hub already teaches its arithmetic).
  Drawn pip Ø10dp; touch target Ø28dp per well (chord between
  adjacent wells at R40 is 30.6dp — targets touch, never overlap).
- **Closure.** Counts 1–3: filled pips, as filed. On the commit that
  lands count **4**, the four filled pips are joined by a bar: an arc
  segment on R40, width 10dp (the pip diameter — the bar is exactly
  the pips, merged), spanning well 1 to well 4 centrelines, drawn in
  the same full-strength ink. The pips remain visible as swellings
  on the bar for one ink-settle, then the bar reads as one object.
  Counts 5–7: bar + 1–3 loose filled pips on the second arc.
  Count **8**: the second tetrad closes identically. Object count
  on the hub by count: 1,2,3,**1**,2,3,4,**2** — never above 4,
  and the two heaviest counts (4, 8) are the *simplest* pictures.
- **Commit mechanics (filed press law, extended).** Press-and-release
  commits, drag-off cancels, hub dead while rim moves — all as
  filed. Direct-set (Phase-2 concession) maps onto wells: tapping
  well *k* sets count *k*; tapping a closed bar sets its tetrad's
  end (first bar → 4, second bar → 8). Wrap 8→1 on cycle-press is
  retained but now visibly *empties both hands* — the hero total
  cutting down in the same frame remains the whole feedback, as
  filed. Under FLOW the hub rests as filed (pips *and bars* dim to
  ~40%, press disabled).
- **Motion / curve.** No new animation system. Closure uses the
  filed ink-settle, stretched for mass: bar lands at 88% ink and
  settles to 100% over **160ms** (vs 120ms for a pip) — the one
  place the hub is allowed to feel heavier. Loose pips settle at
  the filed 120ms. Hub torsional lag unchanged in form; lag now
  also carries the bar (it rides the hub body, as pips did).
- **Haptics.** Loose pip commit: filed **6ms @ 0.6**. Tetrad
  closure commit (4th, 8th): **12ms @ 0.75**, once — a click with
  body, halfway between a pip and the seat knock, because something
  structurally changed in the hand. De-closure on direct-set down
  (e.g. 6→3, bars breaking back to loose pips): filed 6ms @ 0.6
  only; breaking a hand must not feel like a warning.
- **Sound.** None. Shipped silent, as filed.
- **Depth / lighting.** Bar is ink, not relief: same tone as a
  filled pip, no raised step, no shadow — the hub stays one tone
  step up from the wheel recess, hairline edge, as filed. The bar
  must survive Mara's light unchanged: it is print, not material,
  so the fixed light does not strike it.

### Checks

- **ARM cost: +0ms of gestures, +40ms once.** Same taps as the
  Designers' 1–8 (worst case 7 cycle-presses ≈ 1.75s, or one
  direct-set tap ≈ 250ms). Closure adds 40ms of settle over a
  loose pip, twice in the range at most, and settle never gates
  the next tap — input is live during it. Total arm unchanged.
- **HOLD:** hub input still dies on seal. Nothing here is
  revisitable mid-session.
- **TRUTH:** count still lives *only* as hub geometry — no numeral,
  no ×N, anywhere. Grouping restates nothing; a bar is not a "4"
  printed elsewhere, it is the same four wells, joined, in the same
  home. The day-ceiling argument is disclosed above as a design
  law, not smuggled as a perceptual one.

### What it replaces, and why that loses

It replaces my filed 1–4 cap (perception misapplied as a range
limit) and the Designers' 8-loose-pips rendering (a range kept,
a perception fact ignored). The cap loses his chained mornings;
the loose ring loses the glance.

### The strongest case against this beyond

Grouping is a *learned* code where loose pips are a *native* one.
A first-week user who has never seen closure must be taught that
the bar means four — by the spacing, by watching it close, by the
total cutting in the same frame — and until that lands, count 5
(bar + 1) can be misread as 2 at a true 03:40 glance, which is a
worse error than slowly counting eight dots and getting eight.
Subitising research backs chunking in trained counters; it does
not promise the chunk is free on day one. If his phone test shows
one bar+pip misread, this beyond should die and the loose ring —
counted, slowly, correctly — should ship.

## Beyond 2 — The Strait: the FLOW↔10 axis-break as water, gate, and change of material

### The beauty case, in plain words

Every real instrument has one place where the scale stops and
something else begins — the red line, the gate in the fence, the
narrow water between two shores. Ours printed two apologetic
hairlines and whispered *sorry, the maths changes here*. Instead:
make it the rim's one journey. Between FLOW and 10 the knurl
stops. The rim goes smooth and drops into a channel — nine degrees
of bare water, no teeth, no ticks, no numbers — and FLOW sits on
the far shore in its own smooth basin, a word on polished ground.
Dragging across, your thumb feels the teeth end, a hush of smooth
travel, and a landing on the other side. Flinging across, the
wheel pays a toll of speed to the strait, the way a boat does.
You will remember this wheel by its strait. That is what a break
in the scale should be: not an annotation — a place.

### Technique spec

- **Angular budget (exact).** Numeric stops keep the filed law:
  9° = 5 min everywhere on the numeric scale, stops 1–35 spanning
  306° between 10 and 180. The FLOW↔10 interval becomes **18°** —
  two step-widths: one for FLOW's landing, one for the strait
  itself. Total travel FLOW→180 = **324°**; the 6-o'clock dead
  sector narrows 45°→**36°** to pay for it. No other angle moves.
  The scale does not lie about the strait because the strait
  contains no scale: no tick, tooth, or numeral exists inside it.
- **Material change.** Within the strait's 9° and across FLOW's
  basin (FLOW stop ±13.5° of rim): knurl teeth are **cut away
  entirely** — smooth rim face. The strait channel floor drops
  one further tone step to recess `#100E0C`; the basin keeps the
  filed recess tone, smooth. The two filed hairlines survive as
  what they always secretly were: the channel's two *shore
  edges* — 1px catch-lights where toothed rim meets smooth
  channel, top edge on the FLOW shore, bottom edge on the 10
  shore, so the strait reads as a cut *through* the rim, lit
  from Mara's one fixed direction. Under that fixed light the
  smooth strait and basin take a single broad specular streak
  where the knurl takes shimmer: at speed, the shimmer band
  visibly *breaks* once per pass over the strait — the break in
  the scale becomes the break in the light. (Mara mailed — her
  rig gains one smooth sector; the light itself does not move.)
- **The gate, in physics (drag).** Position stays 1:1 under the
  finger — the strait never fakes, lags, or sticks the rim. The
  gate lives in the wells and the hand, not in position: FLOW's
  detent well deepens (capture radius ±6° vs the filed ±4°;
  spring stiffness 260 vs 210, same ζ ≈ 0.38, same two-overshoot
  seat ≤520ms) — once you cross, FLOW *catches*; and inside the
  strait there are no detent ticks at all, so the crossing is
  9° of haptic silence between two shores.
- **The gate, in physics (coast / fling).** The strait is a
  potential step in the integrator. A coasting wheel reaching
  the strait with |ω| < **180°/s** does not clear it: it falls
  back to the shore it came from and seats there (filed spring,
  ≤520ms) — a lazy nudge can never strand him in FLOW, and a
  decaying spin running down the scale dies at 10, not in the
  mode. At |ω| ≥ 180°/s it crosses and pays the toll:
  **Δω = −90°/s** at the strait's centreline (an energy step,
  not a time tax — the hardest fling at ~1,200°/s loses under
  8% of its speed and its seat deadline). A deliberate drag
  always crosses, because the finger, not the energy, is in
  charge while held. Tapping the printed word FLOW, and the
  accessibility step action, command the filed tap-physics at
  400°/s initial velocity — over the step by design: the gate
  refuses accidents, never intentions.
- **Haptics.** Drag crossing, either direction: the last numeric
  tick (filed 5ms @ 0.35) → **9° of silence** → landing tick on
  the far shore: FLOW side **14ms @ 0.8** (a gate dropping into
  a deeper well), 10 side filed 5ms @ 0.35. Coast fall-back at
  the strait: the shore's normal seat knock, nothing special —
  a refusal must sound like an ordinary arrival, not an alarm.
  Coast crossing: one **10ms @ 0.5** toll-thock at the
  centreline, under the tick stream, never over it.
- **Sound.** Shipped silent, as filed. Sound-on fragment only:
  the strait crossing takes the one note *below* Vega's per-stop
  pitch ladder — a gate, not a stop, so it is not on the ladder.
- **Depth.** Channel = one extra tone step down (above), shore
  catch-lights per Mara's fixed light, basin smooth at filed
  recess depth. No shadow, no glow, no orange — FLOW's word stays
  in glyph tone `#EDE3D8`, Anonymous Pro, as filed.

### Checks

- **ARM cost: +~30ms typical, +0ms worst case.** The extra 9° of
  drag travel at ordinary drag speeds (~300°/s) is ~30ms on the
  one journey in the app that goes to FLOW. Hardest-fling seat
  deadline **unchanged at ≤1,400ms** (toll is proportionally
  trivial at fling energies, and FLOW flings are short). Taps
  and accessibility steps pay nothing.
- **HOLD:** unchanged — rim input dies on seal; FLOW's exit is
  still the wheel itself, pre-seal only.
- **TRUTH:** strengthened, not spent. The filed break *claimed*
  non-linearity in print while the rim's body stayed uniform —
  a caption doing the truth's work. The strait makes the body
  itself non-uniform exactly where the scale is: no teeth, no
  ticks, no values, one phantom step-width of nothing between
  the mode and the minutes. FLOW still prints as a word, never
  a number; the strait's width (one step) prints the size of
  the discontinuity honestly — it is exactly one stop wide,
  and not one degree wider.

### What it replaces, and why that loses

It replaces the filed printed axis-break (two hairlines across a
uniform knurled rim, §1.1). That loses because it asks the eye to
believe in print what the thumb denies in material: the finger
crossing the filed break feels *nothing change* — same teeth,
same ticks' spacing in the hand — on the one interval where
everything changes. An instrument whose only discontinuity is
invisible to its operating hand has the apology exactly
backwards.

### The strongest case against this beyond

The energy step is a behaviour a tired man can experience as a
*malfunction*: he nudges toward FLOW at 03:40, the wheel falls
back to 10, and for one bleary second the app "didn't work" —
on the highest-friction morning object in his life, at the
exact stop that means *just start*. Every safeguard above (deep
well, tap-to-FLOW, drag always crosses) is a patch on that one
second. If the fall-back ever reads as broken rather than as a
gate — one phone test decides it — the toll and the step die,
and the strait survives as pure material (smooth channel, deep
well, haptic silence) with free crossing both ways.

## Beyond 3 — The Loaded Wheel: block count has mass, and the flywheel carries it

### The beauty case, in plain words

In the filed spec the hub's lag behind the rim is theatre — one
body pretending to be pushed by another. I held back making it
true because it felt like too much physics for a timer. It is
the whole point of a flywheel. Eight blocks *weigh more* than
one. When you have loaded eight onto the hub, the wheel should
coast like a freight wheel — longer, heavier, harder to hurry —
and its seat knock should land like a pawl stopping real mass.
One block should feel like a bicycle wheel; eight like a mill
wheel. He would learn the size of his morning in his thumb
before the total ever prints it: the pre-commit truth, carried
in the hand, with no number spent. That is the most beautiful
sentence this instrument can say, and it can only say it in
physics.

### Technique spec

- **Two-way coupling (the filed lag becomes a system).** The rim
  and hub are one integrator with two bodies: rim inertia
  I₀, hub inertia added per committed block. Effective relative
  inertia **I_rel(n) = 1 + 0.14·(n−1)**: n=1 → 1.00, n=4 → 1.42,
  n=8 → 1.98. The session's load literally doubles the wheel's
  inertia across the range — large enough to feel in one fling,
  small enough that the wheel never becomes a different wheel.
- **What scales, what never does.** Under a held finger: **1:1,
  zero easing, at every count** — mass never fakes position;
  the load speaks only where physics honestly speaks, in free
  motion. In coast: viscous τ rises 0.34s (n=1) → 0.48s (n=8),
  τ(n) = 0.34·√I_rel. Coulomb braking torque scales ∝ √I_rel
  (an eddy brake coupled to the loaded hub — the heavier the
  load, the stronger the brake), which holds the invariants:
  **hardest-fling seat ≤1,400ms at every count**, magnetic
  well capture and the seat spring stiffness scale ∝ I_rel so
  ζ stays 0.38, the seat keeps **exactly two overshoots and
  ≤520ms at every count**. Capture velocity at wells falls
  540°/s (n=1) → ~385°/s (n=8): a freight wheel blows through
  wells a light wheel would catch in — true, and bounded by
  the brake. Hub torsional lag, now the same spring pushing
  real inertia: peak lag 4° (n=1) → 9° (n=8), settle ≤480ms,
  ζ ≈ 0.45 — the counterweight visibly works harder under load.
- **Haptics — the knock carries the mass.** Detent ticks
  unchanged at every count (5ms @ 0.35; majors 8ms @ 0.55):
  ticks report the *scale*, and the scale has no mass. The seat
  knock alone scales: **16ms @ 0.85 at n=1, rising linearly to
  24ms @ 1.0 at n=8**, trailing pawl 6ms @ 0.3 at +40ms as
  filed. One event, honestly weighted — the pawl stopping
  twice the inertia sounds like it.
- **Sound.** None shipped. Sound-on: the seat thock (only) drops
  in pitch with I_rel; the pitch ladder itself is untouched.
- **Depth / lighting.** None. No visual changes at all — the
  knurl, Mara's light, the recess, the readout are identical
  at every count. This beyond is invisible, which is its
  discipline: mass is felt, never displayed. (The Still Witness
  already prints the load's arithmetic; a second, visual
  rendering of weight would be one fact in two homes.)
- **FLOW.** The hub rests under FLOW, so the load is zero and
  I_rel = 1: an open-ended session rides the lightest wheel on
  the Deck. The contrast is the meaning — FLOW is the unloaded
  instrument.

### Checks

- **ARM cost: 0ms of gestures; +0ms worst case; ~+120ms felt.**
  No input changes. Typical-fling seat time at n=8 runs ~120ms
  longer than at n=1 (longer coast, same deadline) — inside the
  filed ≤1,400ms cap, with the slide and the rest of the ≤10s
  budget untouched.
- **HOLD:** no new state survives the seal; on seal the wheel
  rests as filed, at whatever load was sealed.
- **TRUTH:** the load is not a metaphor pasted on — the coupled
  parameters above are the actual integrator constants, bounded
  and published here; every invariant the filed spec promised
  (two overshoots, seat ≤520ms, deadline ≤1,400ms, 1:1 drag)
  holds at every count, so the wheel never claims a value, a
  stop, or an ending it does not deliver. The readout's numbers
  do not move one digit because of this beyond.

### What it replaces, and why that loses

It replaces the filed one-way hub lag (rim moves, hub performs
being moved, nothing pushes back) and the filed constant-mass
coast (τ ≈ 0.38s, one seat knock for every session size). Those
lose because they make the Deck's central physical claim — *this
is a flywheel carrying your morning* — a visual effect. A 50-
minute session and a 470-minute session ending in the identical
knock is the readout's honesty kept and the hand's honesty
thrown away.

### The strongest case against this beyond

The filed spec's proudest line was that the spin *ends the same
way every time* — learnable in a week, trusted for a year. This
beyond spends that: the ending now depends on a second variable,
and a user who arms ×1 on weekdays and ×6 on Sundays builds two
partial muscle memories instead of one whole one, and may read
the heavy coast not as *load* but as *lag* — the one failure
mode this instrument cannot afford at 03:40, because lag is what
cheap apps do. The bounded ratio (≈2× inertia, invariants held)
is my whole defence, and it is a defence of a *risk taken for
feel* on the screen where feel failures are fatal. If the phone
test produces one "it felt sluggish" instead of "it felt heavy,"
this beyond dies first of the three, and the constant-mass
flywheel — same ending, every time, forever — is the right ship.
