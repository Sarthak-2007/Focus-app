## 1. THE DECK AS ONE ROOM

### 1.0 The beauty case, in plain words

Six seats specced six elements for this screen — wheel, hub, total,
slide, check strip, primed loadout — and a screen assembled from six
good elements is still six objects in a corridor unless somebody
decides what kind of *place* it is. Here is the decision: **the Deck is
one room with five pieces of furniture and one courtyard.** The total
hangs on the wall you face when you enter. The wheel stands in the
middle of the floor, where an instrument stands. Below it the floor
goes empty — deliberately, measurably empty — and at the far end, in
the thumb's reach, one orange bolt waits in its slot. A half-awake man
does not read this room; he *crosses* it, the way you cross your own
kitchen in the dark: number, instrument, empty floor, bolt. The two
remaining "elements" were never furniture at all. The check strip is
the room's *condition* (is the blocking real?) and the primed loadout
is the room's *preparation* (was it laid out rested?) — conditions and
preparations do not get floor space; they change what the furniture
says, never where it stands. That is the whole art of this screen:
five things, each in the only place it can be, and nothing else
standing anywhere.

### 1.2 The wheel's size — Ø288 is the room's true diameter; Ø272 and Ø304 both make worse rooms

The freeze covers margin and radius, not the wheel — so the wheel was
re-measured, not assumed. Verdict: **keep Ø288.** The geometry:

- **Ø288 closes exactly.** Uhr's radial stack sums with zero remainder:
  hub R64 + gap 8 + face field 42 (numeral ring centred R100, 9.4dp
  clearance to the tick band) + tick band 16 (R114–130) + clearance 6
  (reader Ø4 at R133, 1.0dp each side) + knurl 8 (R136–144) = **R144**.
  Horizontally it centres on O with derived gutters of 42dp inside the
  content column each side — a centring residue, not a gap literal.
  Detent pitch at the tick baseline R130: **22.7dp of arc** between
  centrelines; a thumb landing ±11dp off still owns one detent.
- **Ø272 (R136) starves the instrument.** The annulus outside the hub
  shrinks 80 → 72dp; 8dp must be stolen from a stack that has no slack.
  Steal it from the face field and the numeral ring's 9.4dp clearance
  becomes 1.4dp — numerals collide with major ticks. Steal it from the
  knurl and the knurl band (8dp) dies entire. Shrink the hub instead
  and Ø112 is not on Raster's closed size list, and the pip ring
  (R40–44, Ø10 pips) crowds its chord from 22.8dp toward merge. Every
  exit from Ø272 is an illegal size or a dead part. It also buys
  nothing the room wants: +16dp of courtyard the courtyard does not
  need (its floor is already met in Case F at Ø288).
- **Ø304 (R152) bankrupts the courtyard.** Wheel bottom moves
  548 → 564; reserve = H − 760: **Case F = 32dp, below the 48 fence** —
  the filed stack no longer fits the shortest phone in scope, so
  something else must be cut to save a diameter nobody asked for.
  Horizontally the gutters narrow 42 → 34dp and the disc's edge starts
  crowding the margin axis where the readout and NEXT live. And the
  +8dp of annulus has no lawful tenant: face field 42 → 50 is not a
  size, tick lengths 8/12/16 are fixed print, so the extra ring would
  be bare face — a wider emptiness *inside* the instrument, purchased
  with the emptiness *outside* it that actually works (§1.3).
- Ø288 is also the only one of the three on Raster's closed size list
  (§4). 272 and 304 are not "alternatives"; they are amendments, and
  neither earns one.

### 1.3 The reserve — what sits in it, and why 48dp is its floor but not its size

**What sits in the reserve: nothing.** No marks, no registration
crosses (dead, correctly), no check strip, no loadout band, no second
control, no print. Its tone is the ground itself, uninterrupted from
the wheel's bottom hairline to the task line. This is the Shield
Section's courtyard moved indoors: in the Section, the void between
masonry blocks is the drawing's second subject because the built time
has mass next to it; here, the void is what makes the slide's orange
read as *far away and alone* — the one verb, across an empty floor,
from the one instrument. A half-awake eye crosses 48–100dp of nothing
and arrives at the slide with no decision spent on the way. That
crossing *is* the reserve's job, and any tenant evicts it.

**Why 48 is the floor:** 48dp is the touch floor — one full P48 module
of clear runway. Below 48, the wheel's drag-off zone and the task
line's tap zone start to neighbour each other and a drag released low
can be misread as a tap's neighbourhood. At exactly 48 (Case F) the
runway is one module: minimal, lawful, sufficient.

**Why 48 is not the size:** the filed language "reserve = fenced
minimum 48dp" is correct and Sana's D5 tries to improve it by moving
surplus air into the readout→wheel gap (S32 → S48 when flex ≥ 96dp).
**That move is refused here, by name (Fight F-K1, §4).** A gap that
breathes per device moves the wheel: top edge 260 → 276, centre
404 → 420 on tall phones. The wheel is the room's centre of mass; a
centre that moves 16dp between his phone and a taller one is not a
centre, it's a suggestion — and the hand that learns the drag arc on
one device re-learns it on the other. Registration is this seat's
whole discipline from the Section: *blocks never move; the void
absorbs.* All surplus height goes to the reserve, where growth is
invisible because nothing in it has a position. On Case T the
courtyard is 100dp and the room is *calmer*, not emptier — the same
room with a longer floor. D5's instinct (air belongs between the
number and the instrument) is answered directly: that air already
exists — it is the S32 at y 228–260, the largest lawful break inside
the stack, and it is enough because the NEXT slot's 20dp line sits in
it like a lintel. The pair does not need to breathe. The floor does.

### 1.4 The check strip gets no floor space — the check is a word in the beat

The pre-flight / blocking-proof fact (Rosa's queried lamps, Prima's
pre-flight gate — the machinery is theirs) needs a *rendering* on the
Deck, and six-element thinking would give it a strip. Geography
forbids it, with arithmetic: a strip is a band (min L20) + its gaps
(min S20 above, S20 below if it sits between wheel and slide, or S32
if it claims section rank) = **60dp minimum tax**. In Case F the
courtyard holds exactly 48. A strip anywhere in the stack makes the
fence case impossible — the room breaks on the shortest phone to
house a *status*. Ruling: **the check renders as one word in the
Beat, right-flush on D, plate style (Anon Pro 700 10sp, +2.0, quiet
text tier — night `#D2C6BB`, never accent): `BLOCKING ON` when the
proof query has returned true; `BLOCKING OFF` in the same slot when it
has not.** (Ruth's 04:58 frame already draws exactly this word in
exactly this slot — Humans and architecture converge without
collusion.) It costs 0dp of stack, sits in chrome where status lives,
and is readable in the first fixation's peripheral field. If the proof
has not yet returned at first paint, the slot is **blank** — never a
placeholder word: a slot that prints `CHECKING` and then flips is a
lie told twice (Prima's first-paint charge, answered spatially: the
blank slot is the honest first frame, and the word strikes in place
when the query lands, no reflow, because the slot is right-flush and
fixed). The slide's inertness on a dead permission is the check's
second channel and it already exists in the slide's own spec — the
word names it, the tone shows it, no strip is built.

### 1.5 Fixed registers — absence never collapses the room

Filed rule (Designers §3.1, Vau §3.5/§6): when NEXT is absent the
block "closes up"; when no task is cued the reserve "grows by 40dp."
Both are shape changes smuggled in as tidiness, and both are struck
here. **The NEXT slot (y 208–228) and the task-line slot (y 648–668,
Case T) are fixed registers: present or absent, they occupy their
20dp.** Absence renders as blank ground inside the slot — the line is
not printed, and nothing moves to fill its place. Reasons a builder
can execute: (1) the wheel's top edge is y 260 in every state on every
morning — if NEXT's absence pulled it to y 232, the instrument's
position would encode a schedule fact, which is light-carrying-data's
cousin: *position carrying data*; (2) a line striking in at 05:00
(the next routine entering range) must not shove the instrument down
28dp under a resting hand. The Section's law, restated for this room:
displacement is drawn or it does not happen; silent absorption is the
one move architecture never makes. Cost: 28dp of occasionally blank
stack (S8 + L20) and 40dp of occasionally blank bottom stack — in
Case F both are already inside the arithmetic above, because the
fence case was computed with both slots occupied. The room is sized
for its fullest state and stays that size in its emptiest.

### 1.7 ARM / HOLD / TRUTH check

- **ARM ≤10s:** the room adds zero gestures and zero waits. Slide
  position is bottom-registered — identical y on every device and in
  every state (§1.5, §2) — so the thumb's one learned target never
  moves. The check word costs no tap to read and the slide is never
  spent into a dead permission (its inert state is the filed law this
  room houses). Path pricing unchanged from the filed audit
  (0.7–2.2s typical); the room spends none of it.
- **HOLD absolute:** the room is the idle Deck. Mid-session this
  screen is not shown; nothing specified here renders, offers, or
  decides during a session. The fixed registers make absence blank —
  blank offers nothing, which is HOLD's shape even at rest.
- **TRUTH once:** every fact keeps its filed home — total in the
  numeral only, block length in wheel position only, count in pips
  only, next routine in the NEXT slot's three tokens only, check in
  the beat's one word only. Position itself carries no data (§1.5):
  no element's *location* ever encodes a state.

### 1.8 What this replaces, and why that loses

- **Replaces D5's breathing gap** (readout→wheel S32 → S48 on tall
  screens): loses because it moves the instrument per device —
  registration beats ventilation (§1.3). The surplus has a lawful
  home already: the courtyard.
- **Replaces the closing-up NEXT / growing-reserve absence rules:**
  lose because they make the room's shape a second rendering of two
  facts that already have renderings (the printed line; the printed
  task name). Shape is not a channel.
- **Replaces any check-strip band** (wherever proposed): loses on
  arithmetic — 60dp of tax against a 48dp fence — and on rank: a
  status does not outrank the instrument's floor space.
- **Replaces Raster §3.1's Deck map** (cap row + NOW row): loses
  because both rows are second renderings the census already buried;
  the grid file's authority is axes and tokens, and on element
  inventory the merged plan outranks the seat map.

### 1.9 Strongest case against this room

The fixed registers waste the scarcest thing on the fence phone:
28dp above the wheel and 40dp below it can sit blank on a morning
with no next routine and no cued task, while the wheel — the element
he actually touches on the mornings he touches anything — stays at
288 when the blank 68dp could have bought Ø304's finer drag (annulus
+16dp of radius is a real handling gain: detent arc pitch grows
22.7 → 24.0dp) *and* still left a 32dp courtyard that a rested
designer might honestly call enough. My answer prices hand-memory
across devices above handling on one device, and prices a 48dp
runway whose protective value (drag-off vs tap confusion) is argued
from module logic, not observed on his hand. If device evidence ever
shows the low drag-off confusion never happens, the fence — and with
it this section's spine — deserves to fall, and Ø304 + a 32dp
courtyard is the room that should replace it. The evidence does not
exist yet. Until it does, registration holds.

## 2. THE PRIMED ROOM (04:58) vs THE UNPRIMED ROOM

### 2.0 The beauty case, in plain words

At 04:58 the routine has laid the Deck out for the fasted block —
wheel set, count set, loadout seated in the tiers themselves — and a
man two minutes out of meditation picks up the phone. The temptation
is to make the primed Deck *look* primed: grow the slide's territory,
sink the wheel deeper into its recess, warm something. Resist it
entirely. **A room that changes shape when it is prepared is a room
he has to re-learn at his least awake.** The beauty of the primed
frame is that it is *the same room, set*: the same number's place
holding a bigger number, the same wheel holding a longer block, the
same orange bolt in the same slot — and the only way he knows it was
laid for him is that everything is already where his rested self
would have put it. Preparation you can see as *correctness*, never as
*decoration*. Joss draws what the primed frame's states say; this
section draws where they are allowed to say it: **nowhere new.**

### 2.1 The ruling — priming changes contents, never proportions. Shape change is forbidden.

**Forbidden, by name, each with its reason:**

1. **The slide's territory does not grow.** Track stays 372×72 at its
   bottom-registered y; cap stays 84×56. A bigger target for a weaker
   hand is a kind idea with a fatal price: the thumb's learned target
   *is* the ARM budget. Two slide sizes = two rooms = the tired hand
   aiming at a memory of the other one. The filed slide is already a
   372-wide, 72-tall target — the largest lawful verb in the app —
   and Ines's routine-window demotion (the slide *replaced* in its
   slot by a 58dp tap action) is a shape change of exactly this kind:
   **if demotion ever ships, it ships inside the slide's exact 72dp
   box at the slide's exact y, or it does not ship.** A slot whose
   height changes is a door that moves.
2. **The wheel's recess does not deepen.** Disc stays one tonal step
   (recess) in both rooms, day and night. A deeper recess in the
   primed room would make depth carry the primed state — depth is a
   material grammar (Stoff: one step + one hairline, placed by kind),
   and a state rendered in depth is a second rendering of a fact the
   values already state. Mara's law generalises: *no material channel
   carries data* — not light, not depth, not position (§1.5).
3. **No element enters, leaves, grows, or moves ≥1dp.** The y-map of
   §1.1 is the primed y-map, digit for digit. This includes the chrome:
   Ruth's frame puts `FASTED BLOCK · 05:00` in the beat's right slot —
   that slot is the check word's home (§1.4). **Conflict, stated
   plainly:** P2-3 rules next-firing lives in chrome only, and the
   check word is chrome too; they cannot both own the right end of a
   372dp line. Geography's settlement: the beat's right slot carries
   **one** word-string. On a clean primed morning it reads
   `BLOCKING ON` (the check — the fact he cannot act without); the
   firing's name and time live where the merged plan already put
   them, in the NEXT slot at y 208 (`NEXT 05:00 STUDY` — three tokens,
   filed). Ruth's `FASTED BLOCK · 05:00` in chrome *and* a NEXT line
   on the Deck would print the firing twice. If Humans' frame is
   adopted whole, the check word must move — and there is nowhere
   lawful for it to move *to* (every other slot is taken or fenced),
   so the frame's chrome line, not the check, is what yields. Mailed
   to Sana for the joint file's F3 (§4); Ruth owns the frame, this
   seat owns the slot, and the fight is recorded rather than smoothed.

**What priming lawfully changes — contents only, each inside its filed box:**

| Element | Unprimed room | Primed room (04:58, FASTED BLOCK 05:00–08:00) | Box moved? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total numeral | his manual total (e.g. `50`) | the window's set total (`180` — format is the type seats' fight; the box is y 128–200 either way) | No |
| NEXT slot | `NEXT 05:00 STUDY` or blank register | same three tokens — at 04:58 the next firing is this one | No |
| Wheel | at his last/manual detent | rotated to the set's detent — *the wheel's position is its content*; rotation is what a wheel is for | No (centre fixed at (206, 404)) |
| Hub pips | manual count filled | the set's count filled — pips fill, the Ø128 hub and R40 ring do not change | No |
| Task line | cued task or blank register | the set's first task (queue head per the resolver) or blank | No |
| Check word | `BLOCKING ON` / blank-until-proven / `BLOCKING OFF` | identical grammar — priming does not mint certainty | No |
| Slide | cap at rest, track word as filed | **identical** — the slide does not cock, pre-light, or lean. A slide that looks *readier* in the primed room is claiming a state (armed-ness) that does not exist until his hand commits it | No |

**The mid-window and never-comes variants, spatially:** Ruth's
Variant B (arrives 06:10, hero restates the time the window still
holds) changes the numeral's *value* only — same box, same size,
same y; lawful. Variant C (he never comes; at 08:00 the offer closes)
changes every value back to the unprimed room's — with **no transition
choreography**: the room is simply found unprimed next time he opens
it. A primed room that visibly *un-primes* in front of him would be
narrating a miss; the values change between visits, silently, the way
a kitchen is simply not laid when nobody laid it. Mise's own law
agrees (Ines: no Deck-level choreography, no sound of arrival) — this
section extends it to departure.

### 2.2 ARM / HOLD / TRUTH check

- **ARM ≤10s:** priming's whole dividend is paid here, and shape
  constancy is how it is banked: from the primed frame, sealing is
  one gesture at a known address, ≤3s for a weak hand (Ruth's
  pricing), because the address never moved. Any shape change spends
  re-aiming time the budget does not itemise — which is exactly how
  budgets get lied about.
- **HOLD absolute:** priming happens before any session; the primed
  room offers exactly the unprimed room's one verb, no more. A primed
  slide that grew or glowed would be an *invitation rendered as
  furniture* — pressure at the exact hour HOLD's spirit protects.
- **TRUTH once:** the primed state is rendered exactly once — by the
  correctness of the values themselves. Every proposed shape change
  is a second rendering of "a routine laid this out": slide size,
  recess depth, a chrome firing string beside a NEXT line. All cut
  for the same reason. And the frame never claims he came, will come,
  or was recorded (Ruth HPA-02): an unmet primed room leaves no mark
  anywhere in the room.

### 2.3 What this replaces, and why that loses

- **Replaces Ines's demotion geometry** (slide swapped for a shorter
  tap action inside the window): the *act* is hers to argue; the
  *geometry* — a 58dp tenant in a 72dp slot, height change as signal —
  loses because height-as-signal is position carrying data, and a
  moved door taxes every future arrival at that slot, primed or not.
- **Replaces any "primed = presented differently" instinct** (cocked
  slide, deepened wheel, warmed tones, wherever it surfaces in the
  beyond wave): loses because it renders one fact (the set exists)
  in a material channel while the fact's lawful renderings (values,
  pips, NEXT tokens) already fill their boxes.

### 2.4 Strongest case against this ruling

The 04:58 hand is genuinely weaker — grip, aim, and reading speed all
at their daily worst (Muede's whole file) — and this ruling denies
that hand the two cheapest helps a room can give: a bigger target and
a deeper, more findable instrument well. "Same room, set" is a
principle purchased with a tired man's accuracy, and if his mis-tap
or mis-aim rate at 05:00 ever measures materially worse than at
rested hours, the honest response is a primed-room geometry — target
first — and this section becomes the record of the principle that
cost him mornings. The counter-consideration, stated fairly against
myself: the filed slide is already the app's largest target and the
wheel at priming needs no touch at all (it was set rested; touching
it at 04:58 is the failure mode, not the task). The ruling bets that
the primed room's job is to be *not touched except once*. If that bet
is wrong about his mornings, it is wrong in the direction of elegance
over mercy, and Sana should charge it as such in the merge.

## 3. THE BEYOND I HELD BACK — THE DATUM: ONE SURVEYED LINE THROUGH THREE SCREENS

### 3.0 The beauty case, in plain words

Every screen in this app is drawn on its own sheet, and the sheets
happen to align — first content at y 112, margin at 20 — because
Raster surveyed the *tops* and the *edges*. Nobody surveyed the
*middle*, where the instruments live. So here is the too-much idea,
from the seat that drew a section through Shield's day: **survey the
middle too. Give the app one datum line — y = 404, the Deck wheel's

### 3.1 Technique spec — the datum and its three registrations

**The datum, derived (not chosen):** y = 112 + 88 + 8 + 20 + 32 + 144
= **404dp** from content top — the wheel centre falls out of the
filed stack; no new number is invented. x = **206** (axis O). The
datum point is **(206, 404)**. It is a coordinate, Raster's third
legal family — like the dialog width, it is stated once, here, and
derived everywhere else. It is top-registered (measured from content
top, never from screen centre), so it is identical on Case F and
Case T; bottom-registered elements (slide, valve, rail) are untouched
by it, and each screen's reserve absorbs the difference as always.

**Registration 1 — Deck (already true, now made law):** wheel centre
(206, 404) exactly; tolerance ±0.5dp (Uhr's hub-coincidence tolerance,
extended to the sheet). This is §1.1's map, promoted from description
to datum.

**Registration 2 — Running session:** Pit's stepped arc, Ø280

**Explicitly out of scope:** Shield and Tasks do **not** take the
datum. They are lists; a list's geography is its top register
(y 112) and its pitches (P64/P48), and forcing a centre onto a list
would be a concept looking for a wall. The datum governs *instrument
faces only* — the three screens where one object is the screen.

**Tones:** no new tones. Arc, hero, and AOD take their filed tokens
(running hero glyph `#EDE3D8`; AOD true `#000000` ground, filed
luminance steps; Deck as §1). The datum itself is **never drawn** —
no line, no tick, no cross. A surveyed line you can see is a
decoration; a surveyed line you can *rely on* is architecture.

### 3.2 ARM / HOLD / TRUTH check

- **ARM ≤10s:** zero cost on the arm path (the Deck registration is
  the filed map). The dividend lands one step later: the seal's hard
  cut lands the eye already centred — no re-acquisition saccade on
  the running screen's first frame.
- **HOLD absolute:** the running screen still offers nothing but the
  valve (filed); the datum adds no element, verb, or decision to it.
  The valve's axis-sharing with the slide is a *findability* property
  for the one lawful mid-session act — it makes the break easier to
  find, never easier to take (the 60s banked hold is untouched).
- **TRUTH once:** the datum renders nothing, claims nothing, counts
  nothing. It is pure registration — the one kind of structure that
  cannot lie, because it says nothing. Its risk is not falsehood but
  superstition, priced in §3.4.

### 3.3 What this replaces, and why that loses

- **Replaces the running screen's top-stacked hero** (remaining
  numeral in the y 112 register, arc below it as a separate zone):
  loses because it spends the screen's first register on the number
  and its middle on the arc as *neighbours*, when they are one fact's
  two channels (digits = truth, arc position = glance, per Pit) that
  belong concentric. Top-stacking is list-thinking applied to an
  instrument.
- **Replaces AOD's free-floating patrol origin:** loses because an
  origin chosen per-sheet is a centre that means nothing; the patrol
  exists to protect the panel, and translating its grid to the datum
  keeps every protection while making the dark face belong to the
  same machine as the lit ones.

### 3.4 Strongest case against the Datum

It is, precisely, a concept wearing a surveyor's coat — and Sarthak
killed the concept an hour before this wave was seated, in words
this seat has read: a gesture survives 03:30, a concept lives in a
paragraph. The datum's entire value is *felt continuity*, and felt
continuity is unfalsifiable on paper: no render at build time will
show the wheel "becoming" the clock, because the two faces never
share a frame — the claim lives in the half-second after a hard cut,
in a tired man's peripheral sense that the machine didn't jump. If
that sense doesn't materialise on device, the datum is a constraint
that cost the running screen its natural top-stacked reading order
(label → number → elapsed, the order every other screen teaches) and
bought a paragraph. Worse: it invites the next seat to extend it —
datum today, axis tomorrow, a "spine" through Shield by spring —
registration metastasising into a system he never asked for, exactly
the architecture-for-its-own-sake this app was killed-and-reborn to
escape. The defence is only this: the datum is *already true* of the
Deck (derived, not imposed), it costs the running screen one
re-centring and the AOD one translation, it draws nothing, and it
can be killed by a single device test — seal fifty times; if the cut
feels like a screen change anyway, strike Registration 2 and the
idea dies at the cost of one layout constant. A beyond that names
its own execution test is the only kind this seat will file.
