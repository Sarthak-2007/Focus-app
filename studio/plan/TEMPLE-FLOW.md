*Schwelle (threshold), Flow seat in Stein's Temple crew. Oct 29, 2026.*
*Sources, in binding order: plan/FLOW-REGISTER.md §2 (the designed
flow — its beats are law; this file dresses them, it does not
re-time them), plan/FLOW-TEAM-CHARTER.md, plan/DEEPEN-temple.md
§§2/5/7 (the stele, the housings, the repose), plan/PRESENTATION-
temple.md §§1.3/6/9 (the lamp rake, the lamp niche, the feel law).
Where Temple's dress fights a register beat, the fight is filed
as a FINDING (§7) and argued — never smoothed over.*

**Temple's motion law, stated once and used throughout:**
Curve C (slow-in / long-tail, `MonolithCurves.kt` C =
PathInterpolator 0.3, 0, 0.8, 0.15) is the house curve; **520 ms**
is the house duration; the wheel's ζ=0.38 two-overshoot seat
(≤520 ms) is machinery and unskinned. The desk clock's fade is
the register's own number — **420 ms, specB** (FLOW-REGISTER
§2.1) — and Temple does not re-price it to 520 ms; see FINDING
F-2 for why the exception is lawful. Sound law (PRESENTATION §9,
Haut adopted): the Throw is bit-identical and Temple claims
nothing at it; the seat knock lives at its floor (160–220 Hz,
24–32 ms, full amplitude — a bronze bolt dropping); **no bell,
ever** — a long decay is fraud via the queue (160 ms buffer cap);
Temple's resonance is mass, not duration. Light law for every
night face in this walk: numerals / strip / banner composite to
Temple's measured **55% → 0.201 relative luminance**, under
Nacht's 0.25 ceiling (PRESENTATION §6/§10 — Temple's own token
figure, not the register's generic ≈0.216; see FINDING F-3).

A note on palette that governs the whole walk: sealing forces
the night scheme app-wide (`effectiveNight = darkTheme ||
sealed`, register §1.1). In Temple that sentence has a body:
**a running Temple is always the lamplit Temple**, even at noon.
The sunlit stone is the composing face; the moment the bolt is
drawn, the lamps are lit. This is not a repaint accident in this
world — it is the rite: you do not study in the courtyard sun,
you study in the lamplit room. Its price is filed as FINDING F-4.

## 1 · THE BLOCKING START — the bolt, the rake, the stele running, the settle

Worked example, used through §1–§3: a hand-sealed DAY block,
sealed at **14:00**, span **3 h**, ends **17:00**, first task
*Finish Chapter 4 Problem Set*. (The SLEEP span gets its own
walk in §4–§5; the beats are identical, the witness differs.)

### Beat 0 — the hand on the bolt (t < 0, composing)

He is on the stele front (DEEPEN §2): lintel above with the hour
inscribed at its right end, the standing stone with its total
cut on its face, the ring incised into the stele's lower face,
the course of set stones at its base, the courses of inscription
below, three receding steps, and in the top step — the threshold
course — the **gate bolt**: the bronze bar in its black-bronze
channel, its grip the screen's one gold spend. He turns the ring,
lays his stones with the hub tap (each tap a seat knock at the
floor), reads the courses. Nothing in this beat is flow; it is
the workshop. The flow begins when his hand commits.

### Beat 1 — the bolt drawn (second 0, the latch)

Slide or tap — his verdict, both lawful — the bolt travels its
channel. Choreography, exactly:

- The bolt's travel is **state-driven, not house-animated**. The
  slide's own machinery owns the travel (catch/commit at the
  shipped fractions; the latch's two-stage knock, released
  heavier than engaged when it later unbars). Temple adds **no
  520 ms Curve C animation on top of the bolt** — the proving
  cascade runs on hard deadlines (jam declared at latch+500 ms,
  annulment at latch+1500 ms, register §1.1) and a bolt still
  gliding on a slow tail while the machine has already declared
  a jam would be a picture lying about a fact. FINDING F-6.
- The attempt latches; the cascade starts (session row → shield
  service → accessibility arm, each witnessed on its own clock).
  The stele enters the **seal shot**: scroll locked, task taps
  dead, navigation refused, the utilities row *absent* (not
  greyed) until Settle. In Temple's dress the detention reads as
  what it is: the gate is being barred and the building does not
  take visitors mid-barring. No course changes its inscription
  during the shot; a wall does not rewrite itself while you watch
  the bolt move.
- If a link is dark at +500 ms: jam — one shield re-dispatch,
  the shot holds. If a link is still dark at +1500 ms:
  **annulment** — the row is deleted, no Throw ever sounds, the
  bolt stands drawn back in its channel, and the Deck returns to
  its idle face carrying the past-tense sentence in a course, in
  full ink. Temple prints no vermilion here (PRESENTATION §1.1's
  fence: a lack is a fact, not a wound) and no Confession crack
  (§5 of the walk, §8 of the presentation, is for a day that
  *broke*, not an attempt that never stood).

### Beat 2 — t=0, the seal act and the lamp rake

All links witnessed → the seal act in its fixed order — claim
the row → seal the row → publish the sealed state → **the Throw
sounds, last, exactly once**. Temple's hands are off the Throw.

At the same instant, Temple's one consecration: **the lamp rake**
(PRESENTATION §1.3, adopted from Noir's flare via Wetter,
naturalised DEEPEN §11). A warm gradient rakes **once** across
the stele's hairlines, its double rule and its gold, left to
right, **~450 ms**, masked to the hero's strokes — then absolute
stillness. Technique, pinned so it cannot drift:

- The rake rides **inside the 1050 ms shot**, starting at t=0.
  It is light laid over the detained frame; it moves nothing,
  delays nothing, and is finished ~600 ms before the shot lands
  and the utilities row takes its place at Settle. It must never
  extend the shot by a frame (FINDING F-1).
- Its curve is the one lawful deviation from a pure house read:
  a rake is a *pass*, not a settle — it enters already moving.
  Temple files it as a linear-pass gradient with a Curve C
  **tail only** (the light leaves slowly, the way lamplight
  leaves a wall), total envelope inside the ~450 ms. It does not
  borrow the wheel's overshoot; light does not overshoot stone.
- What the rake is *for*: it is the only moment in the start
  where the building acknowledges the rite. After it: no motion
  on the running face except the counts themselves. A seal that
  keeps celebrating itself is a fairground, not a temple.

### Beat 3 — the shot lands (t ≈ 1 s): the stele, running

Navigation unlocks; the utilities row (DESK CLOCK · HELD) takes
its place. The stele now wears its **running face**, and in
Temple the running face is a specific inscription, not the idle
face with a new eyebrow:

- The lintel stands; the hour at its right end now ticks in a
  sealed world (night dress, per the headnote — lamplight at
  14:00, and that is correct in this world).
- The stele's face: the hero counts the **block's** remaining
  (the Deck's remainder — the clock will count the span's; the
  two remainders are both true and Temple labels them by place:
  the stele is the block's stone, the niche is the span's lamp.
  The register §1.3 names the unlabelled pair as today's
  confusion; Temple's answer is architectural — you learn which
  remainder you are reading by which part of the building you
  are standing in, and the dedication line names the block).
- The dedication line on the base course carries the task —
  *Finish Chapter 4 Problem Set* — with DONE as the small bronze
  tablet at its right end, and the **+ MIN extension tablet**
  beside it while the block runs (DEEPEN §10, ST-04).
- The courses read the machine's state, one fact per course:
  the block's state; the apps with the honest split (SHUT and
  PEEK counted apart); the end time — **ENDS 17:00** cut in the
  lowest factual course from the first minute, not saved for
  the last (the register reserves the *clock's* ENDS line for
  the last minute, §2.1; on the Deck, where he is working, the
  end time is a standing fact of the span and Temple has always
  printed TIME ENDS in its band — the two placements are
  different surfaces with different jobs, no contradiction).
- The witness course at the base: HELD, with the vermilion
  *outline* seal struck beside it — the proof, in this world's
  grammar. The frieze course carries the day 03–21 with NOW and
  its hour printed at the marker.
- The threshold course: the bolt stands **drawn** in its
  channel for the whole standing. This is the running Deck's
  most important stillness: at any glance, the gate is visibly
  barred. He does not re-check a setting; he sees a bolt.

This face **holds**. It is the proof the machine did what it
said, and the register's law stands verbatim: nothing is taken
from him yet.

### Beat 4 — the settle (last touch + 20 s): the room darkens to the niche

If his hand leaves the phone — no touch on the Deck for **20 s
after the seal or after his last interaction, whichever is
later** — the desk clock fades in over the stele. Choreography:

- **The fade: 420 ms, specB, per the register** — not Temple's
  520 ms house duration. FINDING F-2 argues the exception and
  Temple *accepts the register's number*: the fade is the
  overlay's existing spec, it belongs to the machinery's
  vocabulary {…, 420, 520, …}, and a room-change that arrives
  half a breath later than every other world would make Temple
  feel late, not heavy. Where Temple's slowness lives in this
  beat is the *shape*: the fade's easing takes Curve C's long
  tail in its final third (slow-in is wrong for an arrival that
  must not startle *late*; the tail is where weight lives) —
  filed as a call-site easing choice for the merge, priced
  view-only, and refused if the merge reads it as re-timing:
  duration stays 420 ms either way.
- **What fades, in what order** — the beat Temple adds to the
  register's single line "the desk clock fades in": the stele
  does not cross-dissolve into the clock (a stone does not
  melt). The ground goes first: over the 420 ms the lamplit
  room darkens to the niche's **true black** — the lamp turned
  to the wall (PRESENTATION §6) — and the clock's column stands
  in that black as the fade completes: span numerals, the brass
  stand hairline beneath them, the strip, the banner. One fade,
  two facts in sequence inside it: darkening, then the lamp.
  Implementable as a single overlay alpha over a black ground
  that reaches full black ahead of the column's alpha — view
  only, no new timing constant.
- At the fade's end the overlay holds the screen awake
  (FLAG_KEEP_SCREEN_ON, existing machinery) and the column
  begins its burn-in walk (§2 below). The app has changed
  rooms: workshop → niche. He puts the phone down and what
  stands on the desk is a clock that knows his session.

Why the settle exists at all, in Temple's voice: the first
seconds after a seal are working seconds — he tears the task,
reads the held count, draws breath. A face that seized the
screen at the Throw would fight the hand that just armed it.
The clock arrives when the hand leaves, which is the moment it
is for. Temple adds only this: 20 s is *short* against this
house's instincts, and Temple keeps it anyway — the register
priced the race against the OS screen timeout, and a temple
that arrives after the screen has slept has not arrived
(FINDING F-5 records the temptation and the refusal).

### Beat 5 — the phone on the desk: minute 1, hour 3, the last minute

The phone lies face-up on the desk. Nobody is holding it. What
it looks like, in the room:

**Minute 1 (14:01).** A black field — true black, the niche's
ground, the only surface in the app that is allowed to be a
void. Centred, the lamp's column: the **span remaining** in
night parchment at the 55% composite (0.201 — lamplight, not a
screen's glare; across a room it reads as a tended lamp reads,
present, not demanding). Beneath the numerals, the single brass
hairline — **the stand** — glyph-dim, moving *with* the column
(see §2: a stand that stood still would burn, so the stand rides
the grid). The progress strip: a hairline of fill at its 0.04
floor, present, not yet a fact. The banner: *Finish Chapter 4
Problem Set* — the task, nothing invented; with no task and no
intention the banner prints **nothing** (register §2.4.3; the
fabricated "Deep Focus Session" dies in Temple first — a temple
does not invent dedications). Under the hero, small, glyph tier:
**the current time** — 14:01 — the hour inscribed on the stand's
right end (DEEPEN §9.3), because at any pickup the first
question is *what time is it* (FINDING F-7 on the two-times
risk and its mitigation). The exit line at the foot, composited
at Salz's cured 0.58: `NIGHTSTAND DESK CLOCK • TAP ANYWHERE TO
EXIT`. Sound: none. Motion: none, except that once a minute the
column steps (see §2).

**Hour 3 (of a longer span — read this beat on the SLEEP span
or a full day block; on the worked example, scale it to the
middle hour).** Nothing has changed, and that is the design
(register §2.1, adopted whole). The strip has filled in
proportion — on a long span it is now the face's largest lit
area and its share of the light budget has grown exactly as
Salz measured (+39% first hour to last across a night); at
Temple's 55% composite the whole face stays under the ceiling
with the strip included, which is why the cure is a condition
of the auto-raise and not a polish. The column has walked its
grid scores of times; no position repeats within the hour
(§2). The banner still names the task — or has changed only
because *he* changed it, on a pickup, at the Deck (§3). No new
information has arrived because no new information exists.
Stille's hush, in masonry: the middle of a session is a lamp
burning in an empty niche, and a lamp does not fidget.

**The last minute.** The strip stands full. The hero counts the
span's final seconds. And the exit line **gives its place** —
register §2.1's words — to the one fact a finishing man wants:
**ENDS 17:00**, in wall time. Temple's placement, argued in
FINDING F-8: ENDS takes the *stand's* line, displacing the
current-time inscription for this one minute (at the last
minute, the current time is the least useful number on the
face — the span's end is the question), and the exit line
**stays** at the foot. A man picking the phone up for the first
time in the last minute must still be told he can tap to leave;
the register's "gives its place" is honoured in substance (ENDS
is promoted to a primary line) without spending the only
instruction the face carries. At the span's end: §5's ending —
the clock closes itself at the finish edge and never outlives
its session by a second.

## 2 · THE CLOCK ITSELF — the lamp niche, as a standing object

The register §1.3 audited the machinery; this section is what
Temple *does* with it, once, so §§1/3/4/5 can refer to it:

- **Burn-in walk.** The column steps on the existing 5×5 grid
  (±6 dp in 3 dp steps, 25 positions, one step per 60 ticker
  seconds, mod 360). Temple changes nothing and names one
  dress consequence: **every lit element rides the step** —
  numerals, strip, banner, current-time inscription, exit line,
  *and the brass stand*. The stand is a hairline of lit pixels;
  a stand left fixed while its numerals walk would etch itself
  into the panel by morning. In Temple the stand is part of the
  lamp, and the lamp moves as one object inside its niche. The
  niche (the black) never moves; black cannot burn.
- **What prints, in hierarchy** (register §2.4's four changes,
  housed): hero = span remaining (timed) / elapsed (stopwatch);
  strip = span elapsed / span target, floored 0.04; banner =
  task, else intention, else **absent**; stand line = current
  time (right end, glyph tier); foot = exit line. Plus, in
  their seasons only: the break line on the banner (§3.3) and
  ENDS in the last minute (§1, beat 5). That is the whole
  inventory. FINDING F-9 counts the accretion risk honestly:
  the niche began (PRESENTATION §6) as numerals + a stand, and
  the register has lawfully added four prints; Temple houses
  them all at glyph tier or below except the hero, and refuses
  any fifth.
- **It is a read face.** No verbs live on it except leaving:
  tap anywhere → the Deck, where DONE and every other verb
  already live (register §2.2, law 3). Temple underlines the
  fit: a lamp does not take instructions. The dismissal's
  choreography is §3.1.

## 3 · THE PICKUPS — the middle of the flow

### 3.1 He wakes the phone mid-session (screen slept, app in front)

The face he left is the face he gets — the register's law, no
re-raise, no surprise. Two cases, in Temple's dress:

- **He left the lamp lit** (the clock had raised): he wakes to
  the niche — black field, span, stand, current time. He is
  across the room or at the desk; the face is legible at both
  distances because it contains one large fact. If he wants
  the workshop, he taps: **dismissal is a decision**, and the
  dismissal's motion is the settle's fade **reversed at the
  same 420 ms** — the room lightens back to the stele. Temple
  files one hardness here: the dismissal must *answer* on the
  tap (the press law — an answer on DOWN, never a lag dressed
  as weight). The 420 ms is the room changing, not the tap
  being considered; the first darkening frame lands with the
  touch, and if the merge cannot start the fade within the
  tap's own frame, the fade shortens — the decision outranks
  the choreography. After dismissal the clock **stays away for
  that session**; the DESK CLOCK chip on the running stele
  remains his road back, by hand, when he wants the lamp again.
- **He had dismissed the clock** (or it never raised — he
  sealed and kept working past every settle): he wakes to the
  running stele, bolt drawn, courses standing. The machine
  does not argue with the hand. Nothing re-raises, nothing
  nags, and Temple prints no "clock available" reminder —
  the chip's presence on the utilities row is the whole offer.

### 3.2 He opens Monolith cold mid-session (app was closed / swiped)

RootApp re-derives the live session from the stored row; the
flow adds one line: **the clock is the first surface**, the
Deck one tap beneath it (register §2.3). In Temple: a cold open
inside a block lands in the niche, not the workshop — he opens
the app *inside* a standing, and the building receives him in
the room the standing is lived in. Choreography: no fade from
a previous surface exists to reverse (the process is cold), so
the clock **stands already lit** on first frame — a fade-in
from nothing would be a small theatre the moment does not
need; the lamp is simply on. One stated exception, inherited
from the register's own pricing (§2.6): the dismissal bit is
in-memory per session, so a cold process may re-raise the
clock **once** even if he dismissed it before the swipe. The
register accepts this; Temple accepts it and prints nothing
about it — one re-raise per cold process is a fact of the
machinery's memory, and a second tap dismisses it again under
the same law. It is not a loophole to be apologised for on the
face.

PARK, note: while sealed, PARK stays closed against him
(register §1.2) — a cold open lands on the clock, and there is
no path from the niche to the settings drawer that does not go
through dismissal and the Deck's own closed door. The sealed
world has no back corridor in this building either.

### 3.3 He picks it up mid-break — the repose, seen from the clock

The break is Temple's **courtyard repose** (DEEPEN §7): the
minutes between standings, inside the walls, doors open. From
the clock, the repose is *one line*, and the register §2.3
fixes its text and its source: the banner gains

> **BREAK — DOORS OPEN · n MIN LEFT**

derived from state already on the row (`isInSafeBreak`,
`safeBreakSecondsRemaining`). Temple's housing of that line:

- It **replaces** the banner's task line for the break's
  duration; it does not stack beneath it. The banner is one
  line deep in this world — a niche holds one inscription at
  a time, and during the repose the fact of the open doors
  outranks the name of the work he is resting from. At the
  break's end the task line returns with the doors' closing.
- The **hero does not change**: span remaining, as always
  (Nacht's law, register §2.3, DEEPEN §7 — the clock tells
  session time; the break earns no hero of its own). The strip
  does not change. The stand still prints the current time.
  A man holding the phone in a break learns, without leaving
  the face, that the doors are open and for how long — today
  he cannot (register §1.4/table), and that was the whole
  charge.
- The boundaries knock (DEEPEN §§7/9.2, ST-03): entering the
  repose, **one low knock** — the unbarring, the seat-knock
  family at its floor; leaving it, **one low knock** at the
  flip. If he is *looking at the clock* when the break ends,
  the knock and the banner line's exchange are the whole
  event — no flash, no banner animation; the line is simply
  different the next time his eye lands, and the knock is for
  the man whose eye is elsewhere in the room. If he is on the
  Deck instead, the repose's own surface carries it (§7's room:
  bolt drawn back in its channel, **BREAK · N MIN LEFT** on the
  base course — Pilger's one-word cure (TEMPLE-DAYFIT tax 4),
  adopted here: DEEPEN §7's bare "BREAK · N MIN" never says
  whether N is the length or the remainder, and every other
  Temple surface means *remaining*; the word LEFT makes the
  Deck mean it too — the END BREAK tablet on the threshold
  course, at Pilger's 56 dp touch floor — and
  ending the break early by that tablet is the one verb this
  walk names as **new machinery, small, Ren's to rule**, as
  DEEPEN §7 already filed it).
- Over other apps, the repose's window is the **hanging
  tablet's BREAK face** (DEEPEN §8: "BREAK / 6 MIN LEFT", body
  tap returns him to the Deck at the END BREAK tablet). That
  surface is Freya's machinery with Temple's face; the flow's
  part is only the sequence: break flips → knock (if a Temple
  surface is his current room) → banner/tablet lines change →
  doors re-close at the flip with the second knock. No face
  counts the break in its hero anywhere in the building.

## 4 · ROUTINE-BORN SEALS — nobody holding the phone

At **05:00 FASTED** and **20:00 SLEEP** the seal fires with no
hand on the phone. This section assumes a seal that lands — the
firing chain itself (F1–F5, plan/ROUTINE-NOFIRE-AUDIT.md) is
Wache's programme, not Temple's dress; FL-07 in §6 keeps the
two apart by name. When the seal lands in an empty room:

- **Nothing lights.** No rake (there is no eye; and the rake is
  masked to a Deck that is not on screen — it simply does not
  occur, and Temple files that as correct, not as a loss: the
  rake consecrates a *witnessed* barring), no Throw chased into
  a dark room is Temple's concern — the seal's voice is
  machinery, bit-identical, and whether a routine-born Throw
  sounds at 20:00 in an empty room is a machinery question the
  register leaves with the firing programme; Temple claims no
  dress at it and invents no silence of its own. What the room
  gets is: the shield's standing notification in the shade —
  "Monolith Locked / Protecting your focus session…" — the
  phone locked, the screen dark. **A routine-born seal's first
  surface is no surface.** That is the designed answer, stated
  as a positive: the building bars its gate at the hour whether
  or not anyone watches it barred.
- **The first touch lands on the clock.** Whenever he first
  opens Monolith inside the routine span — §3.2's cold-open
  rule, which is the same rule — the first surface is the lamp
  niche: for FASTED at his first pickup, the span, his task or
  an honest absence, the current time on the stand. The settle
  beat (§1, beat 4) never runs for routine-born sessions and
  Temple does not miss it: the settle exists to protect the
  working seconds after a *hand* seal; a routine-born session
  has no working hand to protect, so its clock is not *raised
  after a wait* — it is simply *where the app opens*. Same
  destination, different road, and the difference matters to
  the machinery (§6, FL-07/FL-13): one derivation at cold
  open, no 20 s timer owed.
- **05:00 FASTED, specifically.** He wakes at 03:30, bathes,
  meditates — the phone across the room or in another. The
  FASTED seal lands at 05:00 into that stillness. Temple
  designs **no knock at the seal** and adopts DEEPEN §9.2's
  ST-06 pre-knock (one low knock one minute before a routine
  boundary — "the lamp is lit before the rite begins") only
  with the fence argued in FINDING F-10: at 04:59 he may still
  be in meditation, and a knock that cuts a meditation to
  announce a *fast* has misunderstood both. Temple's stance:
  the pre-knock is lawful **only where a Temple surface is
  awake** (screen on, app in front — a man already with the
  phone in his hand); in a dark room it does not sound. Filed
  as a call-site condition for Ren to rule, not as a refusal
  of ST-06 — the register's own yardstick keeps the knock,
  Temple only fences its room.
- **20:00 SLEEP, specifically.** The seal lands; the phone,
  wherever it lies, is locked under the span. If he raises the
  clock by hand that evening (chip on the Deck, §3.1's road
  back) or opens the app cold, the niche becomes what the
  register §2.5 says it is: **the nightstand face for the whole
  span** — current time printing, light under law (0.201),
  walking its grid, span counting down toward 03:30. If he
  never opens it, the phone spends the night dark and asleep
  under the seal — FL-14's open question, and Temple's answer
  is in §6: that darkness is *also* a designed state in this
  world, named, not a default nobody chose.

## 5 · THE ENDINGS

### 5.1 Finish — the clock closes, the detention, the slip under the door

A span can end by expiry or by his hand at the Deck; the
ending's choreography is the same from the clock's side:

1. **The finish edge closes the clock.** The VM already hides
   it at the finish edge (MonolithViewModel.kt:1051) — the
   flow keeps that law, and Temple gives it its motion: the
   niche does not fade out over 420 ms like a dismissed face.
   A finish is not a dismissal. The clock closes as a lamp is
   put out at the rite's end — Temple files the close as the
   reverse of the *stand's* logic, not the settle's: a **cut
   to the underlying Deck within the finish's own beat**, no
   Curve C tail lingering over an ended span. (If the merge
   implements the hide as the existing overlay fade, Temple
   accepts the shipped fade and withdraws the cut — the law
   is that the clock never outlives the session; its exit
   curve is the one beat in this walk Temple prices as
   *indifferent*, and says so rather than inventing a
   preference. What is refused is any *extension*: no ENDS
   epitaph held on the clock after the edge, no closing
   ceremony on the niche.)
2. **Underneath stands the Deck and the ending's own surface:**
   the **detention** (the shot's mirror — the frame held while
   the ending books), then the **numbered receipt**. In Temple
   the receipt keeps its loved print character and its housings
   (PRESENTATION §5, DEEPEN §5): the parchment slip pinned under
   its brass rule, its № struck with the vermilion *outline*
