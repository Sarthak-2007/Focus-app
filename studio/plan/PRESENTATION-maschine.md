# MASCHINE — PRESENTATION, WHOLE

**World:** one machine. Not a machine theme, not machine furniture on
software screens — the app is a single cast apparatus, and every surface
is one of its faces, every control one of its parts, every state one of
its motions. If a surface could be lifted off and bolted onto some other
apparatus, it has failed.
**Designer:** MASCHINE (direction 7, seated Oct 29, 21:32 IST on
Sarthak's direct order: *"Add one a machine a fully one whole machine
only machine."*). Born whole under the wholeness order — this world was
never a skin, so it owes no deepening. Writing only, under the build
stand-down; renders follow at re-open.
**Substrate, untouched:** the blocking stack, the proving cascade and
its physics, the database, the receipt's facts and its numbered-slip
character (he loved the slip), every audited sentence's honesty. New
machinery is named and priced where this design wants it; Ren rules.

## §0 THE MACHINE

There is one machine, and the phone is its casing. It is a bench engine
built from a single casting: a great spoked governor wheel that sets how
long the cycle runs, a clutch lever the hand throws to engage the drive,
a train of three gears that must each mesh and turn before the locking
pin drops and the cycle begins, a mill in its belly that takes the day's
apps as stock — crushing some shut, metering some through a timed valve,
letting the rest pass — a feed rack of workpieces waiting their turn, a
parts drawer in the bed where every finished piece drops and stays, and
at its front a slot that prints the cycle's paper tape when the work is
done. Working it feels like working machinery: everything is heavy in
the hand at the right places, every control moves something you can see
move elsewhere on the casting, and the machine never speaks in
sentences where a needle would do — but it stamps its words into plates
when words are what the fact needs. It is loud exactly once per cycle,
at the moment the pin drops, and silent the rest of the time, the way a
good machine is.

**The constitution — three laws no element may break:**

1. **No decorative parts.** Every visible part is coupled to a fact,
   and when that fact changes, the part moves. A part that does not
   move when its fact changes is paint, and paint is cut. (This is
   the world's whole soul, and it is testable in a still life: point
   at any drawn thing and this spec names the fact that moves it.)
2. **One shaft.** A single drive shaft runs through every face of the
   casting. Rotation anywhere is rotation everywhere, at fixed ratios:
   when the cycle runs, the shaft turns on the Deck, in the Works, over
   the Drawer — the machine cannot be running on one face and idle on
   another. State is a physical condition of the apparatus, not a
   per-screen opinion.
3. **The machine says only what its parts can show.** Gauges read what
   their line actually carries; the valve is offered only where the
   plumbing runs; a plate prints a sentence only when the sentence is
   true. The truth baseline (§6, §11) is not a constraint on this
   world — it is this world's native tongue.

**Palette (zero-green audited — G ≤ max(R,B) for every value):**
Day, a lit workshop: brushed aluminium ground #D6D7D9 · iron panels
#26282C · ink #141518 · steel mid #8A8F96 · brass, small parts only,
#B08D3E · enamel red #A61E22 — spent on exactly three things: the
clutch handle, the emergency stop wheel, the maker's nameplate. Drum
windows are black with enamel-white drums in all lights.
Night, the same machine in a dark room: ground #0B0B0D · casting
#17181B · panels #1E2023 · ink #E9EAEC · brass #8C6F33, lit only where
a needle or a screw needs it · enamel red #C0272D. Nothing glows that
is not a dial, a plate or a lamp — a machine at night shows its gauges,
not its paint.
**Type:** the counter drums and the tape print Anonymous Pro (the
machine's stamped figures); plates and labels Atkinson Hyperlegible;
the one poster-scale readout (§2, D2) Sora Bold. Stamped words are
letter-spaced capitals, the way plates are stamped — never decorated.

## §2 THE DECK — the control face

The Deck is the machine's front: governor, drums, clutch, gauges — the
face a man works at 03:30 with one clumsy interaction. Nothing on it is
a card; the face is one casting with parts mounted on it, small machined
fillets (4 dp) where plates meet the casting, full-bleed elsewhere.

- **D1 · The gauge cluster (header).** The five dials of §1 in a row on
  the casting's top edge — four linkage gauges + the works clock — with
  the stamped plate beneath: `GUARD 4/4` when whole, or the first dead
  line's name when not (`GUARD 1/4 — ACCESSIBILITY`, the shipped
  sentence, now said by a fallen needle as well as in words). The
  cluster is the machine reporting on its own linkages; it is never
  decoration, and tapping a fallen gauge opens the inspection hatch.
  This kills Fremd's confusion by construction: "1/4" is four dials,
  one fallen — countable on the face itself.
- **D2 · The counter drums (the readout).** Where the skeleton puts a
  digital headline, the machine mounts a drum counter in a black
  window: `1H 00M` as rolling drums, Sora-sized in presence (the
  window's figures at poster scale) though cut as drum glyphs in
  Anonymous Pro. A stamped plate under the window names what the drums
  count, and changes with the state — `SET` before the arm, `LEFT`
  while the cycle runs, `RUN` on free-run. (Fremd: "TOTAL — total of
  what?" dies here: the machine labels its counter like a machine.)
  Below the window, one stamped line: `ENDS 15:35` — the end time,
  promoted to the face (the hand decides *until when*), printed only
  when an end exists; free-run prints `NO END`.
- **D3 · The governor.** A spoked flywheel occupying the face's middle,
  rim stamped with minute marks (30 · 60 · 90 · 120 · 150 · 180), a
  brass hub, and an index tooth at the chosen value — the knob and the
  drums can never disagree, because the drums are geared to the wheel:
  turning the wheel rolls the drums (Fremd's dial-lies-to-me fumble is
  a gearing impossibility here, and the spec says so as a build
  requirement: one input, one derivation, drums geared to the rim).
  The wheel's physics are the shared integrator's (no fork — §8).
  Two dots' worth of inherited furniture dies: the unlabelled inner
  8-dot ring does not exist on this machine; the governor carries one
  auxiliary dial only — the block-count sub-dial (block i of n, a small
  drum at the hub), labelled `BLOCK`.
- **D4 · The FREE-RUN key.** Where the skeleton floats a "FLOW" chip
  (Fremd: "a word the app knows and I don't"), the governor carries a
  brass key: turn it and the governor's rim lifts off its detents — the
  cycle runs ungoverned, the drums switch to `RUN` and count up. A key
  is a mode a hand understands; the plate under it reads `FREE RUN`.
- **D5 · The workpiece line.** Under the governor, the feed rack's head
  crosses the Deck: the NOW workpiece's name stamped on a plate, its
  `DONE` — a brass eject lever at the plate's end. Pulling it ejects
  the piece into the parts drawer (§5) and the next piece slides into
  the jaws. `QUEUE n` prints on the rack's end plate — a count of
  pieces waiting, which is all it ever meant.
- **D6 · The proof line.** The skeleton's proof strip becomes a row of
  three small gauges set into the casting under the drums — BIND,
  COUNT, PROOF — each with its stamped micro-label: BIND's needle reads
  the live warrant (live, never frozen past its age — UNA2-03/04 are
  answered by a needle, which cannot hold a stale reading without the
  staleness showing as the needle's fall); COUNT is a small drum pair
  printing the honest split `7 SHUT · 3 PEEK` (UNA2-02's cure, in the
  machine's figures, never a bare sum beside a held/shut claim); PROOF
  is a lamp-gauge: POSITIVE only on the directly observed signal (the
  audit's A-01 definitions, kept verbatim). At the seal the COUNT drums
  freeze as the record of what was sealed (the imprint's discipline,
  A-06, kept) — and print `AT SEAL` on their plate when frozen, so the
  tense is on the face (UNA2-04's cure).
- **D7 · The clutch.** The slide, as a lever in its quadrant at the
  face's foot: an enamel-red handle on a steel arm, tooth notches along
  the slot. Its plate names the act it will perform — `THROW TO START`
  idle, the finish act while running. One handle, red, findable by a
  thumb that cannot see (Fremd's 03:30 test is this lever's design
  brief). Fremd's other fumble — the loudest line on the old screen
  being a tap-to-fix sentence that isn't the start — is answered in
  D8: on this machine the refusal plate is not the biggest thing on
  the face, and the lever is.
- **D8 · The sentence plates.** Refusals and verdicts print on stamped
  plates that swing down over the casting's lower edge — the jam plate,
  the annulment plate, the guard-down plate (`BLOCKING IS OFF — TAP TO
  FIX` still a working verb, still tappable, at plate size — a warning,
  not a headline). The audited sentences print verbatim (§11); only
  their furniture changes. When all is whole, no plate hangs: silence
  is a state (§0, law 3's corollary).
- **D9 · The cam window.** At the face's lower edge, a window into the
  Works: the cam drum's next peg and its stamped plate — `NEXT —
  SLEEP 20:00` (ST-07). The idle Deck never again sits silent about
  what happens next; the drum inside the window turns with the day.
- **D10 · The shaft.** The rail, per §1: four couplings + PARK as the
  braked end. While a cycle runs the shaft's keyway travels; the Deck
  is a face of a running machine, and every other face will show the
  same rotation at the same phase.

**Deck at 03:30 (arm):** drums, governor, clutch; the gauges whisper;
one red handle. **Deck mid-cycle (glance):** drums counting down, the
governor turning at span rate, the proof gauges, the workpiece plate.
**Deck at finish:** the clutch's finish act, then the tape (§5).

## §3 THE WORKS — the Shield, as the machine's belly

Nobody arms from the Works; it is opened at rested hours to load the
mill and set the cams. Its density is a workbench's density.

- **W1 · The loadout gear sets.** STUDY · WORK · SLEEP are three
  interchangeable gear sets racked above the mill; the loaded set sits
  IN the mill's drive (the sliding cap, his pick, kept — as the gear
  set's carrier plate sliding the set into mesh). One glance: the set
  in mesh is the set whose gears touch the shaft.
- **W2 · The feed channels (app rows).** Each app is a channel into the
  mill with a gate on a travelling sled (the shipped sled, kept): SHUT
  — the gate closed, the roller over the channel (the app is milled:
  it does not run); the valve stop — the channel's metering valve,
  printed `PEEK 2 MIN` with the minutes the valve is currently set to
  (never a bare "2m" — Fremd's guess dies; the word is the part's
  name, the number its setting); OPEN — the bypass chute, gate up.
  The section head carries the honest split as drums: `7 SHUT ·
  3 PEEK`.
- **W3 · The metering valve's setting.** On the Works face the valve
  has a setting dial: `PEEK — 2 MIN`, set by direct entry (type the
  minutes; §4's stamp) from 1–30, or 0 = the valve blanked off
  (Freya's bounds, PILL-AND-TIMES §3). This is where his peek minutes
  live: on the part they govern, not in a settings list.
- **W4 · The idle gear (the break's setting).** Beside the valve, the
  machine's second timed part: the idle gear that times the lull
  between cycles — `BREAK — 10 MIN`, set the same way, 0–60 (0 = the
  cycles butt together, no lull; the span arithmetic reads the stamped
  value, ending the 600-seconds-in-four-places disease Freya
  documented). Break and peek, the two durations he named, are two
  dials side by side on the works they govern.
- **W5 · The cam drum (routines).** The drum itself, full width: one
  track per routine, a peg at its engagement, the track's plate
  carrying its name and span (`SLEEP 20:00 – 03:30`). Editing is
  working the drum (presentation over the routine-edit mechanism —
  its landing is a named dependency, §10): tap a peg and the track
  becomes the bench — times set by the stamp to ANY minute (direct
  entry; steppers are a tool for values you don't know, and a man
  knows his own hours), days as seven pin-holes that take or refuse a
  pin, the gear set chosen by its carrier, the name stamped on the
  plate, DELETE as striking the track off with its confirm printed as
  a sentence on the bench, CREATE as a blank track at the drum's foot.
  ON/OFF is the peg itself: a peg in the drum engages; drawn out, the
  routine cannot fire.
- **W6 · The websites channels.** The site blocker as a bank of
  smaller channels beside the mill, same gates, plus an open stamped
  line to add a domain and a strike verb per row (the shipped editor's
  verbs, machine furniture).
- **W7 · The ratchet plate.** The ratchet warning prints as a red
  plate across the Works when true, and not at all when not — the
  bench's only shout, spent on the one warning that must not be
  missed.
- **W8 · The inspection hatch (PARK).** The shaft's braked end opens
  the hatch: the four linkage lines traced at full size with their
  gauges, the FIX keys as the line's own valves, the works clock's
  movement visible — and behind wired glass, the STOP WHEEL: the
  week's one emergency release, its banking chambers marked at
  15/30/45, `N/1 WEEK` stamped beside it. The hatch is also where the
  casing gauges' grant lives (the overlay line — already declared,
  already watched, Freya §1.5): a fifth valve, labelled for what it
  is, never counted among the four linkage gauges.

## §4 SELECTION — one system, four tools

Every choice on the machine is made with one of four tools, and no
fifth exists:

1. **TURN** — the governor (continuous quantity: duration). The drums
   roll as it turns; the index tooth marks the chosen value.
2. **SET** — a dial or a gate sled (one of a few named states: tier,
   gear set, valve minutes by detent). The part's position IS the
   state; any still image of a choice reads correctly.
3. **THROW** — the clutch (commit an act). One red handle per face at
   most.
4. **STAMP** — exact values (a cam peg's 05:40, the valve's 3
   minutes). Tap the part's plate and a stamping press drops: digits
   typed on a key grid, pressed, and the plate re-stamps with the new
   figure. **The exact-value law: a value the hand already knows is
   stamped or turned, never stepped.** ±30 steppers are banned from
   this machine on every face — they are the software habit this
   world exists to kill.

## §5 THE FEED, THE DRAWER, AND THE TAPE

- **The Feed (Tasks).** A rack of workpieces in order — order is the
  only information; a piece carries its name stamped on its face and
  NO minutes, ever (his verdict). Dragging a piece re-racks it. The
  head piece sits in the jaws. Finishing (the Deck's eject lever, or
  the rack's own) drops the piece through the bed into the parts
  drawer with the tear's voice (§7) — the tear is the counterfoil
  stub being ripped off the piece's tag as it falls; the stub stays
  on the rack's spike as the day's counterfoil row. Adding a piece =
  a blank slug at the rack's foot with its name stamped by the press.
  An empty rack prints its end plate: `RACK EMPTY` — an honest plate,
  not an error.
- **The Drawer.** A drawer in the casting's bed that slides out
  (280 ms seat, the engine's drawer motion): inside, finished slugs
  in day order under day plates, stored forever, no count, no grade,
  no totals — a drawer does not judge what it holds. The Last-Finished
  REOPEN (his ruling) is the drawer's return chute: the freshest slug
  sits in the chute at the drawer's mouth and can be lifted back onto
  the rack; older slugs rest. Search is a magnet on a chain
  drawn through the slugs — a working verb printed as an open line
  like every input on the machine. The tally drum stands at the
  drawer's side (§1); the catch tray sits above the slugs: held
  messages, fully readable, live ones discardable, the tray frozen at
  a finish under its `HELD · AS OF {time}` stamp.
- **The Tape (receipt).** At a finish, before anything else moves, the
  tape prints: out of the slot at the casting's front, a paper tape
  with sprocket holes down both edges, the loved slip's own facts in
  the slip's own order — the № struck first by the numbering head,
  then the cycle's figures (planned, lived, the split of SHUT and
  PEEK printed as figures, never a bare sum; a WOUND line when the
  cycle was extended by hand, §9 ST-04; the outcome — COMPLETED or
  BROKEN — as the tape's last stamp). The tape tears at the serrated
  edge with the tear's voice; a reconstructed finish prints its
  figures under the plate `REBUILT FROM THE DRUM` (the receipt law's
  witnessed/reconstructed distinction, in machine words: what the
  tally drum stamped at the time vs what was rebuilt after). The
  tape's styling is the machine's; its facts and its numbered-slip
  character are substrate law and are not redesigned.

## §6 THE GUARD PLATE, THE RESTING DIAL, THE CASING GAUGES

**The Guard Plate (the Held Door).** Mid-cycle, a hand reaches for the
works — opens a milled app — and the machine answers as a machine does:
a steel guard plate drops over the gear train with a clang (the latch
voice, released-weight). The plate is a wall, not a window: full-bleed
casting, the stopped app's name STAMPED INTO the plate at headline
scale (a name pressed into steel is the first fact and the biggest),
and riveted to the plate's centre a dial — the cycle's remaining time
(`1H 12M LEFT` as a drum pair under the dial), because the baseline's
first debt (SIL-1) is paid in the machine's own instrument: the door
carries a gauge, and the gauge reads the span. The plate's verbs are
parts, and there are two:
- **THE VALVE** — present ONLY where the plumbing runs (baseline rule
  3, UNA2-01): on a channel whose metering valve exists, the plate
  carries the valve wheel with its setting stamped (`PEEK — 2 MIN`)
  and, while the valve recharges, its dial printed ON the wheel —
  `RECHARGE 14:20` — with the reason stamped on the valve's tag:
  `THE VALVE MUST REWIND` (SIL-2, SIL-3: the price is printed before
  the act, the refusal carries its reason; a withheld peek never
  simply vanishes). Turning the valve lifts the plate for exactly the
  set minutes — and the machine places him in the app as the plate
  lifts (ST-08: the valve swings the part to the man; he does not
  walk back around his own machine while paid seconds burn).
- **THE RELEASE** — a plain handle: step back from the machine
  (RETURN TO FOCUS, the audited verb). No third verb exists.
On the doors with no plumbing — the Uninstall Guard's plate above
all — the plate is BLANK RIVETED: no valve, no offer, just its stamp
and the release. A machine does not paint a valve where no pipe runs.
The plate never scolds, never counts past knocks, never prints a
streak; it states the name, the time, and what can be done. Its count,
if any surface of this machine prints counts at the door, splits SHUT
from PEEK (baseline rule 4).

**The Resting Dial (the desk clock).** The machine at rest is not an
overlay — it is the casting's front dial, the one part built to be
read across a room. One dial: a single hand on an engraved ring, the
span's remaining as drum figures under it, enamel-white on the true
black of a powered-down face. While a cycle runs it reads the span
(elapsed and remaining — the study display he kept, in the machine's
figures, Nacht's law kept: the break earns no rendering of its own;
the dial reads the span straight through it). At rest — no cycle —
the dial is the works clock at full size: it tells the time
(ST-01's cure at the surface it bit hardest; the idle clock never
again prints a dial setting dressed as a time, and the fabricated
"Deep Focus Session" banner dies: a resting machine with no declared
intention prints its maker's plate and the hour, nothing invented).
The dial assembly drifts slowly against burn-in (machinery, kept);
`TAP ANYWHERE TO EXIT` stays printed small on the bezel (A-12).

**The Casing Gauges (the pill).** Freya's derivation is substrate
here — faces from stored ends, never a counted tick, priority
SESSION · BREAK · PEEK with BREAK outranking PEEK — and the machine
gives it a body: a small gauge housing that protrudes from the
casing's edge, present only while a cycle runs and only while he is
at another bench (in another app). One gauge, its face card turned
by the machine to the live line:
- **SESSION card:** the needle over a drum pair — remaining, and
  `BLOCK i/n`. The body's tap returns him to the machine.
- **BREAK card:** the lull's remaining, and at the card's foot the
  re-engage lever in miniature — the break can be ended from the
  gauge, where he actually is when it ends (ST-02 at the surface).
- **PEEK card:** the valve's own dial, counting the lease down over
  the peeked app, with the red stop-cock at its side — tap it and
  the valve closes NOW (the first reachable hand-back of a peek;
  Freya §2.1, UNA2-06's cure). The gauge is never a launcher for the
  peeked app.
The gauge drags and snaps to an edge like a bolted thing settling
into its bracket; it never appears over the machine's own faces,
never over the Guard Plate, never on the lock screen, never through
a call (Freya Q1–Q5, adopted) — a casing gauge is read at the bench,
not carried into the works.

## §7 FEEL — the machine working

The instrument is Haut's ground truth and this world spends it
without one priced amendment: a machine IS the synth's native
grammar — ratchets, knocks priced by mass, a two-stage latch, a
heavy integrator. All voices below are existing parameter sets at
existing call sites; the Throw is frozen and is never re-voiced;
worlds speak in the approach, never at the seal. Meaning rides
timing (Haut's law: shape by time, colour by amplitude); every
pattern below survives an amplitude-less motor intact.

- **TURN (the governor).** Detents as gear teeth: `playRotaryDetent`
  on the shipped ladder (420–760 Hz), one tick per tooth, the tick's
  *spacing* under the thumb carrying the speed — a gear being wound,
  not a list being scrolled. Haptic: the shipped detent strikes,
  timing-led.
- **THROW (the clutch's travel).** As the lever crosses its quadrant,
  tooth strikes at rising density (segment pattern: strikes at
  90 ms, 70 ms, 50 ms — load gathering), pitch falling as the handle
  nears home (the seat-knock's mass logic applied to the approach).
  The lever pauses at the gate: **silence** — the machine proving is
  a held breath with three small sounds in it:
- **THE PROVING (the gears).** One gear-mesh clunk per witnessed
  link: the seat knock re-pitched (200–240 Hz, short), each landing
  exactly as its gear begins to turn on the face and its proof
  needle jumps. Three links, three clunks, never a chord — a train
  meshing is sequential or it is nothing.
- **t=0 (the pin drops).** The Throw — bit-identical, hand arm and
  cam arm, the one sound the machine is loud with. Haptic: the
  shipped seal stamp. On the face, the pin is SEEN to drop in the
  same instant; light and sound and steel agree because they are
  one event.
- **JAM.** The middle gear chatters: two strikes 12 ms apart,
  repeated at the shipped jam pattern, and the gear's ±2° oscillation
  on the face; the sentence plate swings down. The machine does not
  hide a jam and does not shout about it in the ear — the hand and
  the plate carry it.
- **ANNULMENT.** The lever springs home along its quadrant (the
  shipped seat return), the pin stays up, the plate prints the
  audited sentence. One latch-release voice (520 Hz) — a drive
  disengaging is the whole sound of a refusal.
- **HOLD (the cycle running).** Silence. The shaft turns, the
  governor turns, the drums step once a minute — motion is the
  machine's idle speech, sound is not. No hum exists (a hum would be
  a loop, and loops are fraud).
- **BREAK (the lull).** At the boundary the clutch disengages — the
  latch-release voice (ST-03's first knock) — and the governor
  coasts to creep; at the lull's end, one seat knock a breath before
  the next block engages (ST-03's second knock), so an ending is
  never a surprise to a man away from the desk.
- **FINISH.** The works bell (one completion pulse, re-pitched low —
  a bell struck once, in the casing, from the service layer: ST-05)
  + the tape printing (the tear's family: a run of fine detent ticks
  as the tape advances, then the Tear Pulse at the serration) + the
  finished piece's drop into the drawer, one deep knock, mass-priced.
  Three sounds, three facts, in the order they happen.
- **THE CAM APPROACHES.** One minute before a peg fires, a single
  seat knock from the heartbeat (ST-06): the machine clearing its
  throat before it engages itself. At the boundary, the cam arm
  seals with the same Throw as a hand arm — a seal is a seal.
- **ATMOSPHERE ("smell"), specified in lawful channels only:**
  a machine shop at rest — colour temperature: the coolest neutral
  of the six worlds (unpainted metal takes the room's light, it
  brings none); sound brightness: low fundamentals, all mass;
  motion viscosity: the heaviest lawful coast (the shared
  integrator, unamended — the governor's spokes make the shared
  physics READ heavier without forking it); event density: the
  lowest of the six — a machine that is working well is a machine
  you forget is on.

## §8 WHAT OF THE SKELETON DIES FIRST

1. **The screens.** Rooms die; faces remain. There is no "page" on a
   machine, and no travel — the hand walks along one casting.
2. **The bottom bar as software chrome.** A row of labelled buttons
   is a menu; the machine has a shaft with couplings. (The stations
   keep their stamped names — DECK · SHIELD · TASKS · LEDGER's
   successor DRAWER · PARK — as nameplates, because machines stamp
   their parts' names and because the names are his rulings, not
   the skeleton's.)
3. **The digital headline.** A number rendered as word-processing
   type is software's number; the machine counts on drums geared to
   its wheel, so the readout can never disagree with the control
   (the skeleton's dial-vs-display lie is unbuildable here).
4. **The proof strip as prose cells.** Text cells become gauges —
   needles and drum pairs with stamped micro-labels — because a
   machine reports itself in instruments first and sentences only
   when an instrument cannot say it (the sentence plates remain for
   exactly those).
5. **Cards.** Everywhere. Plates riveted to a casting, filleted at
   4 dp, or the casting itself — nothing floats, nothing is a sheet
   of paper pretending to be a surface. (The TAPE is paper, because
   it is printed by the machine; that is the one paper on the
   machine and it is earned.)
6. **The word FLOW and its chip.** Software slang dies; the FREE-RUN
   key — a brass key in the governor — replaces it. Likewise "2m"
   dies: the part is THE VALVE, its setting its minutes.
7. **The app-as-layers.** The desk clock is not an overlay, the door
   is not a dialog, the pill is not a widget — dial, plate, gauge:
   parts of the casing, drawn by the same casting, obeying the one
   shaft.

## §11 ACCIDENTS AUDIT — the inherited app, in this world's terms

1. **The hero is a picture of a dial, not a dial.** The shipped
   wheel and its readout are separate prints that can disagree
   (Fremd watched them do it). On the machine they are geared —
   disagreement is unbuildable (§2 D3).
2. **The Guard is a sentence in a header.** Four linkages reported
   as a fraction in prose. Machines gauge their lines (§2 D1);
   the sentence survives only as the cluster's stamped plate.
3. **"2m".** A duration standing where a part's name belongs, so a
   stranger must guess it means peek. The part is the valve; the
   number is its setting (§3 W2).
4. **The desk clock is an overlay.** Software thinks in layers;
   the machine's dial is a part of the casting, and its idle face
   was left showing a setting dressed as a time (ST-01) plus one
   invented session name (Una's appendix). Both die at §6.
5. **The receipt arrives as a card in a sheet.** A modal is
   software's furniture; the machine prints a tape from a slot
   (§5). The slip's facts and № are law and stay; its envelope is
   what changes.
6. **The intercept is a dialog.** A dialog asks; a guard plate
   stops. The shipped airlock's hollow intention line (UNA2-07)
   cannot occur on the plate: the plate stamps the declared
   intention under the app name ONLY when one was declared — an
   unstamped field on steel reads as bare metal, honestly blank,
   never a label over nothing.
7. **The emergency break is a software valve metaphor with no
   body.** It is a red wheel behind wired glass (§3 W8) — the
   60-second hold becomes two hands' worth of turning, its banking
   chambers visible, which is what the law's patience was always
   asking the hand to feel.
8. **Rounded cards on a machine that never had them.** The dead
   skin's radii were porcelain's. Castings have fillets; 4 dp,
   plates only (§2).
9. **The unmapped surface family** (the trunk container leak the
   wave found) is answered the machine's way before it is asked:
   every face of this casting is a named token in both lights —
   a machine has no stock grey, because a machine's maker paints
   every face or none.
10. **The simulate-intercept fake (Una's appendix).** A hardcoded
    fake airlock one call from live does not exist in this world
    even as a parameter: the Guard Plate renders only from a real
    intercept's evidence, or the face does not render. Named so
    the merger hears it from this direction too.

## §12 THE SOUL TEST, TAKEN AGAINST MYSELF

Strip the colours, cover the names. The six worlds become, in
greyscale: a dark slab with an ember; a sky; a lit sign; a poster;
a stone tablet; a drawing. This one becomes **a machine** — a
spoked wheel, three gears, a shaft with couplings, five gauge
dials, a drum counter, a tape slot: a silhouette that is not a
screen layout at all but an apparatus's front elevation. And the
test's harder half is not the silhouette but the coupling: in the
running render, the shaft turns on every face at one phase, the
governor turns at span rate, the gears stand where the proving
left them, the pin is down. A coat cannot do that; only an
apparatus can. If any part of this design is found, at render
time, to be a picture of a machine rather than a machine — a gear
that doesn't turn when its link is witnessed, a gauge whose needle
doesn't fall when its line dies — that part is paint, and by this
world's own constitution it is cut, by my hand first.

— MASCHINE, designer (direction 7). Spec: this file. Renders
owed at re-open: the control face day + night, the Works, the
Guard Plate, the tape, the resting dial, the casing gauge in its
three states — the finalist set, whole.
