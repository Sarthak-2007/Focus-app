## 1. SETUP *IS* THE FIRST SESSION — the sheet seals a real 25-minute block

**Crazy:** Kill the tutorial/session divide. The sheet's last act is not DONE → Deck → find the slide.
After the handover line, the sheet offers ONE ink key under DONE: `SEAL MY FIRST SESSION — 25 MIN`.
Pressing it runs the real slide, the real seal, the real room — with the doors exactly as they stand
(unset doors price themselves in the refusal, as today). His first memory of Monolith is not a form
he filled; it is a room he sat in. The sheet becomes the prologue of a session that actually happened,
and its receipt is Receipt #1 in the chain of his Drawer forever.
**Build:** DONE stays exactly as-is (act-line and all); the new key is a sibling that calls the
existing seal path with a preset 25-min span (StudyModePreset already exists). No new machinery —
composition only. If no door is set, the key prints its price instead of sealing (same total grammar).
Price: SMALL. This is the unforgettable first open: onboarding you cannot distinguish from use.

## 2. THE DOORS ARE SET IN THE ORDER THEY WILL BETRAY HIM

**Crazy:** Reorder the four cards dynamically by *failure consequence at 04:00*, and SAY so in the
rank line: the door whose absence silently kills blocking (INTERCEPTOR) first, the door whose
absence merely degrades (MAIL ROOM) last — and when he returns on day 2 with a door regressed,
THAT door's card rises to the top of the sheet wearing the Ledger's words `WAS SET AT SETUP — SINCE LOST`.
The sheet is not a static form; it is triage. First open teaches the priority by layout, not by lecture.
**Build:** card order = pure function of (consequenceRank, regressionFlag from SetupLedger.doorsAtStampMask
vs current prints) — Ledger consumer (b) already ships the words; this gives them a *place*.
Price: SMALL. (Ledger Proposal 4 was deferred for lack of flap data — this needs no threshold:
one observed regression is enough to re-order, and the print says why.)

## 3. THE CONFESSION LINE — he writes the sentence the Airlock will read back to him

**Crazy:** One blank ruled line on the sheet, between the doors and the beats, eyebrow
`IN YOUR OWN WORDS — WHY THE DOOR SHUTS`. Whatever he types (or leaves blank — blank is lawful,
no nag) is stored verbatim and is the ONLY personal sentence in the app: the Airlock, at the
moment he begs it mid-session, prints HIS OWN sentence back above the peek/break offers.
Not a motivational quote. His words, from his first morning, at his weakest minute.
**Build:** one TEXT column (SetupStore, write-once-then-editable-between-sessions like intentions),
one line on the sheet, one print slot in AirlockAndDisarmOverlays beside ACTIVE INTENTION —
and it CURES UNA2-07's hollow-label disease by giving that slot a sentence that is never hollow
when set, and absent (not blank-labelled) when not. Session intention stays as-is; this is the
standing one. Price: SMALL. Unforgettable because it is *his*, and it only ever appears when it costs him something to read it.

## 4. THE SHEET PERFORMS A BLOCK, LIVE, BEFORE HIS EYES

**Crazy (the Blockit-theatre killer):** Blockit wins on theatre (§7) because its welcome *does* things.
Give the sheet a fifth beat — a live demonstration strip under HOW A SESSION RUNS:
`WATCH: ` + a real SHUT-tier app name from his own list (or YouTube/Shorts if none yet) +
a key `TRY TO OPEN IT — IT WILL BOUNCE`. Tapping fires the real interceptor path in a
one-shot demo seal (60 seconds, no session row, no receipt — a rehearsal, explicitly printed
`REHEARSAL — NOTHING IS RECORDED`). He watches the bounce happen on day one, in his hand,
instead of trusting 25 sentences about it. If the INTERCEPTOR door is unset, the strip prints
the price instead: `THIS DEMO NEEDS THE INTERCEPTOR — FIX ABOVE` (another honest refusal, same grammar).
**Build:** demo seal = existing seal machinery with a `rehearsal` flag that skips the session
row/receipt writes (one guard at the repository write). The bounce is the real Airlock.
Price: MEDIUM — the only medium on this list, and it buys the §7 theatre axis outright.

## 5. PROOF AGE AS A NUMBER ON EVERY DOOR CARD

**Crazy:** Replace the STALE adjective arms-race with arithmetic: each card's live word gains
a mono suffix — `SET · PROVED 0:12 AGO` — counting up on the sheet's existing 5s re-derive.
At 45s+ the suffix becomes the gloss S-12 wanted, for free: the number IS the explanation
("the last proof aged out" is obvious when it reads 46, 47, 48…). A stranger at 04:00
understands a counting number instantly; UNPROVED needed a glossary.
**Build:** permPrintAt already carries the measurement mono (chit law uses it); print
`proofAgeLabel` (shared with #5's seam idea — one pure function, two surfaces, one grammar).
Keeps UNPROVED as the word (K-1 lawful reprint stands); adds the number beside it.
Price: SMALL. (Prior crazy seat proposed the number instead of STALE — I propose it *beside*
every state, so freshness itself becomes visible, not just staleness.)

## 6. THE TEAR-OFF STUB IS HIS FIRST RECEIPT — and the Drawer keeps the counterfoil

**Crazy:** The tear already performs (Letzt F-1). Complete the metaphor: the torn-off stub
(the part below the rule — stub note, total, DONE act-line) is *saved as Setup Receipt Zero*
in the Drawer at the stamp moment: doors as they stood, chit verdicts, the Ledger mask,

same numbers, because they are the same record. Day 100, he can open the exact stub from
his first morning. The Setup Ledger stops being invisible machinery and becomes a kept object.
**Build:** at stamp, persist a `SetupStub` snapshot (SetupStore already holds stamp + mask;
add doors-at-stamp prints + chit lines as one JSON/TEXT row). PARK stub row navigates to a
read-only ReceiptSheet-style render of that row (ReceiptSheet.kt exists in this graft).
Price: SMALL. No scores, no nostalgia dates *printed as grades* — it is a counterfoil, kept
like every receipt this house prints. (Respects Funke's kills: no visit counts, no day-100 grade —
the stub is capability history, opened by his hand only.)

## 7. THE ESCORT CHIT ESCAPES — fix the trap by making the chit a *ticket*, not a row

**Crazy (aimed at Wave-2's Consumer trap):** A REPAIRED chit for an ESCORT door can never print
because the verdict travels via PARK while the chit lives on a card that then collapses (F-6).
Stop binding the chit to the card. On ANY return-with-verdict, the sheet grows a single
**verdict ticket** directly under the header — `WENT OUT NOT SET · CAME BACK SET — INTERCEPTOR ·
NEXT: WITNESS →` — one ticket, latest verdict only, dismissed by the next departure.
Cards keep their state words; the ticket owns the *journey*. ESCORT, FIX, CHECK — one grammar.
**Build:** hoist `chitLineFor` rendering from card scope to sheet scope (state already in
SetupStore/host); F-6 collapse no longer hides anything because the ticket was never in the card.
Price: SMALL, and it deletes a whole class of "where did my verdict go" for every future door type.

## 8. FIRST OPEN AT 04:00 MODE — the sheet knows what hour it is in his life

**Crazy:** If first open happens 03:00–06:00 (his real world: wake 03:30), the sheet prints
one extra eyebrow line at the head: `FIRST OPEN BEFORE FIRST STUDY — THE FASTED BLOCK IS NEXT`
and the routine line (S-2 cured form) names the 05:00 FASTED routine as the sheet's closing
beat. At any other hour, that line is absent — no fake intimacy. The sheet acknowledges the
life it is entering, once, factually (derived from the seeded routines + clock, both real),
then never mentions it again.
**Build:** pure derivation over (LocalTime, seeded routine list) at first composition only;
one conditional line. No tracking — it reads the clock, not him.
Price: FREE. Unforgettable precisely because it is right about his morning and says it once.

## 9. THE ANTI-WIZARD OATH — one line, signed by sliding nothing

**Crazy:** Blockit's warmth (§7's remaining edge) comes from a narrator who promises
"I will ask for them one at a time" — a voice this house refused. Steal the *trust* without
the narrator: the sheet's foot, above the tear, prints the house's oath in the total's own
grammar — `THIS SHEET ASKS ONCE. IT WILL NOT ASK AGAIN, NAG, OR GRADE THE ANSWER.` —
and the machinery backs it: sheet gated !sealed, PARK reprint is read-mostly, DONE live
at any state (all already true — the oath just *prints the law that already binds us*).
A promise that is already kept is the only warmth that survives Stachel.
**Build:** one printed line + an audit note tying each clause to its enforcing code
(gate, stamp write-once, no-score line A-7). If any clause's code dies, the line is a new
lie and the register strikes it — that risk is the point.
Price: FREE. This is how a ruled form out-trusts a friendly wizard: it swears, in writing, on the form itself.

## 10. SOUND OF A DOOR — each door, when it first turns SET, knocks its own knock

**Crazy:** The sheet is silent except DONE (sound law closed Wave-1: stamp→pulse, reprint→click,
CHECK borrows FIX's knock). Give each door a distinct, tiny tactile-sound signature at the
*moment of its first SET printing* — interceptor = the seal's firm click, witness = a soft
paper tick, anchor = a low thunk, mail room = a latch — played ONCE per door per setup
(the chit's return moment is the trigger; Letzt A-5 binds: no failure voice, ever).
He will *hear* the sheet complete, door by door, without looking — at 04:00, eyes half open,
the fourth knock IS the total. Haptics ride the same events (one 12ms pulse each, DONE keeps its dip).
**Build:** four `playX()` calls on TactileSoundEngine beside the existing voices, fired from
the chit-verdict state transition (GRANTED first-print), guarded by a per-door `sounded` bit
in SetupStore so reprints never re-knock. Price: SMALL. The sheet gains a sense Blockit's
welcome doesn't have — and it is information, not decoration: knock count = doors set.
