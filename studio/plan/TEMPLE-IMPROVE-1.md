## E1 ★ BUILD-FIRST — The desk clock: one alpha law for the whole frame

**What exists.** PRESENTATION §6 files the numerals' cure only:
Temple's token #F3DFB2 at full alpha measures 0.750 against Nacht's
0.25 ceiling; at a 55% composite it lands at **0.201** (recomputed:
0.2012 — exact). But the shipped overlay (lane code,
AirlockAndDisarmOverlays.kt) prints FOUR things, and Salz's budget
(REFINE-salz-aod.md §1–2, trunk tokens) proves the numerals are
only the largest spender (71.6% of the night integral): the
progress strip's fill is the same full-alpha ink across a 16dp
full-width bar that *grows* all night (the frame's last hour is
its brightest, +39% seal→end; the strip reaches 48% of the
numerals' spend rate), and the task banner's text prints at full
primary too. In Temple's own token those are 0.750 prints, not
0.8125 — the breach class is identical. The exit line ships at
alpha 0.6. Salz's cure for it (trunk token #FFE4CF: 0.261 over the
ceiling) is 0.6 → **0.58**. Temple's token at 0.6 composites to
0.242 — under the ceiling, but by 0.008, a hair; at 0.58 it
composites to **0.223**, a real margin. The brass stand
(PRESENTATION §6, DEEPEN §5: one brass hairline beneath the
numerals, the hour inscribed at its right end) is unpriced as
light: full night brass #A37F35 measures **0.232** luminance —
lawful, by 0.018, and only if it is never brightened.

**What changes.** One alpha table governs every print on the
clock — no element invents its own dimming:

- Numerals: **55%** → 0.201 (as filed).
- Strip fill: **55%** → 0.201, at every progress value. The strip
  stops being the breach that grows; early and late captures
  (Salz §3: ≤0.1 and ≥0.9) acquit the same number.
- Banner text: **55%** → 0.201. (Banner ground #392A16 at 0.026
  is acquitted as shipped and does not move.)
- Exit line: **0.6 → 0.58** (Salz's cure, adopted verbatim) →
  0.223 in Temple's token.
- The stand: full brass, **0.232, capped by law** — it is the one
  element that does NOT take 55% (at 55% it composites to 0.066
  and stops reading as furniture). Instead it gains two short end
  ticks (feet, ~8dp, same brass): across a dark room a lone
  hairline reads as a stray mark; a line with feet reads as a
  stand, and its length anchors the numerals' width. The hour at
  its right end (DEEPEN §9.3) prints in parchment at the
  numerals' own 55% → 0.201 — the hour is a numeral and obeys
  the numerals' law.

**Why it's better.** The desk clock is the surface his day
touches longest (03:30 meditation, glanced across the room; the
SLEEP span all night). A numerals-only cure ships a clock that
still breaches on a bar that brightens toward morning — the
exact hour it must be dimmest. One table is also one thing for
Linea's L05 captures to acquit, element by element, in Salz's
own terms.

**Cost:** view-only. Four alpha constants + the stand's two ticks
in the one overlay composable. No derivation, no machinery: the
idle-clock truth fault (dial setting dressed as a time) is
ST-01's, already ruled, and is not re-filed here.

## E2 ★ BUILD-FIRST — The dial's reading tiers, by surface not by mode

**What exists.** Ruth's fix (a) is in code (CURE-VERIFY §2a): day
mids take text primary, minors primary at 85%, unfilled wells
primary at 45%. It is a *tone* cure, assigned *by mode*. It stops
one surface short. The dial's scale numerals (30/60/90/120/150/
180) and the TOTAL eyebrow sit on the slab, and on the slab the
day's secondary tier measures **3.84:1** (#6E5A3A on #E3C184,
recomputed) — below any body or scale floor. On recess it is
3.92. Secondary is lawful by day only on ground (4.84) and sheet
(5.25). In the phase-1 day frame the numerals, the eyebrow and
the FLOW label are exactly the elements that wash. By night the
same numerals sit on raised bronze, where the muted tier
measures 3.62 — decorative duty, carrying a scale. DEEPEN §3's
chisel pair (1dp dark edge + 1dp light edge) is filed as the
daylight system but not sequenced: applied everywhere at once,
it is a programme; applied to the fine tier first, it is a fix.

**What changes.** A tier-by-surface table replaces tier-by-mode
for everything printed on the dial and slab:

- **Scale numerals, both modes:** glyph tier — day #4A3A24
  (**6.36** on slab), night #EFE0BE (**10.60** on raised). The
  scale is how a duration is set; it is not furniture and never
  takes secondary or muted again, on any surface.
- **TOTAL eyebrow + FLOW label (on slab):** glyph tier, same
  numbers as above. An eyebrow that names the instrument's mode
  is a label, not a whisper.
- **Chisel pair, sequenced:** minors and unfilled wells get the
  pair FIRST (dark edge = primary at the §1.4 depths already in
  code; light edge = sheet tone by day, raised tone by night —
  DEEPEN §3's candidate token, now priced: sheet on slab steps
  only 1.08 in tone, which is the point — the pair encodes
  *shape*, and shape is what survives glare). Majors keep the
  tone cure as-is; they read already.
- **Unfilled wells gain a pit, not just a paler dot:** the chisel
  pair drawn as a 1dp inner arc on the shadow side. Ruth's 45%
  tone stays as the fill; the arc is what lets a fingertip-count
  become an eye-count at glancing light (Fremd's eight dots
  question, answered in shape).

**Why it's better.** §1.4 cured the ticks' *colour*; the scale
they belong to was left at 3.84 on the very stone the cure
deepened. This edge finishes the argument Ruth actually made —
the fine tier is the tier you set a duration by — and extends it
to the numerals that tier is read against, in both modes, with
the deepening's own mechanism spent where glare bites first.

**Cost:** view-only. Same sites as the §1.4 cure
(BlockitCoreViews.kt tick voices + wells) plus the eyebrow/FLOW
label colours in TactileDeckScreen.kt. No geometry moves.

## E3 ★ BUILD-FIRST — Slab shadow: from one shadow to one light direction

**What exists.** Ruth's fix (b) is in code (CURE-VERIFY §2b):
slab #E3C184 (tone step 1.26 off ground) + a real 7dp shadow in
the hero's own shape. DEEPEN §3 budgets three reliefs (stele 7dp,
seated stone 2dp, course joint 1dp). What neither files: the
shadow's *direction and colour*, and the slab's lit edges. In the
phase-1 frame the slab separates mostly at its bottom; its top
and side edges still meet the ground at the 1.26 tone step alone,
which is precisely what noon glare erases first (Ruth: card and
ground barely separate *at the glance* — the first second, not
the inspection). The same whisper repeats one screen over: day
sheets step off ground at only **1.08** (#EFE5CB on #E8DCC0,
recomputed), so Tasks tablets and Shield plaques float on
hairlines exactly as the slab did. And Compose's default shadow
is a neutral dark — a grey shadow on warm stone reads as dirt,
not as shade.

**What changes.** One sun for the whole day face, from top-left,
spent in three places:

- **The slab gets its lit edges:** a 1dp sheet-tone line along
  the slab's top and left edges, the existing 7dp shadow falling
  bottom/right. Tone step (1.26) + lit edge + cast shadow is a
  separation that survives glare at any one of the three; the
  eye needs only one to survive.
- **The shadow is tinted:** ambient/spot shadow colour = the ink
  #2B2013 at ~25% alpha, not default black. Shade on sandstone
  is brown; the shadow joins the palette instead of greying it.
- **The same direction, rationed, on the working screens:** day
  tablets/plaques (Tasks sheets, Shield routine plaques) take
  the DEEPEN 2dp seated-stone shadow, bottom/right, same tint,
  with the same 1dp lit top edge. Their 1.08 tone step stops
  being their only separation. Course joints keep the 1dp
  shadow line, same side. Three reliefs, one sun, no fourth
  size invented.

**Why it's better.** Ruth's charge was a slab that merges at the
glance. A tone step is a fact for a meter; an edge pair plus a
directional shadow is a fact for an eye in sunlight. It also
completes DEEPEN §3's claim — relief as *the* daylight
legibility system — on the screens the deepening left at
hairlines, and it makes the day Deck, Tasks and Shield read as
one building under one light, which is the Fremd version of the
soul test.

**Cost:** small. Shadow parameters + one edge draw at the hero;
the 2dp variant repeated at tablet/plaque sites. View only.

## E4 ★ BUILD-FIRST — Shield: found by marks, not by reading

**What exists.** PRESENTATION §2 adopts Wetter's seal stamp —
each section header preceded by a small vermilion *outline*
seal — and DEEPEN §4 houses the sections as courses and promotes
the frieze to the entablature. All filed, none yet strong
enough. In the phase-1 frames, LOADOUT / ROUTINES / ALL APPS are
the same small-caps tier as the labels beneath them, and the
right-edge counts (7 SHUT · 3 PEEK / 3 ACTIVE / SHOWING 12) sit
at the same weight — a tired eye at 19:30 finds a section by
reading down the page until a word matches. A 10dp stamp beside
a same-tier word is a decoration on a label, not a landmark:
peripheral vision cannot use it, and the stamp column has
nothing connecting it — three isolated marks on a long scroll.

**What changes.** The stamp becomes a wayfinding system of three
parts, all already Temple grammar:

- **Stamp + joint:** each header's outline seal is followed by
  its course joint — the brass hairline drawn the header's full
  width, at the FULL outline token (see E5: not the 46% variant).
  Mark finds the eye; the line confirms the boundary without a
  word read.
- **The spine:** the three seals share one x-position at the
  wall's left edge, and the section body indents past it. Down a
  long scroll (ALL APPS runs twelve rows and more) the eye rides
  the left-edge column of marks the way a hand rides a rail;
  sections are counted by marks passed, then confirmed by words.
- **Header tier split:** headers take glyph ink (day 8.03 on
  ground / night 10.60 on raised) at letterspaced caps; the
  right-edge counts stay glyph but drop one size. Counts are
  facts he checks *after* finding the section; headers are what
  he finds it *by*. One tier difference, spent exactly there.

**Why it's better.** Wetter's charge — sections found by
reading — is cured by shape or not at all. The frieze (DEEPEN
§4) tells him his day at the top; the spine tells him where he
is on the wall at any scroll depth. At the between-block glance
(30 seconds, one thumb) that is the difference between scanning
and searching. The vermilion fence is untouched: outline only,
authentication only; filled stays fenced to wounds (§8 of the
presentation).

**Cost:** small. One header composable changed once (stamp +
joint + tier), spent at three sites; body indent at the same
sites. View only, no state.

## E5 ★ BUILD-FIRST — Night staircase: the hairline defence, at full strength and doubled

**What exists.** PRESENTATION §10 files N1 recess→ground and N2
block bar on ground both at **1.05:1**, defended in one sentence:
recesses are read not by luminance but by the brass hairline
that closes them, **5.06:1** on ground (Salz-recomputed exact).
The defence is true of the *token* and not always of the
*drawing*. Three facts, all recomputed tonight:

- 5.06 is the FULL night brass #A37F35 on ground. The hairline
  token actually spent across the lane's borders is
  outlineVariant — brass at 0x75 alpha (≈46%) — which composites
  over ground to ≈**2.00:1**, and over raised to ≈1.86. The Deck
  hero's own border is drawn in it (TactileDeckScreen.kt:529).
  Wherever a recess is closed by the variant, the filed defence
  overstates the drawn defence by 2.5×.
- Even at full strength, the closing line fades as the staircase
  climbs: brass on raised is **3.72**, on sheet **2.89** — the
  higher the step, the weaker its edge, which is backwards.
- N2's bar is the recess token seated under the slide track: it
  has a top edge against the track and no lit edge of its own.

**What changes.** Stronger, in the staircase's own currency —
two edges instead of one louder line:

- **Closing lines take the full token, by law.** Any hairline
  that *closes* a recess, a course, or the block bar is drawn in
  full NightColorOutline (5.06 on ground / 5.32 on recess).
  OutlineVariant is demoted to non-closing decoration only —
  inner ticks, secondary rules — where its 2.00 whisper is a
  texture, not a boundary. Same rule by day, where the variant
  composites to 1.68 on ground / 1.55 on slab: no boundary by
  day is drawn in it either.
- **The double edge on raised and sheet.** Where a recess or
  sheet edge sits on raised (brass alone: 3.72 / 2.89), the
  closing line becomes DEEPEN §3's chisel pair at night: 1dp
  full brass on the lit side + 1dp recess-black #0E0906 on the
  shadow side (recess against raised steps 1.43, against sheet
  1.84 — weak alone, but the pair's two edges step in *opposite
  directions* off the surface, so the boundary reads by shape
  at any light level, lamplight included).
- **N2's bar gains its top edge:** one full-brass hairline along
  the block bar's top, where it meets the slide track — the lamp
  catches the bar's rim; the bar is read by its rim and its
  words, never by its 1.05 step.

**Why it's better.** §10's defence was an assertion about a
token; this makes it a property of the drawing. It answers the
question put to this pass — *can the hairline defence be
stronger?* — yes, twice over: spend the token the filing
cites, and where one line thins, cut two edges. Night was
Temple's honoured face (Ruth); this keeps it honoured on the
surfaces above ground, where the first filing's numbers quietly
ran out. Nacht's 03:30 test remains the court, as everywhere.

**Cost:** view-only. Token choice at border sites + one added
1dp line at raised/sheet closings. No palette value changes —
not one hex in Color.kt moves.

## E6 — Receipt quiet lines: quiet by placement, never by tone

**What exists.** PRESENTATION §5 writes the slip to the N4
fence: fact rows in primary ink only, the one quiet line (the
WITHIN window) at the glyph tier — **8.24:1** on the night sheet,
against secondary's unlawful **3.49**. That law names ONE quiet
line. A real slip carries more: the № line, the intercept log's
entries, the recovered-finish caption under the brass-outline
seal, the footer. None is assigned a tier by name, and the
temptation at build time is uniform: make a line "quiet" by
dropping it a tone tier. By day the same move passes unnoticed
(secondary on day sheet is 5.25, lawful) — so the breach would
be built in daylight and only exist at night, on the one surface
he reads at 19:30 with tired eyes, and the one surface he has
loved on sight. A second trap of the same kind: the recovered
caption explaining the *brass-outline* seal (§5) set in brass —
brass on the night sheet measures **2.89**, a caption that
cannot be read explaining a seal that must be understood.

**What changes.** An enumerated quiet-line law for the slip,
both modes, no exceptions:

- Every printed line on the sheet is primary or glyph. By name:
  fact rows primary; № line, WITHIN, intercept entries,
  recovered caption, footer — glyph (night 8.24 on sheet; day
  #4A3A24 on sheet **8.71**).
- Quietness is made of *placement and spacing*, never tone: a
  quiet line drops 2sp, gains letterspacing, and sits below the
  brass head rule or above the perforation, separated by white
  space. A slip is print; print has always made hierarchy from
  size and position. Tone is reserved for the seal marks.
- The recovered caption prints in glyph beside its brass-outline
  seal — the mark is brass, the words never are (same rule as
  gold on ground, PRESENTATION §10: metal carries no words).

**Why it's better.** The N4 fence stops being a fence around
one named line and becomes the slip's constitution, checkable
line by line at the merge. It also protects the day/night
symmetry of the loved slip: the same document in both modes,
not a lawful day slip and a breaching night one.

**Cost:** view-only. Text colour/size mapping on ReceiptSheet —
but it must land *with* the receipt's in-world build, not after
it; Setzer should take this edge in the same hand as §5.

## E7 — Tasks sub-steps: work content at a work tier

**What exists.** In the day Tasks frame, each task's sub-steps
("Draw free-body diagrams…", "Solve equilibrium equations…")
print in a pale wash on the sheet — the weakest-reading body
text in the whole Temple set. At night the same lines sit at
secondary on raised (4.48, a hair under the body floor) or
worse. Ruth acquitted Tasks' *titles* and the tear verbs at full
voice; the sub-steps are not furniture under the titles — they
are the work itself, the lines he reads to know what the next
50 minutes are. The ACTIVE/QUEUE distinction that the wash
currently carries is already carried by position, the ACTIVE
label, and the gold START.

**What changes.** Sub-steps take glyph tier in both modes (day
8.71 on sheet; night 10.60 on raised / 8.24 on sheet). The
right-edge step numbers (01/02/03) take glyph too — they are
the index he counts progress through the task by. Hierarchy
inside a task is then made by the title's weight and the tear
line, as on the receipt (E6): size and position, not wash.
Nothing else on Tasks moves; the perforation dashes Ruth found
reading as structure stay as they are.

**Why it's better.** A blocker that stores his tasks owes him
those tasks legibly at the tired glance — this is the
between-block surface, read more often than the receipt and
nearly as often as the Deck. It also discharges, on Tasks, the
residue Ruth folded into her Condition 1 (index and sub-lines
that fade), in the place that residue actually costs him work.

**Cost:** view-only. Two text-colour mappings on the task row.

## E8 — Tab tiers: one law for three tab families

**What exists.** Temple has three tab families and, as rendered,
one habit — dimming the unselected: (i) Shield category tabs
(ALL / SHORT VIDEO / SOCIAL / …), which PRESENTATION §2 already
files a [MERGE] fix for (secondary by day, glyph by night);
(ii) the loadout selector (STUDY / WORK / SLEEP on its track);
(iii) the bottom rail (DECK / SHIELD / TASKS / LEDGER). The
filed fix for (i) is surface-blind like E2's dial fix: secondary
by day is 4.84 on ground but **3.92 on the recess the loadout
track is carved from** and 3.84 on slab; at night, §2's glyph
for tabs is right on ground but the rail's unselected labels sit
on the bar's dark bronze where muted measures 4.93 on ground
and less on the bar itself. In the frames, all three families
show it: category row dimmest at night, WORK/SLEEP washed on
the day track, rail labels the faintest words on both Decks.

**What changes.** One tab law, stated by surface like E2:

- Unselected tab labels never print below glyph tier by day
  (ground 8.03 / recess 6.49 / slab 6.36) or below secondary by
  night **on ground only** (6.10) — on any darker or higher
  night surface (rail bar, raised track) they take glyph
  (10.60 on raised).
- Selected state is carried by the mark, never by making the
  others unreadable: bronze underline (category), the set-stone
  cap (loadout, DEEPEN §4's keystone grammar), the bronze pill
  (rail). Bronze chooses — the marks are already Temple's; the
  words stop paying for the selection.
- The category row keeps §2's spelled fix and gains this floor:
  if a future surface puts the row on a sheet or a plaque, the
  floor travels with it.

**Why it's better.** Tabs are the app's wayfinding at setup
time — evening, tired, one-handed. Ruth named this exact residue
("the tab row is dim in both states, worst at night") and
Blueprint's amendment found the same disease in its own row;
Temple's version is curable in one law because its tiers, unlike
its neighbours', hold on every surface once assigned by surface.

**Cost:** view-only. Label colour mapping in three composables;
no mark, position, or destination changes (the rail's verdict-
fixed destinations are untouched, per DEEPEN §11).

## E9 — The witness line and the course labels at glyph tier

**What exists.** The Deck's status strip prints its values
(OFF / 10 / ENDS 16:27) at full voice and everything that
*decodes* them a tier down: the labels BLOCK / APPS / TIME and,
beneath, the witness line — BIND NOT HELD / PROOF: POSITIVE —
in small muted caps. DEEPEN §2 rehomes these as courses with the
witness course at the base, seal struck beside it when positive.
Housing changed; tiers did not. On the day slab, muted/secondary
labels measure 3.84–3.92 (E2's numbers) — the witness fact, the
one line that says whether the machine is actually holding, is
the hardest line on the Deck to read. Fremd stumbled on both
verdicts in phase 1; making them courses he still cannot read
at a glance repeats the stumble in masonry.

**What changes.** In the courses' grammar: course labels and
the witness line take glyph tier in both modes (day 6.36 on
slab; night 10.60 on raised — the witness course sits on the
stele's base course). The values stay primary. The vermilion
outline seal beside a positive proof stays exactly as filed
(PRESENTATION §1.1) — it is a mark, and with the line at glyph
the mark and the words finally arrive together.

**Why it's better.** PROOF and BIND are truth statements, the
class Temple exists to state plainly (the machine's own state,
never implied). A truth line at a furniture tier contradicts
its content. Cost is one mapping; the gain is that the Deck's
most important sentence survives the same glare test as its
hero number.

**Cost:** view-only. Label/witness colour mapping in the status
courses.
