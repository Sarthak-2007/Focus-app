## 1 — Where may the "what fires next" readout live?

**The question in plain words.** Several screens want to print what
happens next (e.g. "NEXT 05:00 FASTED BLOCK"). The same fact currently
has four printings across two designs: one in the top strip of every
screen, and three inside the routines design (its section header, its
per-routine state line, its edit-panel footer). One fact printed four
times will drift, and two of the copies sit in one glance. Pick its
one home; the other three die.

**Options.**
- **A. Top strip only.** The readout lives in the chrome strip every
  screen carries; all three routines printings are deleted (≈2h of
  deletions in the routines file). Consequence: "what's next" is
  answerable from any screen, including at the arm moment; the
  routines screen keeps its own per-routine facts (last run, pauses)
  but never the global next firing.
- **B. Routines header only.** The strip's slot dies; the routines
  section header keeps the single printing, its state line and panel
  footer die. Consequence: "what's next" is only visible after
  opening the routines surface — invisible from the main screen where
  the morning actually starts.
- **C. Nowhere as a standing print.** All four die; the next firing is
  read inside the routines surface when he goes there, and the main
  screen's primed frame already shows the imminent block. Consequence:
  zero duplication forever, at the price of the one glance-fact the
  strip was built to carry.

**My recommendation: A — top strip only.** The merge conditions already
state this home (condition 7); the wave asked for enforcement, not a
new idea. The next firing is a glance fact needed where he arms, not a
fact that belongs to the editor of routines. Whichever home wins, the
print form is already settled: facts with day-qualifiers, never
minute-precision theatre.

**Sources.** `invention/IMPROVERS-INVENTIONS.md` §5.1 and §2 (07, 15);
`invention/improvers-parts/exam-linea.md` charges 15.2 (four homes,
both cures priced) and 07.1 (the three printings named: section header
`NEXT 05:00 FASTED BLOCK`, state line `NEXT · TODAY 05:00`, panel
footer `NEXT · TODAY 22:30`, all from one `nextStartMillis()` call);
`invention/improvers-parts/15-band-bottom-nav-reinvention.md` §3.1
(the colophon claim: Row 1 right as "its only home in the app").

## 2 — Which word-set governs: the app's word-rules, or the routines' settled sentences?

**The question in plain words.** One seat wrote the app's word-rules:
one word may carry only one fact, and verdict-words like MISSED are
dead — a routine that didn't run prints DID NOT RUN. But the routine
rows carry sentences settled earlier under chair authority (`MISSED 3
IN A ROW`, `KEPT 5 · HELD 1 · MISSED 1`) and were ordered carried
verbatim. Both cannot ship: the same line would be lawful in two
different wordings depending on which file a builder opens.

**Options.**
- **A. The word-rules govern; the settled sentences are reworded to
  match** (≈1h sweep on the routines side). Consequence: one
  vocabulary for one class of fact everywhere, including the
  permanent record he may read years from now; the earlier
  settlement's *mechanism* (the app never pauses a routine by itself;
  pause is written only by his tap) is untouched — only its words
  change.
- **B. The settled sentences stand as a named exception** written into
  the word-rules. Consequence: the exception is permanent — every
  future seat must learn that one surface speaks a second vocabulary,
  and the record side (which prints DID NOT RUN) and the routine rows
  (which print MISSED) visibly disagree in kind forever.
- **C. Split by surface** — word-rules on record surfaces, settled
  sentences on routine rows. Consequence: option B with a blurrier
  border; the first surface that is both (a routine's history) has no
  governing set.

**My recommendation: A — the word-rules govern; reword the settled
sentences.** The settlement protected a mechanism — no auto-pause, ever
— and that mechanism survives any wording; its sentences were the
vehicle, not the verdict. Since the wave, the record side became
permanent storage he explicitly wants to re-read after months and
years, in an app he has ordered to carry no marks or grades; MISSED is
a grade word. One vocabulary for one fact is the truth-once standard
applied to language. (The word-rules' own internal error — `SEALED 5`
for a completed routine, colliding with SEALED the lock state — needs
no ruling: its author cures it to `RAN 5` under his own Rule 2.)

**Sources.** `invention/IMPROVERS-INVENTIONS.md` §5.2 and §2 (07, 10);
`invention/improvers-parts/exam-linea.md` charges 10.1 (`SEALED 5`
self-collision, cure `RAN 5`), 10.2 (the unilateral rewording of
chair-settled text), and 07.4 (the mirror charge on the routines file,
incl. `LAST 7 · KEPT 5 · HELD 1 · MISSED 1` standing verbatim);
`invention/improvers-parts/10-wort-filter-words-reinvention.md`
(the Lexicon: five classes + derived class I, eight decision rules);
`invention/improvers-parts/07-reihe-routine-rows-reinvention.md` §2
(Rung 5 carried verbatim; audited LAWFUL and intact by Linea).

## 3 — Where does the block count live if the round wheel is ever replaced?

**The question in plain words.** The number of blocks (1–8) is set and
shown in one place. Two designs each claim that place: small wells
arranged in two groups of four (the design already built into the
round wheel), and a segmented ring bezel drawn around it claiming
roughly eight times the touch area. A third design — a straight ruler
that replaces the wheel entirely — carries the wells on a spool, and
the bezel physically cannot move onto it (the bezel is wider than the
spool). If nothing is ruled, both files keep pricing a funeral neither
includes, and a naive merge prints the count twice.

**Options.**
- **A. The count lives on the time instrument, in well form — the
  home already built.** The round wheel keeps its wells; the bezel
  dies as a second home (its press-and-direct-set grammar already
  lives inside the wells); if the straight ruler is ever revived and
  wins, its spool inherits the wells and the bezel stays dead.
  Consequence: zero rework on what shipped; one home, ruled now, for
  every branch.
- **B. The bezel becomes the count's home on the round wheel.**
  Consequence: its self-contradictory setting table must be
  re-derived first (one segment is specified as both "5" and "the
  group's end, 8" — a 180-minute error), its "closed ring" claim
  re-argued (the ring as drawn never closes), ≈1.5 days — buying a
  larger touch target that must re-prove its area claim.
- **C. Rule only the conditional, leave today's home open.**
  Consequence: the most expensive silence in the wave — two candidate
  homes stay alive in the files and no builder may touch the count.

**My recommendation: A — wells, on the instrument, ruled now.** The
wells already shipped and passed their five-test gate; the bezel's own
file contradicts itself on the one table that matters and its ring
does not close — it has not earned the home. Fader's charge stands:
rule before either builds, because the transplant is geometrically
impossible later.

**Sources.** `invention/IMPROVERS-INVENTIONS.md` §5.3 and §2 (01, 03);
`invention/improvers-parts/exam-fader.md` X1 (two homes mutually
fatal as filed; bezel outer edge r63.5 > spool r56 — transplant
impossible; bezel re-spec ≈6h at spool radii with its 8× claim not
surviving) and X2 (the shared grammar inherits the contradiction);
`invention/improvers-parts/03-nabe-hub-reinvention.md` §2.2
(direct-set table) and §5 claim 10 (the author's own shrink
concession); `invention/improvers-parts/01-rad-wheel-reinvention.md`
§2.1 (the spool's wells).

## 4 — The two "blocking is off" sentences: final words, final place

**The question in plain words.** When blocking is off, the app must
say so — but two sentences for that one fact exist, in two places,
disagreeing about what to do next: under the start slide, at the
moment an arm is refused, `BLOCKING IS OFF — NOTHING WILL BE HELD.`;
and as the standing warning, `BLOCKING IS OFF — TAP TO FIX`. A man
refused at the slide could be shown both in the same minute. (A third
sentence, `BLOCKING UNVERIFIED — …`, is a different fact — "we cannot
check" is not "off" — and is not part of this ruling.) Since the wave,
the Guard ruling dissolved the settings screen: the top strip now
carries one quiet line (`GUARD 4/4`, naming the first lack when
something is off), and repair lives in the PARK drawer — so the
standing warning's old chrome home no longer exists as designed.

**Options.**
- **A. The examiners' cure, as filed.** The refusal keeps the slot
  under the slide but adopts the action words (`… — TAP TO FIX`); the
  standing warning keeps a sentence in the top strip. Consequence:
  two homes for one fact remain — strip sentence plus the Guard
  line's named lack, saying the same thing twice in two dialects.
- **B. One sentence, one home — the slot in the slide's path; the top
  strip carries state, not a sentence.** Final wording:
  `BLOCKING IS OFF — TAP TO FIX`, printed only in the reserved slot
  under the start slide/track, both when standing on the main screen
  and at a refused arm. Everywhere else, the Guard line carries the
  state (`GUARD 3/4 — ACCESSIBILITY OFF`) and tapping it opens PARK,
  where FIX lives. Consequence: the sentence exists exactly where
  his hand already is and where repair is offered; no screen prints
  the fact twice.
- **C. The consequence wording wins everywhere** (`… — NOTHING WILL
  BE HELD.`). Consequence: a refusal with no repair path — a wall,
  not a sentence — and it contradicts the Guard ruling's build term
  that the reserved sentence at the gesture names the lack and offers
  FIX.

**My recommendation: B.** Fader's cure (A) was written before the
Guard ruling existed; under the ruling, a standing sentence in the
strip duplicates the Guard line's named lack — one fact, two homes,
the exact disease this item exists to kill. The sentence's one lawful
home is the reserved slot in the slide's path: it is where refusal
happens, where the Guard ruling puts the named lack and the FIX
offer, and its words must carry the action (`TAP TO FIX`), because
the ruling makes repair an in-path act, not a fact to contemplate.
The companion sentence for the different fact should be worded as its
pair for consistency: `CAN'T CHECK BLOCKING — TAP TO RETRY`.

**Sources.** `invention/IMPROVERS-INVENTIONS.md` §5.4;
`invention/improvers-parts/exam-fader.md` X3 (the three sentences and
three slots named verbatim; the joint cure ≈2h; "a refusal with no
repair path is a wall, not a sentence");
`invention/improvers-parts/02-zug-ii-slide-valve-reinvention.md`
§2.6 (the rail slot sentence) and the wave's re-spec of its evidence

reserved sentence under it names the lack and offers FIX"; PARK
carries the repair board); `plan/MASTER-PLAN.md` §3 (the strip's
reserved slot as merged).

## 5 — Horiz's proof cursor: may a readout be touched if touching it does nothing?

**The question in plain words.** A thin tape near the top of the task
screen shows the day's tasks laid end to end. One design (MaxImpl)
makes tapping a segment DO the same thing as the task row's own CUE
button — load that task and jump to the main screen. Horiz kills that
and offers the opposite settlement: the tape may be touched, but only
to inspect — press and a hairline cursor snaps to that task's start;
drag and it steps from start to start; let go and nothing has
happened: nothing fired, nothing saved, nothing cued. His charge
against the tap version: the same action would live in two places,
and a scroll that begins on the tape could end as an accidental cue.

**Options.**
- **A. Interrogation only (the proof cursor).** Touch asks; it never
  acts. Haptics: a 1ms detent on press and ticks at boundaries,
  haptic-only; sound silent on every path; passive scrolling over the
  tape stays silent. Consequence: the tape can never spend the day by
  accident; the one CUE action keeps its single home in the row, 8dp
  away.
- **B. Tap-to-cue (MaxImpl's wiring).** Tap a segment = the row's CUE
  exactly (same callback, then navigate). Consequence: a second
  executive surface inside a scrolling list; its own spec flags the
  scroll-interop behaviour as the one thing renders cannot prove — it
  needs a device check to be safe — and its pressed-state re-tones a
  segment to imply a state change before any state has changed.
- **C. No touch at all** (Horiz's own earlier position). Consequence:
  dead on arrival — its author buried it himself: a dead strip among
  touchable instruments reads broken, and the tape now holds facts
  (starts, joints) the rows don't, which a hand may legitimately ask
  about.

**My recommendation: A — the proof cursor.** One verb, one home is
the truth-once standard in its action form; the row is 8dp away, so
the shortcut buys almost nothing and pays for it in accidental cues at
scroll prices. The examiner credited the settlement as genuinely
principled, and Horiz specced his file to survive losing — ruling A
costs the losing side zero rework. His tape's other charges (two
totals in one glance; the minimum-width distortion) are separate and
already priced; they do not touch this ruling.

**Sources.** `mail/ren/0715__from-horiz__proof-cursor-settlement.md`
(his letter to the chair); `invention/improvers-parts/11-horiz-
horizon-reinvention.md` §3 (both old positions killed by their owners;
§3.3 the proof cursor spec: snapped hairline, boundary stepping,
release fires nothing, 600ms fade);
`maximpl/08-horizon-bar.md` §9 (the cue-tap wiring: `onSegmentTapped`
→ `onCueTaskToFocus` + `onNavigateToDeck`, pressed tonal step, the
device-check flag verbatim);
`invention/improvers-parts/exam-linea.md` part 11, "in the part's
favour" (the settlement credited); `invention/IMPROVERS-INVENTIONS.md`

## 6 — Band's sealed line: when blocking dies mid-session, what may the top strip print?

**The question in plain words.** While a session is sealed, the top
strip prints `SEALED · UNTIL 07:00`. If blocking itself dies
mid-session (a permission revoked or a service killed from outside
the app), the design as filed keeps printing SEALED over a guard that
is no longer holding, and queues the bad news until the session ends.
That failed examination: a promise the machinery can't keep, printed
for the rest of the block. Two cures were offered. Since then,
Sarthak has ruled on this exact event: while it happens — silence,
nothing signalled; he is told once, on the finish receipt; the clock
runs to its scheduled end. (Ren's recorded consequence: such a
session books as BROKEN on the receipt, never a clean seal.)

**Options.**
- **A. Name it in place, inertly (≈3h).** On a mid-session death, the
  strip's health slot alone unfreezes: the named lack prints at full
  reading size (`ACCESSIBILITY OFF`), the SEALED line stays, and
  nothing is tappable, sounded, or offered — a fact, not a door.
  Consequence: he learns mid-session — which his 07:22 word now
  forbids; a print is a signal even when it opens nothing.
- **B. The line dies to blank (≈1h).** On death, `SEALED · UNTIL
  07:00` is replaced by nothing — no signal, and no false promise
  either; the failure prints once at session end, on the receipt,
  where his ruling puts it. Consequence: the strip goes quiet in a
  way he may or may not notice; the truth is kept whole and delivered
  exactly where he ordered it.
- **C. As filed** — keep printing SEALED, queue the news. **Dead:**
  FAILS-HOLD at examination; it is the concealment both cures exist
  to kill. Listed only so the record shows it was refused, not
  overlooked.

**My recommendation: B — the line dies to blank.** The examiners
wrote A before Sarthak spoke; his word outranks both cures and
matches only B: silence during, no lie during, truth once at the end.
B is also the cheaper cure and sets the precedent he already chose
for every sealed surface. The receipt consequence is already recorded
with his verdict: the session books as BROKEN, never sealed-clean.

**Sources.** `invention/improvers-parts/15-band-bottom-nav-
reinvention.md` §3.3 (the as-filed sealed strip: NEXT freezes as
`SEALED · UNTIL 07:00`, failure queued to session end);
`invention/improvers-parts/exam-linea.md` charge 15.1 (FAILS-HOLD;
primary cure ≈3h, fallback cure ≈1h, priced verbatim);
`invention/IMPROVERS-INVENTIONS.md` §5.6 and §2 (15); Sarthak's
verdicts of 29 Oct 07:22 IST (blocking death: stay silent; the clock
runs), carried in `plan/MASTER-PLAN.md` §0.
