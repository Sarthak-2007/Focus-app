## 0. THE WORLD, IN ONE BREATH — AND THE SOUL TEST, ANSWERED IN STRUCTURE

Blueprint is a **technical drawing of a day that will be built**.
Deep indigo line-work on a white plate by day; the drawing inverted
at night — deep indigo ground, line-work in plate white. One ink.
Anonymous Pro dominant, because a drawing's figures are its voice;
Sora appears once per plate, in the title block's wordmark;
Atkinson carries names and sentences a man writes himself.

**The Wholeness Order binds, and this world obeys it by
construction, not by argument.** A drawing is not a layout with
rules drawn on it; a drawing *generates* its layout:

- **Every screen is one plate.** A double hairline frame, corner
  registration marks, a tick rule along the bottom margin, and a
  **title block** — the ruled box, bottom-right on a real sheet,
  that states the drawing's name, number, scale and revision. The
  trunk's floating rail and header band **die** in this world
  (§1.1, §1.13): navigation is the plate index in the title block,
  and pre-flight truth is a note in the margin, because that is
  where a draughtsman puts both.
- **The Deck is a dimensioned elevation.** The hero is not a
  monument number on a card; it is a **dimension figure** — the
  span, figured in Anonymous Pro, set between extension lines that
  reach to the wheel it measures. You do not read the Deck top to
  bottom; you read it the way a sheet is read: title, figure,
  detail, tolerance block, stamp.
- **The Shield is a parts list.** A loadout *is* a bill of
  materials: ruled rows, item numbers, a quantity column that is
  the honest SHUT/PEEK split, revision toggles in the margin.
  Routines are not cards; they are the **scale of the day**, one
  horizontal dimension line 03:30→03:30 on which each window is a
  measured segment.
- **The commit is a stamp.** The slide does not change colour or
  acquire a glow; at the seal the drawing is **stamped APPROVED**
  in the title block's revision cell — the one solid indigo plate
  in the world (the Commit Plate, §1.10), because on a drawing the
  stamp is the only filled thing.

Strip the colour (Fremd's soul test) and the other five worlds
collapse to a header, a hero card, a list. Blueprint collapses to
a framed sheet with a title block, a dimension chain and a parts
list. That is a different app, not a sixth coat.

**Grammar laws (every element below obeys them):**

1. **Line hierarchy is meaning.** 1.5dp ink = structure (frame,
   commit, the figure's extension lines). 1dp = working lines
   (rows, wells, tracks). 0.75dp at 0x75 alpha = hairlines
   (separators, the tick rule). A line's weight is its rank; no
   line is drawn for decoration, and no fact is promoted by colour
   — there is one ink, so promotion is weight, size, or position
   on the sheet, nothing else.
2. **One ink, one red.** Indigo draws everything that is true.
   Annotation red (#C8102E day / #FF6B7A night) is the
   draughtsman's pencil: it appears only where the drawing
   *corrects* — a refusal, a lack, the Held Door's HOLD stamp.
   Red never decorates, never fills a commit, never marks a
   selection. (Wetter: "it was never decoration; it is the
   draughtsman overruling the hand." Adopted as law.)
3. **Figures are Anonymous Pro, aligned like dimensions.**
   Every number in the world — spans, clock times, counts, the
   receipt № — is tabular, right-aligned to its column or centred
   on its dimension line, and never set in a display face. Sora's
   single seat is the title-block wordmark. This is my sharpest
   divergence from Obsidian (§1.3 there keeps the 88sp Sora hero):
   a drawing does not shout its largest figure in a second
   alphabet; it figures it precisely.
4. **Flat by default.** Surfaces share the plate; depth is drawn
   (a heavier frame, a wash one step down), never shadowed. The
   one raised sheet in the world is pure white — the receipt's
   kin and the editor sheet — because a loose sheet on the plate
   is the only thing that is ever physically *on* a drawing.
5. **Nothing unlabelled, nothing unmeasured.** Every control
   carries its name in the margin or its figure beside it. If a
   thing cannot be labelled truthfully, it does not ship (§6).

## 1. THE DECK — element by element

Three moments judge every element: **arming** (03:30, dark, phone
just picked up after meditation is still ahead of him — routines
will arm FASTED 05:00, but the hand arm is his own act, one hand,
eyes half shut), **holding** (mid-session glances — the Deck is a
gauge, not a desk), **the finish** (detention, tear, receipt).

### 1.1 The header band — DIES; replaced by the title block
- **What (trunk):** `MONOLITH` wordmark left, Guard line right
  (`GUARD n/4`, or `GUARD n/4 — <FIRST LACK>`, or silence when
  unmeasured).
- **Decision: the band dies; both facts move to where a drawing
  keeps them.** The wordmark goes into the title block (§1.13)
  in Sora, once. The Guard line becomes a **margin note**,
  top-left inside the frame, Anonymous Pro, hairline-ruled above
  and below: `GUARD 4/4` at working weight; a lack steps the note
  to full ink and the lack's name takes annotation red — the one
  header fact that corrects. Unmeasured still prints nothing:
  silence is the honest third state, kept exactly.
- **Against the field:** Obsidian keeps the band and promotes the
  lack to text-1. Mine does the same promotion by line weight and
  spends red on it, because in a one-ink world a lack *is* an
  annotation. Dawn dissolves her header into sky; a drawing has
  no sky — it has a margin, and margins are where warnings live.

### 1.2 The eyebrow — TOTAL / SESSION RUNNING / BREAK · N MIN
- **Decision: KEEP, as the figure's caption.** It sits directly
  over the dimension figure, letterspaced Anonymous Pro, muted
  tier — a caption tied to its figure by a 6dp leader gap, never
  floating as a section header. During a Safe Break it remains
  the break's only in-app rendering, and it gains the break's
  dimension (§3, the Pill carries it out of the app).

### 1.3 The hero — a dimension figure, not a monument
- **What (trunk):** the true span (blocks × length + breaks,
  priced in) at 88sp Sora; FLOW for the stopwatch; the running
  readout while sealed.
- **Decision: KEEP the fact, REDRAW the form.** The span prints
  in Anonymous Pro at the top of the ramp, centred between two
  extension lines that drop to the wheel's diameter — the figure
  *is* the wheel's dimension. Under it, one dimension line with
  arrowheads at both ends spans the wheel's width: the drawing
  states, geometrically, "this number measures that circle."
  While running, the same figure becomes elapsed/remaining by the
  trunk's existing derivation — same seat, same lines; a running
  drawing is the same sheet with its figure struck through by
  progress, not a new screen.
- **Why this beats the monument:** at 03:30 the contract is not
  "a big number"; it is a measured commitment he can check
  against the wheel with one glance — figure and object tied by
  lines, so a mismatch would be *visible*. Obsidian protects the
  hero's size; Blueprint protects its referent.

### 1.4 The wheel — selection I: THE TURN (a drafted circle)
- **What (trunk):** 288dp dial, drag/fling, 10–180 min in 5-min
  steps, one (θ,ω) integrator, knurl teeth at 3°/120 teeth, FLOW
  across the strait, hub of 8 wells (tap cycles 1→8).
- **Decision: KEEP the machinery whole; the presentation is a
  drafted circle.** Centre mark (the draughtsman's plus, already
  in my lane's wheel drawing), a guide ring with its two breaks,
  teeth as radial ticks — long at the quarters, short between,
  exactly like a scale rule. The selected minute prints **only**
  in the dimension figure (§1.3): one fact, one rendering, and
  the leader lines make the single rendering checkable against
  the hand. The strait's dead sector stays empty of tick, tooth
  and numeral — FLOW's door is a *gap in the scale*, learnable by
  thumb before eye.
- **Hub wells:** KEEP — 8 wells over the guide ring, closure bars
  at count 4 and 8 riding the existing settle fraction. In this
  grammar the wells are the count's *drawing*: n filled of 8,
  read as a shape at 03:30, no arithmetic. The seat knock scaling
  with count (16→24 ms) is the count's sound (§5).

### 1.5 The FLOW key
- **Decision: KEEP as a labelled key under the wheel**, taking the
  solid ink fill when active (a mode drawn solid, like a hatched
  section), never red, never a second commit colour. The named
  accident — tapping FLOW silently sets the dial to 5 — is audited
  at §6: harmless because the 5 never prints, but in a world where
  everything is labelled, an invisible state change is a defect
  of manners; the cure is presentation-only: while FLOW is active
  the wheel's ticks sink to hairline (the scale is out of use,
  drawn out of use).

### 1.6 The running form — progress track, % KILLED
- **Decision: the track becomes a dimension line filling between
  its arrowheads; the `%` twin is DELETED.** Dawn kills it, Noir
  kills it, Obsidian demotes it — the convergence is right and
  Blueprint completes it: a percentage is a second rendering of
  the track's fraction, and on a drawing a fraction is read off
  the line, not re-figured beside it. The elapsed figure lives in
  §1.3's seat. One fact, one rendering, no exceptions for habit.

### 1.7 The NOW row — the task, DONE, QUEUE n
- **Decision: KEEP, drawn as a line item.** NOW is item 01 of the
  day's parts list, set in a ruled row: item number, task name
  (Atkinson, sentence case — his words), DONE as a solid-ink key
  at the row's right end (a verb wears a filled body), `QUEUE n`
  as a margin figure. Tap unfolds sub-steps as indented sub-items
  01.1, 01.2… — a drawing's detail callouts, numbered by the
  shared cured formatter (DRW-08's cure: no `010` ever prints).

### 1.8 The armed utilities — DESK CLOCK · HELD n (running only)
- **Decision: KEEP both, as margin instruments.** While a session
  runs they sit in the plate's bottom margin beside the tick
  rule: two small ruled cells, hairline-framed, words at working
  weight. HELD n prints the honest split's total only here (the
  split itself lives on Shield and on the Held Door, §7) — no,
  ruled on myself: the cell prints `HELD n SHUT · m PEEK` if the
  split fits the cell, else it does not print; a compressed lie
  of a noun (UNA2-02) is not rescued by small type. Final form:
  two cells, `HELD — n SHUT` and `m PEEK`, each its own fact.

### 1.9 The proof strip — BLOCK · APPS · TIME — the tolerance block
- **Decision: KEEP whole, and recognise what it already is:** a
  tolerance block — the ruled table on a drawing that states the
  conditions the work must meet. Three cells, same address,
  pinned under the scroll: BLOCK (the seal's state), APPS (the
  held count, honest split in two lines if needed), TIME (ends-at
  or NO END). The seam and the imprint stay: the imprint is the
  stamp's rehearsal (§1.10). This is the best-designed element in
  the trunk and Blueprint changes only its dress: cell rules at
  working weight, figures Anonymous Pro, no fill in any state —
  a tolerance block is never coloured; it is *met* or it is not.

### 1.10 The slide — selection II: THE COMMIT PLATE
- **Decision: KEEP the slide, and keep my world's most exported
  law: commit has exactly one form.** A solid indigo plate, full
  width of the frame, plate-white words `SLIDE OR TAP TO START`.
  No other control in any Blueprint surface is a solid indigo
  plate — not FLOW-active (hatched outline, §1.5), not DONE
  (solid but key-sized and ink-black indigo at row scale — ruled:
  DONE takes the *outline* key with solid only while pressed, so
  the plate's uniqueness survives; Wetter's Obsidian/Signal
  transplants of this law stand, and I hold the original to the
  same fence). At the seal, the plate does not celebrate: the
  title block's revision cell stamps (§1.13) and the plate
  empties to a hairline register reading `SEALED HH:MM` — a fact,
  witnessed, or nothing.
- **Grace ABORT and the emergency valve** keep the same address
  (Δy = 0 is a mercy to a half-awake hand), drawn as the plate's
  negative: hairline frame, red annotation words for the valve —
  the draughtsman correcting the drawing before it is built.

### 1.11 The refusal sentence
- **Decision: KEEP, in annotation red, in the reserved slot under
  the plate.** `BLOCKING IS OFF — …` is a correction note; red is
  its lawful ink, and the slot's emptiness in the healthy state
  is guarded silence — a drawing does not print "no corrections".

### 1.12 What the Deck does NOT present — named gaps
- **A missed routine prints nothing anywhere (the 20:00 SLEEP
  no-fire left no trace by design — audit §L-A).** Presentation
  cannot fix a deleted row. Blueprint names the gap and designs
  its cured face in advance: if Voss's F2/F3 land, a routine that
  materialised and failed to seal leaves a margin annotation in
  red — `SLEEP 20:00 — SEAL NOT WITNESSED` — printed from the
  WatchStore fact F3 records. Until F3 exists, this line prints
  nowhere and I do not fake it.
- **No current clock time on the Deck.** Klar's pass found it in
  0/5 worlds. A drawing is timeless; his day is not. Ruled: the
  title block carries the sheet's *issue time* — `ISSUED HH:MM`,
  sampled at composition, labelled as issue, never ticking (a
  ticking clock is a live fact needing live machinery; a labelled
  issue stamp is an honest static one). Cost: presentation only.

### 1.13 The rail and PARK — DIE; the plate index replaces them
- **Decision: the floating rail dies.** In its place, the title
  block: a ruled box spanning the bottom margin, cells reading
  `MONOLITH` (Sora, its one seat) · `PLATE 01 / DECK` · the plate
  index `01 DECK · 02 SHIELD · 03 TASKS · 04 LEDGER` with the
  current plate's cell solid-inked (the index is a selection,
  drawn as a revision table, not a commit — solid here is index
  ink at cell scale; the Commit Plate's uniqueness is about the
  *act* form, full-width and verb-bearing — distinction stated so
  a critic doesn't have to invent it) · PARK as the index's last
  cell, a verb in a cell, where a draughtsman parks a sheet.
  Navigation feel: changing plates is turning a sheet — 180 ms
  lateral, no bounce, §5.

## 2. THE SHIELD — a parts list for the day's build

### 2.1 The ratchet banner
- **Decision: KEEP, demoted to an annotation.** A mid-session
  attempt to weaken the loadout is a correction the drawing makes
  to itself: hairline-ruled note, red first words, no fill. (I
  second Obsidian's self-catch: a refusal must never wear a
  commit's dress.)

### 2.2 The LOADOUT header — the honest split, as a quantity column
- **Decision: KEEP `n SHUT • m PEEK` — and make it the parts
  list's quantity line.** The header is the BOM's title row:
  `LOADOUT` left, the split right in Anonymous Pro, and beneath
  it a double rule (the BOM header rule). The compressed noun of
  UNA2-02 ("n APPS WILL SHUT/HELD") prints nowhere in this world.

### 2.3 The loadout cap — selection III: THE THROW (his pick)
- **Decision: KEEP the sliding cap (his tap, Oct 29) — drawn as a
  dimension being set.** The cap slides along a ruled track that
  is a scale: each loadout position is a tick, the cap's current
  position figured above the track in Anonymous Pro. Throwing the
  cap is *dimensioning the shield* — the hand sets a measurement,
  the figure follows the hand, pitch follows value (§5).

### 2.4 ROUTINES — the scale of the day (this world's Shield centre)
- **Decision: the routine cards DIE. In their place, one
  horizontal dimension line — the day, 03:30→03:30 — ruled across
  the Shield's width, each routine a measured segment on it:**
  `FASTED 05:00–08:00`, `DAY 09:00–19:30`, `SLEEP 20:00–03:30`
  (the overnight segment wraps the line's end, drawn with a break
  mark and continued at the start — a draughtsman's break, honest
  about the wrap). Segment labels above the line, figures below,
  extension ticks at every boundary. A disabled routine draws
  hatched (the section-hatch: present, not built). This is the
  frieze Temple adopted from my hairlines (§1.15 there) — here it
  is not an ornament on rows; it *replaces* the rows as the
  summary, and the rows survive beneath it as the parts list's
  routine entries for editing.
- **The reset note** (`SCHEDULE RESET TO YOUR DAY — TAP ANY ROW
  TO CHANGE IT`): KEEP once, as a revision note in the margin —
  revision notes are how drawings confess a change.
- **`LAST ROUTINE CHECK HH:MM` (Voss F5): ADOPTED as a margin
  figure** under the scale, printed from `lastRoutineTickMillis`
  (MonolithDao.kt:263 — the fact exists), blank when unmeasured.
  Price (Voss's own): ~10 lines + one render. After 20:00 on
  Oct 29, this line is the difference between a dead chain he can
  see and one he discovers at bedtime.

### 2.5 A routine row and the CURED editor
- **Collapsed row:** item number, name, segment figure
  `05:00–08:00`, days as a figured mask (`DAILY` / `MON–FRI` /
  the named days — never bare letters that collide: trunk's
  `M T W T F S S` dies; a drawing labels its marks), ON/OFF as a
  margin revision toggle (a small ruled cell, hatched when OFF).
- **The editor (designing for Voss's cured car, not the shipped
  sheet):** a loose sheet on the plate (§0 law 4 — the one raised
  white sheet). Direct time-setting to **any minute**: start and
  end each a figured field — tap the figure, keypad or step by
  1/5/15 — with the segment on the day-scale (§2.4) redrawing
  live above the sheet, so an edit is checked against the whole
  day while it is made: overlaps draw their collision in red at
  the touching boundaries (a presentation of the derive's real
  refusal, not a new rule). Rename (his words, Atkinson), create,
  delete — delete is a red annotation verb on the sheet, never a
  filled plate. Days: seven labelled cells, full names in
  miniature. Every field labelled; no time is ever set by a
  wheel here — a routine is a dimension, and dimensions are
  *figured*, not spun. (Against my own Deck: the wheel is for the
  session he is about to live; the editor is for the drawing of
  his week. Different instruments, deliberately.)

### 2.6 ALL APPS — search and filter words
- **Decision: KEEP, as the parts list's index tools.** Search is
  a recessed well (hairline-edged, §8 N1 mitigation), placeholder
  his trunk words. Filter words (ALL / SHUT / PEEK / OPEN) become
  index tabs ruled like a drawing index — selected tab underlined
  at full weight, never filled. `SHOWING n` is a figured count in
  the margin, and it is the count of the *filtered list*, labelled
  as such by its seat beside the filter.

### 2.7 The app rows — selection IV: THE SLED
- **Decision: KEEP the sled, drawn as a revision toggle.** Each
  row: app mark drawn in line ink (no colour icons on a drawing —
  the icon is rendered as its outline glyph in a ruled cell; where
  the OS gives only a colour icon, it sits in the cell unaltered
  and the cell does the drafting), name, and at the right margin
  the sled: a three-position ruled switch — OPEN / PEEK / SHUT —
  positions labelled, the current position solid. Sliding the
  sled is revising the parts list; the revision is figured
  instantly in §2.2's quantity line (one fact, updated in its one
  seat — the header is that seat; the row shows position, not a
  second count).

### 2.8 BLOCKED WEBSITES — the enforced list
- **Decision: KEEP whole.** "The list that is enforced" is
  already a draughtsman's sentence. Rows as parts-list entries,
  REMOVE as a red margin verb, the add field a recessed well with
  his example text. No toggle exists here that is not the list
  itself — the known nick (a browser-blocker toggle that renders
  nowhere, trunk) is audited at §6, not dressed.

### 2.9 What Shield does NOT present — named gaps
- **Generator fossils:** trunk Shield carries state that occurs
  once and renders nowhere (Noir's S9 grep). Blueprint prints
  none of it and names it at §6.
- **Shorts & Reels:** its toggle is memory-only today. Blueprint
  gives it a sled position among the parts **only if** its
  persistence is cured and priced (a stored flag — machinery,
  named, not assumed); until then it does not appear. A switch
  that forgets is a lie with a handle.

## 3. SELECTION AS A SYSTEM — four instruments, one law

**The law: the hand sets a measurement; the figure follows the
hand; the ear confirms the value (pitch = f(value), §5).** Every
selection in Blueprint is dimensioning:

| Instrument | Sets | Figure's seat | Commit |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Turn (wheel) | session minutes, block count | the dimension figure (§1.3); wells | none — selection is not commitment |
| The Throw (cap) | the loadout | figure over the scale track | the throw itself, reversible until seal |
| The Sled | one app's tier, one site's presence | §2.2 quantity line | per-row, instant, reversible |
| The Figured Field (editor) | routine times/days/name | the day-scale, live | the sheet's SAVE — an outline key, never the Plate |
| The Slide | the session | `SEALED HH:MM` in the register | **the Commit Plate — the only irreversible form in the app** |

Tap vs slide on the commit: both lawful, same plate, same stamp —
the tap is the slide's shorthand for a hand that cannot drag at
03:30, and the drawing does not grade his grip.

**The three moments, walked:**
- **03:30 arming.** He wakes; meditation 04:00–05:00 is phone-
  across-the-room; the routines — not his hand — arm FASTED at
  05:00. Blueprint's arming surface therefore serves two different
  acts and says so: (i) *checking the drawing before the day is
  built* — Shield's day-scale read in one glance (three segments,
  LAST ROUTINE CHECK fresh or stale in red); (ii) *the hand arm*
  when he chooses one — wheel by thumb, figure by leader line,
  pitch ladder in the ear, one slide. Night tokens, §8: nothing on
  the arming sheet exceeds the muted tier except the figure and
  the Commit Plate.
- **Mid-session glance.** The Deck is a gauge: figure (remaining,
  §1.3 seat), dimension-line progress, NOW item. Everything else
  sinks to hairline. The glance target is <1 s: figure, line,
  item — three reads, no search.
- **The finish.** Detention, then the tear, then the receipt
  (§4.3): the drawing is signed off. The Commit Plate's register
  reads the seal time; the receipt is the sheet filed (§4.2).

## 4. THE OTHER ROOMS — sketches (a page or less each)

### 4.1 TASKS — a line-item schedule, after the minutes died
The composer asks **nothing about duration** (his verdict; the
trunk composer's minutes and the row's `START ${estimatedMinutes}m`
are cured-state designs here, not prints I defend). The Tasks
plate is a schedule of line items: a ruled table, item numbers in
Anonymous Pro, his words in Atkinson, one item per row, the queue
in order — order *is* the information; a drawing's schedule has no
durations column because none was ever specified. ADD is an
outline key on a fresh ruled row (he writes on the next line of
the sheet — the metaphor is the interaction). DONE — TEAR stays:
the tear is the counterfoil's, and the booking now precedes the
ghost (Truth Car 2's G-01 cure, designed-for). REOPEN is a margin
verb on the Last-Finished strip, which prints `FINISHED HH:MM`
(true clock) and nothing else. SPLIT unfolds an item into numbered
sub-items (§1.7 numbering). CUE promotes an item to 01 — drawn as
the item's number being re-figured, not a colour change. The
removed-item banner is a revision note with UNDO, filed in the
margin, timed by the trunk's existing window. Empty state:
`NOTHING QUEUED` centred on an otherwise blank ruled sheet — a
drawing with no items is honest white space, not a mascot.

### 4.2 THE DRAWER — a drawing register
The Drawer is the register book where issued sheets are filed:
**finished tasks stored forever, no scores, no grades, no
totals.** Blueprint draws it as a register: one ruled row per
filing day — filing date as the row's *heading label*, each row's
finishes with their true clock times in their own column, the two
never composed into a single date-time (DRW-03's cure, designed-
for: the columns are headed `FILED UNDER` and `AT`, so a 02:00
finish under yesterday's filing reads as two labelled facts, not
one impossible moment). The day word (REST / ILL / FAMILY — the
closed list) is the day's register annotation, in the margin, in
his hand's Atkinson. Search is the register's index well.
ALL MONTHS is the register's index page: month rows, figured
counts of *rows filed* — a count of records is a fact about the
register, not a grade of the man; it prints, labelled `ENTRIES`,
and no other number ever appears on this plate.
**The cabinet dies in this world** — not re-dressed: DRW-01's
invented prices, DRW-02's unbuilt toys, DRW-06's dead SELECT,
DRW-07's phantom synth are Vault furniture in a storage room.
What survives is the one true record (A-10): a finish that forged
an artifact carries a register annotation `FORGED — <name>`, with
the one true forge rule stated once in the register's front note
(Truth Car 2's cured wording). EARLIER prints only on a probed
fact (DRW-04's cure), never on a full page's guess.

### 4.3 THE RECEIPT — the revision slip (the loved surface, held to its bar)
His ❤️ on the batch-7 slip sets the bar: **the printed slip's
character survives whole** — narrow measure, mono figures, torn
edge, one fact per line. Blueprint changes only its letterhead:
the slip is the drawing's **revision slip**, the small printed
record pinned to a sheet when it is issued. Title = his declared
intention (frozen at birth, acquitted). № in Anonymous Pro,
zero-padded, never truncated. The cured lines, designed-for:

## 5. FEEL — the sensory signature, moment by moment

*Ground: Haut's sheet for this world (REFINE-haut §6), adopted
and defended. The instrument is the trunk's: procedural voices,
one fixed envelope family, the seal's 1-second buffer bit-
identical at every seal — **Blueprint proposes no seal sound of
its own**; worlds speak in the approach, never at the seal.*

- **The pitch ladder is LAW (Haut §6, my feel identity).** The
  ViewModel already pitches one detent family by value
  (480 + value×2.5, MonolithViewModel.kt:1795). Blueprint
  generalises it: **pitch = f(value) for every detent family** —
  wheel minutes, hub count, cap positions, sled positions,
  editor steps — monotone in the value, same formula family, so
  the ear reads any dial blind. This is the only "more sound" in
  the wave that is information. I defend it as load-bearing, not
  garnish (Haut's warning, accepted in my inbox letter): cut the
  ladder and Blueprint's feel collapses to a metronome.
- **Haptics: uniform ticks, flat amplitude.** Every detent the
  same 7 ms shape at every tooth; every latch one weight. A spec
  does not swell. If two events differ in the hand, the drawing
  says why (a seat knock scales 16→24 ms with block count —
  mass is a fact, so the hand may feel it; nothing else scales).
- **Arming (03:30).** Teeth felt before seen; each tooth the same
  tick, pitch climbing with minutes — he can count an hour in
  the dark by ear. Hub taps: one knock per well, deepening with
  count. The slide: latch strike-gap-seat (the two-stage voice),
  then the seal — the shared buffer, unaltered — then silence.
  The prime's silence before the seal is kept: a drawing holds
  its breath while it is checked.
- **Holding.** Near-silence by design. The glance has no sound;
  the running Deck makes none. Break start/end and peek end are
  the only mid-session voices: one firm click each — an event,
  figured on the Pill (§4.6), never a chime (Noir's rejected
  chime stays rejected here too; a measurement does not jingle).
- **The finish.** The tear pulse (240 Hz, the counterfoil's
  voice) at the tear; the completion pulse's three softening
  ticks as the slip settles. Then nothing: a filed drawing is
  quiet.
- **The Held Door.** One annotation-red fact deserves one sound:
  a single seat knock at the stamp's landing — the draughtsman
  setting his pencil down. No alarm, no buzz phrase. On motors
  without amplitude control the timing survives (Haut §0) and
  the meaning with it, because every Blueprint shape is
  timing-led.
- **Motion.** Near-linear easings (Curve A, the closest lawful
  resident), durations from the small end of the vocabulary
  {110–280 ms}; plate turns 180 ms lateral; the editor sheet
  rises 280 ms and seats without overshoot. **Named, not hidden
  (Haut's charge, answered before it lands): the wheel
  integrator's two overshoots (ζ=0.38) survive in my world as
  the one un-schematic motion** — shared machinery, LAWFUL as
  such, priced here in words so no critic has to find it. A
  drafted circle that overshoots twice before seating is the
  hand's physics, not the drawing's; the drawing tolerates it
  the way a sheet tolerates the hand that turns it.
- **Atmosphere ("smell", restated in lawful channels — Haut §0):**
  a drafting room. Colour temperature: one cold ink, day and
  night the same hue family. Sound brightness: mid-high
  fundamentals, chord ratio 1.0 — dry. Motion viscosity: low.
  Event density: sparse. Precision is this world's perfume, and
  it is specified, not vapour.
