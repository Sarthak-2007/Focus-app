## OBSIDIAN — volcanic black glass, one ember, bone ink

**The world in one line:** a forge at night — light is scarce,
therefore light is information.

### O-1. BANKED LIGHT — the room can read the phone
While a session is sealed, the glass deepens one full step: hero
card, wheel and chrome fall toward true black, and only the
remaining-time numeral keeps full bone brightness. At the finish
the light comes back up with the receipt.
- *Why this world only:* every other world spends light as
  decoration or identity. Only a one-light world can make
  *darkness itself* the signal. His phone sits across the room
  during meditation (his stated fact): a black slab with one dim
  numeral = armed; a lit screen = not. The state becomes legible
  at four metres, in the dark, without touching anything.
- *Machinery cost:* small. A sealed-state dimming step on the
  existing surfaces + the remaining-time element exempted. No new
  data — the sealed state and remaining time are the Now Frame's
  own outputs. AOD desk clock inherits the same law for free.

### O-2. THE EMBER № — the receipt cools, the number stays hot
Obsidian already wants the receipt as a bone print-slip on black
glass. Push it one verb further: the slip is *issued hot*. It
arrives with a hairline ember edge; over the sled's powered
return home the edge cools to bone — the print visibly sets. One
thing never cools: the receipt № stays ember, permanently. In
the Drawer, months later, an archive of cold bone slips carries
one hot line each — the number — findable at a glance in the
dark.
- *Why this world only:* the slip he loved is paper in every
  other world. Only in a forge world is a record *struck* and
  *cooled*; only a world with exactly one ember can afford to
  leave it burning in exactly one place per record, forever.
- *Machinery cost:* small. One animated border token across the
  existing detention/return timing (≤1.8s + 650ms — the cooling
  rides time the machine already spends), plus one static ember
  token on the № in ReceiptSheet and Drawer receipt rows.

### O-3. ASH, NOT ESPRESSO — day changes temperature, not depth
Obsidian's filed risk is the day hero's warm top stop ("espresso
glass"), and its own fix is a deepening. Deepening is the wrong
axis: a darker brown is still brown, and a hard critic will still
call day Deck a dark-beige sibling of the corpse. Change the
*temperature*: day Obsidian is the volcano in daylight — cold
ash and black rock. The day glass gradient runs neutral-cold
(ash grey falling to black), zero warm stops anywhere in the day
scheme; warmth exists in this app only where fire actually is —
the ember, and nothing else.
- *Why this world only:* it is the world's own geology. Volcanoes
  are warm at the vent and cold everywhere else; the current day
  scheme has the whole mountain warm, which is exactly what makes
  it read as a beverage.
- *Machinery cost:* token pass (the top stop and its neighbours),
  then the re-render Obsidian already owes. Small.

**The moment Obsidian owns: 03:30, the arm.** A dark room, a man
half-awake, a screen that asks nothing of his eyes and spends one
ember to say *shut*. Dawn is pre-dawn at that hour and Noir is
shouting; Obsidian is the only face native to the room he
actually arms in — and it owns the other end of the night too,
when the SLEEP routine banks the fire at 20:00.

## DAWN — first light through frosted glass, plum ink, one rose

**The world in one line:** a window at 5 AM — the only world
whose material is time of day itself.

### D-1. THE SKY KEEPS HIS HOURS — keyed to his routines, not the weather
Dawn's own unassigned offer was a sky keyed to his timeline. I am
pushing it from study to spine — with the turn the designer did
not state: key the sky to his ROUTINE WINDOWS, not to the clock.
The app already holds FASTED 05:00–08:00, DAY 09:00–19:30, SLEEP
20:00–03:30. Dawn's phase changes when his blocks change: pre-dawn
violet for the 03:30 arm, first light as FASTED arms, full morning
across the DAY blocks, an ember dusk at 20:00 *because SLEEP arms*
— even in June, when the real sky outside is still bright. The
app's sky follows his day, not the season's.
- *Why this world only:* a hundred apps tint by clock time. Only
  this app knows his blocks — and only a sky world can turn a
  routine table into weather. It also answers the standing charge
  against Dawn (too gentle at 3:30): at 3:30 this world is NOT
  dawn. It is the held breath before it — near-black violet glass.
  The brightness is a promise the fasted block keeps.
- *Machinery cost:* medium-small. A phase→palette keyframe map
  (4–5 phases) driven by routine-window state the repository
  already publishes; interpolation between phases. No new data,
  no sensors, no location.

### D-2. DEFROST — the dial clears as the session elapses
The hero dial is a frosted-glass instrument. Let the frost be
honest: at the seal the dial is at full frost; as the session
elapses the frost clears in a widening field from the dial's
centre; at the finish the glass is clear. Frost remaining = time
remaining, readable peripherally, without reading a single digit.
- *Why this world only:* frost is Dawn's material verb — morning
  windows clear. Obsidian's glass is smoked (it darkens, it never
  clears); Noir's glass is wet (water runs, frost never forms).
  The metaphor also runs the right way for a study block: the
  work is what clears the window.
- *Machinery cost:* medium. A radial alpha/clear mask over the
  dial plate keyed to the Now Frame's verified elapsed fraction
  (already computed, already published). Custom draw, no new
  state. Needs one contrast guard: numerals stay plum-on-pale at
  every frost level (the audit Dawn already offered to file).

### D-3. KILL THE PANEL — the designer's own second commission, taken
Dawn's letter names it plainly: the bright hero is still a big
pale panel, and dissolving the dial into light "is a drawing
change, not a token change." Take it. Bright Deck loses the hero
card entirely: the sky runs uninterrupted behind the type; the
numerals sit in plum over a soft local brightening of the sky (a
glow in the gradient, not a surface); the dial is ink, hairlines
and a frosted halo under its rim only. The panel is the one thing
in Dawn that rhymes with the dead porcelain — cards are the
corpse's grammar. The sky never rhymes with anything.
- *Why this world only:* every other direction's hero IS a
  surface (glass slab, poster plate, black card). Only Dawn has
  a ground strong enough to carry type with no card at all —
  because its ground is a picture of the sky.
- *Machinery cost:* medium. Bright-Deck layout/draw change
  (hero container removed, glow added to DawnGlass brush, type
  contrast re-audited over the gradient's lightest band).

**The moment Dawn owns: 05:00, the fasted block.** He starts
studying in darkness after meditation, and this is the only
world that lightens while he works — the app and the morning
arrive together. Its mirror at 20:00 (dusk for SLEEP) is the
same ownership at the day's other hinge.

## NOIR — wet black, neon signage, one amber lamp

**The world in one line:** a city that never lets you in — the
blocker as a street of lit doors, all of them shut.

### N-1. THE INTERCEPT IS A LIT SIGN — the knock gets a door
When a shut app is opened mid-session, the surface that refuses
him should be this world's masterpiece, not a grey wall: wet
black, the app's name as a neon-gas sign, and beneath it one
line — BACK AT 15:19 — the session's real finish time, which the
Now Frame already derives. The sign strikes once when it appears
(a single flicker, then a steady burn) and then just glows at
him while he decides to put the phone down.
- *Why this world only:* refusal is this app's core act, and
  only a signage world can make refusal *attractive* — a club
  door with a lit sign is more binding than a locked gate,
  because leaving feels like walking away from the one lit place
  on the street. Obsidian would answer with darkness, Dawn with
  light; only Noir has signs.
- *Machinery cost:* medium. The intercept surface the shield
  service already raises gets the world's treatment (black
  ground, gas type, one flicker animation) + the finishes-at
  line from the Now Frame. It sits outside the Deck/Shield
  reskin scope, so it prices as an addition, not a token pass.

### N-2. BROKEN SIGNS FOR BROKEN FINISHES — flicker is reserved grammar
Noir's own want: the magenta bloom flares once at the seal. Give
it its sound twin and its law. The seal in Noir is a sign strike
— bloom flare + the short fluorescent strike in the sound engine,
once, then steady burn. And the flicker is then RESERVED: it
appears nowhere in the app except one place — a shield-gap
finish, which already books BROKEN by his law. That session's
receipt prints on black stock with one glyph of its headline
unlit — a dead letter in the sign. Healthy seals never flicker;
broken ones wear the damage in the world's own grammar.
- *Why this world only:* only a neon world can break a letter.
  A scorch in Obsidian, a crack in Temple — those are damage to
  a surface. A dead tube in a sign is damage to the *message*,
  which is what a shield-gap finish is: the session's story with
  one letter out.
- *Machinery cost:* small. One strike variant in the sound
  engine (assets exist; the engine already voices the seal), one
  unlit-glyph text style on the receipt keyed to the outcome
  stamp that already exists. The reservation law costs a ruling,
  not code.

### N-3. AMBER IS LAW — one warm light, one meaning
#FFB300 already sits in Noir's palette as "one amber warning
lamp." Fence it absolutely: amber appears in exactly one flow in
the entire app — the emergency valve (the bar, the banks as they
fill, the SPENT plate when the token is gone). Not on the Deck,
not on Shield, never for "BLOCKING IS OFF" (that warning stays
violet/magenta — the city's own colours). One exposure teaches
his eye forever: amber = the one door out of a sealed week.
- *Why this world only:* cities run on signal scarcity — a
  traffic light means something because the street isn't lit
  amber. Other worlds have accent economies; only the city has
  *signals*, and a signal everyone ignores is a decoration.
- *Machinery cost:* small. Token discipline on the valve path
  (which already has its own views) + a census line so the fence
  is checkable, same as the zero-green census.

**The moment Noir owns: the knock.** Mid-session, mid-craving,
a shut app opened — the one moment the whole app exists for.
Every other world treats the intercept as plumbing. Noir makes
it the main event: the city, lit and closed, saying no like it
means it.

*One sharpening note, filed in my letter to Noir: his day-state
ghosts are described as misregistration — plates are SIGNAL's
craft (see S-1). Noir's ghosts should read as bloom: light
spreading through wet air, soft-edged. Same offset, different
physics; the two worlds must not share a fingerprint.*

## SIGNAL — flat poster print, giant type, red/black/off-white/deep blue

**The world in one line:** the app as a printed poster — ink,
paper, and the loudest possible clarity.

*Grounding note: Signal's renders are complete on disk (day +
night, every screen) and are what this sheet reads. Its case
letter had not reached mail/ren at my seating, so nothing here
attributes intent the designer hasn't stated; where a render
might be showing trunk's defect instead of a choice, I say so.*

### S-1. REGISTRATION IS THE SEAL — the plates align as the proof lands
Signal's numerals already print with red/blue plate fringes —
the poster's charming sloppiness. Spend it: the fringe offset is
the world's proof meter. At rest, unproven, the type sits out of
register. The proving cascade confirms its three witnessed links
one by one (machinery that already exists); each confirmation
steps the plates closer. At the latch — with the 14ms knock —
the type snaps into perfect register and stays there for the
session's life. A sealed app is a poster in register; an unsealed
one is visibly misprinted. He can see proof from across the room
as *sharpness itself*.
- *Why this world only:* registration is offset printing's own
  word for truth — plates either align or the print is wrong.
  Noir's ghosts are light (bloom); Signal's are ink plates, hard-
  edged and mechanical. No other world has plates to align.
- *Machinery cost:* small-medium. A type-offset parameter driven
  by the cascade's existing link states, animated at the latch.
  No new data — PROVING's links are already witnessed events.

### S-2. THE WARNING TAKES THE WALL — type size is the urgency scale
A poster's grammar is hierarchy, and posters shout what's wrong.
Today "BLOCKING IS OFF — TAP TO FIX" prints as one headline
among several. In Signal, guard state re-ranks the poster: when
anything is unbound, the warning becomes the LARGEST type on the
Deck — bigger than the time, which demotes to second line. When
everything is bound and proof is positive, the warning shrinks
to a footnote rule at the poster's foot and the time takes the
whole wall. The poster is reprinted by its own state, every time
the state changes.
- *Why this world only:* other worlds signal state with colour
  or light, which tired eyes can miss. Signal's material is
  *size* — the one channel a poster has that a dashboard doesn't.
  A 3:30 AM brain reads the biggest word first; Signal makes the
  biggest word the one that matters.
- *Machinery cost:* small-medium. Hero type-scale conditional on
  the Watch Store's existing guard verdict (the same facts the
  proof strip prints) + reflow of the hero plate. No new state.

### S-3. NIGHT IS THE SAME POSTER WITH THE POWER CUT — one black field
The dark Deck render carries a flat charcoal hero plate floating
on a black ground — two greys dividing the poster's authority,
and (flagged, not charged) possibly trunk Theme.kt's unmapped
container family leaking stock grey, the defect Noir caught in
its own first render. Either way the push is the same: a poster
has no dark mode. Night Signal is the same poster in an unlit
street — plate and ground merge into ONE black field, type in
off-white with its fringes, the red cap the only lit ink. Chosen
black, never leaked grey: the charter's "everything intentional"
made visible in a single token decision.
- *Why this world only:* glass worlds legitimately change
  material at night (smoke deepens, sky darkens). Print doesn't
  change — only the light on it does. Signal is never more itself
  than when it refuses to become a different poster after dark.
- *Machinery cost:* small. Dark-scheme token pass (hero plate →
  ground black), then one re-render to prove the field is one.

**The moment Signal owns: the daylight glance.** Between blocks,
in harsh sun, phone at arm's length — every rival is a night
world or a glass world that washes out; Signal is paper and ink
at maximum contrast, the only face that gets *better* in full
light. It also owns the fix-it instant (S-2): no world tells him
something's wrong faster.

- **No new data.** Nothing here prints a fact the app doesn't
  already hold. Edition numbers, streak counts, decorative
  statistics — refused on the truth law, however poster-like.
- **No second ember, no second rose, no second amber.** Each
  world's scarcity is its meaning; two of my inventions (O-2,
  N-3) exist only to fence a scarcity the designer had already
  built but not yet defended.
- **Temple and Blueprint are untouched here** — not from lack of
  appetite. They have no renders; inventing for a world I cannot

## TEMPLE — carved stone & brass, gold spent on commits only

**The world in one line:** a building that is also a clock —
stone laid by day, a lamp tended by night.

### T-1. COURSES OF THE DAY — the day is built, course by course
Beneath the hero slab, a plinth of laid courses grows through
the day. Each finished block today lays one course: a stone
band across the slab's width, its height figured from the
block's minutes (scale ~0.18dp per minute, clamped 6–14dp),
separated from the course below by a 1dp mortar hairline in the
recess tone. Courses not yet laid — up to the count set on the
hub — draw as empty beds: brass outline only, waiting above
the last laid course. The current slab is the course being
laid; at the finish it settles onto the stack (the existing
520ms Curve C, no new motion). No number prints. No total. The
wall is the record, read as height.
- *Why this world only:* masonry is the only material in the
  wave that accumulates as structure. Obsidian's archive is
  struck slips; Signal's is filed posters; a temple's day is
  literally built, and a finished block that changes nothing
  visible is a course laid in the dark. The no-score law
  survives untouched: courses are architecture, drawn from
  facts (today's finished sessions), never graded — a short
  course is a short stone, not a failed one.
- *Machinery cost:* small-medium. One Canvas stack (~50 lines)
  under the hero, reading today's completed session rows (the
  Drawer's own day derivation) and the hub count. No new state,
  no new data. If the hub count is re-set mid-day the beds
  redraw — a builder re-measuring his wall; honest by
  construction.

### T-2. THE LAMP BURNS DOWN — night remaining, read as oil
Temple's night hero already carries a radial lamplight glow.
Give the lamp a fuel: the glow is keyed to the sealed
session's remaining fraction (the Now Frame already derives
it). At the seal the lamp is full — radius 1.0 of the hero's
diagonal, centre high on the slab. As the session elapses the
centre sinks (0 → 18dp) and the radius contracts to 0.55, the
alpha settling 1.0 → 0.72: the light lowers as the oil goes.
It steps on the app's existing minute cadence — no continuous
animation; the stillness law is kept, the lamp moves the way
lamps appear to move, between glances. At the finish the glow
settles to a low ember ring behind the receipt's parchment,
then the desk clock's true black takes the room.
- *Why this world only:* the lamp is this world's timepiece,
  and burning down is what a tended flame does. Dawn's light
  only ever grows — the verbs are opposites and must never be
  confused; Obsidian spends light as one numeral's scarcity,
  not as a room's weather. Only a world with a lamp can run
  out of it, beautifully, on schedule.
- *Machinery cost:* small. Gradient parameters (radius,
  centre offset, alpha) on the existing night hero draw,
  driven by the existing remaining fraction. Zero new state.

### T-3. THE KEYSTONE — the last block carries the arch
When exactly one block remains today (count set on the hub
minus courses laid, per T-1's facts), the hero slab's cap
course draws a keystone: a trapezoid seated centre in the
slab's top rule line — 64dp at the top edge, 44dp at the
base, 26dp tall — bronze outline while it waits. Sealing
that final block, at t=0, the keystone fills with gold leaf
— lawful, because gold is this world's commit metal and this
is the day's last commit — and the slab's inner double rule
completes its run across the keystone's base: the arch
closes. If the final finish books BROKEN, the keystone stays
outline and takes the §8 struck-through treatment, the same
grammar the receipt already speaks.
- *Why this world only:* a keystone is not a trophy and not a
  confetti moment; it is the one stone a building cannot be
  finished without, shaped differently from every other stone
  because the arch's forces meet in it. Signal can snap into
  register and Noir can strike a sign; only Temple has an arch
  for the day's last act to complete.
- *Machinery cost:* small. One shape path + a fill
  conditional on the existing seal hook and T-1's count
  derivation. No new state. If T-1 is refused at judging, this
  prices alone at small-medium (the count derivation is then
  its own).

**The moment Temple owns: 19:30, the last finish.** Courses
below, lamp low, one stone left in the wall — and the day
does not end with a summary. It ends with a building standing
complete, in lamplight, that was not standing at 03:30.

*Refused for Temple: a sundial or moving sun-patch on the day
slab. The sky is Dawn's verb; Temple's light is a lamp —
tended, carried, finite. A temple that borrowed the sky would
fail purity on its own altar.*

## BLUEPRINT — the technical drawing of a day that will be built

**The world in one line:** a drawing office — the day is
issued in the morning, built by evening, and filed as-built.

### B-1. THE AS-BUILT REGISTER — the Drawer keeps what got built
A real drawing office files two drawings: the one issued and
the as-built. Blueprint's Drawer (§4.2's register) gains the
second: each filing day's row carries an as-built strip, the
day-scale drawn twice at hairline height (~14dp) — the planned
segments (routine windows, as issued) in ink outline; the
as-built segments (the session rows' true start/end) laid
over them, solid where they coincide with the plan, and in
annotation red where they depart: a line that stops short with
an end tick for an early finish, a hatched extension for an
overrun. No deviation figure prints; the lines are the fact,
figured by their lengths and nothing else.
- *Why this world only:* as-built revision is a drawing
  office's legal institution — the record of what was
  actually constructed against what was drawn. Every other
  world's Drawer files artefacts or entries; only a world
  that issues a drawing in the morning can file its truth in
  the evening. It also sharpens the Drawer against the
  no-tracker law: an as-built grades nothing — a departed
  line is a surveyed fact, in the draughtsman's red, not a
  mark against the man.
- *Machinery cost:* medium. One composable (~90 lines) in the
  Drawer day row, reading stored session rows and routine
  windows — both exist. No new state, no new facts.

### B-2. THE REVISION CLOUD — an edit shows on the drawing until re-issue
On a real sheet, a changed detail is ringed with a revision
cloud — the scalloped chain of arcs every draughtsman reads
as "this moved" — with a revision triangle in the margin.
Blueprint's day-scale (§2.4) takes the convention whole: when
a routine is edited in the cured editor, its segment wears
the cloud (arc chain, ~6dp arcs, ink weight) and the title
block's revision cell takes the triangle with the drawing's
revision number. The cloud clears at the next issue — the
03:30 day boundary — and the revision number stays in the
title block all day: REV n is the sheet's memory of having
been corrected. A man who moved his SLEEP window at midnight
sees the cloud on it at 05:00 and knows the drawing he is
reading is the drawing he changed.
- *Why this world only:* revision clouds belong to issued
  documents. Obsidian has no documents, Temple's register is
  carved (a carving is not revised; it is re-cut, and this
  world's editor is not a chisel), THE MACHINE will have
  settings, not revisions. Only the drawing office confesses
  its edits on the face of the work.
- *Machinery cost:* small-medium, priced in state, not hidden:
  the cloud path is ~30 lines of Canvas on the existing scale;
  the revision number needs one stored integer (drawing
  revision counter, incremented on editor save) and the
  per-routine last-edited day — one small store, named here so
  judging prices it with eyes open. (Routines already stamp
  their changes for the derive; if an existing updated-field
  covers the day stamp, the cost falls to the integer alone.)

### B-3. THE BROKEN DIMENSION — a break, drawn the way drawings break a line
A draughtsman who cannot fit a length breaks the dimension
line with the conventional freehand break and figures the
omitted part apart. Blueprint's hero dimension line (§1.3 /
§1.6) takes the convention for the Safe Break: while a break
runs, the line breaks at its centre with the zigzag break
symbol; the elapsed chain stands left of the break, the
remaining chain right of it, and the break's own remaining
minutes are figured beneath — in brackets, this world's mark
for a span held off the main line (the same honesty grammar
as the receipt's bracketed reconstructed figures, §4.3: the
bracket says "not measured here," in both seats). At the
break's end the line redraws whole, unbroken, no trace. The
desk clock still refuses the break, per Nacht's law — the
break is figured on the sheet, never on the night plate.
- *Why this world only:* the break symbol is a convention of
  dimensioned drawings, legible to anyone who has read a
  section. Dawn would soften the line, Noir would dim a sign;
  only a drawing can interrupt its own measurement with the
  mark that means "length omitted here, on purpose."
- *Machinery cost:* small. One draw branch (~25 lines) in the
  existing dimension-line composable, keyed to the Safe Break
  state and its remaining span — both already derived
  (WallAccrual). Zero new state.

**The moment Blueprint owns: the morning after a changed
plan.** He opens the Shield and yesterday's midnight edit is
sitting on the scale inside its revision cloud — the drawing
admits it was touched. And on a quiet evening, the Drawer's
as-built week: what was drawn against what got built, in
lines, with nothing totalled and nothing forgiven — the
record a man can actually use.

*Refused for Blueprint: an isometric or rendered plate view.
The instant the sheet pretends to be the building, it stops
being a drawing and becomes a render — flatness is this
world's honesty, the same law that refuses it a seal sound of
its own. Also refused: a scale bar for time on every plate —
a drawing states its scale once, in the title block, or it is
decorating.*
