# LIV — Enthusiast seat, Designers cohort (coordinator: Sana)
## PLAN WAVE — The Beauty Audit + The One-Moment Brief. No code.

Sarthak's grading axis, in his words: *"I prefer prettiness."* He abandoned the
functional blocker and kept the beautiful one. So this file grades one thing
only: **does it look beautiful** — judged in the language of light, weight,
rhythm, and air. Feature talk is someone else's seat.

Method: I read every render in `shots/` and `shots/night/` — all five day
mains, all five night mains, the compact Deck variants (deck2–8, day + night),
Shield variants including the knob crop and the bag, Tasks-now, Guard, and the
dial crop. One absence, stated up front because it *is* an audit finding:
**there is no render of the running state.** The most-seen surface in the app's
life — the hours-long sealed session — has never been photographed. What I say
about Running below is judged from the compact Deck variants that borrow its
clothes, and from that absence itself.

Verdicts use three words, strictly:
- **BEAUTIFUL** — he would keep the app for this alone.
- **TIDY** — correct, calm, forgettable. Tidiness is the enemy wearing a uniform.
- **UGLY** — actively cheapens the object; he feels it even if he can't name it.

## PART 1 — THE BEAUTY AUDIT

### 1.0 The whole object, before the screens

**What is genuinely beautiful today, app-wide:** the *palette's restraint*.
Warm bone ground, ink, one hot orange held back like a struck match. In day
renders the app reads as paper and iron — a printed instrument, not software.
The slashed zero in Sora at hero size is a real glyph with a real posture; it
gives every numeral the stance of a departure board. The hairline discipline
(no shadows anywhere; depth only from tone steps and 1dp lines) is the single
most expensive-looking decision in the app, and it is already locked law.
Good. It deserves to be.

**What is merely tidy, app-wide:** the *card reflex*. Almost every fact lives
inside the same rounded, bordered rectangle at the same radius, so unrelated
things — a telemetry tile, a tower, a task — all wear the same coat. Rhythm
without hierarchy is just wallpaper with good manners. The screens are
well-spaced the way a spreadsheet is well-spaced: evenly, and therefore
without music.

**What is ugly, app-wide:** **orange used as ink.** In the older variants and
still in the Ledger, orange leaks out of its one COMMIT fill and becomes
*text* — orange numerals, orange counts, orange `25m`, orange `02` in chrome.
A fill colour asked to be a type colour turns shrill: it vibrates against the
bone ground, it shouts at caption size, and it spends the one hot note so
often that the actual commit — the slide — stops sounding. Hot colour is a
percussion instrument. Played on every beat, it's noise.

### 1.1 DECK — day

- **The wheel: BEAUTIFUL, and it is the app.** Concentric tick rings, the
  grey arc travelling like a tide mark, the black thumb sitting low-left with
  its short black tail — this is the only element in the app with *weight
  distributed like a physical thing*. The eye orbits it. The cream hub disc
  floats one tone above the ring field, and that single step of light is
  exactly enough depth. In the dial crop, the tick rhythm — fine ticks,
  longer majors — reads as engraving. This is the object he photographs.
- **The hero numeral `25:00`: BEAUTIFUL in posture, TIDY in placement.** The
  glyphs themselves are magnificent — Sora's mass, the slashed zeros standing
  like columns. But it sits in a flat card field with the caption
  `50 MIN TOTAL` stranded beneath it in tracked mono, small and apologetic.
  The biggest voice in the app is followed by a whisper that repeats it
  quieter. Light-wise: one blaze, then a smudge.
- **The void below NOW: UGLY — not because it's empty, because it's
  *undecided* empty.** Between the task line and the slide there is a third of
  the screen of bare ground with no horizon, no registration mark, no tension.
  Air is beautiful when it is *shaped* — a held breath. This is air that was
  left over. The slide at the bottom feels exiled from the panel above it;
  two objects on one screen, not one composition.
- **The slide: BEAUTIFUL as an object.** Black slot, saturated orange cap,
  one white grip line. It is the heaviest, hottest, lowest thing on the
  screen — gravity is correct. It looks like it would make a sound.
- **FLOW key, bottom-left of the panel: TIDY to the point of sadness.** A
  small bordered lozenge in the panel's weakest corner, set in the same voice
  as the captions around it. It reads as a label that wandered in, not a key
  you press.

**Night Deck:** the best night render in the set. The ground goes near-black
and the wheel survives translation — tick rings still legible, the thumb now
*cream*, a pale moon of a knob against the dark ring. That inversion is
genuinely lovely: the handhold becomes the light source. But the hub disc at
night is a flat brown-grey coin, noticeably deader than its day self — the
one element that loses its light when the lights go out. And the orange cap
brightens toward coral; acceptable as a lamp, but it is the loudest object in
a dark room by a wide margin, louder at 3:30 AM than the design intends.

### 1.2 SHIELD (System) — day

- **The loadout band: TIDY, with one beautiful idea buried in it.** A hairline
  rail, three printed stops, a black cap seated at STUDY. The *idea* — position
  as state, read at a glance like a tuner — is beautiful. The execution is
  wan: the rail is too thin to feel mechanical, the cap too small to feel
  seated, and the whole band sits inside yet another bordered card that
  flattens it into a diagram of a switch rather than a switch.
- **The knob/bag crops expose the truth: the bag is BEAUTIFUL.** Twelve
  chunky tiles — black, grey, porcelain — with real app glyphs, tiny orange
  corner dots, `2m` marks. It has the rhythm of a typeset tray, mass and
  repetition with variation, the closest thing in the app to Blockit's tactile
  density. It is also, perversely, *not on the Shield main render at all* —
  the screen's most beautiful furniture is kept in a drawer the hero shot
  never opens.
- **Routine rows: TIDY.** Strong Sora names, quiet mono meta, black ON boxes.
  Clean, well-leaded, and utterly without a moment — two identical stanzas.
  The ON boxes are heavy black rectangles whose weight equals the slide cap's;
  black is spent too casually here, so nothing on the screen is *the* heavy
  thing.
- **App sleds: TIDY verging on handsome.** The SHUT/2m/OPEN track with its
  black cap has real mechanical rhythm down the list — repetition is this
  screen's music, and the sleds carry it. But every row identical in weight
  means the list has no phrase structure; it's a metronome, not a melody.
- **The filter field + category row: UGLY by air theft.** A fat empty input
  and a scrolling strip of category words consume the screen's middle breath
  to serve twelve apps. Dead air in a form field is the cheapest air there is.

**Night Shield:** the sled caps go taupe and *gain* beauty — softer, more
material, less shouty than day's black. The loadout cap, however, nearly
vanishes into its card; at night the band's one moving part is its dimmest
element. Light hierarchy inverted exactly where the hand needs it.

### 1.3 TASKS (Horizon) — day

This is the daily screen and, today, the least beautiful. That is the audit's
central fact.

- **The Horizon strip: TIDY as graphics, UGLY as proportion.** Three rounded
  blocks — black, grey, porcelain — proportional to duration. As pure tone
  rhythm it's pleasant. But it is a *picture of the list directly beneath it*,
  drawn at postcard size, and its rounded blocks make the queue's time feel
  like candy. Time in this app should feel like iron everywhere, including
  here.
- **The composer: UGLY.** An empty rounded field the size of a billboard,
  then a row of five pill keys, one black, one (in Tasks-now) *orange ADD* —
  a second hot object competing with START below. The composer occupies the
  screen's prime light — the top — and gives back emptiness. First fixation
  lands on a void asking for typing. No beautiful object opens with homework.
- **The queue itself: BEAUTIFUL bones, TIDY skin.** Atkinson task titles have
  genuine warmth — humanist letterforms doing honest work — and the numbered
  sub-steps in mono at the right margin create a quiet right-hand rhythm, a
  ruled ledger edge. In the flat main render, though, tasks are separated by
  hairlines alone, all at one weight: the ACTIVE task does not *stand in
  light*, it merely stands first. In Tasks-now, where ACTIVE gets a white
  card and filled black step-pills, the hierarchy finally breathes — proof
  the bones were always good.
- **The strikethrough sub-step: BEAUTIFUL, small and real.** A drawn line
  through finished work, in ink. The one gesture of *evidence* on the screen.
  It should be the screen's signature and today it's a footnote.
- **`START 25m` in orange type (flat render): UGLY.** See §1.0 — the commit
  verb shouted in hot text while wearing no fill. In Tasks-now it correctly
  becomes an orange *fill* and instantly looks expensive.

**Night Tasks:** the Horizon strip's first block goes full cream — a white
lozenge blazing at the top of a dark screen, the single harshest light event
in any night render. The queue text tiers collapse toward one murk; sub-step
numbers and QUEUE labels sink to a grey that reads as fatigue, not quiet.

### 1.4 LEDGER (Vault) — day + night

Judged on the reopened question's own axis: *is it beautiful enough to visit?*
Today: **no. This is the ugliest screen in the app, and it isn't close.**

- **Dot-matrix digits: UGLY, the cheapest ugliness in Monolith.** Beside every
  honest Sora numeral sits a dot-grid ghost of the same number — a number
  rendered twice, once as type and once as a toy. It reads as a calculator
  souvenir glued to an instrument. At night it's worse: the dot ghosts go
  cream and *out-glow* the real numerals beside them.
- **Orange saturation: UGLY.** Orange digits, an orange brick, an orange
  fader — three hot events on one screen. The ration is not bent here, it's
  abandoned. The screen shouts in the app's one shout-voice, continuously,
  about the past.
- **The Streak Tower: TIDY geometry, dead light.** Rows of black bricks are
  honest mass — F01/F02 full black has real gravity. But the empty F04 row
  renders as seven grey *blanks that look like unloaded content*, and the
  whole tower sits in a white card that makes it feel like a widget pasted
  from another app. A tower should rise from the ground tone, not sit on a
  tray.
- **The faders: UGLY at zero.** Four days at `0m` render as four tall empty
  tubes with a black slug at the bottom — absence drawn as *broken
  equipment*. A zero day is a fact; these tubes make it look like a rendering
  error. WED's full black column, by contrast, is genuinely handsome — proof
  the form works at exactly one value and fails at the most common one.
- **Receipts: TIDY.** One hairline-ruled row with a human title is fine; the
  `HMAC•` tag is jewellery for engineers, a tiny ugliness of self-regard.

**Night Ledger is the worst render in the entire set.** The tower bricks go
full cream — fifteen pale slabs blazing on a brown card, a radiator of light
at midnight. Whatever the Ledger becomes, its night face today is an argument
for the kill all by itself.

### 1.5 GUARD — day + night

- **The ARM sleds: TIDY.** Same switch drawn three times; the cream cap at
  night becomes the brightest object on the screen, so a settings toggle
  outshines every fact around it. Position reads, but the cap has no travel
  *character* — a lozenge in a lozenge.
- **The blocked-sites list: one beautiful rhythm, one ugly repetition.**
  BEAUTIFUL: the Anonymous Pro site names down the left margin — mono,
  even, unhurried, like a printed index. It is the calmest column in the app.
  UGLY: eight identical REMOVE lozenges down the right, each one a bordered
  pill shouting the same verb at the same weight. Eight equal shouts = a
  chorus of clerks. A word at text weight, or a single repeated hairline
  gesture, would cost nothing and buy back the column's calm.
- **Overall:** Guard is a *form*, and forms are where beauty goes to be
  processed. Nothing here is loved; everything is filed. If any screen can
  afford to be a drawer rather than a destination, the audit's eyes say it's
  this one — its one beautiful thing (the mono index) survives transplantation
  intact.

### 1.6 CHROME (nameplate + bottom nav) — day + night

- **The nameplate: TIDY with an ugly fringe.** `MONOLITH` in Sora with its
  dot is handsome — a maker's mark. But it shares its line with three pills
  (HELD 02 · GUARD 1/4 · DAY/NIGHT), so the mark reads as one logo among
  four badges. Chrome that repeats *data* on every screen teaches the eye to
  skip the entire top line — including the name.
- **The bottom nav: BEAUTIFUL proportion, TIDY execution.** A floating
  porcelain bar with one black selected cell — the weight is right, the
  floating is right, the radius rhyme with the slide slot above it is right.
  But the unselected stops are pale ghosts — icons and words at a weight that
  reads as *disabled* rather than *unselected* — and the selected black cell
  is so dominant that the bar reads as one button with three footnotes.
- **Night chrome:** the nav bar's brown body is the warmest large surface in
  night mode; it glows like furniture in a lit room while the screens above
  it try to be dark. Chrome should be the *last* thing to hold light at
  night, and today it holds the most.

### 1.7 RUNNING — the missing render

No render exists, so I audit the stand-ins: the compact Deck variants
(deck2 / night-deck), where a top panel carries `25:00`, a thin progress
track, and the active task. Findings: the thin progress track with its small
orange segment is **TIDY** — a line doing a number's job, without the number's
authority. The compact panel's tonal lift (white in day, plum-brown at night)
is the only place in the app where a surface *glows* rather than steps, and
at night that plum cast is **UGLY** — a bruise colour that belongs to no
other screen. The deeper finding stands: the state he will *live inside* —
sealed, hours at a time, AOD across a dark desk — has no composed face at
all in the evidence. Every other state was art-directed. This one was left
to assemble itself. That is the audit's loudest silence.

|---|---|---|
| Deck | **Beautiful core, ugly air** | The wheel + slide are keep-the-app objects; the void and the whisper-caption tax them. |
| Shield | **Tidy** | Competent rhythm, no light event; its best furniture (the bag) is hidden. |

## PART 2 — THE ONE MOMENT, PER SCREEN OF THE NEW PLAN

The law I held for every moment below: **ceremony only on state change;
daily beauty must live in the object's resting state.** A moment that needs
an animation to be beautiful will be dead by month two. A moment that is
beautiful *at rest*, every time he arrives, survives month six because there
is nothing to wear out — you cannot get tired of a well-made object's face
the way you get tired of its trick. Blockit's lesson, precisely: he didn't
keep the animation. He kept the *machine on his desk*.

Each moment names: what it looks like · when it happens · why it survives.

### DECK — *The Seated Wheel*

- **What:** The wheel at rest, already set to his session, with the total as
  the screen's only big number and the thumb resting at its detent like a
  needle parked on a gauge. Around the rim, at true scale, the day's routine
  windows sit as quiet ink arcs — the wheel doesn't just set a duration, it
  *shows the day wound around it*. The slide below is the one hot fill.
  Nothing moves. Nothing needs to.
- **When:** On arrival. Every open of the app, before any touch. The moment
  is not an event; it is the *greeting* — the instrument, wound and waiting,
  looking like an instrument.
- **Why it survives month six:** Because it is a *state*, not a show. A gauge
  at rest never becomes friction. On day 180 the pleasure is the same as day
  1 and costs the same zero seconds: recognition. *My machine is ready and it
  is beautiful.* That is exactly the feeling that kept Blockit armed daily —
  the ritual was in the object's presence, not in a performance. The seal
  thock on commit (state change, once) is the punctuation; the sentence is
  the resting wheel.

### SHIELD — *The Printed Day*

- **What:** At the head of Shield, the 24-hour Section strip: his day drawn
  as one horizontal elevation — routine blocks as solid ink masses, sleep as
  the long low block at the foot of the day, gaps labelled OPEN, and one ink
  now-line standing in the current hour like a bookmark spine. Below it, the
  routine rows register to the strip's blocks. The whole day, visible as a
  *skyline*, in ink, at rest.
- **When:** Whenever he opens Shield to tend anything — and in the half-second
  glance before he does. It is Shield's resting face, not a transition.
- **Why it survives month six:** A skyline doesn't novelty-decay; maps get
  *more* satisfying as they become familiar, because fluency is the pleasure.
  By month six he reads his whole day in one fixation the way you read a
  familiar street. And it is truth-shaped: when a routine dies or a gap
  opens, the skyline visibly changes — the beauty *is* the information,
  so it can never become decoration he learns to ignore.

### TASKS — *The Struck Line*

- **What:** The ACTIVE task standing in light: its title at title size on the
  one lifted surface of the screen, sub-steps as ruled lines beneath it, and
  each finished step *struck through in ink* — a real drawn line, slightly
  heavier than the text it crosses, so the card accumulates visible evidence
  as he works. Queued tasks below sit one full tone quieter, one-line,
  patient. The screen's resting state, mid-day, is a page with lines being
  crossed out on it.
- **When:** Continuously, at rest, all day — it *deepens* with use rather
  than firing at a moment. The only motion is at the state change itself:
  the strike draws once when a step completes, then is still forever.
- **Why it survives month six:** Because it is *evidence, not feedback*.
  Confetti says "well done" and is lying by week three; a struck line says
  "this happened" and is still true in month six. Crossed-out lists are one
  of the oldest satisfactions on paper — they predate apps by a century and
  have never worn out. His page at 21:00, dense with strikes, is a beautiful
  object *because he made it*, and self-made beauty does not decay.

### RUNNING / AOD — *The Dark Instrument*

- **What:** Sealed, the phone becomes a desk instrument: true-black ground,
  one huge remaining-time numeral in warm cream, the current task in one
  quiet line beneath it, and a hairline of elapsed light along the numeral's
  baseline — nothing else. No nav, no pills, no second clock. At desk
  distance it reads as a *clock that belongs to the room*, the way a good
  watch face belongs to a wrist. The patrol shift against burn-in is
  invisible craft; the face itself never performs.
- **When:** The entire session — the longest resting state in the app, hours
  a day. It is not a moment *in* the session; it is the session's face.
- **Why it survives month six:** Because it asks for nothing. Every ambient
  animation ever shipped becomes, by month six, either ignored or irritating;
  a still, dark, perfectly-proportioned face becomes *furniture he trusts*.
  This is the screen he will actually see most in his life with this app —
  glancing across a desk at 3:30 AM — and its beauty is the kind that
  compounds: the calmer it is, the more the room feels like a place where
  serious work happens. Beauty as atmosphere, not event.

### THE RECORD (Ledger, demoted — Receipt + RECORD at Shield's foot)

*My judgment on the reopened question, on my axis only:* a full Ledger tab
**cannot pass the beauty test as a destination** — a room you must travel to
must justify the travel with a face, and a record's face is, honestly, a
table. But the demoted forms can each carry one small true moment:

- **The Receipt — *The Single Line*.** At completion (state change — the one
  legal ceremony), a sheet whose entire content is one ruled line of fact in
  human words — `HELD 50 MIN · STUDY · 3 DEFLECTED` — set like the last line
  of a printed bill, with generous air above and below it and *nothing else
  on the sheet*. The beauty is the restraint: an app that could show him
  forty numbers shows him one sentence. It survives because it is over in
  one tap and never repeats its trick — it is spent only when something real
  just happened, so it never becomes the daily path.
- **RECORD — *The Quiet Foot*.** At Shield's foot, after the day's skyline
  and the apps, one ink hero digit — held sessions this week — with hairline
  rows beneath. Its moment is *tonal*: it is the quietest, darkest-stepped
  section in the app, a footnote that looks like the colophon of a printed
  book. He encounters it standing over the routines it judges, which is the
  right room. It survives month six precisely by never asking to be visited:
  beauty you *pass*, not beauty you must pilgrimage to. **If it cannot be
  made this quiet and this handsome, kill it** — an ugly record kept for
  completeness is exactly the functional-blocker failure in miniature.

### CHROME — *The Letterpress Line*

- **What:** The pills die. In their place, one letterpressed line under the
  maker's mark — war line in mono, counts as quiet ink, the DAY/NIGHT word
  gone (theme follows his routines) — and below, the band nav as a recessed
  rail with a travelling cap and *printed* stops, words engraved at plate
  size. Chrome's resting state becomes what a product's casing is: the name,
  stamped once, and a switch that looks stamped too.
- **When:** Everywhere, always — which is exactly why it must be *resting*
  beauty. Chrome is seen more than any screen and may perform least of all.
- **Why it survives month six:** Stamped things don't age the way glowing
  things do. A letterpress line has the same dignity on day 180 as day 1
  because it was never excited. And it returns the top line to him: the first
  fixation of every screen stops being a data dashboard and becomes a
  *signature* — the feeling of owning a made object rather than operating a
  service.

## PART 3 — CHEAP UGLINESS IN THE LOCKED FOUNDATIONS THEMSELVES

Settled decisions are not indicted. These are indictments of *execution
details inside* the locked frame — each one cheap (a token, a spacing, a
weight) and each one felt daily:

1. **The universal bordered card.** Radius 8/16 with a hairline on *every*
   container makes unrelated facts rhyme falsely. The fix inside the law:
   reserve the bordered panel for true instruments (the wheel's field);
   let lists and records live on bare ground separated by hairlines and air.
   Borders are punctuation; a page of nothing but boxes reads as a form.
2. **One margin (20dp) doing two jobs.** The same 20dp serves the outer frame
   *and* the inner panel inset, so panels nest at identical offsets and the
   eye can't tell frame from content. A single internal beat — panels inset
   by one extra 4dp step from the frame they sit in — would give the page a
   pulse for the price of a constant.
3. **The night staircase's middle step is too close to its ground.** Night
   surfaces hover half a tone above the ground — visible in renders as cards
   that are *felt* more than seen — while the Ledger's night card leaps a
   full brown storey. The staircase has a missing stair: one intermediate
   tone, used consistently, would stop night screens from alternating
   between murk and flare.
4. **Cream as a fill, anywhere, at night.** Locked law already says cream is
   a text tone; the renders still show it filling the nav's active cell, the
   Guard ARM caps, and the Ledger bricks. Every cream fill at night is a
   small lamp left on. The foundation's flaw is not the palette — it's that
   the palette's one rule has no enforcement at the token level: fills and
   text tones should be *different tokens*, not the same hex used politely.
5. **Tracked mono captions as the default "quiet" voice.** `50 MIN TOTAL`,
   `DOT GRID 4DP`, `RUNS ITSELF` — the app whispers in the same letterspaced
   mono for captions, slogans, and genuine telemetry alike, so nothing in
   that voice means anything. The plate style is beautiful *because* it is
   rare; at current density it's wallpaper. Ration it like the orange.
6. **No baseline relationship between the hero numeral and anything.** The
   big Sora time sits on its own optical island; captions and the wheel do
   not share its baseline or its cap line, so Deck's composition is three
   centred objects stacked, not one drawing. Centring everything is the
   cheapest composition there is — the foundations permit it by never
   naming a shared axis. Name one: the hero's baseline is the panel's axis;
   the wheel's centre answers to it.

## Closing note to the cohort

The app today is a beautiful *instrument photographed* — Deck proves the
hands can build beauty. The plan's work is not to add beauty; it is to
*move* it: out of the Ledger's toy shop, out of chrome's badge rack, out of
Tasks' empty form — and into the resting faces of the screens he lives in:
the seated wheel, the printed day, the struck line, the dark instrument.
If every screen in the new plan has exactly one moment that is beautiful
*at rest*, he will still be opening this app in month six — not for what it
does, but for what it looks like sitting there doing it. That was always
the retention mechanism. He told us so himself.

— Liv, Enthusiast, Designers cohort
