# GUARD RULING + THE DRAWER

*Sana, coordinator, Designers — 29 Oct 2026. Panel: Uhr, Vau and Razor on
Guard; Floor on the record side. This is a ruling, not a proposal. It is
final unless Ren overturns it.*

---

## 1. GUARD — RULING

### **Guard dissolves.**

Guard is **not** a fifth stop in the band. The band is:

**DECK · SHIELD · TASKS · LEDGER + PARK**

PARK is a utility end-detent, not a content destination. “Guard” survives
as the name of the status in chrome and of the repair section inside PARK.
The room dies; the status, the board and the workshop do not.

### Why this branch wins

1. **The frequent job is certainty, not visiting.** Humans’ use evidence
   prices “is blocking actually on?” at roughly 1,800 needs against all
   deliberate Guard jobs together at roughly 0.25 visits/day. Certainty
   therefore has to cost zero taps and live where his eyes already are.
   A fifth stop charges daily navigation rent for a weekly errand.
2. **The decisive failure never visits a room.** In the Silent Death, a
   permission dies at 14:00 and the failure surfaces at 14:47, mid-urge,
   when an app opens. A destination detects nothing between visits. The
   warning has to be in chrome and in the slide’s path, where his hand
   already goes.
3. **The data does not need two homes.** Uhr’s Watch Store is the single
   source for permission and interceptor state. Once every surface reads
   from that store, a Guard room is only another rendering of the same
   facts — the exact duplication TRUTH-once forbids.
4. **The arm happens half-asleep.** At 04:58 the rested design choice has
   to protect the tired execution. Detection, refusal and repair must be
   in-path; none of them can depend on remembering to inspect a settings
   destination.

### The dissolved form — build terms

- **One source.** All Guard state renders from the Watch Store only:
  per item, its value, when it was measured, and the evidence class.
  Permissions use **GRANTED / MISSING / STALE**; interceptors use
  **RUNNING / PAUSED / OFF**. No surface runs its own check or keeps its
  own copy.
- **Chrome.** The Deck chrome carries one compact line:
  `GUARD 4/4` when whole — visible but quiet: no colour alarm, motion or
  sound. When something lacks, the same line names the first lack:
  `GUARD 3/4 — ACCESSIBILITY OFF`. It is a readout, not a badge.
- **At the gesture.** Before a slide can commit, the Watch Store must
  prove the chain. If it cannot, the slide is inert and the reserved
  sentence under it names the lack and offers **FIX**. His one committing
  gesture can never be spent into a dead permission.
- **PARK drawer.** Open PARK from the band’s end detent, or tap the chrome
  Guard line while unsealed. Its header is `GUARD`, with
  `LAST PROVED <instant>` beneath it — the instant lives here, not in
  chrome. PARK carries, in this order:
  1. **Permissions** — the full board: one row per permission or
     interceptor, its state word, and a **FIX** key only where action is
     needed. FIX opens the exact Android pane. On return, each line
     re-strikes only when its own fresh query lands; the repaired line
     alone gets the firm click. No fixed stagger, no restored banner.
  2. **Sound** — the existing sound controls/readouts that used to sit in
     Guard.
  3. **Theme** — an **AUTO** readout naming the routine or schedule that
     owns it. No manual theme pill is resurrected.
  4. The static spec plate may sit at the drawer’s foot as identity. It
     is furniture, never a reason to keep a destination.
- **Shield keeps the working lists.** Blocked websites, app targets and
  loadout controls stay in Shield, beside the loadouts that consume
  them. Shield does **not** gain a second detailed permission board:
  PARK is the sole detailed Guard home. This settles the CHAIN question
  for the dissolved branch.
- **First run.** Setup opens PARK directly and walks the permissions
  there. Discoverability is designed into the first run and into every
  refusal; it does not need a permanent fifth stop.
- **While sealed.** PARK cannot open. The chrome Guard line is inert,
  and every Android Settings launcher renders as print and answers
  nothing — Hale’s clause binds this branch exactly as it bound the
  kept branch. Per Sarthak’s death verdicts, a mid-session death is not
  announced by a chrome change or a knock; the clock runs and the finish
  receipt tells the truth once.

### The losing branch, answered honestly

The strongest case for keeping Guard is real: a dedicated back panel is
calm, complete and easy to rediscover after months away. It gives rare,
deliberate setup one obvious home, and its printed plate gives the
machine an identity a drawer may feel too small to carry. Spatial memory
can also outlast typographic attention at a depleted hour.

It still loses. Everything that makes the kept room good — the complete
board, the one FIX path, the plate, the quiet print discipline — moves
into PARK unchanged. What does not move is the separate stop, because
the stop is the part that fails the urgent journey: he will not visit it
at 14:00, and detection that waits for a visit is not detection. The
chrome line is the permanent signpost; the slide’s refusal is the
in-path signpost; first run opens the workshop directly. We keep the
workshop and lose only the room around it.

## 2. THE RECORD SIDE — RESTATED AS THE DRAWER

### Authority

Sarthak’s newest words override the day-grading frame:

> “But this app is not a study tracker?”
>
> “The tasks i do should be stored somewhere, like there is no use to
> see them but i want like maybe after a month and look at what i did
> and type stuff.”

Plain reading: **Monolith is a blocker. It is not his study tracker.**
Study tracking remains YPT’s and GMC’s job. The record side’s job is
narrower: when he finishes a task, Monolith keeps it. Weeks later he can
open the drawer, find it, and read what he did.

The destination he kept remains, but its face changes. The Ledger stop
opens **the Drawer**. It is not a dashboard, report card, attendance
book or weekly judgement. It is storage with a reading surface.

### What is stored

The drawer reads completed task rows only — never the session’s
`completedTasksSummary` string, which would be the same fact in a second
form. When a task becomes **COMPLETED**, retain:

- the task’s internal ID, for identity only;
- **title**, exactly as it stood at completion;
- **category**;
- **notes / substeps**, exactly as stored with the task;
- **estimated minutes**;
- **actual seconds spent**;
- **completion timestamp** — the sort key and the stored date/time;
- **completed-in-session ID**, as internal provenance only; it is not
  printed as a score or code.

No completed-task **Clear** control appears in Tasks or the Drawer.
**Retention is permanent:** a stored task is never auto-deleted,
expired, pruned or cleaned up by age. Years of finished tasks stay.
Completion is not an achievement event: it earns no point, streak,
badge, percentage or total. If a task is deliberately reopened, it is
no longer finished and leaves the Drawer; if it is completed again, it
returns under the new completion timestamp.

If Sarthak explicitly declared **REST / ILL / FAMILY** for a date, that
word may be stored as a date note and printed beside that date heading.
It is his authored word, not an attendance result. An undeclared day
creates no drawer line; absence is never rendered as zero.

### How the Drawer opens

- **Header:** `STORED`, with one search field: `TYPE TO FIND A TASK`.
- **Default view:** the current month’s finished tasks, newest first,
  grouped under plain date headings such as `THU 29 OCT`. A date
  heading is a shelf label only — no total, grade or verdict follows it.
- **Browsing by month:** an `ALL MONTHS` index lists one row for each
  month that has at least one stored task — `OCTOBER 2026`, newest
  month first, label only. No count or total rides on the row, and a
  month with nothing stored is simply absent. Opening a month shows
  its stored tasks by date, newest first, paged internally in chunks.
  A month here is an address for finding things, not a measurement
  scope: no month total, month grade or calendar grid exists.
- **Paging:** within a month, show the newest 50 entries; `EARLIER`
  at the foot loads the preceding 50. The repository pages by
  completion timestamp and never needs to materialise years of rows at
  once. Storage is not the constraint — a task is a line of text; the
  design constraint is that the list stays light in the hand.
- **Row:** task title (up to two lines), category, finish time, and
  time spent. No estimate on the row, no checkbox, no rank, no progress
  bar, no comparison.
- **Detail:** tapping a row opens its stored form — full title,
  category, finish date and time, estimate and actual time as two
  separate plain facts (never a delta or percentage), and the stored
  notes/substeps. The detail is read-only.
- **Empty states:** before anything is stored,
  `NOTHING STORED YET`. For a failed search,
  `NOTHING STORED UNDER “<query>”` — absence in words, never a zero.
- **Chrome:** no badge, count, dot or notification on the Ledger stop.
  The Drawer never summons him.
- **While sealed:** the Drawer is read-only furniture: it can be read,
  but search input, reopening and every edit are inert until the session
  ends.

### What “type stuff” means — interpretation, flagged

**Interpretation:** the most plausible reading is that, after a month,
he wants to **type words to find an old task**. The conservative build
therefore includes search and nothing more speculative.

- Search covers title, category, notes and substep text.
- It searches **all stored tasks across all years** — not queued work,
  not future work, and not only the month currently open. Permanence
  is only useful if the oldest line is as findable as yesterday’s.
- Results stay in completion-date order, with the full date including
  year on every result. No relevance score and no result count are
  printed.
- The Drawer does **not** gain post-completion annotations or a journal
  in this build. Notes already stored with the task are shown as part
  of that task. If “type stuff” meant “write a note on an old task,”
  that is a separate one-field feature to add only on his word; it is
  not silently inferred here.

This search supersedes Old Tom’s earlier search kill for this object.
That kill assumed an auditor asking only “today?” and “this week?” —
the frame Sarthak has now replaced. A month of stored tasks needs a
finding mechanism; search is retrieval, not tracking.

### What happens to the Bound Book faces

- **TODAY face — dies as a landing.** There is no day total at hero
  size and no grade for today. Today is merely the newest date group,
  and only if a task was finished that day.
- **WEEK face — dies as a face.** No week line, no seven-day total, no
  week-on-week comparison, no attendance wording. Loading older
  entries in chunks is plumbing, not a printed week scope.
- **MONTH — survives only as an address.** The month index is how a
  permanent drawer is browsed; a month label is a shelf name, never a
  subtotal, grade or summary. This is retrieval, not a third scope of
  measurement.
- **Aggregates — die.** With no day or week sum left, HPA-35’s “words
  ride inside every aggregate” has no aggregate to ride in. The
  underlying law survives: no total may eat a declared word, because
  no total prints.
- **DAY CLOSED — not a Drawer entry.** It may remain a moment elsewhere
  in the app, but the Drawer stores finished tasks, not a judgement
  that a day is complete.
- **TEST-day marking — dead.** No `TEST` label, marker, special row or
  search tag is created for a test day. This withdraws the record-side
  effect of the earlier TEST-line ruling: there is no longer a day book
  for the word to head. A task actually finished on that date is stored
  under its date like any other task — nothing more is inferred.
- **Sessions, holds and deflections — not Drawer content.** The finish
  receipt remains their moment. The Drawer is for finished tasks; it
  does not become a disguised session-history or attendance feed.
- **Bound Book discipline that survives:** flat ruled surfaces, real
  words, no orange, no charts, no streaks, no percentages, no season
  or all-time totals, no summons. The book’s beauty survives as a quiet
  drawer of ruled slips; its day/week judgement machinery does not.

### Acceptance checks

1. A task completed today appears in the Drawer once, under today’s
   date, and in no second rendering.
2. Thirty days later, typing a distinctive word from its title or notes
   finds it without scrolling through a month of entries. Years later,
   the same search still reaches it, and the `ALL MONTHS` index reaches
   its month in two taps from the Drawer’s landing.
3. No stored task is ever removed by age, housekeeping or a retention
   job; there is no code path in the Drawer that deletes by date.
4. No surface in the Drawer prints a day total, week total, month
   total, streak, percentage, chart, rank or all-time count.
5. A day with no finished tasks produces no zero, no empty grade and
   no manufactured absence line.
6. A declared day word remains a word. It is never converted into a
   number or folded into a total.
7. Nothing in the Drawer calls him back: no badge, no count, no
   notification, no “new” marker.
