## §1 The kept tab, lived — four week-types

The tab has two users, and they are the same man. **The rested auditor** — Sunday, deliberate, drawing strength from the season (Moench: pride needs a standing object). **The depleted wanderer** — 19:30 on a bad day, Friday eve with shaking hands, the morning after a fever — who opens the tab because it is *there*, a destination in the bar his thumb already knows. The demote design only ever had to survive the moments it chose. The kept tab must survive **every hour he can wander into it**, including the hours the record can hurt him. Every amendment below is priced at the worst open, not the best one.

### The normal week

On weekdays he opens it almost never — the day's facts already reach him where they happen: HELD at the intercept (HPA-17), the receipt at the finish, DAY CLOSED once (HPA-19). The tab's weekday opens are the wanderer's: an idle thumb at 19:30, a spare minute between blocks. What that open must find is **today, and only today** — the day in progress, in the receipt's own denominations, nothing summed yet. A weekday open that lands on a week grid teaches him to check the grid nightly, and a record checked nightly is a scoreboard with good manners.

> **HPA-33 — The tab opens on TODAY.** Its landing face is the current day: sessions sealed, breaks named, the break UNSPENT or spent — the day line, nothing above it. Everything older is **one deliberate gesture in** (a step he takes rested, on purpose). The Sunday auditor loses one tap. The depleted wanderer is saved the whole grid at the hour he can least afford to read it. A destination may have rooms; its door must open onto today.

Sunday is the tab's designed appointment — the rested audit, his brutal-review rhythm given a room. That appointment is designed for, explicitly, in HPA-41.

### Saturday — test week

Friday eve is the tab's most dangerous open of the week (Panik's hour: 18:30, depleted, the test un-written-in). He opens the tab and reads the week so far. Two lies are waiting for him if the tab is built naively. The first: **Saturday already exists in it** — a cell, a planned row, an expectation — so the week reads as a sum he is about to fail. The second: on Saturday night, home from the centre, he opens it and the test day has been totalled as a shortfall against the study days around it.

> **HPA-34 — The record has no future tense.** Days not yet lived do not exist in the tab: no cells, no planned rows, no projected totals, no countdown arithmetic. Today renders in progress. Tomorrow is absent. A record is a past-tense object; the moment it prints a future it starts issuing expectations, and expectations at Friday-eve are demands.

> **HPA-40 — Saturday in the tab.** The test day renders as the word **TEST** at day scope (HPA-09, carried) — on Saturday night its day line reads `TEST · [centre]` and nothing else: no sealed total beside it, nothing it can be compared against. Inside any week line, the TEST day rides as a word (HPA-35). Friday eve, per HPA-34, Saturday does not exist in the tab at all yet.

### Sick week — and the morning after

This is where the kept tab can do its worst damage, and everyone in this cohort knows the scene (Wieder W-1…W-13): fever breaks, he is back at the desk, and before his first block he opens the record — and meets the gap. In the demote design this could not happen: the record spoke at closes, never at opens (HPA-12), and there was no room to wander into. **A kept tab re-opens the wound the demote was designed to close**: the ILL days now sit permanently in a grid, one tap from every morning, rendered next to full days where contrast does the accusing all by itself.

The conditions that save it are already written; the tab must inherit them as structure, not as style:

> **HPA-39 — The morning-after landing.** After any gap of one day or more, the tab's landing is **today** (HPA-33 holds), the gap days sit behind the deliberate gesture, and when he does step in they render as his declared words — **ILL / REST / FAMILY** — or the exact sentence `NOTHING SEALED · NOTHING DECLARED` (HPA-11, verbatim). No week sum that spans a gap may be shown to him until he has sealed one block back: sums are a close's speech (HPA-12), and the return morning has had no closes yet.

And one level down, the permanent condition: a declared ILL day does not stop being dangerous when he recovers — it stays in the grid at day 60, at day 100, one wander from a bad evening. What protects him is not hiding it (the truth standard forbids a doctored record) but **its form**: a word among numbers, never a zero among numbers. A word can be read as a fact about a week. A zero can only be read as a failure of the man.

### Family week

Family arrives unannounced (Freund F1–F8): FAMILY is declared at the speed of PAUSED TODAY (HPA-15) or it is not declared at all, because the day happened to him faster than any declaration could. Both forms reach the tab. The declared day renders its word. The undeclared nothing-sealed day renders the exact sentence — never 0 (HPA-11). The family week's distinctive tab risk is the **Sunday audit after it**: the rested man reads his own week and finds it "less" — fewer sealed hours than the week before — and the tab, asked to sum the week, hands him a number that has silently eaten three days of his mother's house being full.

> **HPA-35 — Words ride inside every aggregate.** Any line that sums days must carry the declared words of the days inside it, **on the same line**: `SEALED 41H 10M THIS WEEK · 1 TEST · 1 FAMILY`. A total that cannot fit its words does not print. This is the load-bearing amendment of the kept tab: it makes absence-never-a-number and attendance-never-a-score enforceable at the exact place a tab breaks them — the sum. A week with family in it reads as a week with family in it. Not as a failed week.

## §2 The truth conditions under tab pressure — ranked

The examination filed four conditions (LEDGER-humans): absence never a number · safe-break minutes never focus · attendance never a score · ghost minutes never born. In the demote form, the first three were nearly free — a moment renders one event, and an event cannot be a league table. **A tab is a standing surface, and standing surfaces erode conditions by inches, each inch arriving as a reasonable feature.** Ranked hardest to keep, with the mechanism of erosion named:

**1. Attendance never a score — HARDEST.** A room's grammar is accumulation. The tab exists to hold many days; many days invite comparison; comparison *is* a score, whatever it is named. Every aggregate is one design decision from shipping: a week total, a season total, an all-time deflection count, a streak flame — each will arrive separately, each will look small, and the sum of the small ones is the scoreboard. Moments were structurally score-proof; the tab must be re-defended at every future edit. The defence is scope law, stated once and enforced forever:

> **HPA-36 — Two scopes, no all-time anything.** The tab renders exactly two scopes: **the day** and **the week**. No cumulative season total, no all-time count of anything, no day-counter of the attempt, no streak arithmetic. Deflections render at day scope only, named **BLOCKED** (Freund's word), and are never totalled across days. A season total is a score with a long fuse; an all-time deflection count is a monument to the wrong number.

> **HPA-38 — The tab never summons.** No badge, no dot, no count on the nav word LEDGER; no notification from the record, ever; and per HPA-05 the tab renders nothing new at him after 20:00 — it is pull-only at every hour, and the pull is always his. A record that summons him is a score in disguise: it has decided the number is worth his attention before he has.

**2. Absence never a number — SECOND.** The tab's job is completeness: every lived day gets a line, and a line in a numeric grid begs a value. Sick, family, test — the grid will try to file them as 0 because 0 is the only value an empty day has in a number's grammar. Unlike the score condition, this one is winnable outright, because the alternative already exists and is filed: declared words and one exact sentence (HPA-11), words riding inside sums (HPA-35), and no future tense (HPA-34), so absence is never pre-printed as an expectation either.

**3. Safe-break minutes never focus — THIRD.** This is a pipeline lie, not a form lie — it was winnable in any form by excluding break minutes at the source. But the tab **amplifies** it: inside a day total, banked break minutes are invisible, where a receipt could have named them on the same line. The tab form therefore owes the naming the receipt would have given:

> **HPA-37 — Breaks are named on the day line, never inside the sealed number.** `9H 42M SEALED · 25M BREAKS`. And the day line also carries the hold's state — break **UNSPENT** or spent — because the hold budget is the one piece of evidence that exists nowhere else (Quaere's surviving purpose), and the day line is where the week-scope reader needs it.

**4. Ghost minutes never born — EASIEST in the tab, because it is not the tab's fight.** It is decided at the source (windows, not firings — HPA-01/02/04) before any rendering exists. The tab inherits whatever the pipeline mints; it can neither create a ghost nor forgive one. Listed here so the ranking is complete and the master plan does not mistake a pipeline fix for a rendering rule.

**The binding under all four:**

> **HPA-42 — One pipeline, or the tab closes.** The tab renders only from the same rows the finish receipt and DAY CLOSED print from. Any fact that cannot trace to that pipeline does not render in the tab; any denomination changes everywhere at once or nowhere. (This is merge condition 6's substance, carried inside the destination his verdict kept — one pipeline, one definition per fact, one rendering per fact. The tab's extra room does not buy it a second source of truth.)

> **HPA-41 — The week face is built for the rested auditor.** The tab's week scope is one face: the week line (HPA-35) and the seven day lines beneath it, in denominations and words — nothing else on the face. No control ships on the tab that cannot justify itself to the rested man at his appointed audit. This is the kept tab's retention mechanism and its beauty brief in one: a handsome, empty room he visits on purpose — Moench's standing object — kept handsome precisely by everything this file forbids it to hold.
