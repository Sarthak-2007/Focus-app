## ELEMENT VERDICTS

### 1. Hero minutes — **DEMOTE** (Linea)
The hero form is DEAD at any altitude: the tile prints the same integer
twice in one glance (dot-matrix + numeral, same size class, both
orange); the dot-matrix clamps at 999 while the numeral keeps counting,
so the two costumes eventually *disagree*; the total is floored
(`coerceAtLeast(35)`, initial 75 — a fresh install prints 75 minutes
never studied); it counts broken sessions as "focused" and ticks during
the live session it supposedly summarises. The **fact** survives
re-scoped: *today's minutes under seal* — hero size (44sp) only as the
closing line of the session-end receipt; one labelled fragment in idle
chrome (`SEALED TODAY 2H 25M`, hidden while a session runs — the Deck
clock is that fact's live rendering); lifetime reduced to one 11sp mono
line at the foot of the logbook, if the logbook lives. Precondition
truth repairs: kill the floor and the initial 75; rename the fact
*time under seal* — "focused" is a claim about his mind no sensor here
can make.

### 2. Receipts — **KEEP-AT-PEAK, home: session end** (Linea)
The standing list fails on its own terms: last 8 cards under a header
whose TOTAL counts all sessions; no date or start time on any card (the
most receipt-like fact it owns, unprinted); none of the Monolith-only
facts (deflections, target vs elapsed, hold used) shown. And the
`HMAC •` stamp is theatre — a 32-bit fragment of a MAC, never re-verified
at render, printing empty on unsigned rows under a header that says
VERIFIED. **HMAC/VERIFIED display: DEAD at any altitude, in every home**
(the signing machinery stays inside, where it is honest). The peak is
the **DONE card** at session end: verb line (Sora SemiBold 20sp),
held line in human language (`HELD 50M OF 50M · 14 TURNED AWAY · NO
BREAK USED` — or, same ink, no shame formatting, `OPENED BY THE HOLD AT
31M`), tasks line only if tasks were completed, closing line = today's
minutes under seal at 44sp, and the footer that names the overlap inside
the product: `YOUR STUDY RECORD LIVES IN YPT — THIS IS THE SEAL'S
ACCOUNT.` One stamp knock; dismisses on any tap or on the next slide's
drag — zero seconds added to the next ARM. The standing list is demoted
to a hairline **logbook** (one mono line per session, date-first) *if*
the tab survives; if it dies, nothing is lost — the card carried the
moment, the DB keeps the data, the GCM backup remains the archive.

### 3. Deflection count — **KEEP-AT-PEAK, home: Shield** (Band)
One scoped mono line: `DEFLECTED TODAY 07`. War-line, airlock-moment
and Ledger homes were judged and rejected in the seat file. **The keep
is conditional on a truth fix, stated as a condition, not a hope:** the
count today is a `max()` of two sources, seeded at 6, with peeks counted
as deflections. No truth fix, no line.

### 4. Stat tiles — **DEAD at any altitude** (Band)
The grid self-convicts: on the same screen, SESSIONS is floored at 4
while the receipts TOTAL says 3; FOCUSED is floored and triply stated;
the fourth tile is TOWER BRICKS, not a streak. The form is the lie —
equal tiles assert equal facts. The facts emigrate instead of dying:
deflections → Shield (above); sessions → the logbook's own header count
only (`LOGBOOK — 23`), honest because it sits on the list it counts.

### 5. Streak tower — **DEAD at any altitude** (Fader)
It does not count days at all: bricks = minutes/25 + completed, forced
to a minimum of 16 — a new user is shown sixteen bricks he never laid;
the same fact the tile above it already prints, a third rendering of
one number. It saturates at 70. And the metaphor itself fails TRUTH: a
tower can only accumulate, so on the day the streak breaks it must lie
by standing, accuse by demolishing, or compress into illegibility.
Fader specced the honest peak in good faith (one brick = one sealed
day, strata gaps for breaks) and killed it himself on arithmetic: by
~day 60 the peak must crop, compress, or scroll — before the day-100
morning it exists for. The fact survives as one line of type:
`SEALED DAYS IN A ROW — 12`. The tower also dies in the airlock overlay,
where a monument has no business standing between a man and a decision.

### 6. 7-day columns — **KEEP-AT-PEAK, home: the rebuilt Ledger tab only** (Fader)
The current form lies kindly, which is worse: a zero day draws a 12%
stub (absence rendered as small presence); heights normalise to the
week's own maximum, so the same height means a different number every
week; selection turns the selected day orange, corrupting the value it
inspects; and a short data feed silently draws a hard-coded fictional
week. The peak: header `7 DAYS — SEALED ONLY`; plot 120dp, seven 32dp
columns on a shared baseline; a **printed scale fixed forever, 0–12H**,
gridlines 0/3/6/9/12H, **no target line** (12H is the top of the ruler,
not a demand); the tube and fader notch die; zero days render as true
absence; a tap indexes into that day's receipts. A column, unlike a
tower, has a native grammar for going down — shorter is just shorter.
This is the one element whose peak genuinely requires the tab: it
cannot live on Shield, in chrome, or at session end without becoming a
different, worse element.

## COHORT OPTION — **(b) DEMOTE THE SURVIVORS**

Five of six elements die or find better homes without the tab: tiles
and tower are dead outright; hero minutes demote to session end +
chrome; receipts peak at session end with the list demoted to a
logbook; deflections move to Shield. A destination kept standing for
one chart is a museum with a single exhibit, and every day it exists
it re-invites the tiles, the tower and the double-printed hero back
in — the Ledger's disease was never any single element, it was the
claim that Monolith keeps a second study record. It doesn't. It keeps
the seal's account, and the seal's account belongs at the seal's
moments: the DONE card when a session closes, one line on Shield,
one fragment in idle chrome.

**The decision, reduced to the one question that is actually Sarthak's:**
the 7-day columns are the strongest — the only — argument for (a).
If the sealed-week chart, honestly scaled, is worth a destination to
him, keep the tab rebuilt around it (columns + hairline logbook +
streak line; nothing else returns). If it isn't, (b) is complete and
nothing of value is homeless. The cohort's vote is (b); Fader's
columns are recorded as the live dissent that defines the choice.

**Coordinator's ruling on the examiners' one open clash** (Linea ↔ Band,
deflection scope): both renderings stand. The DONE card's `14 TURNED
AWAY` is the *session's* account, scoped by the card itself; Shield's
`DEFLECTED TODAY 07` is the day aggregate — the only home that can hold
out-of-session bounces, which Band correctly says exist. Part and whole
are different facts, the same distinction both examiners already accept
for minutes. The scopes are printed in the strings themselves. If a
one-number-regardless-of-scope rule is wanted, it is Sarthak's to
impose, not ours to assume.

**Binding condition adopted** (Fader ↔ Linea): any day-aggregate —
columns readout, logbook — derives from the *same* session rows the
receipts print. Today's two pipelines already disagree on one screen
(SESSIONS 4 vs TOTAL 3); an index that can contradict its own list is
worse than no index.

## THE STRONGEST CASE AGAINST (b) — written to win

*Demotion scatters the record to the winds and calls it honesty. Under
(b), the seal's account exists in exactly three places: a card that
vanishes when tapped (ephemeral by design), a chrome fragment that
hides whenever a session runs, and one line on Shield. The one moment
he truly needs the record whole — weeks later, at 1 AM, asking "was
October actually held, which routine is leaking, where did the month
go" — every one of those homes answers with a fragment or nothing.
Linea's own anti-case convicts her verdict: the DONE card taxes the
exact moment the app should disappear, four times a day, seven hundred
times a year, until the thumb dismisses it blind — and her demoted
daily minutes will spend their life printing `SEALED TODAY 2H 25M`
against a 12-hour ambition, a subset number wearing a total's clothes
no matter how carefully it's labelled, teaching shame the machine
didn't earn. Fader's anti-case convicts his: everything under (b) is
amnesiac — the card forgets yesterday, the columns (dead under (b),
for want of a home) forget last week, and nothing in the app thinks in
months for a man whose war is 186 days long. Band's anti-case convicts
his: a lone deflection line on Shield is a score torn from its record,
the most gameable, valence-loaded form the number can take. And the
premise of (b) is false economy: the tab's cost is one band stop he
never has to visit, while its benefit is the only room in the app where
the seal's account exists as one document, readable in one sitting,
at the one altitude — his Sunday review — where a record is actually
read. You are not demoting survivors. You are evicting them into
corridors and calling the eviction a design. The honest option is (a):
a small, severe, rebuilt Ledger — columns, logbook, streak line — that
keeps the seal's account in one place and tells the truth in it.*

The cohort has read that at full strength. It stands as the case for
(a), and the columns remain its load-bearing wall: every other exhibit
in it (the 1 AM audit, the Sunday review) is served — worse, but
served — by the logbook, which survives under (b) only if a home is
found for it… and there, the against-case lands its one true blow:
**under a pure (b), the logbook has no home either.** The cohort's
answer, recorded honestly: the logbook's lines are the DONE card's
facts, and the DB + GCM backup remain the archive of record; a
browsable in-app logbook is the first thing that returns if (a) is
picked for the columns' sake. If Sarthak reads the against-case and
feels the 1 AM question bite, that feeling *is* the (a) vote — the
cohort's (b) assumes it doesn't bite. His call, with both cases at
full strength in front of him.
