## §1 THE SOUL — one sheet, three ranks

A poster is **one sheet**. Not a screen with a poster pasted on it.

- **The sheet has three ranks and no furniture.** THE HEADLINE — the figure, the one number that matters now, at monument scale. THE BODY — what is being protected and chosen, at reading scale. THE COLOPHON — the machine's confession in mono microprint at the foot: its proof, its facts, its date. Every element on every SIGNAL screen is one of the three ranks, or it is furniture and it dies in §12.
- **Type is the image.** The figure is not displayed BY the sheet; the figure IS the sheet's picture. There is no illustration, no panel, no device. A man who covers the words on this sheet has covered the sheet.
- **Registration is the sheet's truth.** The whole sheet is printed from plates (§9 of the presentation spec, generalised from the headline to the composition): while the machine proves itself, the sheet is visibly a misprint; at proof it snaps true and stays true for the session's life. Sharpness is the proof, on every screen the seal touches, not just the Deck's number.
- **Day and night are the same sheet on two stocks** — paper by day, black stock by night (the power-cut ruling stands). A poster does not change its composition when the light dies; it changes its stock.

## §2 THE DIAL QUESTION — the dial dies, the ring remains

The commission asks it plainly: does the selection dial survive in a poster world, or is duration chosen typographically? Answered in two parts, because the dial is two things wearing one name.

**The wheel's physics survive — they are machinery.** One (θ,ω) integrator, shared, shipped; phase 2 declined Haut's zero-overshoot fork as machinery-not-skin and that honesty stands untouched. The hand still turns.

**The DIAL — the pictured instrument — dies.** Numerals around a circle, tick labels, the eight-dot hub: all of it is gauge cosplay, and it was wrong in my own grammar all along:

1. **It violated my law 3** (one fact, one home). The duration printed twice — once as the headline figure, once as dial furniture. A poster prints a number once, at the size it deserves. The figure is the readout; a second readout is a second claim.
2. **Fremd named the cost**: dial-vs-display mismatch was on his confusion list. Two instruments that can disagree in a tired eye are one instrument too many.
3. **Purity:** a gauge is machine furniture — and as of tonight there is a seventh direction whose soul IS the machine. A dial in the poster world is a borrowed organ from a neighbour who now exists. It goes home.

**What remains is the RING:** a bare printed circle — the poster's own graphic, a sun, a target, a full stop at poster scale. No numerals, no labels, a hollow centre (empty paper by day, empty black by night). One red index mark travels the ring as the hand turns: it shows the hand *where it is in the turn*, never *what the value is*. Position without pretence. The ring claims nothing numeric, so it can never contradict the figure.

**And the typographic choosing the commission proposed — adopted, where it honestly belongs: THE PRINTED SCALE.** Under the figure, a timetable line of settable durations in mono — `0:30 · 1:00 · 1:30 · 2:00 · 3:00 · 4:00` — printed values the hand taps to set exactly.
