# Q6 — the slide's sound: a one-page brief for Sarthak

*For Ren to present at his next break. Easy words. No
recommendation is made here — the choice is his.*

## The question

When you slide to start a session — at 3:30 in the morning,
in a quiet house — should the slide make a soft two-note
sound as it locks? Or should it stay with the quiet knocks
it has now?

## Where the idea comes from

In the morning's invention wave, a designer named Orson
proposed a small "road" of sound under the start-slide. As
your finger drags the slide into its last stretch, a first
soft note sounds. At the moment the slide locks, a second
note sounds — the same note, one octave higher. Two quiet
tones under the finger, so your ear knows where the lock is
before your eyes do.

The design was never built. A merge ruling that same
morning ranked it first among the sound ideas — and then
held it, on purpose, until you answer. Two of its reviewers
had refused it for one reason only: whether any sound
belongs in your house at 3:30 in the morning is a fact
about your life, not about craft. So it waited.

One thing it is not: your other sound answer that morning
(a sound when a session dies — you said no) was a different
question. Nobody has counted it as an answer to this one.

## What the app does today

The shipped app is complete without it. The slide today
speaks in quiet mechanical knocks — a soft knock as you pass
the gate, a lower one if you slide back — and the seal
itself has one single voice when the session starts. No
melody anywhere. Nothing is broken; this is a choice, not
a repair.

## Your three options, and what each one commits

**1. "Add the two-note sound."**
The two soft notes get built into the slide in a coming
build, exactly as designed. The design file closes as
adopted.

**2. "Keep the quiet knocks."**
Nothing changes. The design stays on file — not built, not
killed. It can only come back if the question is asked
again.

**3. "Kill the idea for good."**
The two-note road is dead. The file closes in the books as
rejected, and the question does not come back.

## The sibling design — the Octave Gate

The same designer filed a second, bigger idea: a playable
track — the slide's sound as a small instrument, where the
notes follow your finger instead of playing two fixed
tones. The merge ranked the simple two-note road above it
(it is cheaper, and its high note is only the low note's
octave — nothing extra invented), and kept the Gate as the
spare. What happens to it under each of your answers:

- **Add** → the Gate stays on file as the standing spare,
  unbuilt.
- **Keep** → it waits alongside the Road, untouched.
- **Kill** → it loses its only road in. It stays preserved
  on paper, but with sound refused it can never be built —
  unless the whole sound question is reopened one day.

## Until you answer

The design file stands in the registry as HELD ON Q6. The
app does not wait on it — only this one decision does.
