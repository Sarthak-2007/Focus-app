## 1. The one sentence

Stein's sentence, as handed to me:

> *a building he enters — carved stone and brass; bronze
> chooses, gold commits; facts are inscriptions; light is a
> tended lamp.*

It is a good sentence. I sharpen it in one place only, and
argue the change rather than smuggle it: the handed sentence
never says what the building is *for*. A temple you merely
enter could be a museum. This building exists to shut him in
— that is the app's whole job — and the sentence should carry
the purpose the way the lintel carries the dedication. The
sharpened sentence, which I file as Temple's:

> **Temple is a building he enters to be shut inside: carved
> stone and brass throughout — bronze chooses, gold commits —
> where every fact is an inscription cut once and never
> revised, and the only light is a lamp that is tended.**

Defence, in one paragraph. Every clause is a law a builder
can be held to, and each one kills a class of wrong work.
"To be shut inside" makes the blocker the building's purpose,
not its feature: the gate, the bolt, the sealed door are the
point, and any surface that behaves like an open lobby —
welcoming, browsable, entertaining — is off-idea. "Carved
stone and brass throughout" is the material closed-world:
no glass, no paper, no plastic, no screen-native furniture
survives inside the walls unless it is naturalised (§2) or
licensed to leave (§2, the slip). "Bronze chooses, gold
commits" is the metal law, and it is an *interaction* law,
not a colour law: any element a hand selects with is bronze;
gold appears only at the instant a thing becomes binding —
which is why gold can never decorate, and why a gold
selection state anywhere in the build is a concept breach,
not a style slip. "An inscription cut once and never revised"
is the truth law in the world's own grammar: what the
building prints was witnessed, is not edited after the fact,
and a reconstruction never wears the witness's mark — the
receipt's two seals (struck / unstruck) are this clause made
visible. "The only light is a lamp that is tended" fences
light to one source with one behaviour: finite, warm, spent
as the span spends, never ambient weather (that is Dawn's
sky) and never electric signage (that is Noir's burn). If an
element cannot be justified by one of these five clauses, it
does not belong to this world, however beautiful it is.

## 2. The borrowed-furniture register — FOR THE BUILD

(per Stein's crew letter: stele Deck, Shield wall, Tasks/
Drawer, desk clock, held door, receipt, colonnade rail, pill
tablet faces, T-1/T-2/T-3), against DEEPEN-temple as the
build's governing spec. Verdicts: **NATIVE** — could only be
Temple. **NATURALISED** — borrowed, now the building's own;
the how is stated, and the how is what the builders must
actually ship. **FOREIGN** — cut it; replacement named.

## 3. Three temptations this world must refuse forever

The temple bell is confessed fraud already (PRESENTATION §9:
the engine cannot ring; Temple's resonance is mass, never
duration). It is not counted here. These are the next three —
each is a thing this world will be *asked* for, by a future
agent or a future mood, precisely because it sounds like more
Temple. Each refusal is permanent.

**1. THE LITURGY — the temptation to give the building a
priest.** Chanted copy, blessings, invocations, "the temple
welcomes you," a rite for arming, a rite for finishing, any
sentence in which the building speaks as a sacred person.
Refused because: this temple has no priest and no congregation
— it is an empty building and one man, and its holiness, such
as it is, is that it *says only facts, cut in stone*. The
moment it blesses him, it is performing religion at a boy who
came to be shut in so he can study; and performed reverence is
the bell fraud in words — a resonance the thing does not have.
Test for any future copy: if the sentence could be spoken by
a priest, cut it; if it could be cut by a mason, keep it.

**2. THE RELIC — the temptation to make the kept things
shine.** Curios as collectibles with rarity, gleam tiers,
a treasury that glitters more as he achieves more; T-1's laid
courses struck in gold; the niche wall becoming a shrine that
grades him by what it holds. Refused because: Monolith is a
blocker, not a tracker — the Drawer law is a quiet wall of
kept things, no scores, no totals, no judgement — and a relic
is a kept thing that has started demanding worship. The
niche wall records; it must never *reward*. The day the
treasury shines is the day the building has started keeping
score of the man it was built to shelter, and gold spent on
achievement breaks the metal law twice over (gold commits;
it does not congratulate).

**3. THE ORACLE — the temptation to let the building advise.**
A temple is where men historically went to be told what to do,
and this one will be asked: inscriptions that recommend,
nudge, prescribe — "the stone suggests…", a carved hint about
the next chapter, the frieze that starts ranking his routines.
Refused because: the building inscribes facts and nothing
else (§1, clause four). Advice in stone is worse than advice
on a screen — it borrows the inscription's permanence and the
witness's authority for what is only an opinion, and it breaks
his standing law that chapters are never prescribed to him.
The frieze shows the day; it does not judge it. The lintel
prints the lack; it does not scold it. Any future element that
tells him what to do — however wise, however beautifully cut —
is the Oracle speaking, and it is cut on sight.

*(Runner-up, named so it cannot sneak in as a fourth door:
THE RUIN — weathering as decoration: decorative cracks, moss,
erosion, romantic patina, any green-adjacent verdigris. A
tended building is maintained; the Confession's one honest
crack (§8 of the presentation) is a fact about a broken day,
not a style. Decay is Dawn's weather and Noir's street. Not
ours.)*

## 4. The soul test, applied in advance

Greyscale. Names covered. Colours, metals and words — this
world's loudest claims — all struck. What is left is value,
shadow and shape. Judged surface by surface, before a single
deepened render exists, so the builders know where the
building must carry its identity in relief or lose it:

**Still reads as Temple:**

- **The Deck elevation.** Beam, standing slab with a ring cut
  into its lower face, a course of loose stones at its foot,
  inscription bands separated by shadow joints, a notched
  band, three steps with a bar across the top one. This reads
  as a monument's face in pure greyscale — no other world in
  the wave has steps, courses, or a stone standing off a wall.
  This is the world's soul, and it is carried by relief
  exactly as DEEPEN §12 claims.
- **T-1's courses of the day** — a wall visibly *growing* by
  laid courses under the slab. Growth-by-course is a shape
  fact; it survives greyscale whole.
- **The repose** — paradoxically strong: the *same elevation
  with the bolt drawn back and the stone unoccupied*. An
  emptied temple reads as an emptied temple, because the
  architecture is still all there.
- **The niche wall** — a regular grid of sealed openings,
  each with its own small lintel. Reads as a columbarium wall,
  nothing else in the wave is a grid of sealed niches.

**Collapses — and the fix for each:**

- **The Shield wall collapses into a list.** In greyscale,
  courses-with-seal-marks are rows; rows are every app's
  furniture. *Fix (binding on Setzer):* the wall must differ
  from a list in shape, not just dress — course heads carry
  the seal at the left and close with a joint shadow, row
  heights vary as wall courses vary (register heads taller
  than plaque rows), and the travelling capstone's trapezoid
  silhouette must be visible mid-groove. If a greyscale
  screenshot of Shield could be any settings page, the wall
  has failed and the fix is structural, not tonal.
- **The Held Door collapses into Noir.** Bronze-black ground,
  a name at centre, darkness: greyscale cannot tell a sealed
  gate from an unlit sign. *Fix:* the door's identity is its
  furniture, and all of it is shape — the double rule above
  and below the name, the large outlined square across it,
  the notched frieze miniature, and the side door's tablet
  (present / barred / absent). If any one of the four is cut
  for tidiness, the collapse returns; the frieze miniature is
  the one most likely to be cut and the one that carries the
  most difference. Protect it by name.
- **The desk clock collapses into every AOD clock.** Black
  field, big numerals: greyscale Temple, greyscale Noir,
  greyscale anything are one picture. *Partially accepted,
  partially fixed:* across a dark room at 03:30 the collapse
  costs nothing — he is reading a clock, not touring the
  building. But the fix is already specced and must ship:
  the brass stand hairline beneath the numerals with the hour
  inscribed at its end (§9.3 of the deepening) — one line of
  furniture that makes the dark face *this* building's lamp
  niche and no one else's slab.
- **The hanging tablet collapses into a generic pill.** A
  small rounded-edge rectangle docked at a screen edge, in
  greyscale, is every overlay ever shipped — and greyscale
  is doubly unfair here, since the tablet's naturalisation
  (§2) leans on its bronze. *Fix:* shape must do what colour
  cannot — chiselled (never rounded) corners, the brass
  hairline edge as a drawn frame inside the silhouette, and
  on the PEEK face the incised ✕ square at its right end.
  If the rendered tablet could be mistaken for a system
  bubble, it is foreign again; the frame and the chisel are
  the difference.
- **The receipt collapses into Signal.** Paper slip, printed
  rows, a stamp: in greyscale it is the poster's family, not
  the building's. *Accepted, by the licence in §2:* the slip
  is the one object that leaves the building, and print is
  its native country. Its Temple citizenship is carried by
  marks (the seal at the №, the brass rule), which are shape
  enough at reading distance. No fix — a named exception,
  not a failure.
- **The night Deck is at risk from the lamp itself.** A warm
  radial glow over a dark ground, greyscaled, is a glow over
  a dark ground — Noir's spill and Dawn's pool are cousins.
  *Fix:* the glow must stay *centred on the stele and shaped
  by it* — light falling on a standing stone from a lamp in
  front of it, brightest at the inscription, dying at the
  stone's edges — never a free radial wash over the whole
  screen. T-2's burn-down helps: a glow that visibly *spends*
  is a lamp; a static glow is ambience, and ambience is
  foreign in this world (§1, clause five).

**The test's verdict in advance:** Temple's soul is real but
it is not evenly distributed. The Deck carries it almost
alone today; Shield, Door, tablet and clock each have exactly
one or two shape-facts standing between them and another
world, and those facts are named above so they can be
defended in the build by name — not rediscovered as losses
at judging.
