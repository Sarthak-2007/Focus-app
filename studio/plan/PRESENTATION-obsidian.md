*Scope law, unchanged: presentation is mine; mechanism is not. Where this

## 0. THE PRESENTATION GRAMMAR (the rules every element below obeys)

**Surfaces — four depths of glass, no fifth.**
Ground (the room) → recess (unlit wells: tracks, search fields) →
raised (the one card: the hero) → sheet (what rises to be touched:
caps, chips, the receipt's kin). A thing's depth is its rank: what is
raised is what the moment is about.

**Ink — light is the ink, in three tiers.**
Text-1 bone (facts that decide: the hero, names, the selected word).
Text-2 smoke-bone (facts that support: ends-at, counts, secondary
lines). Muted deep smoke (print that must exist but never speak first:
hints, unselected words, footers). If a fact matters, it climbs a tier;
if it doesn't, it sinks or goes. There is no fourth tier and no
decoration tier.

**The ember — one per screen, spent on the commit.**
The ember is not an accent colour; it is the temperature of the one
irreversible act on the screen. On the Deck it is the slide cap and
nothing else. On Shield it is spent NOWHERE — Shield is preparation,
nothing there is irreversible, so the screen stays cold: bone caps on
black glass. A screen with two embers is a screen lying about what
matters.

**One fact, one rendering.** The trunk already holds this law (the
count lives in the strip, not the eyebrow; the ends-at lives in TIME,
not the hero row). This spec extends it: any element found rendering a
second copy of a fact is demoted or deleted below.

**Print dialect.** Measures and clock facts in Anonymous Pro; names
and sentences in Atkinson; the hero alone in Sora. Caps for state
words (SHUT, RUNNING, BREAK), sentence case only inside input fields,
where a man types like he talks.

## 1. THE DECK — element by element

The Deck has three moments, and every element is judged against the
moment it serves: **arming** (3:30 AM, dark, half-awake, one hand),
**holding** (mid-session glances — the session is running and the Deck
is a gauge, not a desk), and **the finish** (detention, tear, receipt).

### 1.1 The header band — MONOLITH · GUARD n/4
- **What:** the wordmark (left) and the Guard line (right):
  `GUARD 4/4`, or `GUARD n/4 — <FIRST LACK>`, or nothing at all when
  unmeasured (the print function returns null — silence is the honest
  third state).
- **Job:** at arming, one glance answers "will the block actually
  hold?" before he commits an hour to it. It is the machine's
  pre-flight, printed in the band so it is read before the wheel is
  touched.
- **Decision: KEEP, exactly as printed.** It is the most honest
  element in the app — it can say "I don't know" by absenting itself.
  In obsidian it prints in text-2 (it is a supporting fact) and steps
  to text-1 only in the lack state — a lack is the one header fact
  that decides. The band itself stays flat on the ground: no card, no
  border. The door behind it (HB-1/HB-2's fault) is mechanism; its
  cure is in Truth Car 2 and this spec assumes the cured door.

### 1.2 The eyebrow — TOTAL / SESSION RUNNING / BREAK · N MIN
- **What:** one word-line over the hero, naming what the hero number
  means right now.
- **Job:** the hero is a bare number; the eyebrow is its caption.
  During a Safe Break it is the break's only rendering (Nacht's law —
  the desk clock refuses the break; the Deck owns it).
- **Decision: KEEP.** Text-2, Anonymous Pro, letterspaced — a caption,
  not a headline. In obsidian the running eyebrow never takes the
  ember: running is a state, not a commit.

### 1.3 The hero readout — the total, once
- **What:** the true span (blocks × length + breaks, priced in) at
  88sp Sora; FLOW for the stopwatch; the running readout while sealed.
- **Job:** the monument. At 3:30 AM it is the whole contract in one
  number — this is what you are giving the morning. Mid-session it is
  the only number a glance needs.
- **Decision: KEEP, untouchable in size and font** (the finish-list
  already protects it; in this world it is the reason the card
  exists). Its ghosts (the ±1dp print shadows inherited from the
  porcelain press logic) are audited in §6 — kept at night, where the
  catch-ghost reads as the glass's edge-light; the device removal
  trial the trunk comment prescribes still stands.

### 1.4 The wheel — selection I: THE TURN
- **What:** a 288dp black-glass dial. Drag or fling to set minutes
  (10–180 in 5-minute steps, one (θ,ω) integrator, seat springs,
  knurl teeth at 3°), FLOW across the strait, and the hub: 8 wells,
  the block count — tap the hub to cycle 1→8, or set directly.
- **How selection works, redesigned as presentation:** the wheel is
  the only control in the app you operate *by feel with your eyes
  half shut*, and its presentation is built for that. The selected
  minute is never printed anywhere but the hero directly above it —
  the hand turns, the monument changes; there is no second readout to
  cross-check (one fact, one rendering). The teeth are felt, not
  seen: at night the tick ring sinks to muted so the dial is a dark
  disc with a bone pointer — you count teeth with your thumb, the way
  you count a safe's dial. The strait's dead sector stays EMPTY of
  tick, tooth and numeral (as built) — a gap in the teeth is FLOW's
  door, and the hand learns the gap before the eye does.
- **The blocks hub:** the wells are the count made visible — n lit
  wells of 8, no numeral. At 3:30 AM "how many blocks" is a shape,
  not arithmetic. KEEP exactly; the seat knock that scales with the
  count (16→24ms) is the count's sound (§5).
- **Decision: KEEP whole.** Demote only the tick ring's contrast at
  night (muted tier) — done via the night token set in phase 1.

### 1.5 The FLOW key
- **What:** a key-cap under the wheel: tap → stopwatch mode; the hero
  prints FLOW, the strip's TIME prints NO END.
- **Job:** the honest escape from planning — some mornings are not
  measured in blocks.
- **Decision: KEEP as a key-cap** (a verb wears an action's body —
  the same grammar as DONE). Named accident, §6: tapping FLOW also
  sets the dial to 5 — invisible but harmless, because in FLOW the
  wheel is a frozen ghost and the 5 never prints as a plan. The key
  when active takes the ink fill (bone in this world), never the
  ember: choosing FLOW is a mode, not a commit.

### 1.6 The running form — progress track + %
- **What:** at the seal the wheel hands its room to a 6dp track and a
  percentage; the track's fill is the ink, post-exit (the accent
  leaves at the seal — C7's law, kept).
- **Job:** the mid-session glance. Not a score — a position.
- **Decision: KEEP, demote the %.** The track is the fact; the number
  is its echo. In obsidian the % prints muted (it currently rides
  text-2). A man glancing mid-session reads the bar's length in his
  periphery; the digits are for the rare second look, and they should
  wait quietly for it.

### 1.7 The NOW row — the task, DONE, QUEUE n
- **What:** NOW · the active task's name (tap: sub-steps unfold) ·
  DONE (key-cap) · QUEUE n (tap: the Tasks screen).
- **Job:** the session's one sentence of content. The Deck does not
  manage tasks; it names the current one and offers the tear.
- **Decisions:**
  - The task name is the row's text-1 — it is the only content word
    on the Deck and it earns the tier.
  - **DONE stays a key-cap** (ESR-07's cure — a verb in an action's
    body). In obsidian: sheet glass, bone word.
  - **QUEUE n is a door dressed as print — that ends.** It is
    tappable (it opens Tasks) but renders as a bare count beside
    DONE's cap, so the hand reads two different kinds of thing as
    one kind. In obsidian QUEUE takes the same hairline key-cap body
    as DONE, one step quieter (no fill — outline only): same grammar,
    lower rank. Priced in §7 as a presentation change on an existing
    clickable (a border + padding; no new machinery).
  - Sub-steps print `0${idx+1}` — the tenth step prints **010**, the
    same defect Hale filed as DRW-08 in the Drawer at
    FocusSequencerScreen/DrawerScreen. It lives here too
    (TactileDeckScreen's step rows). Presentation verdict: steps
    print as plain numerals (1, 2, … 10) — the leading zero was a
    porcelain mannerism and it is arithmetically false at ten. The
    cure rides whichever car touches the row; the spec's dialect is
    *numerals print true*.
  - A done sub-step strikes through and sinks to text-2 (as built).
    KEEP.

### 1.8 The armed utilities — DESK CLOCK · HELD n (running only)
- **What:** two chips that exist only while a session runs: the desk
  clock's door, and the held-notifications door with its count badge.
- **Job:** mid-session, these are the two lawful reasons to look at
  the phone at all. They appear at Settle, not during the shot
  (F-Z3 — during the seal the room is empty of everything but the
  seal).
- **Decision: KEEP both, cold.** Sheet glass, bone words, the HELD
  count in Anonymous Pro. No ember: opening the digest is a peek, not
  a commit. The count badge is a fact (captured notifications) and
  prints whole or the chip doesn't — it always exists while running,
  so it prints.

### 1.9 The proof strip — BLOCK · APPS · TIME, the seam, the imprint
- **What:** three cells pinned under the scroll, at the same address
  in every state: BLOCK (ON / OFF / UNKNOWN), APPS (the count, or
  UNKNOWN), TIME (ENDS hh:mm / NO END / INVALID). Below them the seam
  stamps (BIND NOT HELD · PROOF: POSITIVE and kin) and the imprint
  line — what the seal will swear to.
- **Job:** this is the Deck's conscience. Before the slide, it is the
  last read: *this is what will actually happen if you commit* — how
  many apps, whether the block is real, when it ends. During the
  press it freezes at the DOWN frame's facts (the latch) so the hand
  commits to what it read, not to a number that slid mid-press.
- **Decision: KEEP whole — it is the best-designed element in the
  app.** Three presentation rulings in this world:
  - UNKNOWN is a first-class print, never a failure colour. It takes
    the muted tier and stands as tall as ON: the machine saying "I
    don't know" is information, and in obsidian ignorance prints in
    the same bone family as knowledge, one tier down. It never takes
    the alert tone.
  - The strip never scrolls, never moves, never resizes (Δy = 0
    across the seal, as built). In a world of glass, the strip is the
    etched plate at the instrument's base.
  - The imprint line is the strip's footnote and prints in muted —
    until the seal strikes it. The strike is the one moment the
    imprint climbs to text-1 (as built — the struck key). KEEP.

### 1.10 The slide — selection II: THE COMMIT
- **What:** a 72dp register under the strip: the ember cap on the
  black-bar track, the words SLIDE OR TAP TO START etched on the
  track face, GATE 0.68 (felt, never printed), CATCH 0.84 (the one
  commit coordinate), the tap-mesh and TAP AGAIN TO UNDO inside its
  window.
- **How selection works:** this is the app's only irreversible
  choice, and its presentation is a *weight*. The cap is the screen's
  one ember; the track is the darkest surface the app owns (1.01:1 on
  the ground — a well, not a panel). A slide is a drag against
  damping past the gate; a tap is a press that must be *meant* (140ms
  mesh to 0.92, then the assist). Both end at the same seat because
  there is exactly one commit in this app and it should feel like
  closing a vault door, whichever hand you use.
- **Decisions:**
  - **The words are cured.** Phase 1 shipped them painted in a
    hardcoded near-black constant (#17110C) on the black track —
    black on black, invisible in both modes (an inherited accident,
    §6). They now etch in the text-2 smoke tier (prototype, §8):
    an instruction is print, and print is legible or it is not
    printed. During a drag they still fade under the travelling cap
    (occlusion, R5) — that fade is chosen, and the mesh state never
    fades them.
  - The cap's marker (the 1dp line at its leading edge) stays the
    marker cream at 90% — on the ember it is the one pale mark, the
    fire's wick-line. KEPT, named.
  - The trail behind the cap (accent at alpha 55) prints only travel
    that happened (suppressed on the return, as built). KEPT — a
    trail that printed the journey home would be a lie in miniature.

### 1.11 The slide's other tenants — GRACE WINDOW ABORT · the valve
- **What:** the same 72dp address, other hours: for 10 seconds after
  the seal, GRACE WINDOW ABORT with its countdown badge; after that,
  EMERGENCY BREAK VALVE (60S HOLD) with the token badge `n/1 WEEK`.
- **Job:** the two lawful exits, at the address where the commit
  lived — the hand already knows where the door is.
- **Decision: KEEP both at the address (Δy = 0 is a mercy to a
  stressed hand).** Presentation: both chips are ink-primary (bone on
  black glass), never ember — an exit is not a commit, and the valve
  must not glow like one. The countdown badge and the token badge are
  facts (seconds left; the week's one token) and print in Anonymous
  Pro at text-1 on the chip. The 60-second hold's banking (15/30/45)
  is felt, not printed (Barriere's numbers; the register holds them)
  — the chip does not narrate the hold; the hand learns it.

### 1.12 The refusal sentence
- **What:** one reserved slot under the slide: BLOCKING IS OFF — TAP
  TO FIX, a jam's named link, an annulment's past-tense sentence —
  or silence (proving, running).
- **Job:** when the machine cannot do what it was asked, it says so
  in a sentence, in the same place, with the fix as the tap.
- **Decision: KEEP, and guard its silence.** The slot's emptiness is
  designed (a proving machine does not narrate itself). In obsidian
  the sentence prints in Atkinson bold at text-1 — it is the one
  sentence on the Deck and it should read like one. It never takes
  the alert tone: a refusal is a fact with a door, not an alarm.

### 1.13 The rail and PARK
- **What:** DECK · SHIELD · TASKS · LEDGER as a floating band, PARK
  at the end-detent (the settings/utility drawer).
- **Decision: KEEP the chrome as ruled.** In obsidian the band is a
  black-glass bar with the selected word on a bone cap (phase 1);
  unselected words at text-2. Navigation is silent (§5) and carries
  no ember anywhere — moving between rooms is not an act the machine
  celebrates. PARK's detent stays a notch, not a word: it is a
  drawer-pull, and it presents as one.

### 1.14 What the Deck does NOT present — named gaps
- **Which routine holds this session.** The receipt prints WITHIN
  from frozen stamps; the running Deck prints nothing — a man who
  wakes inside the SLEEP window's session, or glances during FASTED,
  gets no line saying which of his own blocks he is inside. The fact
  EXISTS (the session row's routine id + frozen window; the receipt
  assembles from exactly these). This is an addition, priced at §7-A1
  — not a gap I fill by invention here.
- **A routine that failed to fire.** Tonight the 20:00 SLEEP routine
  did not fire on his phone ("No it didn't", 20:21 IST). The Deck has
  no surface that could have said "your 20:00 did not start" — and
  inventing one is machinery (detection, a missed-window fact, a
  print law), not presentation. NAMED as the Deck's largest honesty
  hole and left to the no-fire audit (Voss) and Ren: if a
  missed-routine fact ever exists, this spec claims its presentation
  in advance — it belongs in the refusal sentence's slot, in
  Atkinson bold, past tense, with no colour: the machine confesses
  in the same chair it refuses in.
- **Tomorrow.** Nothing on the Deck speaks of the next block, the
  next routine, or the day. KEPT absent, deliberately: the Deck is
  the present instrument. The Drawer's months and Shield's routine
  column own time's other directions.

## 2. THE SHIELD — element by element

Shield is the armoury: where the day's defences are chosen before
they are needed. Its moments are **preparation** (evening, unhurried,
deciding what tomorrow's machine will hold) and **inspection**
(mid-session visits are rare and ratcheted — you may tighten, never
loosen). No ember lives here (§0): nothing on this screen is
irreversible, so nothing glows.

### 2.1 The ratchet banner
- **What:** a banner that exists only when a mid-session attempt to
  weaken the rules is refused, carrying the refusal's sentence.
- **Job:** the ratchet made visible at the moment it bites.
- **Decision: KEEP, and demote its dress.** It currently fills with
  the primary-container — in the old world an orange wash; in
  obsidian primary-container IS the ember token, and a refusal is
  not a commit (§0). In this world the banner is sheet glass with a
  bone sentence — the same chair as the Deck's refusal slot. One
  refusal grammar across the app: glass, bone, no heat.

### 2.2 The LOADOUT header — `n SHUT • n PEEK`
- **What:** the section word and the count of what the current
  loadout holds shut and what it lets peek.
- **Job:** before touching the selector, the cost of each loadout is
  already known in aggregate — the header is the selector's proof
  strip in miniature.
- **Decision: KEEP.** Counts in Anonymous Pro at text-1, the words at
  text-2 — numbers decide, words support. The counts re-derive from
  the app list on every change (as built); they are the selector's
  shadow and must never disagree with the rows below.

### 2.3 The loadout cap — selection III: THE THROW
- **What:** Sarthak's own pick (Oct 29, 10:16 — the sliding cap over
  the knob): a recessed track, three stops (STUDY / WORK / SLEEP),
  an ink cap that THROWS to the tapped stop on the 260ms spec-A
  curve, the selected word riding in the cap, stop ticks printed
  under the track.
- **How selection works:** tap a stop; the cap travels and seats.
  There is no drag on this control and that is CHOSEN, not missing:
  the loadout is a mode the whole armoury re-dresses for — a
  considered switch, thrown like a breaker, not a fader to fondle.
  The sleds below are the drag-and-seat grammar; the cap is the
  throw grammar; confusing them would blur which choices are heavy.
- **Decisions:** the cap in obsidian is bone (the control fill), the
  word its dark ink, the marker line **the cap's own ink** — my lane
  already replaced trunk's porcelain-cream marker hex (#FBF1EC) with
  bone-family print; the surviving rule is *the marker is printed in
  the cap's ink family, never a foreign cream* (§6). Unselected stop
  words print at the text-2 tier measured for the recess (the trunk
  comment carries Linea's 6.07:1 night measurement — that discipline
  is kept). Ticks: selected tick in bone, others hairline — position
  is the print; the tick does not need the ember.

### 2.4 The ROUTINES header — `n ACTIVE`, and the reset note
- **What:** the count of enabled routines; and, once, the reset
  note: SCHEDULE RESET TO YOUR DAY — TAP ANY ROW TO CHANGE IT.
- **Decision: KEEP both.** The note is a one-time confession that the
  machine changed his schedule — it prints until he touches a row,
  then never again (as built). In obsidian it prints at text-2, not
  the accent: a confession is not a commit. (His Oct 29 fury —
  "you cant even edit them" — was aimed at what the rows HID; §2.5
  answers it.)

### 2.5 A routine row — the day in one line, the editor in one sheet
- **What (as built):** collapsed, a row is a time column (start over
  → end, one axis for every routine, so the day reads as a column),
  the NAME in Sora, `DAYS · LOADOUT` in mono, and an ON/OFF cap.
  Tapped, it expands to ±30 steppers, a loadout cycle-chip, and day
  letter chips.
- **The verdict this design must answer:** his, verbatim — "even
  after opening u can only make it 30 minutes later or earlier."
  Voss's lane is curing the verbs (any minute, rename, create,
  delete, all persisted, all rescheduling). What follows is the
  PRESENTATION of those verbs, in this world:
  - **The collapsed row stays one calm line.** A routine you are not
    editing is a fact, not a form. Time column at text-1 (the times
    ARE the routine), name in Sora, days · loadout at text-2, the
    ON/OFF cap in the sled grammar (bone fill when on — a state, not
    an ember).
  - **Tapping the TIME opens the time, not a stepper.** The start
    time becomes the editor in place: the digits grow to the hero's
    dialect (Sora numerals, Anonymous units), settable to the minute
    by direct entry — type it, or turn a small black-glass minute
    wheel that obeys the Deck wheel's physics at a desk's scale.
    The ±30 steppers survive ONLY as fine-adjust keys flanking the
    digits (his words, via Ren's brief: steppers may live as
    fine-adjust). The end time edits the same way; a window that
    crosses midnight says so in words (`→ 03:30 · NEXT DAY` is
    already half-printed by the arrow column — the crossing is
    stated, never implied).
  - **The NAME is text and edits as text** — tap it in the open row,
    it becomes a field with an ink caret (the house caret law: ink,
    never ember).
  - **Days are seven key-caps** (the letter chips, kept) — selected
    days in ink fill, and the row's summary line re-prints DAILY /
    MON–FRI / the day list from the mask, as built. A mask of zero
    prints NO DAYS SET — an honest dead routine, never a silent one.
  - **DELETE is a held verb.** In a world where the emergency valve
    takes 60 seconds to turn, deleting a routine should at least
    take a press-and-hold on a sheet-glass key that fills as you
    hold (the valve's grammar at furniture scale) — a tap is too
    cheap for erasing part of his day. CONFIRM by hold, ~1.2s, with
    the row's name in the key so the hand knows what it is erasing.
  - **CREATE is a word at the column's foot:** `+ NEW ROUTINE` as a
    key-cap, which births a row open in the editor, named by him,
    timed by him — a seed is a starting point, never a cage (the
    reseed law's spirit, carried into presentation).
  - Machinery honesty: every verb above is Voss's car (his lane,
    running). This section spends NO machinery of its own; it is the
    dress his verbs will wear in this world, filed now so the dress
    is designed before the merge, not improvised after it.

### 2.6 ALL APPS — search and the filter words
- **What:** `SHOWING n`; a recessed search field ("Filter apps…" —
  sentence case, the input dialect, §0); the category words (ALL ·
  SHORT VIDEO · SOCIAL · GAMES · MESSAGING · WEB & NEWS) as a
  scrolling row of words, the selected one ink-bold over a 2dp ink
  bar.
- **How selection works:** a filter is a *word you stand on*, not a
  pill you press — no containers, no borders, position and weight
  carry the state (trunk's S2 ruling, kept whole). In obsidian the
  bar under the selected word is bone; the unselected words ride
  text-2 and never sink to muted: a category you cannot read is a
  filter you cannot choose.
- **Decision: KEEP whole.** The search field is a recess (the well
  tier) with an ink caret; SHOWING n re-counts as you type — the
  number is the filter's proof, and it prints at text-1 like every
  deciding number.

### 2.7 The app rows — selection IV: THE SLED
- **What:** each app a row: its name, and a three-stop sled —
  SHUT / 2m / OPEN — a cap that seats at the chosen stop.
- **How selection works:** the sled is the armoury's atom. Three
  stops because there are three truths about an app: it is held,
  it may be peeked at for two minutes, or it is free. The cap seats
  with a click the hand feels (the sled grammar the whole app
  speaks: selector, sleds, routine caps — one family).
- **Decisions:**
  - **SHUT wears the bone cap** (the control fill): shut is the
    armoury's resting state and it should look like the default the
    machine wants. **OPEN wears the raised glass** — freedom is the
    lighter, emptier stop. **2m wears the sheet glass between them**
    — and here my phase 1 had a filed flaw: by day the 2m cap filled
    with the secondary-container (#4A4038), a muddy brown slab beside
    the bone SHUT cap. CURED in the prototype (§8): the mid-stop is
    the sheet token in both modes (#38302A day / #241C16 night) —
    half-shut is a depth of glass, not a colour.
  - The word `2m` keeps its lowercase m (audited §6): it is a
    measure, not a state, and measures keep their case in this
    dialect.
  - A row's name is text-1; a row is 54dp — a thumb's height, no
    more. The list is long (every app he owns) and its presentation
    is deliberately boring: in an armoury, the racks are not the
    show.
  - Mid-session, the ratchet rules the sleds (tighten freely, loosen
    never). Presentation: a sled that refuses prints the refusal in
    the banner (§2.1) — the row itself does not shake, grey, or
    plead. The machine's no is a sentence, not a shiver.

### 2.8 BLOCKED WEBSITES — the list that is enforced
- **What:** `n SITES`; each domain a mono row with a REMOVE key;
  an add field and an ADD key.
- **Job:** the editor edits the list the blocker actually enforces
  (the chrome wave's transplant law — a MOVE, not a copy).
- **Decision: KEEP whole.** Domains in Anonymous Pro (they are
  addresses, and addresses are telemetry). REMOVE is a quiet
  key-cap, never ember — unblocking a site is loosening, and
  loosening never glows. ADD is ink-primary (adding a defence is the
  screen's one constructive verb) but still cold: bone, not fire.
  The placeholder names a real example domain (as built) — a prompt
  that teaches the format is worth more than "Enter URL".

### 2.9 What Shield does NOT present — named gaps
- **The Shorts & Reels sniper and the browser-URL blocker.** Their
  parameters ARRIVE at this screen (the composable receives both
  toggles) and are never rendered here — the controls live elsewhere
  (the suitcase views carry the sniper's switch) or nowhere a user
  can see on Shield. A screen that receives two switches and shows
  neither has a hole in its presentation the size of two features.
  NAMED, not filled: which room owns them is Ren's ruling, and the
  sniper toggle's persistence is a known open nick in the record —
  I do not dress a control whose fact is unproven. When they are
  homed, this spec claims their dress in advance: sled grammar,
  cold, words in caps.
- **Why a loadout holds what it holds.** The header counts SHUT and
  PEEK, but no surface says which apps a loadout changed when it was
  applied — applying STUDY re-dresses every sled at once, silently.

## 3. SELECTION AS A SYSTEM — four grammars, one law

Every choice in this app is made one of four ways, and the four are
never interchangeable:

| Grammar | Where | The act | Its weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| THE TURN | the wheel, the hub | drag/fling, tap the hub | setting — reversible until the slide |
| THE THROW | the loadout cap | tap a stop | a mode — heavy, instant, whole-screen |
| THE SLED | app rows, routine caps, day chips | tap/seat at a stop | a state per object — light, many, quiet |
| THE COMMIT | the slide, the valve hold, DONE's tear | drag past CATCH, or the tap-mesh; hold 60s | irreversible — the ember lives here, and only here |

**The law:** a selection's presentation must tell the hand its weight
BEFORE the act completes. Turns can be wandered back. Throws announce
themselves by travelling. Sleds seat with a click and can be re-seated
freely (outside the ratchet). Commits resist — damping, a gate you
feel, a hold you count — and they are the only selections that burn.
Any future control added to this app must declare its grammar in its
spec before it is drawn; a control in the wrong grammar is a lie
about its own weight.

## 5. FEEL — the sensory signature, moment by moment

*Charter v3: feel is designed, not emergent. Every sound below names
an existing engine voice (TactileSoundEngine) — no voice is invented
that the machine does not have. Every silence is a decision.
Haptics ride the same discipline: a knock is a fact's weight in the
hand.*

**The world's acoustic:** glass and latches. Short, low, dry sounds —
seat knocks at 300Hz, the rotary detent at 680Hz, one harmonic seal.
Nothing rings, nothing chimes, nothing sustains. In a silent house at
3:30 AM this app should sound like a well-made lock in the next room:
present, final, quiet.

### 5.1 Idle / rest (the Deck at rest, any hour)
- The room is still. No idle animation anywhere on the Deck; the
  hero does not breathe, the ember does not pulse. A machine that
  fidgets at rest cannot be trusted to be still during a session.
- The slide cap's lit top edge is the only standing light besides
  the type (§7-A3 proposes its slow dim after a minute's rest —
  until then it stands, chosen).
- Sound: none. Touch: the wheel turns with detent ticks — the only
  idle voice, and it speaks only when spoken to.

### 5.2 Arming (the press, the drag, the seal)
- **DOWN:** the imprint freezes (the facts latch). No sound — the
  machine listens first.
- **The drag:** damping past GATE 0.68 is felt as a haptic thickening
  — the gate is haptic-only by law, no colour changes, no sound. The
  words fade under the cap (occlusion). The trail prints behind the
  cap — travelled distance made visible, never on the return.
- **CATCH 0.84:** the seat spring takes the cap; the latch knock
  (14ms @ 0.85) is the sound of the decision becoming physical.
  (§7-A2 proposes the seat mark igniting at this coordinate.)
- **PROVING:** silence, by law and by design. The wire is being
  judged; the machine does not narrate its own examination. The
  refusal sentence slot stays empty. If a link fails: the JAM —
  one sentence, past the latch, naming the unanswering link. The
  jam's sound: none. A jam is a confession, and confessions are not
  scored.
- **t=0 — THE SEAL:** exactly one voice: playSealThrow through
  SessionSealTransition, the ONLY call site in the app, identical
  for a hand arm and a routine arm (Slice E's law; the golden hash
  stands). The harmonic seal (playHarmonicSeal) is its body. At the
  same instant the room performs the shot: the hero recedes (the
  vertigo — scale, rise, a breath of blur), the ground steps to
  night at t+400, the wheel freezes as its own ghost. The ember
  EXITS in printed steps (×0.75, ×0.50, gone — zero orange 200ms
  past t=0): the fire's job is done and it leaves the room.
- **SETTLE (t+1050):** the progress track takes the wheel's room,
  the utilities row arrives, input returns. The seal's whole
  ceremony is ~1 second — long enough to feel a door close, short
  enough to never delay a man who is already late to his desk.

### 5.3 The hold (a session running)
- **Silence is the design.** No ticks, no reminders, no progress
  sounds. The Deck is a gauge that speaks when read: the track, the
  eyebrow, the strip — all frozen at the seal's facts or honestly
  re-derived (TIME from the frame clock, never stored).
- **The break:** the eyebrow changes its word (BREAK · N MIN) —
  silently. A break that announces itself would spend the break.
- **HELD arrives:** nothing sounds. A captured notification is the
  machine doing its job quietly; the badge count changes on the chip
  and that is all. An app that pings you about what it protected
  you from is a dog that barks at the fence it built.
- **The desk clock (§4.4):** near-silence made visible — dim bone,
  slow drift, no sound ever. Opening it clicks (playFirmClick —
  a door's sound); closing it clicks. Between those two clicks, the
  night is undisturbed.

### 5.4 The finish
- **DONE's tear:** playTearPulse at the booking (which, under G-01's
  cure, IS the tap — sound and record now share an instant, and the
  feel finally matches the print). 260ms of Abriss motion: the card
  tears, drops 14dp, is gone. The tear is the finish's whole
  ceremony at task scale.
- **The session's end:** detention first (the machine holds the
  figures frozen while it makes sure — ≤1.8s of stillness that reads
  as deliberateness, not lag, because nothing on the screen moves),
  then the receipt: the sled goes home powered (650ms), the slip
  rises and SETTLES — it does not bounce, spin, or count up. Its
  numbers were true before it arrived.
- **playCompletionPulse** exists in the engine and is RESERVED (the
  register: verified fully-elapsed sessions only). In this world it
  sounds only for a session that ran its full span — the machine's
  one note of acknowledgment, earned, never spent on an aborted or
  broken finish. A shield-gap finish gets the slip and silence.
- **Dismissal:** the next DOWN anywhere, or navigation — and the
  tear-out (§7-A4). The slip does not linger for admiration.

### 5.5 The emergency (the valve, the grace)
- The grace chip counts down in print (10s badge) — silently. Ten
  seconds is not long enough to deserve a sound, and a countdown
  that ticks teaches panic.
- The valve's 60-second hold: the banks speak at 15/30/45 (the
  pitches fall 560→470→390Hz — Barriere's numbers) — three low
  confirmations that the hold is real, and the spend writes at 60.0
  in the same act as the disarm. The feel is a weight slowly
  accepted, not a button slowly filled. The screen does not
  narrate; the hand counts banks the way it counts wheel teeth.

### 5.6 Routine firing (the seal in an empty room)
- A routine arm at 05:00 or 20:00 sounds the SAME seal as a hand arm
  (Slice E: one voice, bit-identical) — heard from another room, he
  knows the machine kept its word without looking. That sameness is
  the feel signature of trust in this app.
- **Tonight it did not sound.** The 20:00 SLEEP firing failed on his
  phone; the audit is Voss's and the fix rides the gated build. This
  spec records the feel consequence plainly: every designed moment
  above is worthless at the one moment that matters most — the
  machine keeping an appointment nobody is watching. Presentation
  cannot cure it and does not pretend to; §1.14 claims the
  confession's chair in advance, if a missed-firing fact is ever
  built.
