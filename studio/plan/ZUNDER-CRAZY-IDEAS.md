## ZUNDER — CRAZY IDEAS for #16: the pill as an OBJECT, not a countdown label

Grounded in the cell's own reads: `PillController.kt` (Route A window, drag = raw 1:1 offsets, snap = one 140 ms fixed tween, X only, no clamping, no `VelocityTracker`), `PillFace.kt` (`PillDerivation.derive` — pure function of stored ends + `nowMono`; Absent / Session / Break / Peek; break outranks peek; ceiling-rounding on the peek fuse), `PillTheme.kt` (palette as data, one accent `#F47442`, cream figures, never green). Feel-1's verdict stands: **WEIGHTLESS — birth is a teleport, death is a cut, a flick and a nudge are indistinguishable.** These ideas give the pill a body. Laws honoured in every line: pure derivation stays pure (all physics lives in the controller/presentation layer, never in `derive`), one derivation two mouths (Route A window and Route B notification must never disagree on *facts* — only on *choreography*), no green, no AI images, blocker not tracker (nothing here scores, stores history, or grades a day).

### 1. THE PILL HAS MASS — and its mass IS the remaining time
Spring physics on release (replace the 140 ms tween): `VelocityTracker` during drag (the data Feel-1 showed is currently thrown away), fling velocity inherited, spring to edge with stiffness/damping from a real `SpringAnimation`-style integrator in the controller (~60 lines). The crazy part: **mass scales with the fuse.** A fresh 2:00 peek pill is light — flicky, quick settle (~300 ms, slight overshoot ≤4 dp). At 0:10 it is *heavy* — drag gets 8% lag (stored offset smoothing), settle is slow and overshoot-free, like moving something about to matter. The user feels the deadline in their thumb before they read a single figure. Spec: `massFactor = 1 + (1 - remaining/120) * 1.5` for Peek; Session face = constant 1.2; Break = 0.8 (a break should feel light). All in `PillController`, zero derivation change.

### 2. THE BURNING FUSE — the pill's body shrinks as the peek burns
Peek face only: pill width interpolates from 100% at lease start to 62% at expiry, driven by the *stored end* (`remainingSeconds / leaseDuration` from the lease row — derivation already has both numbers; expose `fractionRemaining` on `PillFace.Peek`). It never changes height, never changes the figures — the object literally gets consumed. At ≤10 s the shrink accelerates perceptually (ease the last stretch) and the border swaps to the accent `#F47442`. Death at 0 is then not a cut: the final 8 dp of width collapse over 120 ms *as* the lease dies — the pill is eaten by its own deadline. Route B can't shrink (notification), so Route B prints the same fraction as a row of 8 block glyphs `▮▮▮▮▮▯▯▯` in the peek face text — same fact, two mouths, each native to its surface.

### 3. SQUASH & STRETCH — a jelly moment on every snap and landing
On snap-landing: scaleX 1.06 / scaleY 0.94 for 90 ms, elastic back over 180 ms. On drag-start: a 3% stretch toward the drag direction. On edge-fling impact: squash proportional to impact velocity (cap 12%). This is the cheapest weight illusion in animation and the pill currently has *none* of it. Implement as a wrapper `View` scale in the controller's snap animator — ~25 lines, no new assets, no colour change. Combined with #1 the pill stops feeling like a cursor and starts feeling like a capsule of gel with something inside.

### 4. BIRTH AND DEATH ARE EVENTS — the pill arrives and leaves like an object
Birth: pill slides in from its docked edge over 220 ms with the #3 squash on arrival + a single soft haptic tick (10 ms) — you *feel* the lease start without looking. Death (lease end / ✕ hand-back): pill doesn't vanish — it **drops**: 140 ms fall with gravity easing + fade in the last 40 ms, like it lost its grip when its time ran out. Absent-gate deaths (keyguard, call, own-package — the derivation's suppression gates) are the exception: those stay instant cuts, because a pill animating out over the lockscreen would break the very law that suppresses it. `PillFace` transitions in the controller already know which change happened; key the choreography off old→new face pairs. ~50 lines.

### 5. THE SQUEEZE-TO-SURRENDER ✕ — ending a peek costs a deliberate squeeze
Today the ✕ is a tap. Make ending a peek early a **press-and-squeeze**: hold the ✕ (or long-press the pill body) and the pill visibly compresses over 600 ms — width −18%, figures dimming — and only at full compression does `recordPeekEnded` fire, with a haptic release click. Release early: it springs back, nothing recorded, no harm. This matches the app's soul (the 60-second emergency-break hold is already the house grammar for "this exit must cost intent") at pill scale: 600 ms, not 60 s, because a peek is a small door — but never a free accidental tap while dragging. Drag and squeeze must be disambiguated: squeeze only starts on a ≥250 ms stationary press (movement >6 dp Manhattan — the existing slop constant — cancels into drag).

### 6. TWO PILLS, ONE RACE — stop letting Break *hide* Peek; let them fight on screen
Derivation law says Break outranks Peek because printing a fuse on an open door is a false urgency. True for *one* pill. Wilder: when both a break and a live lease exist, the pill becomes a **split capsule** — left segment prints BREAK remaining, right segment prints the peeked app's remaining, joined by a hairline; the segments' widths are proportional to their remaining times, so the split point *moves* as the two clocks race, and whichever dies first expands to reclaim the whole pill with the #3 squash. No new facts invented — both numbers already exist in stored ends; `derive` gains one composite face `PillFace.BreakPeek(breakRemaining, peek)` used only when both are live, and Route B prints two lines (it has the room). The false-urgency law is preserved: the peek segment is rendered in the muted tier, the break segment in full figures — rank is shown by ink, not by hiding.

### 7. THE PILL REMEMBERS ITS PERCH — per-context homes, learned silently
The pill currently snaps to an edge and forgets. Give it **three remembered perches**: one Y-position per face class (Session / Break / Peek), persisted as three ints in the existing settings store. Your peek pill lives low (thumb-near while you're inside the peeked app), your session pill lives high (out of the reading line). Switching faces glides the pill to that face's perch over 200 ms — the face change becomes *visible as travel*, so a session→peek transition is felt, not just re-read. No settings UI, no tracker nonsense: it just remembers where you last left each one, because objects live somewhere. ~30 lines + 3 stored ints.

### 8. THE MAGNETIC DOCK & THE PEEK-A-BOO EDGE REST
At rest ≥5 s untouched, the pill **nests**: it slides 40% off its edge (only the inner 60% visible — figures always fully visible, only the tail tucks), alpha to 0.92, and sits there like something parked, not floating. Any face change (renderKey move), touch, or new lease pulls it fully out over 160 ms. This solves the real complaint about floating countdowns — they *hover over content forever* — by giving the pill a parked state with a silhouette you can still read at a glance. Hard rule: the tucked pill must never hide the figures or the ✕; tuck is tail-only, enforced by min-visible-width = figures width + ✕ + padding, measured at layout.

### 9. THE TICK YOU FEEL, NOT HEAR — haptic second-hand for the last 10 seconds
No sound (the pill must never chirp over a call or a quiet room — and Absent gates already say a human voice outranks it). Instead: at peek remaining = 10..1, one 8 ms haptic micro-tick per second *only if the pill is currently on screen and the phone is awake*; at 0, the drop (#4) plus one firmer 25 ms pulse. Session face: a single soft tick at each block boundary (blockIndex change in the renderKey — free to detect). Haptics are the pill's voice; figures are its face. Gate everything behind the same Absent conditions as rendering so it can never tick over the lockscreen or a call.

### 10. THE ENGRAVED INTENTION — the session pill carries his words like a locket
`PillFace.Session` already carries `intention` and mostly the face prints figures. Flip the hierarchy at rest: intention in full cream figures (it's *why* the block exists), time in the muted tier smaller beneath/beside — and on every block change, a 300 ms "turn of the locket": the pill does a quick scaleY flip (1→0 in 120 ms, swap face content, 0→1 in 120 ms) as if the capsule rolled over to show its other side. Intention never scrolls, never truncates mid-word (shrink type to a floor, then wrap to a second line and grow pill height — the pill is an object, it can be taller when it has more to say). This is the one idea here about meaning rather than physics: the pill should remind him what the block is *for* at the exact moment he's inside a blocked app staring at it.

### 11. IMPACT SCARS — no. Instead: THE CLEAN HAND-BACK RECEIPT
Rejecting my own first instinct (a pill that shows wear = tracker thinking, banned). Instead, the death of a peek does one beautiful thing: as it drops (#4), it leaves a **ghost outline** at its perch for 900 ms — an empty hairline capsule, figures gone — then the outline fades. A hand-back you can see having happened, with zero data stored, zero history kept. The ghost is pure presentation state in the controller (one delayed fade runnable), appears only for natural expiry and ✕ hand-backs, never for Absent-gate cuts.

### 12. THE PILL IN EVERY LOOK — palette is data, so make MOTION data too
`PillTheme` already holds the palette as data "so a look can reach it." Extend the holder with a `PillMotion` data object beside it: spring stiffness/damping multipliers, squash cap, birth/death choreography selector (DROP / FADE / SLIDE), nest tuck fraction. Each of the seven look-worlds then doesn't just redress the pill — it re-*bodies* it: one world's pill is machined and snappy (high stiffness, no overshoot, fade death), another's is heavy and gel-like. Cost: one data class + the controller reading constants from it instead of literals (~40 lines of plumbing). Without this, every look ships wearing the same *movement* — and movement is half of what an object is.

**My ranking if the cell takes only three:** #1 (Mass = time — the signature idea; the deadline becomes physical), #4 (Birth/death choreography — kills the teleport/cut verdict Feel-1 filed), #2 (Burning fuse — the peek's truth told by the body itself). #3 is nearly free and should ride along with any of them.
