# Fresh eyes — Fremd

I know nothing about this project. I was shown nine pictures: three designs, three screens each (a main screen in "day", the same screen in "night", and a settings screen). Everything below is what a stranger makes of them. Where I sound stupid, that's the point — if I can't decode it in five seconds, neither can a half-asleep person at 3:30 in the morning.

First, one thing nobody warned me about: all three designs are the same skeleton wearing different clothes. Same layout, same words, same giant dial, same orange/pink pill at the bottom. So a lot of my confusion is shared by all three, and I'll say it once instead of three times.

---

## 1. What each design is, in one sentence

**Obsidian (the dark brown one):** A vintage espresso machine crossed with a pilot's instrument panel — black, cream and one orange lever — the dial of an expensive old radio that happens to live on a phone. If a friend showed me this I'd say: "your timer looks like it costs money."

**Dawn (the pastel one):** A sunrise turned into an app — peach, cream and lavender, like a skincare ad or a gentle alarm-clock app for people who journal. If a friend showed me this I'd say: "that's the prettiest study app I've ever seen, does it also sell moisturiser?"

**Noir (the white/purple one):** A neon sign at night and a science poster by day — stark white with dark purple ink, and one slider the colour of a bar sign at 1 AM. If a friend showed me this I'd say: "your study app looks like it goes clubbing."

---

## 2. What confuses me

### Shared by all three (the skeleton)

- **"GUARD 1/4 — ACCESSIBILITY"** across the top of every screen. One of four *what*? Is something broken? Is this a warning, a score, or just the app's name for a settings page? It reads like an error that never goes away.
- **The dial.** Two rings. The outer ring has numbers — 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180 — minutes, I assume. The inner ring is eight dots, two lit, rest dark. Dots for what? Sessions? Days? Battery? No label anywhere. And the big display says **1H 00M** while the knob on the dial is sitting down near 25. If the knob sets the time, why isn't it pointing at 60? Am I supposed to drag it? Nothing says so.
- **"FLOW"**, a little chip under the dial. A button? A mode? It just sits there like a word the app knows and I don't.
- **"TOTAL"**, tiny, at the top. Total of what?
- **"BLOCK: OFF · APPS: 10 · TIME: ENDS 15:19."** The block is OFF, but the time *ends* at 15:19? Ends what, if nothing is running? And if the session is 1 hour (the big display), ending at 15:19 means it started at 14:19 — but it's off. These three facts fight each other.
- **"BIND NOT HELD"** and **"PROOF: POSITIVE"**, in tiny spaced-out capitals. I have read these six times. I believe they are English. I have no idea what they mean. Worse, "PROOF: POSITIVE" sounds reassuring while everything else on the screen says OFF — positive proof of *what*, exactly?
- **Three different things tell me the block isn't started**: "BLOCK: OFF", a slider that says start, and a big bold line "BLOCKING IS OFF — TAP TO FIX". So which do I touch? The bold line literally contains the instruction "TAP TO FIX" — if I'm groggy, that's the one I'm tapping. Is tapping it the same as starting? Is the slider decorative then? One screen, three doors, no sign on any of them.
- **"QUEUE 2"** next to the current chapter task. Two more tasks waiting? Two chapters? Probably, but I had to guess.
- **The bottom bar**: DECK, SHIELD, TASKS, LEDGER, and an icon that is just two vertical lines with no label. Deck? Ledger? This is a blocking app — why does it have a *ledger*? And the two-lines icon could be pause, could be a handle, could be anything.
- **"SHIELD" vs "GUARD"**: the tab is called Shield, the top of the screen says Guard. Two different words for protection in the same app. Are they different things? I still don't know after nine pictures.

### Obsidian specifically

- The slider is a big orange lozenge with a thin white line in it, and the words "SLIDE OR TAP TO START" next to it are printed in dark red-brown **on black** — functionally invisible. I can see a pill; I cannot see the instruction. Do I tap the pill? Drag it? Where does it go — the track next to it looks like empty black space with a faint outline.
- The "day" and "night" main screens are nearly identical. Both dark brown. If there's a day face, this isn't it — or the difference is so subtle (slightly brighter pill, cream tab) that it may as well not exist.
- Everything small — TOTAL, the strip labels, "BIND NOT HELD" — is dim grey-brown on dark brown. It looks expensive and it reads badly. Beauty you have to squint at.

### Dawn specifically

- The day main screen is a *cream card on a peach gradient*. At arm's length it reads as blank paper with a clock drawn on it. The dial's ring is beige on cream — the lowest-contrast dial of the three. Pretty from across the room, vague up close.
- The word "FLOW" is in a beige pill that looks greyed-out/disabled. Is it off? Is it broken?
- On the settings screen the whole background is a peach-to-lavender gradient, so the page feels like wallpaper; the "Filter apps…" box is pale yellow on peach and the placeholder text nearly vanishes. Several labels ("SHOWING 12", the category row) are light grey on pastel — lovely, faint.
- The night version is deep purple and much more legible — which is odd: the design named after morning works best after dark.

### Noir specifically

- In the day version, the dial is washed out — pale lavender-grey rings on white, and the inner dots are so faint I had to zoom to see them. The most important control on the screen is the hardest thing to see in this version.
- The slider is a magenta pill in a black bar, with a thin white vertical line near its right edge. Is that line the handle? The finish line? A decoration? In the night version the same pill is hot pink — the single loudest object in all nine pictures. It doesn't look like it belongs to the same family as the calm purple dial above it.
- The bright white day screen with giant purple type is striking, but next to its own night version it feels like a different app wearing the same layout.

### The settings screen (all three)

Mostly this one I *can* read — routines with times, apps with SHUT / 2m / OPEN. But:

- **What is "2m"?** Every app has three choices: SHUT, 2m, OPEN. The header says "7 SHUT • 3 PEEK" — so "2m" apparently means *peek*? Two minutes of peeking? Then why is it labelled with a duration and not the word? And YouTube has "2m" selected while the others are SHUT — did someone grant YouTube a daily two-minute look? I can guess all of this, but guessing is the problem.
- **"LOADOUT"** with STUDY / WORK / SLEEP under it. I get it — presets — but it's a gamer word in an app that otherwise talks like a laboratory ("PROOF: POSITIVE"). Pick a personality.
- The little tick marks under STUDY/WORK/SLEEP on the settings screen — pure decoration that looks like it should mean something (a slider? a position?). Under STUDY it's black, under the others faint. It took me a moment to realise it just underlines the selected one, twice (the pill is already dark).

## 3. 3:30 AM, dark, half-asleep, start a 3-hour block — which do I trust?

Understand what the moment actually is: eyes barely open, room dark, phone was across the room. I get *one* clumsy interaction before my brain files a complaint.

**I trust Noir's night screen with my thumb.** Reasons, in order: the background is properly dark — it won't flashlight my face. The big numbers are white on near-black — the highest contrast of the three night screens, readable through a squint. And the start pill is hot pink in a black bar: it is physically impossible to miss where my thumb is supposed to go, and the words "SLIDE OR TAP TO START" are printed in clear white. It looks like a sign lit up in a dark street, which is exactly what a half-asleep person needs — one lit thing, obvious.

**I would fumble Obsidian.** Not the starting — the orange pill is findable — but everything around it. The instruction text is invisible, so on my first morning I genuinely don't know whether to tap or drag. And I need *three hours*: the screen shows one hour, and changing it apparently means dragging a knob around a dim dial with grey numbers — in the dark, half asleep, that's a precision task, and the dial is lying to me anyway (knob near 25, display says 60). Obsidian is the design I'd love at noon and curse at 3:30.

**I would resent Dawn — specifically its day face.** If the app wakes up wearing that cream-and-peach face, it's a light bulb. A wall of pale brightness in a dark room at 3:30 AM is a small act of violence, and I'd resent it every single morning no matter how pretty it is at breakfast. Its night face (deep purple, soft pink slider, readable text) is actually gentle and fine — second place. But a design whose bright face is a sunrise has no business being opened before the sun is.

And one fumble lives in **all three**, because it's in the skeleton, not the skin: the loudest line on the screen is "BLOCKING IS OFF — TAP TO FIX". Half-asleep me taps *that*, not the slider, because it's the biggest, boldest instruction on the page. If tapping it doesn't start the block, the app has just punished the most natural tap a groggy person can make — on morning one, when habits form.

## 4. What is missing from ALL of them

**The app doesn't remember him.**

Every great daily tool I've ever used shows me *my own trail* the second I open it — the streak, yesterday's session, the graph creeping up, *something* that says: you've been here before, it counted, don't break the chain. These nine pictures show a beautiful machine in an empty room. No yesterday. No streak. No "day 41". And — this is the strange one — **no sign of the exam itself**: no date, no countdown, no "days left" for the thing this entire brutal schedule exists for. The most important number in this person's life appears nowhere on the screen he opens every morning.

Two smaller absences that are really the same absence:

- **There's no clock.** A tool opened at a sacred hour shows no current time, no "morning", no date — nothing that acknowledges *when* it is. The only time on screen is an end time for a session that isn't running.
- **Every picture shows the app OFF.** Nine pictures, nine off-states. The state he'll actually live inside — a block *running*, hours ticking down, this thing standing guard while he studies — is not shown by any of the three. I was asked to judge three designs and all of them showed me the lobby, none showed me the room he lives in.

---

## 5. The hundredth morning

**Obsidian.** Not close, for me.

By morning one hundred, novelty is dead — the only question is whether the thing feels like a *possession* or a *poster*. Noir is a poster: that magenta slider is a thrill on morning one and a shout by morning forty; neon dates fast, and I'd start opening it with a tiny flinch. Dawn is a mood — the pastel gradient is scented-candle pretty, and moods curdle; also I'd never forgive the bright face at 3:30. Obsidian is an object. It looks like an instrument you'd keep on a desk for a decade — dark, quiet, a bit severe, with one orange lever. I would stop *noticing* its beauty around morning twenty, the way you stop noticing a good watch — but I'd still be pleased it's mine. That's the difference between decoration and equipment.

Honest caveat: Obsidian wins the hundredth morning *despite* its screen, not because of it — its tiny dim labels and invisible slider text are real daily friction, and if those aren't fixed, its good looks are a coat of paint on the same curse at 3:30. If Noir turned its slider down from "bar sign" to "banked ember", it would steal this answer. And Dawn's purple night face is the runner-up — it's the only other one I can picture a tired person still liking in month four.

— Fremd, who owes this project nothing and hopes it stays that way
