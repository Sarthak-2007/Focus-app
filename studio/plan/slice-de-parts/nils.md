## 2. The tear's exact deltas, where it changes a joint

### 2.0 Withdrawn deltas (hosts killed by Live Wire)

- **Check-Drag retarget (invention §S2) — WITHDRAWN.** Check Drag and
  Proving Creep are dead (Ines §3: "there is no proving interval long
  enough to render"). There is no 6dp lag to retarget onto the front.
  Under the merged form, **front = displayed cap position, exactly, at
  every frame.** Simpler — and one of my invention's prettiest arguments
  ("paper straining against what hasn't been proved") is gone with its
  host. Recorded as a loss, not spun.
- **Park behaviour (invention §S4) — WITHDRAWN.** No Park exists. A
  parked cap tearing to its park position has no state to live in.

### 2.1 Commit 0.84 — the sacrificial bridge, exact geometry

- Travel 180dp ⇒ catch coordinate = 0.84 × 180 = **151.2dp** of travel.
- The bridge is a single dash **9dp wide centred on 151.2dp**: it spans
  **146.7–155.7dp**, i.e. progress **0.815–0.865**, ink alpha **0.32**
  (perforation field: 0.18). D4's notches, the bridge, and the catch are
  one fact at one x.
- **Grid re-phasing (a real builder delta my invention file glossed):**
  the filed perforation pitch is 3dp dash / 3dp gap = 6dp, and 151.2 is
  not on that grid from the origin (151.2 / 6 = 25.2). The grid is
  therefore **anchored at the bridge, not at the band's left end**: dashes
  are laid at 6dp pitch outward from the bridge's two edges (146.7 and
  155.7), and the terminal dash at each band end clips at the 8dp insets.
  The catch stays exact; the field absorbs the remainder at the edges,
  where no landmark lives.
- **Bridge exemption rule:** the bridge segment is exempt from per-dash
  extinction. As the front enters 0.815–0.84 the jagged edge stops at the
  bridge's leading edge (146.7) and strands strain across it (stretch
  allowance 14dp, the invention's damped-gap allowance, reused here);
  the bridge holds whole until the crossing frame. Otherwise the front
  would eat half the sacrificial segment before the sacrifice.
- **The snap (crossing frame, under contact):** void gap 0 → 3dp across
  the full 9dp in one frame; 4 strands spawn and snap within 60ms; the
  strip remainder beyond the catch (0.16 × 180 = 28.8dp of band) settles
  **1dp down over 90ms EaseOutQuad**; fibrous crack **90ms @ −18dB** at
  the snap frame. The powered seat then runs as filed.
- "Crossing 0.84" under the tear therefore means: *displayed progress
  crosses 0.84 under contact, the verdict at that frame is live, commit
  fires — and the last intact segment of paper parts in the same frame.*
  The legal event and the material event are one frame, neither leading.

### 2.2 Gate 0.68 — footnote only

Strand spawn pitch steps from 11dp (FREE) to 6dp (DAMPED) at the zone
boundary, and dash ticks (4ms @ 0.25, ≥35ms throttle) continue across it
unchanged. No colour change, no word, no haptic added to the filed 8ms @
0.5. If a reviewer rules any visual regime step at 0.68 a violation of
haptic-only, the pitch step moves to a continuous function of progress
(11dp → 6dp interpolated across 0.68–0.84) at zero cost — the law is
Fen's, the texture is mine, and the texture yields.

### 2.3 OFF-stop 0.65 — pose and aftermath

Front at the stop = 0.65 × 180 = **117.0dp torn, 63.0dp intact**, bridge
untouched at 146.7–155.7. The front can never overshoot the stop: front
is the high-water of *displayed* cap position, the Live Wire clamp
(0.65) binds displayed position, and the return spring only decreases
it. After the filed 300ms hold and universal-spring return, the re-sheet
(§2.10) runs. The merged form's slide is reusable the frame its spring
lands; the tear's slide is *also* reusable that frame (interrupt rule,
§2.10) — the difference is purely what an idle eye sees for the next
≤720ms.

### 2.4 Tap path — coexistence, with the tear on probation

- **State machine: coexists, no delta.** Front is a function of displayed
  cap position, not of gesture source. The mesh (140ms to 0.92) and the
  assist spring (110ms) drive displayed position; the front follows
  frame by frame; the bridge snaps on the frame displayed progress
  crosses 0.84 *during the mesh* — which is **not** a commit (no contact;
  legal commit on the tap path is the seat = onLatch, exactly as merged).
- **Rendering: changed, because physics.** Mesh peak velocity ≈
  (0.92 × 180dp) / 140ms ≈ **1180dp/s**. At that speed the invention's
  strand system (spawn, stretch to 12dp, snap) degenerates into flicker.
  Spec for the tap tear, replacing invention §S4's strand language:
  **strands suppressed during driven travel** (spawn rate 0 while the
  mesh/spring drives); jagged edge draws clean behind the front; dash
  ticks coalesce under the existing 35ms throttle (≈7 ticks across
  250ms — a rattle, not a buzz); the crack is replaced by one **180ms
  rip burst**, envelope = mesh velocity profile, ceiling **−20dB**.
- **Brush (<90ms): invariant** — cap never moves, front never moves,
  nothing tears.
- **Second-touch abort during the mesh: see §2.10(b)** — the one place
  a torn bridge meets no commit, and it is handled, not hidden.

### 2.5 Strip interplay — firewall and names

- Rosa's standing strip: 56dp, 8dp above the track, BLOCK/APPS/TIME,
  heartbeat-driven under the Live Wire form. Tear band: 28dp, centred
  on the track's vertical centre, *inside* the slide box. **No dp of
  either moves; the 796dp fixed sum and Kato's room math stand.**
- **State firewall (one fact, one rendering):** the tear reads *nothing*
  from the readiness store — the only readiness input to the slide is
  Live Wire's boolean travel limit, exactly as in the merged slide — and
  writes nothing back. The front must never encode verdict, readiness,
  or elapsed anything. It encodes hand travel, full stop. The moment a
  builder makes the front lag, stall, or tint on verdict state, the tear
  has become a second rendering of BLOCK and it dies under the studio's
  own law (this is a named kill condition, §5).
- **Names:** in code and docs the two objects must split — `proofStrip`
  (Rosa's) vs `tearBand` / `TearStripState` (mine). One shared word for
  a proof instrument and a consumed material is how a future builder
  wires the wrong state into the wrong strip.
- **D8 skirt:** the tear still asks for the slide box's 2dp recess skirt
  to die under the band (a laid strip is not milled into a slot), with
  the invention's printed fallback standing: skirt survives above the
  band only, band keeps its 1px deckled fibre edge. Sana's joint; priced
  at ~10 lines either way.

### 2.6 Refusal rendering — intact bridge vs torn bridge

- **Wire dead (the merged refusal):** stop 0.65 (§2.3), Suki's sentence
  in the reserved slot, dull knock, hold, return — then re-sheet.
  Sequence law, unchanged from my invention: **refusal first, in words,
  at the thumb; replacement second, silently, after the cap is home.**
  Dashes never re-ink over a tear at any frame.
- **Torn bridge + refusal: does not exist in the merged form, and the
  tear keeps it that way.** Bridge snaps only on a crossing frame whose
  verdict is live; that crossing *is* the commit. Mid-drag wire death
  (Ines §3: registry clears, limit collapses on the next MOVE frame)
  can strand the front anywhere below 0.84 — bridge intact — and the
  tie-break is stated here so no builder invents another: **the verdict
  sampled at the crossing frame's start governs that frame**; a
  same-frame death does not annul a commit already fired.
- The one surviving torn-bridge-without-a-session case is therefore not
  a refusal at all: commit fired, post-latch proof failed (a machine
  fault, post-seat). Its rendering is the fault path's (sentence +
  return), and the fully torn strip is discarded by the re-sheet after
  the cap returns. That picture is *truthful*: the hand committed, the
  machine failed — paper records the intent, the sentence records the
  outcome, one fact each.

### 2.7 The seat — what seats when the paper is gone

Nothing changes, and the brief's question dissolves on inspection: the
seat spring (400/16, 110ms) drives the **cap** into the latch; the latch
knock (14ms @ 0.85 at 0.97) seats the **pawl's** expectation; the strip
was never a seatable part — it is the thing the cap was holding. At the
seat frame the strip exists as two pieces: the torn field behind the
cap, and the 28.8dp remainder lying 1dp down (settled at snap +90ms,
20ms before the seat lands). "Nothing left to seat" is true of the
paper and irrelevant to the spring. **Spring, dwell, onLatch: filed
numbers, untouched.**

### 2.8 t=0 — separation, priced in ms

Crack (snap frame) → seat (snap +110ms) → t=0 (≥ latch +210ms). Minimum
crack-to-t=0 gap = **320ms**. The seal cadence owns t=0 alone; the
eyes-closed indistinguishability test (Orson's two entries) is
unaffected because the crack is never inside the cadence window. The
fully torn strip remains visible in the slide box through the proving
pose — a torn strip over an unproven seal is the honest picture — and
exits with the slide territory in the filed Vertigo transition, adding
no choreography, no running-state presence, no receipt glyph, no count.

### 2.9 The ending — the cross-slice delta (Fen's joint)

Cap: receipt +1000ms, powered return 650ms, silent, interruptible —
Fen's numbers, kept entire. Strip: at cap arrival, re-sheet feed-in
**380ms EaseOutCubic** from the left inset, dashes printing only where
paper has arrived, ending tap 50ms + tick 6ms @ 0.35. **Total ending
1030ms post-receipt vs merged 650ms** — all post-session, none on any
arm path. Fen's intent ("rest state true again without his hand
tidying it") survives; his claim ("true again" = "as if untouched")
does not. This is the tear's one true displacement and it sits in
Slice E. If Fen's adjudication rejects the replacement ending, the tear
shrinks to a **reduced variant**: drag+tap rendering only, merged
ending, re-sheet retained *solely* for abort/refusal (where the merged
form has no ending to displace — there, replacement is the only honest
option a torn strip has). Price delta of the reduction: −65 lines (§4).

### 2.10 The abort — answered in the spec, not around it

**"What does an aborted drag at 0.90 mean when the bridge is already
torn?"** Three cases, because the merged law makes them three different
objects:

**(a) Drag path, release at 0.90: not an abort.** Crossing 0.84 under
contact committed at the crossing frame. Release changes nothing; the
powered seat runs. There is no gesture in the merged form — and none in
the tear — that un-commits a contacted crossing. Anyone who builds a
"drag abort past 0.84" has built a lie in both forms equally.

**(b) Tap path, second touch during the mesh at displayed 0.90: a real
abort with a torn bridge.** The mesh crossing was not under contact, so
nothing committed; the buttonhole abort (250ms spring-back) stands as
filed. The bridge is torn and no session exists. The tear's answer:
**the strip is spent and is replaced — the abort costs one strip,
exactly as tearing a ticket you then don't use costs the ticket.** On
cap reaching rest, the re-sheet runs (not optional on this path: a
torn bridge left lying in the track would render "committed" on an
uncommitted Deck — a false frame). No session, no count, no record of
the abort anywhere (Ines's annulment discipline: a non-commit renders
nowhere). Cost to the next arm: **0ms** (interrupt rule below).

**(c) Drag path, release at 0.83: the near case.** Front at 0.83 sits
inside the bridge's span (0.815–0.865) but short of its centre; per the
exemption rule (§2.1) the bridge is intact, strained strands across it.
Cap springs home; re-sheet discards a strip torn to the bridge's edge.
The bridge's 9dp width is what makes 0.83 and 0.84 different *materials*
and not just different numbers — under the merged form alone they are
different only in consequence.

**The re-sheet (one procedure; callers: abort (b)/(c), refusal §2.6,
session end §2.9):** cap must be at rest → torn band exits downward
+28dp over **220ms EaseInQuad**, fading to recess tone, jagged to its
last frame → **120ms bare track** (non-negotiable; it is the frame that
proves replacement happened) → fresh strip feeds in **380ms
EaseOutCubic** (feed-in spec §2.9). Total **720ms**, always post-gesture
or post-session. **Interrupt rule:** any ACTION_DOWN on the cap at any
point completes the re-sheet in one frame; bare or partial strip is
never rendered under a live press; worst-case added latency on any arm
= **0ms**.

## 5. Risks and kill conditions

**Pre-registered kill condition — THE CAP-CROP TEST (mine, binding):**
on device renders at front = 0.30 / 0.55 / 0.80 with the cap cropped
out, if a viewer can estimate the front's position to within ±10% of
travel from the tear alone, the tear is a progress bar with noise — a
second rendering of cap position — and **it dies, untuned, unappealed.**
The mitigations (seeded per-attempt jag, no numerics, strand noise) are
already in the spec; if the read survives them, no further mitigation
round is authorised by this file.

**Standing kill conditions (from my invention §8, carried verbatim in
force):**

1. **The healing implementation.** Any build that re-inks dashes in
   place on abort/refusal instead of running the full re-sheet (exit →
   120ms bare track → feed) asserts un-tearing. That build is worse
   than the merged slide and I disown it. Audit point: the bare-track
   beat's presence in a screen recording.
2. **Tap flicker.** If the mesh-speed tear, *with strands already
   suppressed* (§2.4), still renders as a zipper flicker that reads
   cheaper than the drag tear, the tap tear is cut to bridge-only
   (strip intact through the mesh, tear in the snap frame at seat).
   One honest path + one plain path beats two paper paths, one lying
   about speed.
3. **Frame cost.** Strands/jag capped hard (12 / ~72 vertices). If the
   slide's frame time on the target device rises measurably against
   the merged slide in the same build (bar: >1ms p95 added), strands
   die first, vertex density halves second, the whole tear third —
   the wheel's physics never pays for paper.
4. **Sound collision.** If the rip or crack is confusable with the
   seal's cadence in the eyes-closed test at any gain, the rip is cut
   to haptics-only. The seal owns the terminal sound of this gesture.
5. **Verdict leak (new, from §2.5).** If any shipped frame lets the
   front encode readiness/verdict state, the tear has broken
   one-fact-one-rendering and dies regardless of beauty.

**Other risks, plainly:**

- **Author bias is the meta-risk.** Every number above was estimated by
  the tear's inventor. The line estimates are checkable; the *feeling*
  estimates are not — which is precisely why §6 does not recommend
  adopting on this file's word.
- **Two-objects-one-word** (§2.5) will cause at least one wiring mistake
  if the naming split is skipped. Cheap to prevent, expensive to debug.
- **Re-sheet fatigue:** on a morning of three aborted drags, he sees
  three replacement cycles in peripheral vision. All are post-gesture
  and interruptible, but "paper being changed" three times before 05:00
  may read as fuss — the merged form's silent spring has no such cost.
  Unmeasurable from this desk; belongs in the gate's device trial.
- **The 68dp trap:** if the merged box does not actually land at 72dp,
  band/label crowding (§3) squeezes the band to 24dp and the tear's
  presence thins toward the progress-bar failure mode. The gate renders
  must be shot in the real merged box, not in my invention's frame.

## 6. THE FORK — for Ren

**The question:** Slice D's slide ships as (A) the merged Live Wire
form, (B) the Tear-Seal form, or (C) the merged form by default with
the tear built as a priced variant behind a named gate.

**My recommendation: C — behind THE CAP-CROP GATE.**

The gate, named and pre-registered: four device checks on the real
merged box, all pass/fail, no taste votes — (1) the cap-crop test (§5);
(2) cruise stillness: at rest, screenshot diff across 60s is empty
(front static, zero live strands); (3) eyes-closed: crack and rip never
confusable with the seal cadence; (4) mesh legibility: tap tear at full
mesh speed, strands suppressed, does not read as flicker (else §5.2's
bridge-only fallback engages automatically, not by re-vote). **Plus one
studio ruling, not a device check:** Fen's verdict on the §2.9 ending —
replacement ending in, or the reduced variant (−65 lines) is what the
gate tests.

Rules of the variant: merged form is the default and the shipping
build; the tear is a flag-selected rendering (`tearBand` path in the
one slide view — no second view class); **sunset: if the gate has not
been run by the Slice E freeze, the variant code is deleted, not
carried.** A gate nobody runs is a second slide forever, and the
standing dual-maintenance tax (§4) is the variant's true price.

Why not B (my own form, adopted): its entire value claim is a *felt*
claim — that a commitment the hand can feel happening retains him at
03:30 on the two-hundredth morning — and felt claims are settled on
devices, not in adjudication files, least of all the inventor's. Six of
eight joints being invariant proves the tear is *safe* to test; it does
not prove it is *worth* 500 lines. Why not A alone: the merged slide is
a good slide that commits irreversibly in law and reversibly in
picture — the thumb springs home on today's code and drives home on
the merged code, and the Deck at rest after an arm looks untouched.
The one truth the tear tells that no other Slice D element tells is
that the act *cost something material*. That is worth one gated trial,
priced and sunsetted — not worth blocking a build-ready merge, and not
worth discarding untested because its author was told to be honest.

**The honest anti-case against the tear, at full strength (again,
because a fork filing that buries it is advocacy):** HOLD is already
absolute; the seal already does not walk back. The tear may merely
*illustrate* a commitment the law guarantees — 500 lines and 3 days
decorating a truth, on the app's single most-used surface, with a
replacement animation he must watch the edges of every aborted morning
for the rest of the app's life. If the gate's renders do not make a
viewer *feel* the difference in the first three seconds, that paragraph
is simply correct, and A ships alone with my signature on the kill.
