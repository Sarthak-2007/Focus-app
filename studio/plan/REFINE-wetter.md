## 0. The organ inventory — what each world actually owns

An organ is not a colour. It is a working device — a mechanism of
attention, information, selection or feeling — that a world has grown
and the others have not. Six worlds, six toolkits:

- **OBSIDIAN** owns **the Ember Law** (the accent is spent exactly once
  per screen; on Shield, zero times), **the Monument** (hero numerals
  at 88sp — type as carved mass, not label), and a glass ground that is
  the same object day and night, only lit differently.
- **SIGNAL** owns **the Red Square** (a small registration mark before
  every section header — wayfinding by pre-attentive colour-spot),
  **flat field furniture** (the proof strip as three flat colour
  blocks; nothing casts a shadow anywhere), and **the anaglyph fringe**
  (red/blue misregistration on the hero — a print error worn as energy).
- **DAWN** owns **the Living Sky** (the ground itself is a full-height
  dawn gradient — the screen tells you what part of the day you are in
  before you read a single number), **translucency as depth** (surfaces
  are glass laid over light; nothing is opaque, nothing casts shadow —
  depth is permeability), and **the Rose Fence** (one hard colour,
  fenced to the single touch that starts).
- **NOIR** owns **signage as the only light source** (the ground emits
  nothing; every lit thing is a sign), **the bloom ghost** (type glows
  with a magenta halo — light behaving like gas, not ink), **the Amber
  Lamp** (a danger colour that works precisely because it is the only
  warm light that isn't magenta), and **the power-cut day** (day state
  is the same city with the power off — flat, graphic, unlit).
- **TEMPLE** owns **carved depth** (elevation is stone-work: recesses
  are darker carved shadow, raised faces are polished slab — never
  shadow, never glow), **the engraved register** (bronze-ink text,
  brass hairlines, one gold-leaf accent under a written spend rule),
  **vermilion** (a temple pigment reserved for alert), and **the
  lamplight inversion** (at night the ink itself becomes the light —
  parchment on bronze — while the metal never changes).
- **BLUEPRINT** owns **the Commit Plate** (commit has exactly ONE
  visual form — a solid indigo plate, nothing else; every committing
  control in the app wears it), **line-weight hierarchy** (all
  information ranked by value steps and weight of a single ink, like a
  drawing's title block), **hairlines as structure** (rules that carry
  measurement, not decoration), and **Annotation Red** (red is the
  draughtsman's correction pencil — it means "this needs your hand,"
  never decoration, never brand).

## 1. The fifteen transplants

One organ per pair, argued. Each names its technique and its machinery
cost honestly (token = a colour/constant change; view = drawing work in
existing views; state = new plumbing from existing data; machinery =
new behaviour — none of these fifteen needs machinery, and where one
nearly does, it says so). Verdicts: TRANSFORMS (the host gains a sense
it lacked), ADAPTS (the organ survives only in the host's native
material — which is usually the more interesting outcome), KILLS (the
obvious candidate, named and refused).

### 1.1 Obsidian × Signal — flat-field furniture into the glass

**Signal's flat proof strip → Obsidian (ADAPTS).** Obsidian's proof
strip is three tonal whispers on black — the same near-invisibility its
own designer flagged in the slide hint. Signal's strip is furniture:
three flat blocks you could read across a room. Transplant the
*furniture*, not the colours: Obsidian's three proof cells become cut
slabs — smoked glass with hard sawn edges and a single polished top
edge, separated by black gaps, no tonal blending between cells. The
information gains Signal's certainty; the material stays quarried
glass. Technique: replace the strip's tonal steps with three discrete
fills (two glass steps + true black) and a 1dp bone edge on the top of
each cell only. Cost: view (one band's drawing), no state. The reverse
candidate — the Ember Law into Signal — **KILLS**: Signal's red is a
repeated wayfinding system (squares, fringe, cap); fencing it to one
spend per screen doesn't refine the poster, it deletes its grammar.

### 1.2 Obsidian × Dawn — the fireplace that knows the hour

**Dawn's Living Sky → Obsidian (TRANSFORMS).** Obsidian's black is
static — the same true black at 05:00 and at 19:00, though the man
lives by a routine clock and the app already knows which block he is
in. Dawn's organ is not gradient; it is *the ground knows the hour*.
In Obsidian's grammar the sky is a fire: the ember temperature inside
the glass gradient tracks the routine phase — banked to near-ash in
SLEEP, low coal at FASTED, the one full burn reserved for the slide
cap as now. He would feel the day turn without reading anything; the
Deck becomes a fireplace that keeps his hours. Technique: lerp the
hero gradient's warm stop and the proof strip's ember tint between
three priced states keyed to the existing routine phase (the state
already exists — routines reseed shipped in build 2; this reads it,
it does not build it). Cost: token + state (read-only), no machinery.
Identity: safe — nothing new is added to the screen; the one ember he
already owns learns the time. The reverse (Obsidian's zero-accent
Shield into Dawn) **KILLS** — Dawn's Shield *is* its sky; fencing its
colour off the settings screen leaves a window with nothing outside it.

### 1.3 Obsidian × Noir — light answers the lock

**Noir's seal flare → Obsidian (TRANSFORMS).** Noir's own NEXT letter
names the organ: at the seal, the bloom should flare once — light
answering the lock. Obsidian has the better fuel for it: the single
ember. Transplant the *gesture*: at t=0 of the proving cascade, the
slide track's ember ignites — a radial bloom of #FF6A2A blooming out of
the cap across the glass gradient, peaking as the seal sound lands,
then decaying to banked over ~900ms. One flare per arm, never looping,
never anywhere else — the Ember Law is not broken, it is *fulfilled*:
the one spend happens at the one moment that matters. Technique: one
radial-gradient overlay on the hero, alpha/radius animated on the
existing seal path; the cascade already has a t=0 (build 6) — this
hangs a picture on it. Cost: view + the seal hook that already exists.
No new sound, no new state. Identity: safe, and it answers Obsidian's
own filed wish ("the sealed states in obsidian dress — the seal moment
is where this app's soul shows") with Noir's match.

### 1.4 Obsidian × Temple — carve the glass

**Temple's carved depth → Obsidian (TRANSFORMS).** Obsidian calls
itself "carved, not drawn," then builds its Deck from layered cards
with hairline borders — drawing wearing carving's clothes. Temple owns
the real technique: depth as *work in the material* — recess darker,
raised face polished. Transplant: the proof strip and the block bar
sink INTO the glass — recessed channels, each with one inner highlight
on its top inner edge (the cut catching light) and its floor a step
closer to true black; the hero plate stays the one polished face.
The Deck stops being a stack and becomes a single worked slab — which
is what "monolith" has always claimed to be. Technique: inset shading
in the core views (top-edge 1dp bone at ~40% alpha + floor fill one
staircase step down — Noir's letter proves the staircase family exists
and must be mapped anyway). Cost: view only. Identity: safe — this is
Obsidian's own metaphor made honest. (The pair's second vein, noted
not taken: Obsidian's Monument into Temple — an 88sp engraved hero
turns Temple's Deck into a stele. Temple should steal it himself if
his render's hero feels like a label.)

### 1.5 Obsidian × Blueprint — one material for every commit

**Blueprint's Commit Plate → Obsidian (ADAPTS).** Look at Obsidian's
own Shield render: the committing things disagree about what
"committed" looks like — the slide cap is ember, DONE is a bare
outline, the ON pills are frosted bone, the selected sled is a
mid-brown slab its own designer called "slightly muddy." Blueprint's
law cuts this knot: *commit wears exactly one material, everywhere.*
Obsidian cannot spend ember on every commit (that breaks the Ember
Law, §1.1), so the organ adapts into bone: every committing control —
DONE, ON, the armed loadout — is polished bone glass (bright, raised,
polished top edge, per §1.4's carve); every non-committing control is
frosted. A user learns it in a day: polished = live, frosted = offer.
The muddy sled dies as a side effect — it becomes polished, because it
is a commit. Technique: unify three views' selected/committed fills to
one token pair (polished/frosted). Cost: token + view, no state.
Identity: safe — the ember stays singular, the grammar gets a verb.

### 1.6 Signal × Dawn — overprint, print's own translucency

**Dawn's translucency → Signal (ADAPTS).** The naive transplant
(glass blur on a poster) is dead on arrival — posters are opaque.
But Dawn's organ, stated abstractly, is *surfaces let what's beneath
them change their colour* — and print owns exactly that device:
**overprint**. Where Signal's flat fields cross — the selected sled
riding its track, the fringe on the hero — the crossing should print
as a third colour (red overprinted on the slate blue yields the deep
violet already hiding in Signal's fringe), instead of the top shape
simply covering the bottom. Signal already flirts with this in its
anaglyph hero; making overprint the system's depth law gives the
poster depth with zero shadows, zero gradients, zero betrayal.
Technique: multiply/overprint blending on the handful of overlapping
draws (sled/track, hero fringe, selected states). Cost: view (canvas
blend modes), no new tokens — the third colour is *derived*, never
specified. Identity: strengthens — overprint is not a metaphor borrowed
from glass; it is what ink actually does.

### 1.7 Signal × Noir — the one yellow panel

**Noir's Amber Lamp → Signal (TRANSFORMS).** Signal's palette has a
hole in it: red does two jobs — it is the brand (squares, fringe) and
it would have to be the alarm. But in Signal's renders the most
important sentence in the app, "BLOCKING IS OFF — TAP TO FIX," prints
in the same black as everything else. Noir's organ solves it: *danger
gets a hue used nowhere else in the world.* Transplant: Signal adopts
one ochre-yellow field, reserved by law for the guard-lack sentence
and nothing else, ever — the panel turns yellow, the sentence stays
black on it. A single yellow panel in a red/blue/black poster is the
loudest object Swiss print ever invented, and it costs the system
nothing because it appears only when something is wrong. Technique:
one token + a spend rule in the census. Cost: token only. Identity:
safe — Swiss poster grammar historically keeps exactly this one signal
colour in reserve. (Convergence noted, not a transplant: Noir's
power-cut day and Signal's flat day are cousins; neither needs the
other's.)

### 1.8 Signal × Temple — the seal stamp

**Signal's Red Square → Temple (ADAPTS).** Temple's Shield, when it
lands, will head its sections in bronze ink and rely on reading.
Signal's square is a better device: a colour-spot the eye catches
before it reads — you navigate by marks, then confirm by words.
Temple's grammar already owns the native form of this mark: the
**seal**. Transplant: each section header on Temple's Shield is
preceded by a small vermilion *outline* seal — a square stamp, bronze-
ruled, unfilled — the section is stamped, not labelled, like entries
in a temple register. The dilution risk is real and is fenced in the
design: vermilion FILLED stays alert-only (the pigment at full body
means danger); the seal is an outline — a stamp's ghost, authentication
rather than alarm. Technique: one glyph drawn before section headers
(a stroked square + the existing hairline system). Cost: view, trivial.
Identity: transforms — a list becomes an inscribed register, and the
register is what a temple keeps. (From Temple's token file: his
grammar is written with unusual discipline — every token carries a
named job. The seal gives that discipline a face.)

### 1.9 Signal × Blueprint — three jobs, three colours

**Blueprint's Commit Plate → Signal (TRANSFORMS).** Signal drags its
brand colour: the slide cap — the one thing you pull to commit — is
red, the same red as the wayfinding squares and the fringe. Blueprint's
law separates the jobs absolutely: commit has one form and it is never
the annotation colour. Transplant the law: in Signal, every commit
wears the black plate (slide cap, DONE, ON — most already do); red is
demoted to pure mark-making (squares, fringe, the one yellow's
counterpoint in §1.7 is separate); the deep blue stays what it already
is — the colour of *chosen* (the selected sled). Three jobs, three
colours, learnable in a week: red marks, black commits, blue chooses.
The poster gets more poster by subtraction. Technique: cap fill red →
black plate; the cap keeps a red *edge tick* (registration mark) so the
hand still finds it — the mark survives, the job changes. Cost: token.
Identity: safe. The reverse candidate — Signal's fringe into
Blueprint — **KILLS**: misregistration on a draughtsman's plate reads
as an error on the one document whose entire claim is precision; the
plate must be exact or it is nothing.

### 1.10 Dawn × Noir — the power cut, for glass

**Noir's power-cut day → Dawn (TRANSFORMS, with a named risk).**
Dawn's filed risk is its bright Deck: a big pale translucent panel
whose glass reading "comes from translucency over the gradient, not
from dissolving the panel" — in bright light it risks reading as a
washed card, the one blandness charge pending against any finalist.
Noir owns the cure and it is a *principle*, not a colour: a day state
is not a lighter night — it is the same world with the power off,
rendered flat and graphic. Transplant: Dawn's bright instrument goes
flat — kill the translucency gradient inside the hero plate by day;
the dial prints as flat pale shapes with plum line-work, a diagram of
the glass, while the sky around it stays alive. True glass-glow is
reserved for the dark state, where it is Dawn's crown. The risk,
named: Dawn's identity is light through glass, and a flat day dial
could read as surrender. The fence: the SKY never goes flat — only the
instrument does, and an instrument drawn flat at noon and glowing at
3:30 AM is a true thing (that is what instruments do). Technique:
bright-scheme hero redrawn with flat fills + line work (the drawing
exists — it is the night dial with its glow unplugged). Cost: view.
The reverse — Dawn's living sky into Noir — **KILLS**: a gradient sky
behind the city turns wet black into dusk; Noir's ground must emit
nothing or the signs have nothing to burn against.

### 1.11 Dawn × Temple — frost is glass's carving

**Temple's carved depth → Dawn (ADAPTS).** Dawn has exactly one depth
device (translucency) at exactly one strength — hero, strip and pills
are all the same frost, so nothing on the screen is nearer or farther
than anything else. Temple's organ translates word-for-word into
glass: *recess = more frost, raise = clearer glass.* Transplant a
two-step frost scale: the proof strip sinks into deep frost (blurrier,
dimmer, a step toward the sky's own colour), the hero plate is the
clearest glass on the screen (sharpest, brightest), pills sit between.
The eye learns the topography without a single shadow. Technique: two
additional frost levels in DawnGlass.kt (blur/alpha steps — the file
exists and is the world's only new brush). Cost: view, small. Identity:
safe — frost is not borrowed from stone; it is what glass does when
you work it. The reverse (Dawn's sky into Temple) **KILLS** — stone
must be lit, never backlit by weather; a gradient behind the carving
turns a temple into a diorama.

### 1.12 Dawn × Blueprint — etch the small print

**Blueprint's line hierarchy → Dawn (TRANSFORMS).** Dawn's Shield
floats its type on the sky — gorgeous at headline size, but its small
print (proof labels, routine sub-lines, filter words) drifts in plum
at assorted alphas: soft where it should be exact. Blueprint ranks
information the way a drawing does — one ink, priced value steps,
hairline rules that carry the structure. Transplant the discipline,
translate the material: on Dawn, the rules are *etched* — hairlines
scored into the frost under each Shield section, and all secondary
text steps to ONE fixed softer plum (a token, not an alpha habit),
with figures set in Anonymous Pro and aligned like dimensions on a
drawing. The Shield stops floating and starts registering — etched
glass is still glass. Technique: token/type pass on Shield + etched
hairline rules under sections. Cost: token + view. Identity: safe.
The reverse (Dawn's gradient into Blueprint) **KILLS** — a plate's
ground is a single flat wash; a colour gradient on it is weather on a
document, and documents don't have weather.

### 1.13 Noir × Temple — the lamp passes the inscription

**Noir's seal flare → Temple (ADAPTS).** §1.3 gives the flare to
Obsidian in ember. Temple should not copy that — it should translate
it, because Temple's gold leaf exists for exactly one physical
purpose: to catch light. Transplant the gesture into Temple's
physics: at the seal, a light rakes once across the Deck — a warm
gradient sweep, ~450ms, travelling left to right over the engraved
hairlines and the gold, so the inscription flashes as a lamp passes
it — then absolute stillness. Not a glow, not a loop: a consecration,
once, at the one moment the temple shuts. Technique: one animated
linear-gradient sweep masked to the hero's hairline/gold strokes on
the existing seal path (t=0 hook, build 6). Cost: view + the same hook
as §1.3 — if both worlds ever shared a build, this is the same organ
in two materials, which is the definition of a house style. Identity:
safe — stillness is preserved; the flare is an event, not a state.
(The reverse candidate — Temple's vermilion into Noir — is refused as
redundant: Noir's amber lamp already holds the danger job, and a
second danger hue dilutes the lamp, §1.7's law in mirror.)

### 1.14 Noir × Blueprint — the city measures your hour

**Blueprint's dimension lines → Noir (TRANSFORMS).** Noir's wheel is
a beautiful dark-glass mood with ticks — but ticks are vague; Noir's
city should be *precise* about the one thing it sells: measured time
withheld. Blueprint's signature device is the dimension line: thin
rule, arrowheads, the span annotated as a measurement. Transplant:
beneath Noir's dial, the chosen span is drawn as a neon dimension —
a hairline violet rule spanning the dial's width, end ticks, and the
span printed in Anonymous Pro as a sign-maker's spec (e.g. "60 MIN"
centred on the rule, breaking it, the way dimensions break their
lines). It binds to the same span state the hero reads — no new data,
no fake numbers. The dial stops being a mood and becomes an
instrument the city has calibrated; precision deepens the danger (a
city that *measures* you is more noir than a city that merely glows).
Technique: canvas dimension rule under the dial + one text element
on existing state. Cost: view, cheap. Identity: safe — spec plates
and measured signage are native to cities. (Glow/bloom into Blueprint
is refused per §1.9: on the plate, bloom is dirt.)

### 1.15 Temple × Blueprint — the frieze of the day

**Blueprint's structural hairlines → Temple (TRANSFORMS).**
Temple's Shield will list his three routines as rows (start — name —
end). Blueprint's hairlines are never decoration: they carry
measurement. Transplant: beneath Temple's routine rows, one engraved
**scale of the day** — a horizontal brass rule ticked at the hours
03–21, and each routine's span cut into it as a filled notch between
its start and end ticks, FASTED / DAY / SLEEP reading left to right as
three carved blocks. His whole day — the thing the app exists to
guard — becomes visible at a glance as a frieze, in the oldest
information format temples have: a band of inscription running across
a wall. The data already exists (routine rows, start/end minutes);
this is a drawing of it, not a feature. Technique: one canvas strip
(height ~28dp) under the routine rows + span math from existing
routine state. Cost: view + read-only state. Identity: safe — it
makes Temple MORE Temple. The reverse — gold leaf into Blueprint —
**KILLS**: metal on a drawing is treasure glued to paper; the plate's
honesty is that everything on it is ink.

## 3. THE MISSING SEVENTH THING — the Held Door

Count the surfaces all six worlds have designed: the Deck at rest,
the Shield settings, the slide, the proof strip, the receipt. Now
count the surface he will actually see most in his life with this app.

It is none of those. **It is the interception** — the screen that
appears in the half-second after he opens a SHUT app mid-block:
TikTok at 14:20 on a weak afternoon, Instagram inside the FASTED
block. That surface fires dozens of times a week. It is the product.
The Deck is the showroom; the intercept is the machine doing its one
job, in his hand, at his weakest moment. And it appears in **no
direction's renders, no case letter, no NEXT list** — all six worlds
designed the door on the wall; none designed the moment someone tries
the handle. (The mechanism exists — the shield service intercepts —
but as a designed surface it is currently whatever the machinery
happens to throw up: undesigned, therefore, under Charter v3's
everything-intentional clause, unfinished in all six worlds equally.)

Why it outranks every other candidate addition: retention. His own
law, from the Blockit lesson, is that beauty is the retention
mechanism — and blockers are uninstalled at the intercept, not at the
settings screen. The intercept has the hardest brief in the app: it
must land in under a second of attention, often in a flash of
frustration; it must not shame him (shame teaches him to hate the
app, not the app he opened); it must tell the truth fast — what he
reached for, how much of the block remains, what he is in the middle
of (the current task's name is already state) — and it must offer the
one lawful door (the 2-minute peek, already machinery) as a designed
choice, not a loophole. Feel: one low knock of haptics, no animation
that delays dismissal, the blocked app acknowledged, not mocked.

**I argue it as a direction-independent law: THE HELD DOOR — whichever
world wins, the interception is a first-class designed surface in that
world's grammar, designed and judged to the same standard as the
Deck; and no direction may be picked a winner until its Held Door has
been rendered.** A blocker is judged at the door it holds, not the
door it shows off.

Each world already knows how to hold its door — one line each, as
proof the law is buildable in all six grammars:
- **Obsidian:** true black glass; the reached-for app's name in bone;
  the remaining span as a small monument; the ember lit once, low, on
  the peek key — the only warm thing in the room is the lawful door.
- **Signal:** a flat poster — the app name huge, a red square before
  it, the remaining time as a flat block, PEEK as the black plate.
  A closed sign with perfect typography.
- **Dawn:** the blocked app visible BEHIND frosted glass — you can
  see its colours blurred in the sky and cannot touch them; the
  remaining span etched on the frost. Longing, made literal, kindly.
- **Noir:** a CLOSED sign in the city — the app name in unlit tube,
  one amber lamp lit beside the remaining minutes; the peek key is
  the only sign switched on.
- **Temple:** a sealed gate — the app name engraved, a vermilion
  outline seal across it; the remaining span as a notch on the scale
  of the day (§1.15); the peek is a side door in bronze.
- **Blueprint:** the plate stamped — the app drawn in line ink, the
  remaining span annotated as a dimension (§1.14's rule, reused),
  HOLD stamped in annotation red: a correction the drawing makes to
  your afternoon.

Machinery cost, honestly: no new machinery. The intercept already
fires; remaining-span, current-task and peek are existing state and
existing affordances. The cost is one screen's design + view build per
finalist, and — if Ren rules the render-before-pick clause — one
render run per finalist before judging closes. That is the cheapest
law in this document relative to how often the surface will be seen.

**Runner-up, named so it is not lost:** the Confession — the designed
face of a broken day (shield gap, a routine that never fired — the
app already books BROKEN in its ledger by his ruling, but no world
has designed how the machine *says so*). It matters; it fires rarely.
The Held Door fires daily. The ring should take the Confession next;
I will argue it then.
