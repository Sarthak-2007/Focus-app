## PART A — THE EN-BLOC ADOPTION IS ITSELF THE FIRST FAILURE

### A-1 · CHARGED S3 — There is no 18th ratification. The count is wrong in the brief itself.

The Ruling letter adopts, in one sentence: *"RATIFICATIONS F3,
F4, F7-F13, F15-F18, F22-F24: ADOPTED as recommended, each"*
(Ruling letter L29–30). That string names **16** forks. §6 of the
build-ready file lists **17** ratification bullets
(Build-ready L696–766) — the seventeenth is F14, which the letter
disposes of separately as superseded in part. Marlowe 0826's
ratification paragraph likewise enumerates 17 (F3, F4, F7–F14,
F15–F18, F22–F24). No eighteenth ratification exists in either
letter or in §6. Any audit brief or ledger that counts 18 is
counting a phantom — or has silently lost one fork. My verdict
table in Part B therefore covers all 17 that exist, plus this row
for the missing one. A chair who adopts en bloc cannot afterwards
say which forks he adopted; the arithmetic proves nobody counted.

### A-2 · CHARGED S2 — "As recommended, each" adopted seventeen recommendations, none of them accessibility-read, and the two notes misdescribe the filings they claim to honour.

Not one of the 17 recommendations was filed or reviewed by an
accessibility seat — yet at least six of them (F4, F7, F10, F12,
F16, F24) decide how a signal reaches, or fails to reach, a tired
or trembling hand. The en-bloc form is what concealed that: read
one by one, F12 is a live dispute between registered law and this
seat's filed floor (Part C-1); read en bloc, it is a comma in a
list. The letter's two notes then claim a fidelity they do not
have:

- *"F12's constant settles with Ton on device pre-build, as you
  filed it"* (Ruling letter L30–31). Joss filed the opposite
  urgency: *"do not let the slide reach his hand before Ren
  settles the floor question with Ton on device"* and *"What must
  not happen is shipping 0.40 untested and retraining the hand
  later"* (joss.md L451–458). "As you filed it" converts a
  build-gate into a scheduling note.
- *"F13's reserved-voice sweep is Ivo's, outside this slice, as
  you filed it"* (Ruling letter L31). Orson filed the sweep as a
  fork — (a) in this slice, or (b) *"leave them and sweep"* later
  as *"a named reserved-voice sweep as its own"* assignment
  (orson.md L413–418). The adoption names no date, no trigger,
  and no owner beyond a seat that cannot self-assign. "Outside
  this slice," unconditioned, is how a deferred item becomes a
  dropped one.

The chair is charged under the widened scope in his own person:
en-bloc adoption is a ruling method, and on accessibility-bearing
forks it is the method that guaranteed none of them was read.

## PART C — THE CHARGED RATIFICATIONS, WITH PROOF

### C-1 · F12 — CHARGED S1 (worst charge in this audit)

The refusal is the interlock's safety signal: the moment the
machine tells a man his arm will not be held. It speaks in two
channels — the sentence under the track and the knock in the
thumb. The adoption keeps the knock at **10ms @ 0.40** in spec
(Build-ready L720–723) against this seat's filed floor of
**≥12ms @ 0.70**, *"priced from Barriere's measured tick"*
(joss.md L444). Joss — the fork's own author, no ally of mine —
states the accessibility case in his own recommendation: *"Zug
is right on the only ground that matters at 05:00 — a stop you
cannot feel is not a stop, and the sentence under the track is
the channel least likely to be read by a man whose thumb just
got refused"* (joss.md L451–455). The change, if the floor wins,
is one constant — `fireWeightedKnock(14, 0.70f, 220f)` *"and one
REGISTER line; nothing else in this file moves"* (joss.md
L455–457).

What the one adoption line conceals: (1) 0.40 amplitude at 10ms
sits at or below the perception threshold of common haptic
motors, through a case, in a depleted grip — the exact hand
Barriere's table designs for (MASTER L1726–1728: half-asleep,
trembling, depleted grip). A refusal the thumb cannot feel reads
as a dead slide; the man drags again, harder, against a machine
that has already answered him. (2) The visual backup channel is
itself compromised — the sentence lives in a 22dp box at lh22
with *"zero leading slack. At system font scale >1.0 the glyphs
can clip vertically"* (suki.md L437–439; see W-2). (3) The
deferral has no gate: the Ruling letter notes the constant
*"settles with Ton on device pre-build"* (L30–31) but names no
consequence if that session slips — and the spec value that
ships by default in that event is the sub-floor one. A safety
signal whose perceptibility is a calendar item is not a spec;
it is a hope with a constant attached.

### C-2 · F4 — CHARGED S2 — strict abort makes the tap path hostile to the one-handed grip

Rhea's fork is honestly filed: strict = *"any second touch on
the view aborts"*, including `ACTION_POINTER_DOWN` from any
finger, vs inner-bounds-only with *"edge/palm contacts ignored"*
(rhea.md §7, L469–477). Her defence prices a false abort at
*"≤590ms and nothing else."* That price is per abort. What the
adoption line conceals is the population for whom the abort is
not occasional: a one-handed user grips the phone with the same
hand that taps, and on a near-bezel-less handset the gripping
fingers and palm edge are second contacts by design. For that
hand, strict abort does not cost 590ms once — it makes the tap,
his verdict's equal commit path (MASTER §0, "Slide → TAP CAN
COMMIT"), unreliable on every attempt, at 03:30, with the drag
as the only fallback — the drag being the higher-motor path the
tap exists to relieve. Rhea's own remedy is post-ship: *"let the
device trial earn the narrowing… Do not pre-narrow on my
guess"* (rhea.md §7). The adoption ships the strict form with
the trial as a hope, not a gate: no named trial, no threshold of
palm-aborts that triggers the one-constant narrowing, no date.
The user is the instrument, and nobody wrote down what reading
would change the constant.

### C-3 · F7 — CHARGED S2 — annulment-by-deletion leaves an ephemeral sentence as the only trace of a destructive machine act

Opal's mechanics are exact (opal.md L275–285): at L+1500 any
unconfirmed link → `annulProvingSession` = row deletion +
`stopShield`; *"No tombstone, no Ledger line, no history row…
it is an arm that was refused late."* The Deck *"prints the
refusal in Rosa's slot, refusal tense: `〈LINK〉 — NO PROOF.
NOTHING WAS SEALED.`"* That sentence is the event's entire
record, and it is: (a) transient — it tenants a slot whose
healthy tenant is nothing, and the hand-back law fades it;
(b) printed in the same zero-slack 22dp box charged at W-2;
(c) unaccompanied by any persistent, reviewable surface. A man
who looked up during the ≤1500ms proving window — or whose
process died mid-proving, where recovery annulment is announced
by a Guard sentence on a different screen (opal.md L308–315) —
returns to an idle Deck indistinguishable from a morning he
never touched. "No verb for him" (Build-ready L704–706) also
means no accessible account of *why*: he cannot re-read, cannot
review, cannot distinguish "the machine jammed" from "I did
something wrong." Truth-wise the deletion is defensible — a
session that never reached t=0 was never a session. As
notification design it fails the only test that matters: the
sole record of the machine's refusal is a fading line in its
least legible slot.

### C-4 · F10 — CHARGED S2 — the scoped label fade deletes the instruction before the decision point

The adopted scoping keeps the shipped `1 − 1.3·frac` alpha fade
for hand-drags (Build-ready §3-R5; L711–714). The arithmetic is
the charge: at the gate (frac 0.68) the cap word's alpha is
1 − 1.3 × 0.68 ≈ **0.12**; at the catch (0.84) it is **zero**
(the fade completes at frac ≈ 0.77, before commit). The only
printed instruction on the gesture — set at plate size, 10sp
Anonymous Pro (Build-ready D10) — is at 12% opacity at the
moment of decision and gone before the moment of commitment.
Aldo's defence is occlusion: the thumb covers the label as it
travels (Build-ready §3-R5). But a user who pauses mid-drag —
the tired user's actual behaviour — lifts or shifts the thumb
to re-read, and there is nothing to re-read. Teo's dissent (kill
the fade outright; aldo.md's state table shows the alternative,
a frozen string throughout the drag) was the accessibility-
correct position, and it lost without the adoption naming the
cost: legibility is spent exactly in proportion to commitment.
A label that fades as you approach the act it names is a
countdown to illiteracy.

### C-5 · F13 — CHARGED S2 — the seal's voice stays spent in ~13 other places, so the ear cannot learn what "sealed" sounds like

Orson's finding: `playHarmonicSeal(true)` *"is spent in at
least thirteen other places"* (orson.md L401), breaking the
treaty that seal and completion voices are reserved (orson.md
L410–413). For a sighted user at arm's length this is a craft
debt. For the user this seat represents — eyes off the screen,
phone across the desk, reading the app by sound and haptic —
the seal's voice *is* the state channel: it is how he knows,
without looking, that the arm landed. Slice E builds two new
seal voices (Throw, and the B-5 Bit-Identical Seal) while the
adoption exiles the sweep that would stop the old voice from
meaning thirteen other things. During the interim — unbounded,
unowned (see A-2) — every task-row seal-sound is a false
"sealed" spoken into the room. The one line conceals that the
slice ships a new word for its most important event into a
vocabulary where that word is already ambiguous.

### C-6 · F3 — CHARGED S3 — the TIME cell's anchor moves, and nothing on the cell says so

Adopted: primed for a future window, `ENDS` anchors to the
window start, not to now (Build-ready L697–700; Pia's example:
`ENDS 07:50`, not `07:48`). Pia's reasoning is coherent — the
anchor *"is the window start, so the priming window's own start
is never reported as 'crossed'"* (pia.md L258, §3.4). The
concealed cost is verification: in every other state the cell is
`now + span`, checkable against the hero; in the primed-future
state the same glyphs are `window start + span`, and the cell
carries no marker of which anchor produced it. The tired reader
cannot sanity-check the one absolute time on the Deck, because
its derivation changed tense without changing form. The fix is
one word of anchor print or a primed-state distinction; the
adoption prices neither. Small minutes, real principle: a number
whose anchor is invisible is a number that must be trusted, not
read.

### C-7 · F8 — CHARGED S3 — holding the Shield session-scoped keeps pre-arm shield state unknowable on the Deck

The adoption (Build-ready L707–709) is one line: hold closed
unless the §F7 gate names L2 a chronic jam. What it conceals is
already proven elsewhere in the same file: the Shield service
*"stopSelfs with no session"* (Build-ready D6, citing
MonolithShieldService.kt:66–73), so shield-protecting is
*"refused as a pre-arm conjunct"* — requiring it *"would print
OFF on every healthy morning."* Consequence the line never
states: before he slides, the Deck can tell him about the a11y
bind, the residency stamp and the usage grant — and nothing
about whether the Shield half of his seal exists. That fact is
only provable inside the cascade, after commitment (link L2).
D7's row 3 (`BLOCKING STOPPED — TAP TO RESTART`) covers the
failure after the fact, with a documented fallback — *"if that
joint slips, row 3 falls back to not printing"* (Build-ready
D7). The information gap is honestly specced in the joints; the
ratification's one line is where it disappears. A pre-arm screen
that cannot state one of its seal's two services is a screen
whose readiness claim is partial by construction.

### C-8 · F16 — CHARGED S2 — the track face saves the words' size and spends their readability

The measurement is not in dispute: Mutter's strings compose at
≈155dp / ≈125dp against a 68dp sled (aldo.md L103–106); they
cannot live on the cap. Aldo's placement — track face, START
*"centred in the free span right of the sled at rest"* — fits
with *"≈5dp slack per side, which is why the measure gate (§5)
is not optional"* (aldo.md L109–112). Three costs the adoption
line conceals. (1) **Size:** both strings print at plate —
Anonymous Pro 700 **10sp** (Build-ready D10) — the smallest tier
in the app, for the tap path's only instruction and its only
mercy word. (2) **The mercy window:** `TAP AGAIN TO UNDO` prints
only inside the tap-mesh window — 140ms mesh + 110ms spring ≈
**250ms** (aldo.md, state table S2). No human reads 17 sorts of
10sp tracked caps in 250ms; the word exists for the record, not
for the man. The mercy is real only as a motor act (second
touch), which F4's strictness then governs. (3) **The slack:**
5dp per side at 1.0 font scale; at >1.0 the measure gate fails
and no fallback (wrap, shrink, re-home) is specced — the same
font-scale cliff as W-2, on the Deck's committing surface. The
rejected alternative (widen the sled to ≥165dp) was priced
against travel; nobody priced the words at 15sp on the track
with the rest span re-cut. The adoption chose the home; it never
priced the legibility of what lives there.

### C-9 · F17 — CHARGED S2 — ghost retention adopted with no reduced-motion or low-vision clause

Wren's amendment is load-bearing for the shot: wheel and sled
*"persist as ghosts through Settle"* instead of being deleted
at the seal frame (Build-ready E3; wren.md L227–231). The
ghost values are filed: the wheel settles to **scale 0.80,
y+20dp, α0.50, blur 6dp** (wren.md L218). For a low-vision user,
a blurred half-opacity wheel and sled are not a memory of
controls — they are controls, apparently present, on a screen
whose input is dead (E3's input-death amendment). For a
vestibular user, the shot itself — a ~1050ms scale + translate
+ blur over those ghosts — is the motion class (large-surface
autonomous movement after the finger stops) that requires a
reduced-motion fallback everywhere else this plan remembers to
ask for one (see W-5). Neither the fork (Build-ready L744–746)
nor the adoption carries a reduced-motion variant, a static-
ghost alternative, or a name for who prices one. Ratified
unconditional, the shot ships its spectacle to the eyes least
able to parse it.

### C-10 · F18 — CHARGED S3 — one number, honestly derived, that still overstates shut-ness to the man reading it

Uma files the tension herself: *"PEEK is a judgement wearing a
fact's clothes"* (uma.md L340) — enforcement is unambiguous
(both enforcers intercept PEEK; Build-ready D6), and the sealed
tense's word is HELD, not SHUT, *"precisely because PEEK is
held-with-a-lease"* (uma.md L376–379). The count is truthful.
The concealed cost is presentational: on the seed registry the
cell prints **10** where 3 of the 10 are apps that will open —
for two minutes, on a knock, on a 20-minute cooldown, and
freely in every Safe Break. The single numeral cannot carry
that; the entire nuance rides on the word HELD at plate size in
the imprint. A rested reader with the fork file open gets the
truth; a tired reader at the strip gets "10 held." I do not
propose Option B — two counts on one screen is worse (uma.md
L379–392, agreed). The charge is narrower: the adoption treats
the fork as closed by arithmetic, when its remaining cost is
typographic — and nobody was assigned the typographic fix
(e.g. the cell's word, not its numeral, carrying the lease).

### C-11 · F24 — CHARGED S2 — "direct to the cause's pane" ejects the tired man into Android Settings, serially, on a slot-wide hair trigger

Suki's fork (suki.md §9, L453–470) recommends direct honestly
and prices its own anti-case: *"a triple-failure morning costs
up to three Settings round trips, one sentence at a time"*
(suki.md L441–443). What the adoption line conceals, from the
accessibility seat: (1) The destination for rows 1 and 4 is the
system Accessibility / Usage-Access pane — a long, alphabetised
list of services in small system type, the single hardest
search task in Android, assigned to a man at 04:58 with one
hand; the app offers no deep-link highlight, no "find Monolith
in this list" aid, and the hand-back (≤890ms, Build-ready D7)
only begins when he returns. (2) The tap target is the whole
slot — full width × 48dp (suki.md §3, L179–181) — which is
generous as a target and therefore also generous as an
accident: any tremor tap in a 372 × 48dp zone under the track
ejects him from the app mid-refusal. (3) The PARK alternative's
real cost was +1 tap and a board read; the adoption never
weighs ejection cost against board cost for the worst morning,
only for the common single-cause one. Direct is probably right
for row 3 (in-app restart) and row 2 (in-app retry) — those
never leave the app. The charge lands on rows 1/4: the route
was adopted as a table, not per-row, and the two rows that
leave the app are the two whose destination is least navigable
by the man being sent there.

## PART D — WHOLE-PLAN CHARGES (MASTER-PLAN.md / REGISTER.md)

### W-1 · CHARGED S2 — the valve still has no non-contact, screen-reader, or switch path; the plan's own law says it must not test grip, then tests nothing else

§5.3.1 states the law in the plan's own words: the valve *"must
never test grip endurance, aim, or steadiness"* (MASTER
L1721–1722). The mechanism then offered is sustained contact —
up to 60.0 held seconds of it — with no alternative path
anywhere in §5.3: no accessibility action, no switch-access
route, no hands-free countdown, no `contentDescription` or
announcement spec. The contrast is documented in the studio's
own files: Barriere's Demand 3 (humans/10-accessibility.md §3)
specified both equivalents — a hands-free 60s countdown and a
cumulative hold — at identical friction and identical token
cost; neither appears in the plan of record. The shipped code
this plan replaces had *"zero contentDescription, semantics,
focusability or accessibility events"* on the valve
(plan/critics/zug-hold-exits.md, valve table), and §5.3 as
merged adds geometry, haptics and sound — but never semantics.
§5.9's acceptance tests include an eyes-off run (*"screen
covered, full 60s by feel alone"*, MASTER L2769) and no
TalkBack or switch run. A TalkBack user cannot find the valve,
cannot start it, and cannot hear its countdown; the plan's
"worst hand" table (MASTER L1726–1728) lists tremor and grip
fatigue and then designs only for hands that can hold.
**Partial acquittal inside the charge:** for tremor itself the
mitigations are real and strong — presence-never-position
contact law (MASTER L1741), 16dp spill apron, banks at
15/30/45, *per-slip uncapped* 800ms grace, drain floored at the
last bank (§5.3.4). A tremor slip costs tail, never bank. The
charge is the missing path, not the present one.

### W-2 · CHARGED S2 — the refusal sentence is specced in the one box that clips at the font scales accessibility users run

Suki, against her own joint: *"lh22 in a 22dp box has zero
leading slack. At system font scale >1.0 the glyphs can clip
vertically"* (suki.md L437–439). The sentence is additionally
single-line and *"never wraps"* — on columns under ~340dp it
*"truncates never and the slot is the red flag"* (suki.md §3),
i.e. the overflow behaviour is undefined by design. Her remedy
is a check, not a spec: *"the honest device check is a
font-scale render pass before this ships"* (suki.md L440). No
ruling — not the merge's §3-R6 (which preserves lh20-setting /
lh22-slot as two numbers, Build-ready L557–559), not the
Ruling letter — conditions shipment on that pass. So the app's
most important sentence (it is the refusal, the crossing
advisory, and the annulment notice — C-3) is the one text in
the plan guaranteed to degrade for exactly the users who run
large text. Every other surface gets a font-scale law or a
growth rule; the refusal gets a hope and a device check nobody
was assigned to run.

### W-3 · CHARGED S2 — the Frozen Digest promises "read everything here" and specs everything except the reading

§5.5's promise is in its header sentence: *"Read everything
here. Nothing here opens."* (MASTER §5.5.) The spec then
provides: a header plate at **10sp** (below this seat's filed
12sp functional floor — critics/17-accessibility.md, type-floor
§4); the sentence at 15sp; and for the message bodies — the
actual content he is invited to read in full — **no type size,
no font, no contrast, no line-height, and no TalkBack/focus
specification at all**. Rows *"carry no tap target"* (correct
under HOLD) but the plan never states their semantics: a screen
reader's ability to enter, scroll and read a snapshot list is
simply unbuilt language. The one reassurance the surface
offers, `It will keep until {finish}`, is timed like an advert:
it *"fades in with the dismiss (~280ms), holds ~900ms, fades in
200ms, fires once per session at most"* (MASTER L2273–2277) —
under 1.5 seconds of total visibility, pointer-through, never
re-summonable, for a 15sp sentence a slow reader may need three
seconds to finish. The Landing Line repeats the pattern (2,500ms
of held ink, then a fade — MASTER L2285–2289). A digest whose
readability is asserted in its first sentence and unspecified
in every line after it has a marketing claim where its
accessibility spec should be.

### W-4 · CHARGED S2 — the AOD's across-room legibility decays by design, and its second reading falls below the floor unlabelled

The AOD's job, in his life, is to be read across a room — the
idle face is *"his bedroom clock"* (MASTER §5.8.6) and the
running face serves the meditation fact: phone in the room,
across the room. Size acquits (REMAINING at 104sp, §5.8.3). The
staircase does not. Converting §5.8.5's filed luminances to
contrast against the face's true black: Stage 0 REMAINING
0.215 → ≈5.3:1 (passes) · Stage 1 0.174 → ≈4.5:1 (the line) ·
Stage 2 0.130 → ≈3.6:1 (large-text floor only). ELAPSED is
worse: Stage 2 files it at lum **0.075** (MASTER L2494) →
≈**2.5:1**, below the 3:1 floor for even large text — and from
Stage 1 onward *"labels drop"* (MASTER L2500), so that
sub-floor numeral stands without its `ELAPSED` plate, its only
disambiguator from REMAINING. My wave-1 charge on the shipped
clock was the missing state word (critics/17-accessibility.md,
AOD §1); the plan answers it with hierarchy + label, then
deletes the label at minute 20 and dims the number below the
floor at minute 50 — i.e. for the whole length of a long
session, the across-room reader gets two unlabelled numbers,
one of them illegible, distinguished by size alone. The colon
compounds it: set at *"45% of the numeral tone"* (§5.8.3,
MASTER L2452), at Stage 2 it renders near lum ~0.03 — `H:MM`
reads as two separate numbers. Burn-in is a real constraint
and the patrol + ceiling are honestly argued; the charge is
that the staircase spends legibility below the floor instead
of stopping at it: Stage 2's ELAPSED could hold 3:1 and the
label could stay. Nothing in §5.8 prices that refusal.

### W-5 · CHARGED S2 — reduced motion exists in this plan exactly once, and none of the Slice D/E spectacle carries it

The plan knows how to write the clause: the Tasks gate's
reduced-motion form collapses travel to *"instant state +
120ms alpha — the gate jumps detent to detent (position is
state; it may never fade), haptics and punch untouched"*
(MASTER L1436–1438). Barriere's inventory (humans/10-
accessibility.md §2) files a required fallback for every
motion in the app, gated on the system animator scale *and* an
in-app toggle. Against that standard: Slice D/E's Vertigo shot
(C-9), the powered 650ms Sled Goes Home, the seal shot's held

Register's motion registry. The system signal the Humans file
names (`ANIMATOR_DURATION_SCALE == 0`) is mentioned nowhere in
MASTER, Build-ready, or REGISTER (grep-verified across all
three). A vestibular user gets a plan that remembers him on
one Tasks element and forgets him on every surface Slice D/E
just ratified. The adoption's silence here is the en-bloc
method again: no fork asked the question, so no fork carries
the answer.

### W-6 · CHARGED S3 — MASTER-PLAN still prints the dead refusal pair, so the merged plan disagrees with the slice about what a refusal says

MASTER §2.6 still carries W4's sentences as operative text:
*"`BLOCKING IS OFF — NOTHING WILL BE HELD.` / `BLOCKING
UNVERIFIED — NOTHING WILL BE HELD.`"* (MASTER L441–442; again
in the §8 retelling, L4303). Build-ready §3-R2 declares that
pair *"dead text"* everywhere, replaced by Suki's cause table
(Build-ready L551–555), and D7 builds the replacement. Both
documents are "the plan." A builder — or the next auditor —
working from MASTER alone ships a refusal with no cause
distinction and no tap route: precisely the unreadable refusal,
a sentence that tells a tired man *that* nothing will be held
and nothing about *why* or *what to tap*. Under TRUTH-once,
two homes for the refusal text is one too many; under
accessibility, the surviving-in-MASTER version is the worse
one. (Converges with Orig's register-divergence finding on
F1/F19 — different documents, same disease: rulings announced
in letters, never landed in the governing text.)

### W-7 · CHARGED S3 — tap-to-commit passes the touch-area law on its face; the law's blind spots are the AOD finish tap and the abort window

The Register's touch-area law as amended — *"every control's
effective touch area is ≥48dp in both dimensions"* (REGISTER,
Amendments) — is met by the headline tap targets: slide cap
84×56, refusal slot 48dp hit area (suki.md §3), valve rest slot
200×48 (MASTER §5.4), FLOW key 64×48 (MASTER L551). Acquitted
in A-3. The residue the law doesn't reach: (1) the AOD's finish
minute makes the whole face a tap target — *"A tap during the
finish minute goes to the Deck"* (MASTER L2552–2554) — while
§5.8.9 rules a tap on the same face, one minute earlier, a
transient that changes nothing. Same gesture, two effects,
separated by a boundary the user cannot see; a hand groping
for the phone on a nightstand in the finish minute navigates
where the same hand, at minute 59 of the session, safely woke
the tones. My wave-1 charge against tap-anywhere dismiss
(critics/17-accessibility.md, AOD §3) returns here in milder
form. (2) The tap-mesh's commit is a 250ms precision window
policed by F4's strict abort (C-2) — a timing-precision test
that no dp floor measures. The law certifies the size of
targets; these two failures are about the *timing* of taps,
and nothing in the Register prices timing.

## ACQUITTALS

Filed plainly, with proof, so the charges cannot be read as a
general indictment:

- **A-1 · The valve's contact and drain engineering (MASTER
  §5.3.2, §5.3.4).** Full-width 112dp bar (exceeds the Register's
  ≥88dp valve floor), 16dp spill apron, presence-never-position,
  any-finger/palm contact, banks at 15/30/45, uncapped per-slip
  grace, drain floored at the last bank. For tremor and grip
  fatigue this is the strongest exit design the studio has
  produced; my charge at W-1 is the absent alternative path,
  not this mechanism.
- **A-2 · The exit chip's size (MASTER L1756).** The valve
  overlay's KEEP FOCUSING chip is specced **≥56dp tall** and
  inert while gripped — my wave-1 finding (42dp chip, the
  smallest target on the exit screen; plan/critics/zug-hold-
  exits.md §f) is answered in the plan of record, and Freya's
  0711 letter concedes the same fix into the host spec. The
  safe choice is no longer the smallest target. Acquitted.
- **A-3 · Headline touch targets.** Slide cap, refusal slot,
  valve bar and rest slot, hub (*"touch ≥ drawn size
  everywhere"*, MASTER §2.7 check 8), FLOW key at 64×48: the
  48dp touch-area law is genuinely met where the plan names a
  tap. The failures in this audit are timing, semantics and
  legibility — not target size on the committing surfaces.
- **A-4 · F9, F11, F15, F22, F23 STAND.** F9: the rack is a
  display, not a target, is gated by §F7, and its build price
  already carries a *"reduced-motion law"* (opal.md L392) — the
  one Slice D/E element that remembered W-5's clause. F11: the
  integrator is the option that *guarantees* knock-before-latch
  on a dropped frame (Build-ready L715–719) — haptic ordering
  is an accessibility property, and Cato's engine is the one
  that protects it. F15: persisting `heldAppsAtSeal` is what
  keeps the sealed count truthful across process death instead
  of silently recomputing from edited tiers (Build-ready
  L729–733) — information accessibility, correctly bought.
  F22: re-toning the born carriers at birth (mara.md L330–331)
  prevents an orange progress fill and chrome dot from leaking
  into the running screen — the alternative was editing the
  print to excuse the leak. F23: holding the pre-seal scheme
  through the exit prevents every contrast ratio on screen
  from changing at the commit instant (mara.md L366–380); a
  scheme flip mid-gesture is a legibility event, and the
  adoption correctly refuses it.
- **A-5 · The refusal sentences themselves (Build-ready D7).**
  Cause-named, distinct per cause, Atkinson 700 15sp in glyph
  ink, one line, with the tap named in the sentence (`— TAP TO
  FIX`). As words, they pass — readable, honest, actionable.
  My charges land on their box (W-2), their knock (C-1) and
  their route (C-11), never on their text.
- **A-6 · The strip's state grammar (Build-ready D6).** BLOCK /
  APPS / TIME carried by position + fill density + word +
  edge, *"greyscale-legible; zero orange"* — state never by
  colour alone, the hard rule of both accessibility seats
  (critics/17; designers/18 D3) honoured in construction.
- **A-7 · The AOD's motion and silence discipline (§5.8.4,
  §5.8.1).** Patrol by 1-frame cut, never a glide; no specular;
  unconditionally silent; wake by ramp, never a flash. The
  vestibular and night charges I filed against the shipped
  clock's behaviour are answered here; W-4 charges only the
  dimming staircase's floor.
- **A-8 · The Frozen Digest's snapshot discipline (§5.5).** No
  live appends under his eyes, no pull-to-refresh, no ration,
  no record of opens. Freezing the list is the attention-
  accessible choice; W-3 charges the unspecified reading
  surface, not the freeze.

**Closing line:** the merged plan is, surface by surface, the
most accessibility-literate document this studio has produced —
and its ratifications were adopted by a method that read none
of them through the worst hand. The pattern in every charge
above is the same: the accessible clause exists somewhere in
the files (Joss's gate, Suki's render pass, Opal's reduced-
motion law, Barriere's countdown, Teo's dissent) and the
adoption is exactly where it gets dropped. Fix the method —
no en-bloc adoption of forks that carry a signal, a sentence,
or a timed surface — and most of these charges never file
again.
