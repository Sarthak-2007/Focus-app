## Ruling 1 — NEXT → top strip only

**Verdict: CHARGED — S2.**

**Acquitted part.** The global next firing does belong where he arms. The letter rules "Top strip only. Reihe's three printings die" (LETTER:7-9); the dossier's Option A consequence is that "'what's next' is answerable from any screen, including at the arm moment" (DOSSIER:32-37). That serves ARM and truth-once, and the ban on minute-precision theatre (LETTER:8-9; DOSSIER:52-54) is acquitted without reservation.

**Charge: the ruling kills two facts that were never the global fact, on a dossier premise that flattens them into one.** The dossier's question asserts one fact with "four printings across two designs" (DOSSIER:23-29) and its sources claim the three routines printings are "all from one `nextStartMillis()` call" (DOSSIER:56-60). The routines file itself refutes the flattening for two of the three:

- The per-routine state line's `NEXT · TODAY 05:00` is item 7 of *that routine's own* state line (`invention/improvers-parts/07-reihe-routine-rows-reinvention.md:201-209`) — this routine's next firing, a different fact from the global next whenever more than one routine exists.
- The edit-panel footer is computed "**against the draft**" (`…/07-reihe-routine-rows-reinvention.md:271-273`), inside a panel whose stated principle is "the panel's preview is the board. While a panel is open, the Week Board repaints from the *draft* state" (same file:265-269). A draft-next is a prospective fact — *what your edit would make next* — and by definition it cannot live in the top strip, which prints the committed next.

Killing the header printing is lawful. Killing the draft footer removes the editor's only prospective feedback: the rested user — the one the workshop law says decides — must now close the panel and read a strip that answers a different question (what *is* next, not what *would be* next) to verify his own edit. That is a busier, colder routines surface, taxed on exactly the surface Sarthak owns outright (routines fully his).

**Unpriced cost.** The letter prices only deletions — "≈2h deletions on item 1's routines side" (LETTER:32-33). Nowhere in letter or dossier is the preview loss priced, because the dossier's premise made it invisible. Cure asked: strip keeps the global fact; the panel footer survives as a draft rendering (it exists only inside an open editor, against uncommitted fields — a relay of a hypothetical, not a second home for a fact).

The death is told once, on the finish receipt" (`plan/MASTER-PLAN.md:76-79`). Blanking `SEALED · UNTIL 07:00` *is* a screen change — the most legible one the strip can make. If Ruling 6 is meant to amend §5.6/§6.1, the letter does not say it amends anything; it says "exactly." A chair ruling that overclaims the sovereign's word is a truth-once failure in the ruling itself, whatever the rendering's merits.

**The dossier knew, and the letter erased it.** Option B's own stated consequence: "the strip goes quiet **in a way he may or may not notice**; the truth is kept whole and delivered exactly where he ordered it" (DOSSIER:304-309). That sentence is the whole problem in miniature. If he notices, the blank has signalled — breaking the silence law it claims to obey, and signalling in the least interpretable form available: not a fact, an absence. The letter converts this admitted coin-flip into the clean triad "silence during, no false promise during" (LETTER:27-28) without mentioning the notice-risk its own dossier printed.

**Retention consequence, unpriced in both files.** `SEALED · UNTIL 07:00` (DOSSIER:285-287) is not decoration; it is the reassurance fact — the line that tells a man who glances at his desk clock / AOD face that the machine is holding and until when, so he can put the question down and study. Deaths are rare, but they are the trust-critical events: on the one morning the machine has actually failed, the instrument's face goes blank — indistinguishable at a glance from *session over*, *app crashed*, or *phone unsealed by something else*. Blank invites exactly what HOLD exists to abolish: a mid-session investigation, phone in hand, diagnosing whether he is still sealed. The as-filed alternative was worse — printing SEALED over a dead guard is a false promise, "the concealment both cures exist to kill" (DOSSIER:310-313) — and the receipt booking BROKEN, never sealed-clean, is acquitted below. But between a lie and an ambiguity there was a third thing the chair owed him: honesty about the tension. His two laws collide here (no lie during vs no signal during); the dossier priced the collision at ≈1h vs ≈3h of build (DOSSIER:298-305) and the letter resolved it by declaring one side "exactly" his word. It is not.

**Unpriced cost.** Item 6's ≈1h (LETTER:33-34) is build time only. Unpriced: every future death event now carries an ambiguous-face tax at the moment of maximum distrust, and the merged plan's §§5.6/6.1 text now contradicts the ruling in force — the next builder who reads the plan first will build the wrong death.
