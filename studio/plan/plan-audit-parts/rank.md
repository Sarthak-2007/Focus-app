## BOTTLENECK VERDICT (named, as ordered)

**The bottleneck is MERGE LABOUR — the chair as sole integrator — sitting on a
compute ceiling that caps every fix, with question load on the user as the most
expensive symptom, not the cause.**

- **Merge labour is the binding constraint on throughput.** Evidence: "code lands
  through one builder at a time" (BUILD-CHANGES 06:40, repeated 06:55); token work
  deferred twice for exactly this; every ruling/ratification/record in the log is
  Ren's hand (08:10, 08:58, 09:05 entries). Plans arrive at ~85 seats per 9 minutes;
  builds ship one slice at a time. Widen the producer side and the queue in front
  of the integrator only grows.
- **Compute is the hard ceiling underneath it.** 2 cores / 7 GB on this VM (Rank's
  measurement, 09:09), load 6.84 before builds; GUH journal-lock wars and a
  load-killed test suite already at 3 lanes (BUILD-CHANGES 08:20, 08:52); 15m59s
  for one uncontended build (Oct 28). Even a perfect integrator cannot run 3–4
  Gradle builds at full speed on this box. This caps the remedy, it is not the
  remedy's target: the integrator saturates first, at one lane.
- **Question load is third for throughput — and first in AIR-1 currency.** It does
  delay decisions (knob deferred 07:24, sync held 07:46, Guard standing since
  05:05), and each verdict loops back into the integrator's queue to be recorded
  and broadcast. But builds that needed nothing from him (Slices A/B1/B2/C, truth
  fixes) still shipped strictly serially — his taps were not what made them slow.
  What his taps cost was HIM: 21 touches out of a dropper's morning, ~186 days
  from the only exam that matters. Throughput is measured in builds; AIR-1 is
  measured in his hours. The studio is optimising neither when it spends the
  second to feed the first.

**Answer to the 200 question, in one line: doubling seats doubles throughput of
nothing except input — merge queue, claim volume, and question candidates — on a
machine and a chair that were already saturated at half the roster.**
