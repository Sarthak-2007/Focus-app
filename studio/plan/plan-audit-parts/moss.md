## CHARGES

### M-01 · S1 — The fifth freeze lives inside §4, and §4 denies it in its own verification

MASTER §4.4, L1162–1163 specs the ordinary sub-step strike with a
**40ms hit-stop at ~70%** (Zeile Rung 2). MASTER §4.8, L1450–1455
("Freeze verification") states this screen "spends exactly two of
the Register's four freezes" and convicts the same item by name:
"ordinary sub-step hit-stops (Zeile 40ms, Luz 60ms) spend 0ms."
Both texts are operative, in the same section, four pages apart.
Built from §4.4, the app ships a fifth freeze event — a 40ms
arrest on *every ordinary step check*, the most frequent gesture
on the screen — that the ledger (REGISTER, L106–107) does not
carry and no death was named for. Built from §4.8, §4.4's strike
spec is wrong at its centre frame. The merge cannot have both;
§9 carries no charge for it (Part II, L5024–5197, has no §4.4/
§4.8 item).

### M-02 · S1 — The wheel has no integrator, and two rival seat springs where the law allows one

The locked law is one (θ, ω) loop whose capture spring is
ζ ≈ 0.38, stiffness ≈ 210, exactly two overshoots, seat ≤520ms
(source: plan/wild-seats/01-vega.md, L15). In the merged plan:

- **ζ = 0.38 appears nowhere.** Grep over MASTER-PLAN.md and
  REGISTER.md: zero hits. No (θ, ω) state, no integration step,
  no stiffness 210 is specced in either document.
- MASTER §2.4, L266–267 gives the wheel a **"snap spring with
  overshoot ≤2° one pass"** — Kin's SNAP behaviour — while §2.4,
  L277–278, eleven lines later, holds the Loaded Wheel to
  **"two overshoots, seat ≤520ms."** One wheel, two incompatible
  seat shapes, neither labelled by regime in the operative text.
- REGISTER, L253–259 ratifies the split instead of resolving it:
  coast/fling seat = Vega's spring (two overshoots, ≤520ms);
  drag/programmatic seat = **SNAP-WHEEL (280 · 0.78, overshoot
  ≤2°, one pass, settle ≤380ms)** — a second spring with a
  different damping ratio (0.78 vs the locked 0.38), a different
  overshoot count, and a different settle cap, declared "not a
  rival value" by regime label. Under the one-loop law there is
  no second regime: a programmatic seat is the same integrator's
  capture. The regime split is the fork, filed as a footnote.
