## F1 · TRAVEL — fractions of actual travel, 180dp floor

**Verdict: CHARGED S2** — the geometry decision is acquitted
(see Acquittals); the charge is on the ruling's own sentence:
*"The register's literal loses to the view's real geometry;
register corrected."* (Ruling letter L7–8).

**Proof the decision follows the file:** Build-ready §6 F1
(lines 661–664): register literal 180dp vs `maxTravel = width −
88dp ≈ 248dp`, rec "fractions of actual travel, 180dp as floor;
register amendment drafted in vega.md." Vega's part, Amendment A:
"Register says TRAVEL 180dp. The code's travel is `width − 88dp`
≈ 248dp… zones are fractions of actual `maxTravel`; 180dp is the
floor," and §7: "The register's 180dp was written against an
imagined geometry; the code's geometry is real, the fractions
are the actual law in every source." Marlowe 0826, fork 1, is the
same recommendation. The chair followed the evidence.

**Proof the "corrected" claim is false:** `plan/REGISTER.md:44`
still reads "**TRAVEL: 180dp**" at audit time, and the register's
own amendment law (REGISTER.md:179–180) is that "amendments enter
by a dated line here, never by editing a number in place." No
dated travel amendment exists in the register. The only register
correction the chair actually transmitted that morning is the
specular one (`mail/sana/0905__from-ren__register-correction-
specular.md` — specularSwept/LUTs only). Build-ready §3-R9
itself lists the travel/register items as "Register corrections
**owed** (to Voss/Sana with this file)" — owed, not made. The
chair declared a governing document corrected while it still
governs the old number for anyone who reads it first.

**The precedent (the question this seat was asked):** the ruling
states a general rule — a register literal *loses to the view's
real geometry* — with no descriptive/normative line drawn. That
line is the whole ballgame. Vega's 180dp was a *descriptive*
number (a guess about a screen that turned out to be ~248dp);
the register's other literals are *normative* — they exist
precisely to overrule the shipped view (commit: register 0.84
vs shipped 0.82-on-release, Build-ready D1; nav: filed 64dp vs
shipped 80dp, Build-ready D12). Under the ruling's unbounded
sentence, a builder can cite F1 to demote any normative register
constant that disagrees with the code in front of him — the code
the register exists to correct. The lawful route was already in
the file (Build-ready line 18: "Register constants govern except
where a fork in §6 amends them" — an *amendment*, entered in the
register by dated line), and the chair used that route correctly
for the specular correction in the same hour. F1 needed one
clause — "a register number that purports to *describe* geometry
yields to measured geometry; a register number that *prescribes*
does not" — and the ruling does not contain it. As filed, the
register's authority everywhere else now rests on a distinction
nobody wrote down.

**Unpriced consequences:**
- The floor has no mechanism in the ruling. Vega's amendment
  carries the enforcement: if a device yields `maxTravel <
  180dp`, "the host must widen the bar (drop the 12dp box
  padding) before any zone number is re-cut" (vega.md,
  Amendment A). The ruling adopts "180dp floor" (L7) and never
  adopts the widening duty — on a narrow device the floor is a
  word, and no fallback is named.
- Two-authority divergence until the dated amendment lands: a
  builder reading REGISTER.md builds 180dp; a builder reading
  the Slice file builds fractions. Neither document names which
  wins in the interim (Build-ready line 18 says the register
  governs *except* §6 forks — the fork exists, the register
  entry does not).

## F6 · STAMP GRADE — LIVE BIND adopted with Ren's truth condition

**Verdict: CHARGED S2 — the worst charge in this audit.** This
is the one ruling where the chair departed from the
recommendation in substance, on no new evidence, and the
departure cannot be executed as written.

**The recommendation he departed from:** Marlowe 0826 fork 4 and
Build-ready §6 F6 (lines 676–680): "Rec: LIVE BIND — a
framework-held bind plus this residency's processed events is
not a self-report; END-TO-END stays forbidden on this evidence
either way." Flat adoption was available. The ruling instead
reads (Ruling letter L15–21): LIVE BIND "only where the verdict
reads SYSTEM-side state (the binding as the system reports it).
Any link whose evidence is the service merely asserting itself
stamps PROOF: SELF-REPORT… If the build finds a link that cannot
meet the condition, that link's stamp drops."

**Why it is unenforceable as written — three independent
failures, each with proof:**

1. **There is no per-link stamp to drop.** The grade in the
   files is a single seam mark for the BLOCK cell (rosa.md
   §3.2 table: one right mark per cell state — ON → `PROOF:
   LIVE BIND`, UNKNOWN room i → `PROOF: BIND ONLY`, etc.) and
   Opal's seam grade word for t=0 (opal.md §8, F-OPAL-1).
   Opal's three cascade links (Build-ready D8) carry
   **CONFIRMED timestamps** on the `elapsedRealtime` clock, not
   grade words. No surface in the build-ready file renders a
   stamp per link, so "that link's stamp drops" instructs the
   builder to invent a surface — unpriced, unspecced, and
   unowned by any seat.
2. **No checker, no check time, no test.** Nothing in the
   ruling, Build-ready §5 (build order), Opal's §F7 gate
   checklist (Build-ready D8: proving-time drop, removal trial,
   forced-jam/log audit), or Rosa's test list (rosa.md §5:
   state-table combinations, stamp-string selection *per cell
   state*) assigns anyone to classify a link's evidence as
   system-side vs self-asserted, at build time or any other
   time. A condition with no checker is a hope printed as law.
3. **The binary fails on the file's own witnesses — every link
   is mixed.** L1 SESSION-ROW (opal.md §link table): insert +
   snapshot read of the app's *own DAO* — the app asserting its
   own row, neither system report nor service self-assertion.
   L2 SHIELD-PROTECTING: a `@Volatile protecting` flag the
   service sets *itself*, albeit after `startForeground`
   returns. L3 A11Y-SESSION-ARMED: a system callback
   (`onServiceConnected`, instance) *plus* the service's own
   Flow collector stamping `armedMono`. The ruling gives no rule
   for mixed evidence, and mixed is 3 of 3. Applied literally to
   the cascade, the t=0 grade collapses toward SELF-REPORT —
   while Rosa's BLOCK cell, whose definition already rests on
   system-mediated facts (framework bind callbacks, a root
   acquired from the system, live grant cross-checks — rosa.md
   §3.2 grade definitions), prints LIVE BIND pre-arm. Two grades
   for one proof story is exactly the confusion Rosa's grammar
   was built to prevent (rosa.md §7: under the losing option the
   grades must stay distinguishable or the distinction "the
   residency stamp was built to print" dies).

**The condition also solves a problem the file had already
solved.** Rosa's filed definition of LIVE BIND "claims liveness
+ pipeline and expressly not an intercept" (rosa.md §3.2), and
END-TO-END was already forbidden by both converging seats. The
chair's addition adds no truth the definition lacked; it adds
an unclassifiable per-link test the build cannot run.

**Unpriced consequences:**
- A builder blocked on classification must either stall the
  cascade (Slice D step 4 in Build-ready §5, on which E1's call
  site and D9's Strike hang) or invent the classification
  privately — two builders will invent it differently.
- If any link is stamped SELF-REPORT on a surface the builder
  invents for it, the app gains a printed confession with no
  filed grammar, no fit/degrade rule (Rosa's seam fit rule,
  rosa.md §3.2, governs two marks, not per-link marks), and no
  test.

## ACQUITTALS

Filed plainly, with proof, so the charges above cannot be read
as a general indictment of the letter:

1. **F1's geometry, F19's arithmetic, F20's re-base, F21's
   restore, F2's powered default, F5's merged-form-ships** —
   each follows the evidence its own file presents and matches
   Marlowe 0826 forks 1–6 in substance. The chair overrode no
   seat's numbers on any of the six. (Build-ready §6 lines
   661–695 against Ruling letter L7–27, line by line.)
2. **F6's END-TO-END prohibition** — unanimous in the evidence
   (rosa.md §7 Option A; opal.md §8 F-OPAL-1; Build-ready §6
   F6) and correctly retained by the chair (Ruling letter L19).
   Acquitted without reservation.
3. **F6's LIVE BIND for Rosa's BLOCK cell as Rosa defined it**
   — the grade as filed rests on system-mediated evidence
   (framework bind callbacks; a root acquired through the
   system, stamped before the own-package return at
   MonolithAccessibilityService.kt:41; live grant cross-checks),
   and its definition expressly disclaims an intercept
   (rosa.md §3.2). That portion of the adoption stands; the
   charge is solely on the chair's added per-link condition.
4. **The specular register correction (Ruling letter L49–54)**
   — confirmed from the chair's own B2 build, grep-verified by
   Mara (Build-ready §3-R9), and actually transmitted to the
   register's keeper (`mail/sana/0905__from-ren__register-
   correction-specular.md`). This is the lawful correction
   route, used correctly — and it is the contrast that proves
   the F1 charge: the chair knew how to correct the register,
   and for travel and room he announced/adopted without sending
   the line.
5. **F2's reversal price** — Fen priced the kill in advance
   (animator is the only casualty; rest contract survives under
   both outcomes, fen.md §7). The chair adopted a fork whose
   exit was already paid for. Acquitted of adopting an
   unpriced reversal.
6. **No invented evidence anywhere in the letter.** Every
   number the chair cites (821, 841, 650ms, 45ms, 1dp, ~1,550 /
   ~1,700) traces to the build-ready file or a seat part. The
   letter's failures are failures of condition, scope and
   completion-claims — not of fabrication.
