# MONOLITH — the whole plan, in plain words

*What the app is, screen by screen, and what it means for your
day. Nothing here needs any inside knowledge. If a line sounds
strange, that is the plan's fault, not yours.*

## What this app is

Monolith is a blocker. It holds the apps and websites you choose
shut for a set time, so a study session stays a study session.

It is **not** a study tracker. It does not grade your days, keep
scores, or judge you. Your study hours live in your timer app
and your syllabus lives in GMC — this app never touches either
job. Its one record is simple: the tasks you finish are stored,
so months or years later you can open the drawer and see what
you did.

You set the aim for it yourself: **make it art — the most
beautiful, best app.** That is the standard every part below was
built to.

## The rules underneath everything

- **Sealed means sealed.** Once a session starts, the blocked
  apps stay shut until the time is up. No call gets through —
  not family, not anyone. You call back after.
- **The app never lies to you.** It checks that blocking is
  really working before it lets a session start. If it cannot
  check, it says so in plain words instead of pretending.
- **If blocking breaks mid-session, the app stays silent.** No
  alarm, no warning on screen, the clock keeps running to the
  scheduled end. You find out once, at the end, on the receipt.
  That session is written down as broken — never dressed up as
  a clean finish.
- **The app does not know what a test day is.** It has no idea
  when your tests are, and nothing in it is allowed to need
  that idea. A Saturday looks like any other day to it, unless
  you yourself change a routine.
- **Nothing of yours is ever quietly deleted.** Finished tasks
  are kept permanently. Even the app's own database is built so
  that if an update cannot carry your records across, it fails
  loudly instead of wiping them.

## The bottom bar

Four stops: **DECK · SHIELD · TASKS · LEDGER** — and at the far
end, a small extra notch called **PARK**. PARK is not a fifth
screen. It is a utility drawer: permissions, repairs, sound,
and theme live there (see PARK below).

At the top of every screen, one small quiet line tells you the
state of your four permissions — it reads `GUARD 4/4` when all
four are in place. If one drops, the line names it. That is the
only place this is ever shown; there is no separate Guard
screen anymore.

## Screen 1 — DECK (where a session starts)

This is the home screen and the arming screen. On it:

- **The total, once, big.** The full time you are about to
  commit — blocks and the short rests between them added up
  honestly — written once, in the biggest type in the app. It
  appears nowhere else in disguise.
- **FINISHES.** Under the total, the clock time the session
  will end, worked out from the total. It is arithmetic, not a
  promise, and it is printed as arithmetic.
- **The wheel.** A big wheel you turn with your thumb to set
  how long each block is — 5 to 180 minutes, in 5-minute steps.
  It has weight: more blocks make it feel heavier, and it
  settles with a soft knock you can feel. One setting, FLOW,
  means no limit.
- **The dots in the middle of the wheel.** Eight small wells
  that hold your block count — tap to add a block, up to 8.
  A group of four fills in as a solid bar. The count lives
  here and nowhere else.
- **The next line.** What is coming next — for example the
  morning routine — and when it fires.
- **The task line.** The task lined up for this session, one
  line. Nothing on it can be tapped or changed mid-session.
- **The slide.** At the bottom, the slide you drag to start.
  A tap can also start it: tap once, and the cap travels on its
  own; tap again to undo before it seats. A quick accidental
  brush does nothing.

**Starting is checked, every time.** The moment you slide or
tap, the app reads whether blocking is truly alive — right
then, not from a memory of an earlier check. Three outcomes:

- Blocking is on → the session seals.
- Blocking is off → the slide stops short, and one sentence
  appears under it: `BLOCKING IS OFF — TAP TO FIX`.
- The app cannot tell → the slide stops the same way, with
  `CAN'T CHECK BLOCKING — TAP TO RETRY`.

There is no fourth outcome and no quiet override. If a
permission is dead, the fix is one tap from that sentence —
never a popup, never a spent slide.

## Screen 2 — SHIELD (your desk when rested)

Shield is where everything is arranged while no session runs.
Nothing here can be changed mid-session; this is the rested
desk, not a control panel for a running lock.

- **One verdict line.** A single sentence about the last
  finished session — for example: *Nothing tried to get in.* /
  *Everything that tried was turned away.* / *The Shield was
  off for part of that session.* One sentence, dated, replaced
  by the next session. No counts are kept here, ever.
- **The sets (loadouts).** A control that chooses which set of
  blocked things a session will hold — the set is chosen here,
  before the day is read. A choice lands when the control
  settles, not a split-second before it arrives, so what you
  see selected is what is selected.
- **Your routines.** Rows in a time column: name, days, start
  and end, and what the routine holds shut. The starting set is:
  - **FASTED BLOCK** — 05:00–08:00, every day
  - **DAY BLOCKS** — 09:00–19:30, every day
  - **SLEEP** — 20:00–03:30, every day

  Every part of a routine is editable by you — times, days,
  what it blocks. A routine never starts a session on its own
  in a way that surprises you: sessions inside a routine
  window are still started by your slide, and a routine window
  opening by itself never seals anything or starts counting.
- **What the rows remember.** Each routine row keeps a small
  memory of itself: whether it ran, ran partly, or did not
  run — in plain neutral words, never as a score and never as
  a scolding. The app never pauses a routine by itself; if a
  routine is paused, your hand paused it.
- **A strip under the rows** draws your day as a simple column
  shadow — which hours hold routines, which are free.
- **Apps and sites.** The working lists: which apps are
  blocked, which websites are blocked. Each list is edited
  with a sliding switch control. These lists are the lists the
  session actually enforces — what you see here is exactly
  what will be held shut, no second hidden copy.
- Shield uses no orange anywhere. Orange in this app means one
  thing only: committing — starting something.

## Screen 3 — TASKS (your list)

- **The top number** is the total of the task you are on plus
  everything queued — *if you started now, this much work is
  lined up* — with the finishing time printed beside it, as
  arithmetic. Finished tasks never count into it.
- **The queue.** Your tasks as ruled rows. The one you are on
  sits in a raised panel with its steps inside it; each step
  is a row you tap to mark done. You can add steps on the spot.
- **Finishing a task tears it.** Each row is topped by a
  perforated edge, like a ticket stub. Tap DONE and the row
  tears off — drops and fades — and the task is stored (see
  the Drawer). That tear is the moment of finishing; nothing
  else celebrates it.
- **A mistaken finish can be undone.** Right after you finish
  a task, one line under the queue shows what you just
  finished, with a REOPEN on it. It shows only the latest one,
  and only while no session is running. Reopening takes the
  task back out of the Drawer, as if the finish had not
  happened.
- **Adding a task.** The writing line at the top is always
  there, always saved as you type — close the app mid-sentence
  and the half-written task is still there when you return. You
  set its minutes on a fine dial. Filing it drops it into the
  queue with a stamp and a single sound.
- **Empty is just empty.** No tasks queued prints a plain ruled
  blank: `NOTHING QUEUED`. No pictures, no pep talk.

## Screen 4 — LEDGER, which opens THE DRAWER

The fourth stop keeps its old name on the bar, but what it
opens is a drawer, not a ledger book.

- **What is stored:** every task you finish — its title as it
  was, its group, its notes and steps, how long you gave it,
  how long it actually took, and when it finished. Sessions are
  not stored here; tasks are.
- **Forever.** Nothing in the Drawer is ever deleted by age.
  There is no clear button. What you finished at 19 is there
  when you are a doctor, if you want it.
- **How it opens:** this month's finished tasks, newest first,
  grouped under plain date headings — `THU 29 OCT` and so on.
  A heading is a shelf label. No totals hang off it, no grade,
  nothing summed.
- **ALL MONTHS** is an index of month labels — addresses for
  finding things, nothing more. A month with nothing stored
  does not appear. Fifty entries show at a time; `EARLIER`
  loads the fifty before.
- **Search.** One field: `TYPE TO FIND A TASK`. It searches
  titles, groups, notes, and steps, across every year, oldest
  promise in the app: type a word, find the task. Results keep
  their full date, and each carries one line of the note that
  matched, so you can recognise it.
- **A day can carry your word.** If you marked a day REST, ILL,
  or FAMILY, that word stands beside the date — your word about
  your day, changeable all day, never turned into a number or a
  judgement. A day you never marked has no line at all. There
  is no mark for a test day. There never will be.
- **While a session runs,** the Drawer can be read but not
  touched: search, reopening, and editing all rest until the
  session ends.

## While a session runs

Starting a session changes the whole phone face. Everything
from the Deck — wheel, slide, menus — is not dimmed; it is
simply not drawn. Five things exist, and nothing else:

1. A small state word at the top: `RUNNING` — or, during a
   rest, `BREAK` with its countdown.
2. The current task, one or two lines. It offers nothing to
   tap. It changes only when the session itself moves to the
   next block.
3. `REMAINING` — the time left, in the app's biggest numerals.
   This number is honest: if extra time was ever added to a
   session, the remaining time counts it. It is worked out
   from one single calculation that every screen shares, so no
   two screens can ever disagree about the time.
4. A ring, with the emergency valve nested in its gap (below).
5. A second reading: how long has passed, and when it finishes.

**Rests between blocks.** Between blocks — never after the
last one — you can take a 10-minute Safe Break. The word BREAK
and its countdown show on the running screen and on the Deck.
During a break, nothing about the session's record changes;
the break is a pause inside the session, not an ending.

**Held messages.** Notifications that arrive during a session
are held. You may open the held list and **read everything in
it, fully** — reading is free. But the list is frozen at the
moment you first opened it: nothing in it opens, nothing can
be cleared, and anything newer waits silently until the
session ends. When the session is over, the list is live
again.

**The desk clock (always-on display).** When the phone sits
with its always-on display, the clock face shows the session:
`REMAINING` in huge dim numerals, with how far along things
are kept truthful. The picture slowly changes position so it
never burns into the screen, and it dims in stages the longer
a session runs. During a rest break, this face does not change
at all — the break shows on the phone's screens, not here.
With FLOW (no limit), this face shows only how long has
passed — there is no remaining to show, so it invents none.

## The emergency break

One break per week. That is the whole allowance, and the app
treats it as a written record, not a counter that can be
reset: every use is written into a permanent list inside the
app, and the week's remaining break is simply *one minus what
the list says*. Restarting the phone changes nothing. There is
no way to delete a line from that list.

To use it, you hold the valve — a wide bar on the running
screen — filling 60 seconds of held time:

- Every 15 seconds held is **banked**: a tone drops in pitch
  at 15, 30, and 45, and that part is yours to keep.
- If your hand slips, there is a short grace, then the
  un-banked part drains away — slowly at first, then faster —
  down to the last banked mark, never below it. Grab again and
  the drain stops at once.
- Only a full 60 held seconds spends the break and ends the
  session. Slips, drains, and giving up write nothing down and
  cost nothing.
- If the week's break is already spent, the valve is not even
  there. In its place: a plate that says the break is SPENT.

The words on that screen are fixed and plain. With the break
available: *"This ends the session early. It is the only break
this week."* Under the valve: *"Hold to fill 60 seconds. Every
15 seconds banks. If your hand slips, what isn't banked drains
away. The banked part stays."* Once spent: *"This week's break
is already spent. Sessions end when their time does."*

If a break ends a session that a routine had framed, the
routine's own memory is settled honestly in the same moment —
attended, partly, or not — never by a rough guess from the
clock.

## The finish receipt

When a session ends — on time, or by the break — you get a
receipt: how long it ran and how it ended.

- A session that ran its full time finishes clean, with a
  single completion sound.
- A session whose blocking died midway is booked **BROKEN**,
  with when it broke. It gets no completion sound and no clean
  wording anywhere. This receipt is the one and only place that
  failure is told.
- A session ended by the emergency break shows it on the
  receipt: `BROKEN AT {time} — HOLD SPENT`.
- After the receipt, the start slide travels home on its own,
  slowly, and the app is back at the Deck.

## PARK — the repair drawer

Slide the bottom bar past LEDGER, or tap the `GUARD` line at
the top, and PARK opens. It holds:

- **The board.** Every permission and every blocking part,
  one row each, with its state in plain words — working,
  missing, or stale (measured too long ago to trust). Where
  something needs action there is a FIX key; FIX takes you to
  the exact phone settings page for that one thing, and when
  you come back, that row — only that row — is checked again
  fresh.
- **Sound.** The app's sound controls and readouts.
- **Theme.** Shown as AUTO — whichever routine or schedule
  owns the theme right now. There is no manual theme switch
  to resurrect.
- At the very bottom, a small maker's plate, like the plate
  stamped on a machine: the app's name and unit marks. It is
  furniture — identity, not information.

PARK opens by itself the very first time you run the app, so
setup walks the permissions there. While a session is running,
PARK cannot open at all.

## Your day, in this app

- **03:30 — you wake.** The app's day also starts at 03:30 —
  the Drawer's dates follow your day, not the calendar's
  midnight. Nothing in the app needs you yet.
- **04:00–05:00 — meditation.** Your phone is across the room.
  The plan knows this and asks nothing of the app in that hour.
- **05:00 — the FASTED BLOCK routine's window opens.** It does
  not seal anything by itself. When you sit down, you slide,
  the app checks blocking, and the session starts.
- **Morning study** runs in the blocks you set on the wheel —
  rests between blocks if you take them, held messages frozen
  if you peek, the desk clock showing remaining if the phone
  sits face-up.
- **08:00 — gym; then sadhana; then food.** No routine fights
  this: the morning window has ended, the day window is yours
  to use or leave.
- **Day blocks, 09:00–19:30** — the DAY BLOCKS window. Same
  law: the window frames the day; your slide starts each
  session.
- **Saturday — a test day.** To the app, it is a Saturday.
  Nothing is marked, nothing is paused for you, no special
  face appears. If you want a routine quiet that day, you
  quiet it yourself, with your own hand, in its days.
- **20:00 — SLEEP.** The sleep routine holds the night set
  shut until 03:30. The day is done; the app says nothing
  about how it went. It never will.

## What this app will never do

- Never know, guess, or mark a test day.
- Never grade a day, keep a streak, or total you up.
- Never delete a finished task, and never wipe your records
  silently — a broken update fails loudly instead.
- Never let a call or message through a sealed session.
- Never count your attempts at the emergency valve. Only the
  one completed break is written down.
- Never pause, move, or edit a routine by itself.
- Never pretend it checked blocking when it could not check.
- Never announce a blocking failure while you are inside a
  session. Once, at the end, on the receipt.

## Not decided, held, or waiting

- **A second device (sync).** You said you want one — a
  tablet — and then you said: hold this feature, finish
  everything else first. So nothing syncs. Everything lives
  on this phone until you lift the hold.
- **Answer keys held till Sunday.** You tapped "hold them
  till Sunday" — and then questioned the whole idea yourself,
  out loud: why would the app hold answer keys at all, when it
  does not block all day? It stands undecided, exactly there.
  Nothing is built on it.
- **The Shield knob.** One control on Shield still has two
  possible shapes — a turning knob or a sliding cap. You said:
  show me both pictures first. The pictures come before any
  building; the pick is yours when you see them.
- **Reordering your task list.** Whether the app may ever
  shuffle your queue on its own is an open question in the
  plan. Today it never does.

## What already exists, and what is still being built

Already built and in the app: the wheel and its feel, the
block dots, the perforated task rows, the once-a-week break
kept as a written record, and the truth fixes — remaining time
that counts added time, a desk clock that no longer invents
progress.

Being built now: the routines rebuilt properly underneath,
the Drawer and the frozen held-messages sheet, the new slide,
and the PARK drawer with the rebuilt bottom bar.

Still only pictures and words: everything in "not decided"
above, and the final polish pass that makes each screen
gallery-clean before it is called done.
