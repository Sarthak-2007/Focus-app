## Three laws the supplement stands on

- **L-A · A running sheet prints its STAMPED figures, never the
  setting.** (Signal's mid-flight law, adopted; Freya §3.4 is its
  machinery proof — an edit prices the future only, because no

  print law below is this law applied.
- **L-B · A warning lives only where his eyes are not.** Peek is
  spent in another app; a break is spent away from the desk; a
  routine arms itself while he is mid-sentence somewhere else.
  Those ends get a warning *before* the fact. An end he is
  already looking at (a block ending on the sheet in front of

  §2.5 rules that case out by name, with the argument.
- **L-C · The warning form is promotion + one knock, never
  colour, never a phrase.** The affected figure steps exactly
  one rank up the line hierarchy (§0 law 1: hairline → working,
  muted → full ink, working frame → structure weight) and the

  fired once per end per state. Never red: red is the

  correction — nothing here is wrong. Never a chime, never a
  repeated knock, never a buzz phrase: a measurement does not

## 1. K-5 SETTLED IN GRAMMAR — peek and break as figured, settable dimensions

*Klar's finding, now six of six: no world had a stance on the two

### 1.1 The seat — argued: a TOLERANCES plate on Shield

**Ruled: the durations live on Shield, as a ruled plate headed
`TOLERANCES`, seated between the loadout cap (§2.3) and ROUTINES
(§2.4).** The argument, in the grammar's own terms:

- In drafting, a tolerance is the allowed deviation from the
  sealed dimension. That is exactly what these two numbers are:
  the peek is the allowed excursion *outside* the span; the break
  is the allowed gap *inside* it. They are the session machine's
  two tolerances, and a drawing keeps its tolerances in one
  table, beside the parts list they qualify — not scattered
  across the plates that merely *print their consequences* (the
  Deck's hero span, the door's offer, the pill's countdown).
- Shield is where every other settable machinery fact in this
  world already lives: the cap is thrown there, routines are
  figured there, tiers are sledded there, the website list is
  enforced there. A man setting his day opens one plate. The
  durations join that plate's setting cluster — loadout (what),
  tolerances (how far the seal may bend), routines (when),
  apps (the parts) — in that order down the sheet.
- **PARK — ruled out as the seat.** In this world PARK is the
  title block's last cell (§1.13): a navigation verb, where a
  draughtsman parks a sheet. No editor lives in a title block,
  and a setting buried behind the park verb would be found by
  accident or never. PARK keeps its one job.
- **The Deck's tolerance block (§1.9) — ruled out as the seat,
  kept as a print surface.** BLOCK · APPS · TIME is a *proof*
  strip: it states the conditions of the session in front of
  him, read at arm time, per-session, stamped (L-A). An editor
  there would put a future-pricing control on a present-tense
  proof — and at 03:30, half-awake, a fat thumb on a tolerance
  figure must never be able to re-price tomorrow while trying
  to read today. The block prints; the plate on Shield sets.

### 1.2 The plate — exact form

One ruled table, two rows, in the Figured Field instrument
family (§3 of the filed spec — the same instrument that sets
routine times; no new control is invented):

```
TOLERANCES
PEEK    [ 2 MIN ]    0–30 · 0 = OFF
BREAK   [ 10 MIN ]   0–60 · 0 = NO GAPS
```

- Each row: the word (Atkinson, working weight), the figured
  field (Anonymous Pro, right-aligned, working frame — the
  editor's dress from §2.5), and the tolerance range figured in
  the margin at muted tier, including what zero *means*, in
  words, on the row itself. A bound a man has to remember is a
  bound the drawing failed to print.
- **Setting:** tap the figure → keypad, or step by 1 / 5 / 15
  (the §2.5 editor's steps, unchanged). The figure follows the
  hand; the pitch ladder (§5) pitches each step by value — the
  ear confirms the number, as everywhere in this world. The
  setters clamp at Freya's bounds (§3.5 of her spec); the field
  can therefore never display an out-of-range figure, and no
  rejection state exists to design.
- **PEEK at 0:** the field prints the word `OFF` in place of a
  figure and the row draws hatched (the disabled routine's
  dress, §2.4 — present, not built). Downstream prints obey at
  their source: the Held Door renders no peek offer and no
  reason is owed (§4.5 point 3's seat stays empty — the offer
  was switched off by his own hand on a labelled plate, which
  is not a silence, it is a setting), and no lease can be
  granted. One fact, figured once, obeyed everywhere.
- **BREAK at 0:** the field prints `0 MIN — NO GAPS` (figure
  and its meaning, one cell). Downstream: the span figure on
  the Deck prices (n−1) × 0 and stays true; the break state is
  simply never entered; the eyebrow never prints BREAK; the
  pill never takes its BREAK state.
- **The cooldown is not a row.** It is the peek's price, not
  its length (Freya §3.7, adopted whole). It prints where it
  already prints — the door (§4.5 point 4) and the pill's last
  frame (§4.6) — figured from the existing derivation, never
  as a third dial. A tolerances plate with a price row would
  teach the hand that every constant is negotiable; two rows
  is the order he gave, and two rows is the plate.

## 2. BEFORE-END WARNINGS IN GRAMMAR — and the register's verbs

*The warning form for every case below is L-C: the figure

### 2.1 The peek — warning at 30 seconds (K-6, K-2's other half)

- **Moment: 30 seconds before the lease's stamped end, once.**
  Argued: a peek is spent typing, reading, mid-thread in
  another app. Thirty seconds is enough to finish a sentence,
  send what is written, and close the app himself — the
  hand-back (§4.2) done by his hand instead of the sniper's.
  Ten seconds (the dead state's detent grain) buys nothing but
  a startle; sixty would land while he still has a minute to
  spend and teach him to ignore it. At the shortest lawful
  peek (1 minute), 30 seconds is the halfway mark — still
  exactly one warning, still lawful. No second warning exists
  at any setting.
- **Form:** the pill is the seat (he is in the other app; the
  in-app mirror is named in the seam note below). The tag's
  hairline frame steps to working weight, the figure steps to
  full ink — one rank, L-C — one seat knock sounds from the
  shield service's ticker, and the figure's grain changes:
  inside the last minute the pill stops figuring whole minutes
  and counts seconds — `PEEK · 30 SEC LEFT`, descending. A
  warning you must do arithmetic on is not a warning; the
  seconds *are* the last minute, figured at the grain the
  minute deserves. At the end itself, the filed behaviour
  stands (§4.6/§5): the firm click, the tag's last frame
  stating the cooldown it starts, dismissal.
- **Seam note:** if he has already returned to Monolith, the
  pill is lawfully absent (Freya §2.4 Q1) and the same
  promotion + knock applies to the lease figure wherever
  Truth Car 3's in-app half prints it (Freya §4.2) — Kleinteil
  specs the form; that half's seat is the car's, and the form
  travels unchanged.

**Disposition: BUILT-IN-LANE (Einbau — pill face, warning
frame; knock and timing ride Freya's §2.2 service driver, the
1 Hz derivation over stored ends — no new ticker, no new
sound).**

### 2.2 The break — warning at 60 seconds; both flips voiced (ST-03)

- **Moment: 60 seconds before the break's end, once.** Argued
  against the peek's 30: a break is spent *away* — water,
  stairs, another room (his day: breaks sit between blocks at
  a desk he has left). The return is a walk, not a thumb-move;
  sixty seconds is the walk-back budget. Thirty would strand
  him mid-corridor with the next block already sealing around
  peek apps under his thumbs — the register's exact bite.
- **Form:** the pill's BREAK state — frame and figure promote
  one rank, one seat knock, and inside the last minute the

  shape for every away-state. In-app, the Deck eyebrow (§1.2)
  applies the same promotion to its figure — one fact, one
  form, two seats, no new element anywhere.
- **The flips themselves (ST-03's charge: neither boundary
  made a sound):** the filed world already voices both flips
  *at* the fact — §5: break start and break end, one firm
  click each. This supplement confirms that as the answer for
  the start and adds the before-warning for the end, and
  rules the remaining case out explicitly: **no warning
  sounds before a break *starts*** — that knock would land in
  the block's last minute, taxing study to announce a rest he
  will hear begin one minute later anyway. The block's end
  and the break's start are one instant; one click at it is
  the whole truth.

**Disposition: BUILT-IN-LANE (Einbau — pill BREAK state;
Ausbau — Deck eyebrow promotion) for print and promotion;

here by name.**

### 2.3 ST-02 — END BREAK: the verb, its seat, its form

The verb is a *word*, not a glyph — it advances the session
(the next block starts now), a state change with a
consequence, and in this grammar consequential acts are
spelled (§4.2 sets the ✕/word distinction as law).

- **Seat 1 — the pill (primary; he is in other apps):** the
  BREAK-state tag divides into two cells by a working-weight
  rule: figure cell `BREAK · N MIN LEFT`, verb cell
  `END BREAK` as an **outline key** — never a solid plate (the
  Commit Plate's uniqueness, §1.10, survives: ending a break
  is a mercy, not a commitment, and no tag cell may wear the
  seal's dress). Verb cell minimum touch width 48 dp; the
  rest of the tag keeps §4.6's law — dismisses only at its
  state's end, never by accidental touch; the verb cell is

  boundary — the firm click at the flip (§5), the pill

### 2.5 The block's end — before-warning RULED OUT BY NAME

No knock sounds before a block ends. Argument: he is *at* the
sheet for a block — the Deck is in front of him or the session
is the room he is in; L-B's condition (eyes elsewhere) fails.
The block's end is already one of the best-voiced moments in
the app: an interior block's end is the break's start (firm
click, §2.2 above); the final block's end is the finish,
which has its own witnessed voices (the completion pulse, the
tear — §5, and the register's acquittal: a finish the app

T−60 knock inside a block would be the supervisor behaviour
Noir's rejected chime argued against — and Blueprint accepts
that argument *for the at-desk case*, while noting it was
never an argument against warning a man who cannot see the
end coming (§§2.1–2.2, 2.4). The refusal and the warning are
different objects; this world keeps them apart, by name, in
both directions.

**Disposition: RULED OUT BY NAME (before-warning at a block's

### 3.2 ST-01, first half — the ISSUED stamp, K-8 satisfied inseparably

The filed answer stands (§1.12: the title block carries
`ISSUED HH:MM`, sampled at composition, labelled as issue,
never ticking — A6, ~10 lines, already priced). This
supplement closes Klar's K-8 condition on it, as a drafting
rule with no exceptions:

- **Label and figure are one cell, one string, one
  composable.** The title-block cell prints `ISSUED 19:41`
  as a single text — never a label element beside a figure
  element, never two spans that a layout pass could divorce.
  If the cell is ever too narrow, the cell widens; the word
  is never abbreviated (`ISS.` prints nowhere), never
  demoted in size or weight below the figure it qualifies,
  never muted while the figure stays full ink. A stale
  figure that still reads as a clock is UNA2-03 in
  miniature — the label is what makes the stamp honest, so
  the label is part of the figure.
- **Re-issue is lawful:** each composition stamps its own
  issue; turning away and back re-composes and re-stamps.
  The stamp claims the sheet's birth, never the present
  moment, and under the inseparability rule it cannot be
  misread as claiming more.

**Disposition: BUILT-IN-LANE (Ausbau — Deck title block;

- **Print:** one figure — the current wall time, `HH:MM`,

### 4.2 ST-08 — the peek hand-back: the ✕ print spec (Einbau's face), and the grant's launch

Two halves: the return (a print, specced here) and the
departure (machinery, proposed).

**The ✕ — exact print spec for the pill tag's PEEK state:**

- The PEEK-state tag divides into two cells by a
  working-weight rule: figure cell `PEEK · N MIN LEFT`
  (counting per §4.6, seconds-grain inside the warning
  minute per §2.1) and verb cell containing one glyph —
  **✕** — at working weight, minimum touch width 48 dp,
  spoken label "End peek". The glyph is a draughtsman's
  cancel mark: it strikes the lease, it does not advance
  anything — which is why it is a mark and not a word,
  the mirror of §2.3's law (advance = word, return =
  mark; the hand learns the pair once).
- The ✕ exists **only** in the PEEK state. The SHIELD
  state has nothing to hand back; the BREAK state has its
  own spelled verb. A ✕ that appeared in every state would
  read as "dismiss" — and the tag dismisses only at its
  state's end (§4.6), a law the ✕ does not break: tapping
  it *ends the state* through the existing early-end verb
  (Freya §2.1: `recordPeekEnded`), after which the tag
  takes its filed last frame — `COOLDOWN 20 MIN`, the
  constant figured from the derivation, Freya §3.7 — and
  dismisses. The hand-back and the cooldown print are one
  gesture, witnessed on the glass where the peek was spent.
- The tag's body (figure cell) tap keeps Freya §2.1's
  semantics: return to Monolith. It is never a launcher
  for the peeked app.

**The departure — PRICED PROPOSAL (machinery, small):** at
grant, one launch intent for the peeked package — the

that buys the lease lands him *in* the app, the pill's
first frame already counting over it, and the paid seconds

register's bite, priced small there: the intercept launches

The door's peek key keeps its face (`PEEK n MIN`, §1.3);
only its landing changes.

**Disposition: ✕ face and body-tap print BUILT-IN-LANE
