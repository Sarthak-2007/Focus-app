## 1. THE SENTENCE, DEFENDED — one page, no more

A drawing is the only one of the seven forms that tells the truth
about what this app is holding. At 03:30 the day does not exist.
Nothing has been studied, blocked, or built. What exists is an
intention specified exactly enough to be executed — wake, meditation,
the fasted block, gym, sadhana, the day blocks, SLEEP at 20:00. That
is not a thing, a place, or an event. It is a *plan of a thing*, and
the plan's native form, in every culture that builds, is a drawing.

Not a poster. Signal's poster announces a finished message to a
public; it persuades, at scale, in public space. His day is not an
announcement and he is not an audience — a poster of a day would be
the day performing itself before it happens, and its grammar
(headline first, everything else microprint) ranks by loudness,
where a day must rank by measurement. Not stone. Temple's stone is
what a decision becomes *after* it is beyond revision: you do not
edit a carving at midnight, you re-cut it. His day is edited at
midnight — routines moved, windows redrawn — and a world that makes
every edit a re-cutting teaches him his plan is more permanent than
it is. Not a machine. Maschine is the strongest rival and the
clearest error: the machine is real, but it is the *substrate* —
the blocking stack every world shares, invisible, already built.
The face he plans with is not the engine; it is the sheet the
engine will execute. Confusing the plan with the apparatus is how
an app starts admiring its own gears instead of stating his hours.
Glass, sky, and city fail the same way, faster: they describe a
material, a weather, a place. His problem at 03:30 is none of
those. It is: *is the day specified, is it specified truly, and
can I check the specification against the thing it specifies?*

A drawing answers all three in its grammar, which is why this
world's laws are not decoration laid over the truth laws — they
are the same laws. One fact, one rendering *is* the draughtsman's
rule that a dimension is figured once, in one seat, and read off
the line everywhere else. Nothing prints that no fact backs *is*
nothing drawn that was not measured. A refusal is not an alarm in
a drawing; it is an annotation in red, because the drawing is not
failing — it is correcting the hand before the build goes wrong.
A finished day is not scored; it is filed as-built, the issued
sheet and the built day laid side by side, departures in red,
nothing totalled and nothing forgiven. Even the world's shame
fits: a routine that never fired is a seal not witnessed, a line
the drawing refuses to fake (§1.12 of the spec) — a poster would
have shouted over the gap, stone would have carved it permanent,
a machine would have hidden it in a log.

And the form fits the man, not just the app. He is building a
future that does not exist — a doctor, AIR 1, a white coat — out
of identical days, each one drawn the night before and built the
day after. A drawing is the one artefact that is *about* a future
thing without lying that the thing exists: it carries scale,
tolerance, and revision on its face, it expects to be corrected,
and it is judged, in the end, by one test only — whether what got
built matches what was drawn. That is the app. That is the year.
The sentence stands.

## 2. THE BORROWED-FURNITURE AUDIT — spec + build plan

**Method, stated once.** Three classes, never blurred:

- **Substrate** — trunk machinery, house law, and Sarthak's own
  verdicts (the wheel, the slide, the seal's shared sound, the
  sliding cap he picked, the font law, burn-in drift, Nacht's AOD
  ceiling). Every world stands on this ground. It is not furniture
  and cannot be "borrowed"; a verdict below says *substrate* where
  an element is only this.
- **Exports** — Blueprint is named the organ bank in Wetter's
  register, and the register proves the direction of travel: the
  Commit Plate went to Obsidian (§1.5) and was argued for Signal
  (§1.9); line hierarchy to Dawn (§1.12); dimension lines to Noir
  (§1.14); structural hairlines became Temple's scale-of-the-day
  frieze (§1.15). Those are debts *owed to* this world, not by it.
  Nothing below re-audits them as imports.
- **Furniture** — an element whose *form-language* grew in another
  world. Each gets: home address, evidence, and one verdict —
  **KEEP** (naturalised: I say how it became ours), **CUT**, or
  **ARGUE** (sent to Riss, §2.3 collects them).

### 2.1 The four elements the brief named

**A. The receipt's loved paper character — KEEP, naturalised.**
Home address: *no world.* The slip predates all seven looks — it
is trunk, batch-7, and Sarthak's ❤️ (Oct 29, 20:12 IST) lit on it
before any look existed. Proof of substrate status: every other
world also claims it in its own grammar (Noir's "print is print"
corollary, Signal's white slip, Temple's receipt sheet, Maschine's
paper tape) — a thing all worlds inherit is ground, not furniture.
Why it stays, stated without apology: his love is a verdict, and
verdicts rank above purity. Purity's job here is naming, not
deleting. How it is naturalised in the spec (§4.3): the slip is
re-seated as the drawing's **revision slip** — the small printed
record pinned to an issued sheet — with its letterhead redrawn,
its № figured, its derived figures bracketed. The fence that
keeps the naturalisation honest: paper character (narrow measure,
torn edge, paper feel) is confined to this one loose sheet and to
its counterfoil kin (the Tasks tear, §4.1) — i.e. to *paper
documents*. It never spreads to a plate. A plate that gains paper
grain, a torn edge, or slip typography fails Test 3 in §3.
Spec §4.3 already holds this fence in one line ("the slip is the
one surface allowed to feel like paper"); this audit ratifies it
as the clause that makes the KEEP lawful.

**B. The desk clock's span figures — KEEP, naturalised.**
The facts (ELAPSED, REMAINING) are substrate — his kept AOD study
display. The risk is the *form*: a clock is an instrument, and
instrument dials are Maschine's furniture (the Works Clock dial,
the counter drums) and Obsidian's (figures as lit objects in
glass). The spec (§4.4) does not borrow either: there is no dial,
no hand, no circular face, no drum. The two figures sit in the
§1.3 dimension seat, tied by the same hairline dimension line
that ties the Deck's hero to its wheel, filling as the span is
consumed. A span on a sheet, not a face on an instrument — the
clock has been *drawn*, not built. The night ground it sits on is
this world's own inversion (§0: the night plate is the true
cyanotype — white line on indigo — where the day plate is its
whiteprint twin; two reprographic processes of one office, which
also answers §6.3's porcelain risk in kind). The 55% composite /
0.238 figure is Nacht's ceiling — a studio-wide night law, house
law class, not furniture. Fence: the day a circular face, a hand,
or a ticking second appears on this surface, it is Maschine
furniture and it is CUT without a hearing.

**C. The pill's tag — ARGUE to Riss.**
Home address: the workshop, not the drawing office. Spec §4.6
draws the pill as "the tag a draughtsman ties to a part" — a
hairline-ruled **lozenge**. Two charges. (1) A tied tag hangs on a
*part*; parts are Maschine's world ("the valve's tag", the piece's
tag its stub is ripped from — PRESENTATION-maschine). A
draughtsman does not tie anything; he annotates the sheet, in the
margin, with a leader. (2) The lozenge itself breaks this world's
own law: §6.12 keeps the drafted plate's square corners, and no
element anywhere else in the spec is a lozenge — it is a shape
from a keyring, not from a sheet. What is native in §4.6 and must
survive any ruling: the three states, the figures, the bottom-
margin seat, the cooldown stated at the peek's end. My
recommendation to Riss: naturalise by redraw — the pill becomes a
**detached margin strip**: a small rectangular fragment of the
plate itself, frame line on, figured in Anonymous Pro, seated in
the bottom margin exactly as specced — a piece of the drawing
carried outside the app, not a tag tied to a machine part. Rename
follows form: not a tag, a strip. If Riss instead keeps the tied
tag, the file must say "borrowed from the workshop, kept for
hand-feel" — named, per the sharpening order. What it may not do
is stay a lozenge while claiming to be a drawing.

**D. Hatching — split verdict: the section hatch is OURS; the
OFF hatch is not; and it was never Signal's.**
First, the false address, cleared: Signal has no hatching.
Signal's plates are flat colour fields (Wetter §0; PRESENTATION-
signal §0 law 2) — a hatch is not a flat field, and anyone filing
"hatched = Signal plate" has confused a filled area with a ruled
one. Hatching at 45° is section drawing's own convention, older
than every world here: it marks material *cut by the section
plane* — the most present, most solid thing on the sheet. So:
(1) **KEEP** hatch wherever it would mean cut/section, if such a
seat is ever drawn. (2) **ARGUE — the filed uses invert the
meaning.** Spec §2.4 draws a *disabled* routine hatched ("present,
not built") and §2.5 hatches the OFF toggle — but in the grammar
being claimed, hatch means present-and-solid, the opposite of
off. What those seats actually borrow is generic UI's "patterned
= disabled" convention — Material furniture in drawing dress.
The native convention for *present but not to be built / alternate
position* already exists in drafting: the **phantom line**
(dash–dot, thin). Recommendation to Riss: disabled segments and
OFF cells take phantom outline; hatch is struck from both seats.
(3) The same inversion sits inside the spec's own Commit fence:
§1.5 gives FLOW a "solid ink fill… like a hatched section" (solid
is not hatch — the sentence conflates the two) while §1.10 rules
FLOW a "hatched outline." The two sections contradict each other
before any critic arrives. Riss to rule one form; my position:
FLOW takes neither — it is a labelled key that sinks to hairline
in use (§1.5's own cure), and hatch, reserved for section, appears
nowhere on the Deck. (4) Zunder's unadopted B-1 gift (hatched
extension for an overrun, REFINE-zunder) inherits this ruling
when adoption is argued: an overrun is built, not cut — figure it
solid past the plan line with an end tick, not hatched.

### 2.2 The rest of the room, walked element by element

**E. Corner registration marks — KEEP in part, CUT in part.**
Spec §0 and lane `BlueprintPlate.kt` both draw crosshair **plus a
ring** at the corners, the lane comment saying "the way a plate
is aligned on the machine." A target ring is press furniture —
it belongs to Signal's printing press (registration is how plates
are aligned *for printing*) and the lane's own comment betrays
the address ("on the machine"). A drawing sheet's native marks
are its border, zone ticks, and the centre mark — the plus, which
§1.4 already uses lawfully on the wheel. Verdict: the plus
**KEEPS** (centre mark, ours); the ring **CUTS** in both spec
language and lane code at merge. Nobody will miss it; everybody
would have filed it.

**F. The lane's title block — CUT (build plan, not spec).**
The spec puts the title block bottom-right / spanning the bottom
margin (§0, §1.13) — a real sheet's address. The lane code as
built puts a one-line strap at **top-centre**: `DWG NO. … ·
SCALE 1:1 · REV C`. That is the trunk header band in disguise —
the very band §1.1 kills — wearing drawing vocabulary. Worse, two
of its three claims are unmeasured: `SCALE 1:1` is false of a
day (a phone sheet is not the day at full size; a drawing states
its scale truthfully or not at all — this world's own refused
scale-bar, per Zunder, says a drawing states scale once, truly),
and `REV C` figures a revision no fact backs — Zunder's B-2
revision counter, the machinery that *would* make a REV figure
true, is unadopted and unbuilt. Verdict: CUT the strap at merge;
the title block is built where the spec puts it, and a REV cell
prints only when B-2's counter (or an equivalent fact) exists.
Filed here so the merge owns it, per §6's own method.

**G. The lane's dimension rule with no number — CUT as built.**
`BlueprintDimensionRule` draws a dimension line whose own doc
says "with no number… the value itself is stated by the hero
number above." In this grammar that is a second, figure-less
rendering of the wheel's measurement — it violates the spec's
one-fact-one-rendering law and §0 law 5 (nothing unlabelled,
nothing unmeasured) simultaneously: a dimension line that
figures nothing is decoration, the one thing law 1 forbids a
line to be. CUT as a standalone; its drawing work folds into
§1.3's dimension figure, where the figure sits *on* the line.

**H. The hero's size — KEEP, naturalised, with a fence.**
A single dominating figure is shared ground (trunk's 88sp hero;
Obsidian's Monument and Signal's 92sp headline are its cousins),
so size alone is substrate, not theft. What would make it
furniture is *independence*: a monument stands alone, a poster's
headline shouts alone. §1.3's figure cannot be lifted off its
extension lines without losing its meaning — the lines reach to
the wheel it measures, and a mismatch would be visible. That
tether is the naturalisation. Fence, operationalised as Test 2
in §3: if a render's figure still "works" with its lines removed,
it is a monument in Anonymous Pro and the seat has failed.

**I. Shield as parts list / bill of materials — KEEP, ours.**
A bill of materials is a drawing-office document — the parts
list printed on an assembly drawing. It is not Maschine's mill:
Maschine has *parts you touch*; Blueprint has a *list that
describes* parts, on paper, with item numbers and a quantity
column (§2.2's honest split). Same word, opposite substance —
metal vs. record. Fence: the moment a row is rendered as a
physical object rather than a list entry, it has crossed into
the mill (see J).

**J. The Sled (§2.7) — ARGUE to Riss.**
A three-position sliding switch is hardware — Maschine's gates
and Noir's switch-room are its home addresses, and "sled" itself
is trunk/Improver vocabulary (Schlitten's seat), not drafting
vocabulary. The spec mitigates in dress (ruled, labelled, current
position solid) but not in kind: a drawing does not slide a
switch to revise a parts list; it *re-figures the entry*. My
recommendation: the row's tier becomes three figured cells in the
row's margin — the same three-cell language as §2.6's index tabs
and §1.13's plate index — current cell solid, tap to re-figure,
quantity line updating in its one seat. If Riss keeps the sled
for the hand (it is, in fairness, shared trunk machinery — the
cap he picked travels the same way), then it files as
*substrate, named*, and the drawing dress stays as specced. It
may not file as a drafting invention. It is not one.

**K. The day-scale (§2.4) — KEEP, ours; direction of travel
noted.** Wetter §1.15 records Temple adopting this device *from*
Blueprint's hairlines. The scale is the creditor's original, here
at full strength: not an ornament under rows, as Temple took it,
but the summary that replaces the rows. No verdict needed beyond
KEEP; filed so no future audit reverse-charges it.

**L. The approval / HOLD stamp — KEEP, ours, two fences.**
Stamping a drawing is drawing-office practice, not Temple's
ritual (her vermilion *outline* seal authenticates; it is never a
filled verdict) and not Maschine's enamel nameplate. Blueprint's
stamps are ink, rectangular, seated in the title block's revision
cell, and they state a witnessed fact with its time (`SEALED
HH:MM`). The Held Door's diagonal HOLD (§4.5) is likewise native:
construction sheets carry diagonal HOLD / NOT FOR CONSTRUCTION
stamps across the face — the rotation is convention, not theatre
borrowed from Signal's closed sign. Fences: a stamp never takes
gold, vermilion fill, or glow; and a stamp never lands on an
unwitnessed fact (§1.10's register rule; Verlauf's FL-01 filing
in this crew agrees — a stamp on an unwitnessed seal is a
forgery in this world).

**M. The Drawer as register — KEEP, ours.** A drawing register —
issued sheets filed by number and date — is the drawing office's
own book, distinct from Temple's inscribed register (bronze,
seals) by substance: rows, columns `FILED UNDER` / `AT`, figured
entry counts, no inscription voice. The cabinet's death (§4.2)
is ratified from this seat: DRW-01/02/06/07's furniture was Vault
furniture, and cutting it was purity work, not tidiness.

**N. The raised editor sheet (§2.5) — KEEP, naturalised, one
watch.** "Sheet" as a rising surface is generic Material/Obsidian
vocabulary, and a bottom-sheet with elevation and shadow would be
borrowed UI furniture outright. The spec's version is lawful
because it is literal: a loose *paper* sheet on the plate — pure
white, square, hairline-framed, shadowless (§0 law 4; §8 N3 files
its shallow luminance step as a risk carried by its edge, not its
lift). KEEP under that description exactly. Watch, for the
builders: any shadow, radius, or elevation token on this surface
voids the naturalisation and it re-files as CUT.

**O. Recessed wells (search, add-site) — KEEP, rename owed.**
A "well" is Obsidian's and Temple's depth word (unlit wells,
carved recesses); a drawing has no wells. In substance the spec
already naturalised them — §8 N1: the step is read by its closing
hairline (3.17:1), never by its lift. What remains borrowed is
only the noun. KEEP the form; the vocabulary should follow the
substance at merge ("entry rule" / "ruled entry"), so the next
reader is not sent to a glass world for a paper thing.

**P. Everything else, cleared in one pass.** The Turn, the Throw,
the slide, the seal sound, the pitch ladder: substrate and §5
feel law — the ladder is this world's own generalisation
(pitch = f(value): a measurement read by ear), exported nowhere
and borrowed from nowhere; a musical instrument metaphor would
only arise if pitch carried anything but value, and it carries
nothing else. Sora's single seat: house font law, substrate.
Colour app icons inside ruled cells (§2.7): OS substrate, the
only non-ink colour admitted, admitted *as specimens pasted in
cells* — they tint nothing. The `%` deletion, the `%`-twin's
cousins, the fossil parameters (§6): already cut by the spec;
this seat ratifies. Zunder's B-2 revision cloud and B-3 broken
dimension: drafting conventions both, native by design, awaiting
adoption rulings — not furniture, and B-3's bracket grammar
correctly extends §4.3's. Isometric/rendered plate views:
refused by Zunder, refusal ratified — the instant the sheet
pretends to be the building, this world is a renderer, not a
drawing.

## 3. THE PURITY TESTS — what the renders must pass at the end

The soul test (Fremd's), operationalised for this world. Run on
the owed render set (Deck day/night, Shield day/night, Tasks,
Drawer, receipt, desk clock, Held Door, Pill ×3) when the compile
gate re-opens. Every test is failable; a test that cannot fail is
a compliment, and this crew does not file compliments as tests.

**Test 1 — The stripped sheet (soul test, literal).**
Print every render in greyscale, wordmark and plate titles
covered. Hand the set, shuffled, to three readers who have not
seen any look's spec. Pass: all three name the set, unprompted,
as a *technical drawing / drawing-office sheet* — and none names
poster, stone, machine, glass, city, or sky for any frame. Fail
conditions, any one: a frame reads as a poster (figure shouting
without lines — see Test 2), as an instrument panel (dials,
hardware), or as a plain app with a border pasted on. The frame,
title block, dimension chain, and parts list must survive the
stripping *structurally* — if removing colour removes the world,
there was no world, only a palette.

**Test 2 — The referent test (no line without a measured thing).**
On the Deck and desk-clock renders, an auditor points at ten
lines or elements at random. For each, the spec must name, in
one sentence, the fact it figures or the object it measures.
Pass: 10/10. Then the destructive half: in a copy of the Deck,
delete the extension lines and dimension line only. Pass: the
hero figure is now visibly *orphaned* — a number measuring
nothing — proving the lines were load-bearing. Fail: if the
composition reads unchanged after the deletion, the lines were
decoration, law 1 was broken in the original, and the seat is
redrawn. (This is the fence on §2.2H, made checkable.)

**Test 3 — The physical-object census (nothing in the room but
paper).**
List every element in every render that implies a three-
dimensional object. Pass: every item on the list is a lawful
paper-office object — a sheet, a slip, a stamp, a register, a
rule — drawn flat, square, shadowless. Fail, any one sighting:
a lozenge or tied tag (the pill, §2.1C, until Riss rules), a
sliding hardware switch drawn *as* hardware (the sled, §2.2J,
until ruled), a dial face or hand (desk clock, §2.1B fence),
a shadow, an elevation lift, a rounded Material sheet, an
isometric or rendered view. The census is taken from the
renders, not the spec — what is drawn is what is judged.

**Test 4 — The correction test (a drawing can be corrected, and
stays a drawing).**
Stage three renders: a routine edited at midnight (revision
evidence on the day-scale), a refusal state (annotation red in
its reserved slot), and a receipt carrying a derived figure
(bracketed). Pass: all three corrections read *inside* the
grammar — red only where the drawing corrects, phantom/bracket
marks doing their one jobs, the sheet's frame, title block, and
figure seats unmoved — and no state resembles an alarm banner, a
settings form, or an error dialog from any other world. Fail:
red used as decoration or selection anywhere; an edit surface
that reads as a generic form; a derived figure printed
unbracketed even once.

**Test 5 — The night inversion (same sheet, other process).**
Lay each night render beside its day twin, both then stripped
to greyscale. Pass: day and night are the *same sheet* — same
seats, same lines, same hierarchy — the night reading as the
cyanotype twin of the day's whiteprint, with no element gaining
glow, bloom, halo, or lit-sign behaviour, and annotation red
still only correcting. Fail: any night frame that reads as a
city, a glass room, or a lit instrument; any figure that moves
seat, changes alphabet, or grows at night. A drawing office does
not become a different building after dark; it switches the
printing process and keeps drawing.
