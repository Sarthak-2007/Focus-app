# ISSUE #9 — PARK: THE 1 AM TEST

*Feel Specialist 5, holding Muede's seat — the tired human at 1 AM.
Ground: `AUDIT-park.md` (Muede's charges P-1, P-5, P-6), `issue-9-plan.md`,
and `ParkDrawer.kt` as amended in this cell: the CHECK key, the
`NOT PROVED` line, the re-sweep on open, and the header's
`GUARD — NOT CHECKED`. Three walks, in the dark, with one thumb.
No build was run. Every line below is read off the code as it stands.*

---

## SCENE 1 · COLD OPEN AT 1 AM, BEFORE ANY SWEEP LANDS

He has just opened the app. Nothing has been measured yet in this
run. He taps PARK — or, on the very first run, PARK opens itself.

**What he sees, frame by frame:**

- The top line of the whole app, from the very first frame:
  `GUARD — NOT CHECKED`. Not a number. Not blank. The old code
  printed *nothing* here until a count existed (P-6) — and at
  1 AM, nothing reads as "all fine."
- The drawer rises (about a quarter of a second; no key works
  until it has fully landed). Inside: the word GUARD, then
  `LAST PROVED` with an empty slot beside it — no time, because
  no full set has ever landed. Then nine names in a column —
  ACCESSIBILITY, USAGE ACCESS, and the rest — with **no word
  beside any of them**. No FIX key: nothing has been measured
  missing yet, so there is nothing to fix. One solid black key:
  CHECK.
- Opening the drawer has already started the new check running
  behind his back (the host fires the sweep on open). Within a
  breath, the words land one by one beside the names, and if the
  full set lands, `LAST PROVED` takes a time.

**What he believes:** "Not checked yet" — which is exactly true.
He does not believe "fine." He does not believe "broken." He
believes the app has not looked yet, because the app just told
him so, in words a man with his eyes half shut can still parse:
the missing count *is* the message.

**The one tremor:** the check that opening sets running is
invisible. The key still says CHECK, sits undipped, looks idle,
while the words pop in one at a time. If that first sweep ever
hung, he would wait in front of a wordless board with no sign
that anything was still coming — calm, but curdling. And a row
that has not landed yet sits blank next to rows already marked
GRANTED; for a few seconds the board is half a sentence.

### VERDICT — CALM KEPT

The exact moment: **the first frame.** The top line prints
`GUARD — NOT CHECKED` where the old build printed nothing.
Muede's P-6 is dead on that frame: blank can no longer lie to
him by looking like peace. This is the calmest cold open PARK
has ever given a tired man — an honest "not yet," said once,
said plainly, with one black key under it if he wants to ask
again himself.

---

## SCENE 2 · A GRANT DIED IN THE NIGHT. MORNING. HE OPENS PARK.

Setup, stated plainly: the SLEEP seal fell at 20:00. Sometime in
the night, one grant died. While the seal held, PARK could not
be opened at all — the host refuses the tap — so nobody could
have looked, and nothing warned him. Worse, the header line was
frozen at the moment of sealing: if it read `GUARD 4/4` at 20:00,
it kept reading `GUARD 4/4` all night over a door that was
already dead. A true print, gone stale in silence.

Morning. The seal has lifted.

**Taps to the truth: ONE.**

- Tap 1 — PARK. That single tap is now also the check: opening
  fires the sweep. The drawer lands, the words reprint, and the
  dead door reads MISSING in full dark ink while the live ones
  sit quieter beside it. He does not need to press CHECK at all.
  Truth costs one tap and one breath of waiting.
- One grace worth naming: MISSING never goes stale. If he
  stands there groggy for a minute, the healthy rows will turn
  STALE after 45 seconds and start wearing the *same full dark
  ink* as the dead one (the softer dress for STALE was ruled in
  the audit but is not in this branch) — the board gets louder
  the longer he looks. But the dead door's word never moves.
  The worst fact on the board is also the steadiest.

**Taps to repair: TWO inside the app — then he is on his own.**

- Tap 2 — `FIX — {the dead door's name}`. The key names the
  lack. Good. Then it drops him on Android's general list for
  that kind of permission, and PARK's help ends (P-5, still
  open — the escort and the walking-him-through-each-door cure
  were ruled for a later pass and are **not** in this branch).
  Nowhere does anything say "find MONOLITH in this list."
  Nowhere warns him about the long warning paragraph Android
  is about to show him. Out there it costs him roughly three
  more taps — the app's name in the list, the switch, past the
  warning — if he finds the right line first try.
- The trip back: the app re-checks on its own when he returns,
  waits a beat, and then — **prints nothing. Ever.** The code
  says so in its own words: the hand goes back in silence,
  "no word prints, ever." If he fixed it, one firm click sounds
  and the row quietly reprints GRANTED, and FIX either vanishes
  or names the next lack. If he did *not* fix it: silence, and
  the same FIX key still sitting there. A man who was looking
  at the board can tell the difference. A man who was looking
  at the road, the kettle, his feet — cannot. Fixed and
  still-broken feel almost the same, so the tired move is to
  take the whole trip twice, just to be sure. Muede charged
  exactly this (P-5), Dens let the silence stand, Muede
  overruled him — and in this branch the overrule has not
  landed for FIX. CHECK got its printed verdict (`NOT PROVED`).
  FIX, the older key, still has none.

### VERDICT — CALM BROKEN

Truth in the morning is the best this drawer has ever been:
one tap, no asking twice, the dead door named and steady.
The exact moment calm breaks: **the return from Settings** —
the one second where he is owed a word and gets silence.
Everything before that moment kept faith with a tired man.
Everything at that moment makes him do the trip's work twice:
once with his feet, once with his doubt.

## SCENE 3 · HE PRESSES CHECK, ONE-HANDED, HALF-ASLEEP

The phone is in one hand. His eyes are decoration at this hour.
What reaches him *without reading*?

**At the press — almost nothing.** The key answers with the
softest knock the app owns: a ten-millisecond tap in the hand
at the night setting — near the floor of what the motor can
say — plus a short, low sound at the same low setting, if
sound is on. It also dips by one step, which is a thing for
eyes. A loose, sleepy grip will miss the knock entirely. He
cannot be sure his press even landed. (One mercy: while the
check runs, the key takes no second press, so a doubtful
double-tap does no harm. It also tells him nothing.)

**While it runs — nothing he can feel.** The word becomes
CHECKING and the dip holds. Both are for eyes. No hum, no
pulse, no slow beat in the hand that says "still working."
He waits on faith for about a breath — the sweep is quick
when it is healthy.

**When it lands whole — one clear thing, and it is good.**
A firm click: a hard strike in the hand, a short pause, a
softer seat — two beats, strong, nothing else in the app
sounds or feels like it. And it survives muting: turn the
sound off and the hand still gets the strike, because the
buzz does not ride on the sound switch. Eyes shut, he knows:
*it worked, all of it.* This is the best single moment in the
whole drawer for a tired man.

**When it lands with a lack — nothing at all.** No click. No
buzz. The only answer is print: a new line under the key,
`NOT PROVED` with the dead door's name beside it — a line
that needs light, focus, and reading. So "done, and the news
is bad" feels exactly like "not done yet," which feels
exactly like "my press never landed." Three different truths,
one silence. He is left poking the key again or sitting up
and reading — the two things this scene exists to spare him.

**The bad edge:** if one door's answer never comes back at
all, the key sits on CHECKING forever. There is no clock on
it in the code as amended — the word only clears when every
door has answered since the press. No click will ever come,
the key will take no new press, and nothing will ever tell
him to stop waiting.

### VERDICT — CALM BROKEN

The exact moment: **about a second after the press, on any
board that is not whole — when the click doesn't come.**
A whole board answers in his hand; a board with one dead door
answers nowhere he can feel it. "Yes" has a voice in this
drawer. "No" has only print. At 1 AM, a man does not need the
bad news shouted — he needs it to *exist* somewhere other
than in letters.

## PATCH-READY FIXES — WHAT A TIRED MAN WOULD BEG FOR

Plain asks, each small, each with its place named. In the
order he would beg for them.

1. **Give "no" a feel.** When a CHECK lands with a lack, fire
   one low, dull knock in the hand — one soft beat, clearly
   not the firm click — at the same moment the `NOT PROVED`
   line prints. Sound and buzz together, as the app already
   pairs them, so muting the sound never mutes the answer.
   *Place:* the landing beat beside where `lastCheckLackName`
   is set in `ParkDrawer.kt`; one call next to the existing
   `playFirmClick()` branch, using the seat-knock voice at a
   heavier, lower setting.
2. **Let CHECKING end.** If not every door has answered
   within about two and a half seconds of the press, end the
   wait on its own: key back to CHECK, print `NOT PROVED`
   naming the first door that never answered, and fire the
   "no" knock from fix 1. No man should wait forever on a
   word that cannot clear.
   *Place:* a timed effect keyed to the press instant already
   kept in `ParkDrawer.kt`, beside the landing effect.
3. **Make the night press land in the hand.** The press knock
   at night runs at the floor setting (0.06 — the quietest
   the app owns). Raise the floor for the CHECK and FIX keys'
   press knock so a sleepy hand can feel that the press
   landed — the press receipt, not the whole app, just these
   two keys.
   *Place:* the amplitude passed to `playSeatKnock` at the
   CHECK and FIX press sites in `ParkDrawer.kt`.
4. **Show the opening check on the key.** When PARK opens and
   its automatic sweep is running, the key should wear
   CHECKING and hold its dip until the set lands — the same
   face a pressed check wears. Today the opening sweep is
   invisible and the key looks idle while the board fills in.
   *Place:* the `checking` test in `ParkDrawer.kt` — let it
   also be true while the open sweep is in flight, not only
   after a press.
5. **Print FIX's verdict on return.** When he comes back from
   Settings, print one line on the board: `FIXED` on the
   repaired door's row if it now reads whole,
   `STILL OPEN` if it does not — next to the click, never
   instead of it. This is P-5's printed half, and CHECK
   already proves the drawer knows how to print a verdict.
   *Place:* the return beat in `ParkDrawer.kt` that today
   plays the click and sets nothing.
6. **One line of escort under FIX, before he leaves.** Print,
   in small plain words under the key:
   "Settings opens a list. Find MONOLITH. Turn it on."
   One line, no card, no new surface — the cheap half of
   P-5's escort, small enough to ship alone, big enough that
   a tired man does not hunt a long list unwarned.
   *Place:* directly under the FIX key in `ParkDrawer.kt`,
   shown whenever FIX is shown.
7. **Let STALE sit quieter than MISSING.** A healthy door
   whose answer is merely old should not wear the same full
   dark ink as a dead one: give STALE the softer, half-strength
   dress the audit already ruled, so a long morning stare does
   not turn the whole board into shouting. The dead door
   should be the loudest thing on the board — alone.
   *Place:* the row dress in `ParkBoardRow` — STALE takes the
   softer ink, MISSING keeps the full ink. (Ruled in the
   audit's merge list; simply not in this branch yet.)

Nothing above asks for a new surface, a new permission, or a
new kind of object. Every fix is a word, a knock, or a dress
on things the drawer already has.
