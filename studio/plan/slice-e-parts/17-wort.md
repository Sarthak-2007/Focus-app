## 2 — ITEM BY ITEM, RULE BY RULE

### 2.1 STILL BLOCKED — receipt-only home confirmed; the printable form is cured to one line

**Home: STANDS.** Every E source agrees, and the Lexicon's
one-home rule (R7) ratifies it: STILL BLOCKED lives **on the
receipt at issuance, once, never reprinted**. It never tenants
the imprint slot (wren.md §4.5: the slot's tenant at the finish
is the SEALED→IDLE swap, §2.2 below), never the sentence slot
(19-linea.md tenancy table: sentence slot tenant = nothing at
every finish phase), never the strip. A healed fault is never
announced and never amends the issued receipt (wren.md §4.3) —
R8 (silence): the vocabulary contains no "restored" line at the
finish either.

**Form: CURED.** Three forms circulate in the files; only one
may print.

- D/E §2-E4 and MASTER §5.7 print the shorthand ``faults print
  once as `STILL BLOCKED` ``. That is a family name in prose,
  not a line. A builder who prints the bare pair ships a claim
  with no subject and no cause — fails R2 (one-word-one-fact:
  *what* is still blocked is the fact) and the F-class shape
  (named lack first).
- Wren §4.3's worked example, inherited from Opal's beyond
  filing: `INSTAGRAM — STILL BLOCKED (PERMISSION)`. **Dead,
  two counts.** (a) It names a per-app link; D8 killed per-app
  links by name — apps are intercepted on attempt, never shut,
  never opened — so the line asserts a link that does not exist
  (a fabricated fact, the worst R2 failure). (b) Its cause word
  is wrong for every link that does exist: no permission is
  involved in a commanded stop going unanswered.
- **The exact printable form is Draht's (05-draht.md §2.4),
  filed this wave and adopted here whole:**
  `SHIELD — STILL BLOCKED (SERVICE DIDN'T STOP)`
  — zero or one lines, never more. R1 SESSION-ROW cannot fault
  (its flip *is* E0); R3 A11Y-GATE is derived, not witnessed, and
  has no fault state — printing `SESSION —` or `ACCESS —` forms
  would claim evidence that cannot exist, and under F6's applied
  reading (§5) an unwitnessed claim drops. The one line's
  evidence is system-side (`instance != null` at E0+400 in the
  framework-callback registry after a commanded stop) — the same
  grade as LIVE BIND, so the line itself passes the F6 test.
  Cause wording `(SERVICE DIDN'T STOP)` states the machine's
  failure, not his — R3 (grading test) passes: the fault is
  confessed by the machine, in the machine's voice (Atkinson,
  class R/F body), once.
- **Stale count cured in passing:** 19-linea.md §5.1 prices
  `` `STILL BLOCKED`, per faulted link (≤3, faulted branch) ``.
  After Draht, the faulted slip carries **≤1** such line; the
  "+28 each × ≤3" measure overstates the faulted sheet by up to
  56dp. Linea's F-L1 arithmetic should be re-run at ≤1 before
  Ren rules that fork. (Mailed implication is in my wall post;
  the cure is arithmetic, not vocabulary.)

### 2.2 The imprint slug swap — SEALED tenant out, re-measured IDLE tenant in, at the E1200 cut

**The tenants, exact (emil.md §3.1, MASTER §2.6):**
sealed `"{n} APPS HELD"`; idle/primed `"{n} APPS WILL SHUT"`,
singular `"1 APP WILL SHUT"`. Plate class (Anonymous Pro 700
10sp +2.0) — counts are furniture-grade facts frozen by the seal
act, and R1 (engraving test) is satisfied in its plate form:
the *form* is timeless even though the numeral is a snapshot.

- **HELD in `{n} APPS HELD`: STANDS, and only here.** My Lexicon
  killed `HELD` as a session-state word (it wore three facts:
  BLOCKED / PAUSED / RUNNING — R2). The imprint's HELD is a
  different, single fact — *these apps are held shut, PEEK tier
  included* — in exactly one home, defined in MASTER §2.6
  ("HELD, not SHUT — the count includes the PEEK tier"). One
  fact, one home, one form: R2 and R7 both pass. The lock is
  directional: HELD may never migrate from this count form to
  any state surface (Deck eyebrow, AOD, running screen), and
  SHUT may never be substituted for it in the sealed tense
  (SHUT is the future/idle verb; a sealed app is not "shut"
  in the same sense once the PEEK lease exists — §2.6's own
  reasoning, kept).
- **The swap's frame: E1200, not E0 — STANDS as amended, and the
  Lexicon seconds the amendment on truth grounds.** Emil's part
  filed the SEALED → IDLE swap "on the end flip"; Wren's
  amendment E2 (wren.md §4.5, carried into D/E §2-E4) re-times
  it to the **E1200 cut**, slug swap completing by E1350 inside
  the rack, the pressure lip fading E1200–1440 under the rack.
  Wort's ruling: the amendment is not merely timing taste.
  Printing IDLE over a held frame that still shows the sealed
  world would put two contradictory facts on one screen for
  1200ms — a one-fact violation in time instead of space.
  Builders: any code that swaps on the `isActive` true→false
  flip (Emil §3.4's filed trigger) is superseded at this joint;
  the lip's fade follows the swap, not the flip.
- **Re-measured, never restored: STANDS.** The IDLE tenant at
  the swap is counted from the *current* loadout at E1200
  (wren.md §4.5). A restored pre-seal snapshot could print a
  count that stopped being true during the session — a record
  stating an unmeasured present. R-class honesty applied to a
  plate tenant: the idle count is a present-tense claim and
  must be measured in the present.
- **The word IDLE itself never prints in this slot.** IDLE and
  SEALED are tenant *names* in the specs (and SEALED is a lawful
  class-S state word in its own homes). The slot prints counts,
  never the tenant's name. A builder who renders a bare `IDLE`
  or `SEALED` slug in the imprint has invented a string; there
  is none.
- **GAP, ruled by Wort (no fork needed): the sealed singular.**
  Emil files the singular only for the idle form (`1 APP WILL
  SHUT`); the sealed form's singular is nowhere specced, and
  the shipped trunk prints `1 APPS` today (§4). Grammar is not
  optional in a count that prints at plate size — the exact
  sealed singular is **`1 APP HELD`**, by the same
  singularisation the idle form already files. This is the
  Lexicon deciding, not drafting: parallel forms in one slot
  take parallel grammar (R2, exactness of the count fact).

### 2.7 The routine line — `within his routine, never by it` — stands; exact form now filed by Reihe

The drafting rule (MASTER §5.7, Uma's provenance) is a Rule 6
case (the machine-edit rule, lexical form): *by* would make
the routine the session's author; *within* makes it the
container, which is the truth — his slide sealed the session
inside a window the routine merely opened (HPA-01: windows,
not firings). Reihe (04-reihe.md §4) files the exact printed

`WITHIN FASTED BLOCK · 05:00–08:00` — form
`WITHIN {ROUTINE NAME AS AT SEALING} · {HH:MM–HH:MM}`, name
and window frozen at insert, read at issuance. Never `BY`,
never a verb with the routine as subject, never the live name
after a mid-window rename. Boundary kept: a manual session
that merely overlaps a window gets **no** routine line
(recomputing a past overlap at issuance would print an
unfrozen fact). The reseeded names print as proper names —
`WITHIN DAY BLOCKS · …` is a name, not a plural error, and is
not "corrected." One placement gap in Reihe's filing is
drafted in §2.9.

### 2.8 The № counter — `№ 048` — stands; the pad law is ruled here

§5.7: a monotonic sequence counter, incremented only at
genuine completion, is the receipt's only permanent artefact;
`№ 048` proves `№ 047` existed and nothing of 047 is stored.
Form audit: `№` + one space + the count **zero-padded to
three digits as a minimum width** — `№ 007`, `№ 048`,
`№ 312`. **Ruled by Wort (form law, no fork):** at four
figures the count grows — `№ 1000` — never truncates, never
rolls to `№ 000`, never gains a thousands separator. A
counter that wraps would print a false identity for a slip
(R2: the №'s one fact is *which* completion this was).
Forbidden substitutes, for the builder's avoidance: `NO. 048`,
`#048`, `RECEIPT 048`, a date in its place. The faulted sheet
carries a № like any issuance (§3): the session genuinely
completed; the fault is post-completion, in the release.
Plate class, header home only — the № never repeats in the
slip's body (R7).

## 3 — THE GAP: THE ENDED-FAULT SHEET'S WORDS (E4) — DRAFT-FOR-REN

**The gap, stated plainly.** D/E §2-E4 specs the faulted
branch's *behaviour* completely — detention formula, E1800
ceiling, "faulted = an ENDED-FAULT sheet over the
still-defocused Deck" — and its *words* not at all. Wren
§4.3, the seat filing, adds only that the receipt "issues
anyway as ENDED-FAULT" and (her risk 5) that the panic read
is a wording problem owned by "Emil/Opal" — neither of whom
filed sheet text; Emil's receipt composition is expressly
excluded from E4's price, and MASTER §5.7's receipt list is
written for the slip that issues in the happy and slow
branches. **No file in the D/E merge, the E seat parts, or
MASTER §5.7 gives the sheet a title, a body order, or a
single sheet-specific string beyond the STILL BLOCKED line
itself.** `ENDED-FAULT` is a VM state name (RELEASING /
RELEASED / ENDED-FAULT in the finish state machine) — a
name for code, filed nowhere as print.

**What the Lexicon decides by itself (ruled, not drafted):**

1. **`ENDED-FAULT` never prints.** It is a state name, and
   printing it would (a) collide under R2 with
   `ENDED BY EMERGENCY BREAK` — two "ENDED…" record words
   for opposite causes (a completed session whose release
   faulted vs a session the man ended); (b) fail R3's
   reading at his worst hour — bare `FAULT` on the slip of a
   session he completed in full reads as a verdict on the
   session, when the fault is the machine's release, after
   the session ended clean. State names stay in code
   (PROVING and RELEASING already do).
2. **The sheet is the receipt.** One document, one name (its
   № header), one footer — the faulted branch is the same
   slip detained, not a different artefact. A second document
   form for the same session would be one fact, two
   renderings (R7), and §5.7's separation law is about
   *endings* (finish vs break), not about release proof.
3. **No verb, no act, no tap route on the sheet.** The
   release fault offers zero decisions (HOLD; silent retry at
   30s / 5min / Deck-open is the machine's). A `— TAP TO …`
   line would be a class-F sentence with no lawful act to
   name, and a class-V word on a non-pressable slip is a
   command the app gives him — both banned by the class
   definitions themselves. `RETRY`, `FAILED`, `ERROR`,
   `SOMETHING WENT WRONG`, and any apology form are likewise
   not in the vocabulary for this surface.
4. **The fault line prints last among body lines** (the
   slip's own order: provenance → deflections → death line →
   fault line → footer), so the slip reads as a completed
