## 1. Brick — physical NFC puck as the unlock key

**Source:**
- https://www.ubergizmo.com/2026/09/59-brick-helps-you-stop-doomscrolling-without-giving-up-your-phone/
- https://previewer.co/brick-phone-distraction-blocker

**What makes it elite / best-in-class:**
The only mainstream blocker that moves the unlock *off the phone entirely*. Tap phone to the $59 battery-free NFC puck to arm; tap again — in person, wherever you left the puck — to disarm. Reviews keep landing on the same mechanism: "a walk to the fridge beats Ignore Limit." You can *start* a session remotely in the app, but you cannot *end* one remotely. Strict Mode prevents deleting the app as a bypass. Budget is explicit and scarce: 5 Emergency Unbricks per device, up to 10 custom modes, scheduled daily sessions, no subscription.

**Steal for Monolith:**
- **Asymmetric arming:** Monolith already allows starting anywhere — keep ending hard. Formalise the rule in Shield copy and logic: *arm in one tap, disarm only through the expensive path.* No symmetrical "stop" button parity.
- **Cheap-NFC-tag mode (optional, inventive):** any blank NFC tag (~₹100) stuck to a desk/fridge becomes a "physical key" — ending a session early (outside the weekly emergency break) could optionally require scanning it. Zero hardware to ship, same spatial friction: leave the tag in another room and the impulse has to walk.
- **Scarcity accounting, made visible:** Brick's 5-ever emergency budget is the lesson, not the number. Monolith's 1 emergency break / week should show its remaining budget on the block screen itself ("Emergency break: 1 left this week") — scarcity you can see changes the decision before you spend it.

## 2. Cold Turkey Blocker — the anti-circumvention checklist, and Frozen Turkey

**Source:**
- https://www.sipandscroll.app/blog/cold-turkey-blocker-review.html
- https://qwit-block.com/blog/posts/cold-turkey-vs-alternatives.html

**What makes it elite / best-in-class:**
The strictest consumer blocker on desktop, famous precisely for what happens *when you attack it*. During a locked block: it blocks its own uninstaller, blocks Task Manager, blocks system-clock changes (no fast-forwarding past the timer), and the block resumes after a reboot. "Frozen Turkey" goes further — it locks you out of the whole computer, repeatedly re-locking on login, with no override, no emergency unlock, no "I changed my mind."

**Steal for Monolith:**
- **Adopt its attack list as Shield's acceptance checklist, Android-translated:** during an active lock, Monolith must explicitly survive / block — (1) reboot, (2) uninstall attempt, (3) clock / timezone change, (4) force-stop via Settings, (5) Play Store install of a clone/browser workaround, (6) split-screen / PiP side-door, (7) accessibility / device-admin revocation screens. If an item can't be fully blocked on Android, the block screen must *detect and name it* — truth over silence.
- **Frozen Turkey as a named session tier:** a "full lock" session type where the launcher surface offers *nothing* but calls/allowed essentials — no peek at all. Offer it as the deliberate nuclear option next to normal sessions, with a short first-use warning to start small (Cold Turkey's community learned this the hard way).

## 3. one sec — the breathing pause, with peer-reviewed proof

**Source:**
- https://impresskit.net/f5e21739-afc2-4450-a6a5-e7dd111703d0

**What makes it elite / best-in-class:**
It doesn't block — it inserts one forced deep breath between impulse and app, every time, automatically (the insight: a voluntary breath only works if you open consciously, which is exactly when you don't). Studied with the Max Planck Institute / University of Heidelberg: **57% average reduction in app usage over 6 weeks.** The mechanism retrains over time — opening stops promising instant dopamine, so attempts themselves fall. Variants worth noting: "Mirror" intervention (look at yourself and say you really want to open it), intention/emotion prompt before opening, and a Phone-Free Morning rule.

**Steal for Monolith:**
- **Make the 2-minute peek start with a breath, not a tap:** peek gate = one slow animated breath (~6–10 seconds) + the user's stated intention shown back ("You said: check the test date"), *then* the 2 minutes start. This protects the peek from becoming a reflex door while keeping it humane.
- **Re-intervention inside the peek:** one sec re-prompts on re-open. Monolith equivalent: at peek expiry, no silent slide into a second peek — a second breath + attempt counter ("3rd peek attempt today") before any further action is even discussed.
- **Show the attempt count on the block screen:** testers report the sheer number of daily open-attempts was itself the shock that changed behaviour. A quiet attempt tally on Shield ("Instagram — 14 attempts today") is cheap, truthful, and needs no score/dashboard.

## 4. AppBlock — Strict Mode's menu of unlock conditions

**Source:**
- https://appblock.app/how-to-use-strict-mode/

**What makes it elite / best-in-class:**
The most developed *commitment layer* on Android. Strict Mode doesn't have one unlock — it has a menu you choose *before* you're tempted: **Cooldown** (request unlock, wait a set time before it takes effect), **Timer**, **Follow schedules** (strict only while a schedule runs), **Approval** (a trusted person approves your unlock — called out as the strongest option), **PIN**, **Charger** (strict until you plug in — specifically praised for breaking phone-in-bed habits), and combinations (Timer & PIN, Timer & Charger). Granular toggles decide exactly what is locked: editing blocks, uninstalling *any* app, installing new apps, changing device time, even Face ID/passcode settings. It also blocks *inside* apps — Reels / Shorts / Stories only, without blocking the whole app.

**Steal for Monolith:**
- **Cooldown unlock for the emergency break:** Monolith's 60-second hold is a start; AppBlock proves a *delayed-effect* unlock is stronger — press "request break," the break only becomes available after a cooldown (e.g. 10 minutes), by which time most urges have passed. Hold + cooldown stacked beats either alone.
- **Charger condition as a routine end-rule:** a Night routine that can only be ended early while plugged into the charger at a fixed spot = Brick's spatial friction with zero hardware.
- **Sub-app blocking in Shield's allowed/peek design:** block Shorts/Reels surfaces while leaving the parent app peek-able — directly relevant to a NEET user whose leak is short-form video inside otherwise-useful apps, not the apps themselves.
- **Pre-commitment wording:** the user picks the unlock condition *at arming time*, in calm state. Copy that framing verbatim in spirit: "Choose now how future-you is allowed out."

## 5. Opal — Deep Focus sessions + the milestone "reveal moment"

**Source:**
- https://apps.apple.com/us/app/opal-screen-time-control/id1497465230

**What makes it elite / best-in-class:**
The design leader of the category. Deep Focus sessions are uncancellable once armed; recurring schedules, app limits, and the deepest usage analytics in class ("Know your Culprits" — today's top 3 apps surfaced on Home). Its craft signature is the Gems system: milestones (10 / 50 / 100 / 500 / 1,000 focus hours, streaks of days) earned as gems that you *tap to crack open* — earning is staged as a small reveal ceremony, and locked gems show how close you are.

**Steal for Monolith:**
- **The reveal ceremony, not the scoreboard:** Monolith is a blocker, not a tracker — no scores, no day-grading. But a finished long session can still land in the quiet drawer as a single crafted artefact with a *moment* (a stamp/seal animation on completion, kept forever, searchable). Opal proves people keep blockers whose completions feel like objects, not log lines.
- **"Top culprits" honesty on setup, not on a dashboard:** when arming a session/routine, show the 3 apps that broke the last sessions as the default pre-checked blocklist. Analytics used *at the decision point*, not as a guilt dashboard.
- **Uncancellable means uncancellable:** Opal doesn't apologise for Deep Focus. Monolith's lock copy should be equally plain — state at arming exactly what will and won't work, so mid-session there are no surprises to litigate.

## 6. ScreenZen — escalating delay + per-app friction profiles

**Source:**
- https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.screenzen&hl=en

**What makes it elite / best-in-class:**
Completely free, and the most *tunable* friction engine available: a customisable delay before a flagged app opens that **can grow with each subsequent open that day**, mindful prompts ("Is this important?" / "Why am I checking?"), per-app schedules and daily goals, and interruption *during* use — access in short bursts so a 2-minute check can't silently become 40 minutes of scrolling. A 2019 ACM CHI study cited in comparisons found interstitial pauses cut visits by up to 47% at high friction.

**Steal for Monolith:**
- **Escalation curve for peek attempts:** 1st peek attempt in a session = breath + intention; 2nd = longer wait; 3rd = wait + attempt counter + the session's remaining time shown. Friction that *learns the day's pattern* instead of repeating identically — identical friction gets trained into muscle memory and dies.
- **Burst architecture inside peek:** peek shouldn't be one open door — deliver it as ScreenZen-style bursts with a micro-interruption (e.g. a brief Shield flash at the halfway mark: "1 minute left — still intentional?"). Keeps the 2 minutes *felt*, not drifted through.
- **User-written prompt:** let the user write their own block-screen question in their own words at setup. A self-authored line ("AIR 1 or this reel?") outperforms any stock copy we ship.

## 7. minimalist phone — the block screen *is* a launcher

**Source:**
- https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.qqlabs.minimalistlauncher&hl=en_US

**What makes it elite / best-in-class:**
5M+ downloads by *replacing the home screen itself*: text-only app list, no icons to trigger muscle memory, apps hidden rather than shown-then-blocked, notification filtering (unimportant notifications moved out of sight), app renaming, and grayscale/monochrome mode to strip the dopamine colour cues from whatever remains. The detox is environmental — there's no grid of temptations to resist in the first place.

**Steal for Monolith:**
- **Direct blueprint for his launcher ruling:** Shield's block surface = a text-first launcher — allowed apps as plain words in a list, no colourful icons, generous blank space, clock + session remaining as the hero. Peek apps live on a *separate* screen (one deliberate swipe/tap away), exactly as ruled — minimalist phone proves users will live on a surface like this for months, not just tolerate it for a session.
- **Monochrome the allowed screen:** render the allowed/peek launcher in grayscale during a lock. The allowed apps stay fully usable but visibly *de-glamourised* — the phone feels like a tool for the duration, and colour returning is itself the session-end signal.
- **Hide, don't badge:** blocked apps should be *absent* from the block launcher, not greyed-out icons with locks on them. A visible locked Instagram icon is an advertisement for Instagram; absence is quiet.

## 8. Freedom — set it once, it runs without you (and everywhere)

**Source:**
- https://freedom.to/freedom-for-android
- https://freedom.to/lp-focus-apps

**What makes it elite / best-in-class:**
The cross-device incumbent: one blocklist and one session enforced simultaneously on phone, laptop, tablet and browser, so the distraction can't just migrate devices. Two craft ideas stand out in its own positioning: (1) **Locked Mode** — session can't be ended early and the app can't be deleted mid-session; (2) its explicit anti-gamification stance — *"a block you set once doesn't need you to show up for it"* — recurring schedules run with zero daily re-arming, plus "Block All Except" (allowlist-first) sessions and session history you can annotate.

**Steal for Monolith:**
- **Routines must never need daily arming:** Freedom's retention lesson for Monolith's routines — a routine that requires the user to remember to start it will die in a bad week; routines fire on their own, and the block screen (not a notification) is the proof it fired. ARM-fast means: set once, trust forever.
- **Allowlist-first as the default session shape:** "Block everything except…" is cognitively simpler and leak-proof compared to maintaining a blocklist (new apps/distractions are blocked by default). Offer Monolith sessions as allowlist-first, blocklist as the advanced option.
- **One-line annotation in the drawer:** after a session, a single optional line ("sat the full physics block") attached to the finished-session record — Freedom's annotation, stripped of all scoring. Memory without judgement.

### Cross-find pattern (one line for the Shield builder)

Every elite product wins on the same three moves Monolith can combine like nobody else currently does: **the unlock is more expensive than the lock** (Brick, Freedom), **the friction changes each time you test it** (ScreenZen, one sec), and **the blocked thing disappears instead of sitting there locked** (minimalist phone) — all sealed with **an anti-circumvention checklist treated as a feature, not an afterthought** (Cold Turkey, AppBlock).

— Riegel (Scout 1)
