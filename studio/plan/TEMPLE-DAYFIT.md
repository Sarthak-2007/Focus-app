## 03:30 — the desk clock across the room · TAXES (spec)

The first surface of his day, read at the worst reading
conditions in it: ~4 metres, dark room, eyes half asleep, from
a bed — before the bath, before any light. Two questions must
both pass: *can he read it*, and *does it stay a clock and not
become a lamp*.

**The lamp question passes — in the combined spec, not in
Temple's alone.** Temple's own cure (P §6) composites the
numerals to 55%: token #F3DFB2 at full alpha measures 0.750
relative luminance against Nacht's 0.25 ceiling; at 55% it
computes to **0.201** — under law. But Salz-AOD measured the
shipped overlay whole and found the numerals are only ~72% of
its night spend: the progress strip's fill is the *same*
0.8125 ink in trunk, grows all night, and by the last hour runs
at ~48% of the numerals' rate; the banner text is a third
full-primary print; the exit line composites to 0.2607, over
the ceiling even dimmed. Temple §6, read alone, dims one of
four prints. The cure that covers all four is the Flow
Register's (F §2.4.4): numerals, strip fill and banner text all
composite to the 55% family (≈0.216), exit line 0.60 → 0.58.
**Temple's 03:30 verdict is FITS only as Temple + F §2.4
together; Temple alone carries a strip that brightens the room
as the night ends — the frame's instantaneous spend rises 39%
seal-to-finish on trunk's numbers (Salz-AOD §2), i.e. the
brightest hour of the face is the hour right before he wakes.**
That is the wrong direction for a face pointed at a sleeping
man, and it is the single most important sentence in this file.

**The reading question taxes.** The hero is 96sp Anonymous Pro
bold in the shipped overlay (Salz-AOD §1); Temple keeps the
face's scale (P §6, D §5). Geometry, stated as an estimate:
a 96sp digit stands roughly 11–12 mm cap-height on a typical
panel; at 4 m that subtends ~10 arcmin — about twice the
5-arcmin letter a fully awake, fully adapted eye resolves at
20/20, i.e. readable in principle, with almost no reserve.
Three taxes eat the reserve: (1) at 0.201 luminance his pupil
and acuity are both working in the mesopic range, where
resolution falls off well before contrast does; (2) half-asleep
accommodation at 4 m is the worst focus of his day; (3) Temple
prints *two* facts — elapsed AND remaining (P §6) — and only
one of them can be the 96sp hero; the second fact, at glyph or
secondary size, is below the 4-metre threshold computed above
and will read as texture, not number. He will get the big
number across the room. He will not reliably get the second
one, and at 03:30 the second one — *how much of SLEEP is
left / what time is it* — is often the one he woke for. F §2.4.1
partly answers this by putting the current time on the face,
but as "a small line under the hero" — small is exactly what
4 metres refuses.

**Smallest cure (flips TAXES → FITS):** one print-layer change,
no machinery. On the desk clock only, promote the *second*
fact to the hero's own tier whenever the face is in its
across-the-room state — i.e. the clock carries TWO monument
lines (span remaining up top; current time beneath it at the
same 96sp class, same 55% composite), and elapsed demotes to
the glyph tier, since elapsed is the fact a waking man needs
least. Total lit area rises by roughly the numerals' own term
(~3.2% of screen, Salz-AOD §1) at 0.201 — the night budget
stays under law with room; what it buys is that every number
on the night face is a 4-metre number. If two monument lines
offend the niche's stillness, the cheaper half-cure — current
time at ≥48sp (half hero scale, ~5 arcmin at 4 m, threshold
legibility for a high-contrast mono digit) — is the floor
below which this surface should not ship.

*Render debt standing: the desk clock is unrendered in Temple
(P §13). L05 captures per Salz-AOD §3 must acquit numerals,
strip at BOTH early and late span, banner, and exit line —
a single early-night capture acquits nothing, because the
strip's breach is smallest when the night is young.*

## Mid-block — the Held Door, wet gym hands · TAXES (spec)

The scenario, concretely: the FASTED block runs to 08:00; the
gym is at 08:00, but the door's wet-hands hour is wider than
the gym — any block, any reach for a SHUT app with hands wet
from the bath, sweaty from a set squeezed into a break, or
damp from washing before a meal. He meets the sealed gate
(P §7, D §6) one-handed, thumb only, on a screen whose
capacitive layer is already misreading a wet palm's edge.

**What Temple gets right for this hand:**

- **Two affordances, no third** (P §7): RETURN as a plain
  tablet, PEEK {n} MIN as the bronze side door at the right
  edge. A wet hand cannot mis-tap what is not there; there is
  nothing else on the gate to hit.
- **The facts survive a glance:** the app's name engraved at
  centre with the double rule, the remaining span printed
  ("the block ends 16:27 · 41 MIN LEFT"), the frieze miniature
  with NOW carrying its hour (D §6), the current task beneath
  in secondary parchment. Nothing on this surface requires a
  second look, and a wet hand buys exactly one look.
- **The cooldown is printed, in words** (D §6): "THE LAST PEEK
  IS STILL COOLING" / "PEEK RETURNS IN 14 MIN", and where no
  lease can be honoured the door is *not drawn at all*. A man
  who cannot peek is told why, on the door — he is never left
  jabbing a dead tablet with a wet thumb, which is the precise
  micro-frustration that teaches people to resent a blocker.
- **No animation delays dismissal; one low knock on arrival,
  silence on exit** (P §7, §9). The door does not argue.

**The tax — geometry the spec never gives.** Neither P §7 nor
D §6 states a size for either tablet. The sketch (P §7) draws
RETURN and PEEK as modest text tablets at the foot of a tall
gate, at opposite edges. For a dry two-handed user that is
plenty. For the wet one-handed case it is the weakest drawing
in the Temple file, in three specific ways: (1) **opposite
edges** — a right-thumb grip reaches the right-edge PEEK door
easily and RETURN, across the screen, worst of all; yet RETURN
is the safe, intended, ninety-percent action and PEEK is the
exception. The layout makes the exception the easy reach and
the rule the stretch. (2) **Text-tablet height** — a tablet
sized to its words is a ~36–40dp-class target; wet-screen
touch error and one-handed thumb-arc error both grow exactly
as target height shrinks, and this is the one surface he
operates with both errors at once. (3) **The bottom edge** is
where a wet palm heel rests; tablets seated low without a
guard margin invite the palm's phantom touch to arrive first.

**Smallest cure (flips TAXES → FITS):** presentation only, no
machinery, three numbers on the existing drawing. (a) Both
tablets take a minimum **56dp touch height** (visual tablet may
stay its carved size; the touch area is the tablet's course,
full-width halves of the gate's foot — left half RETURN, right
half PEEK, divided by the frieze's centre joint). (b) When the
side door is in its *cooldown* or *absent* state (D §6 ii/iii),
RETURN's touch area takes the **whole foot** — the only action
gets the whole landing zone. (c) A 12dp dead margin at the
screen's bottom edge where no touch registers, against the
wet palm heel. The gate's face does not change by a pixel a
dry eye would notice; the door becomes a surface a wet thumb
cannot miss.

*Render debt: the Held Door is unrendered in Temple (P §13);
D §13 owes it in BOTH side-door states. Add to that debt, from
this seat: the render review should state both tablets' touch
geometry in dp on the frame, or the cure above is unverifiable.*

**The tax is the instrument, not the text.** The wheel is how

As rendered (phase 1), the dial's fine tier washed pale-on-
pale and the slab merged with its ground (slab-to-ground

on file: slab deepened to #E3C184 (step **1.26**) plus a true
7dp cast shadow; fine ticks and hub wells re-cut at the ink's
own depth (mids at text primary #2B2013, minors at 85%, wells
at 45% — code-applied, Noir-verified per D §0); and the
deepening's structural answer, the **chisel pair** (D §3):
every glare-critical mark drawn as a 1dp dark edge + 1dp light
edge, so the mark encodes *shape* and survives the palette
lightness that killed tone-only marks. The chisel argument is
sound — a groove is legible at glancing light precisely when
ink washes out — and it is also, today, **an argument, not a
render**. P §1.4 states the proof law in Temple's own words:
a cure claimed without a render is not a cure; the re-render

the shadow step may read in ordinary daylight (Ruth's frames
suggest the room it must survive) and still flatten in the
worst hour. The chisel pair does not have that failure mode;
the shadow does. So the midday verdict rests on the chisel
pair landing, not on the shadow.

**Verdict: TAXES, held open on one render.** The cure is
already designed and partly coded; what is owed is proof.
**Smallest cure:** none new — discharge the standing debt:
re-render the deepened day Deck (D §13) and let the fine tier
be judged on that frame, at full size, in the brightest

**The frieze at midday, a genuine plus:** the day carved as

§9.3) is the fastest midday orientation in the app — one

single row. Brass on ground measures 3.56:1 (P §10, line duty

the shape carries the fact and the ink carries the number.

## Between blocks — the repose · TAXES (spec)

Ten blocks means roughly nine landings in the courtyard
(D §7): the minutes between standings, phone in hand, body
between rooms — standing, walking, water, a doorway. He meets
the repose one-handed, in motion, for seconds at a time.

**What fits:** the repose is *inside* the standing, not a
second standing (D §7) — the hero keeps counting the span,
the desk clock reads the span likewise with the banner
carrying "BREAK — DOORS OPEN · n MIN LEFT" (F §2.3), and the
boundaries knock once each, low (D §7, §9.2) — entering and
leaving the break are announced to a man who is not looking
at the phone, which during a break is most of the time. The
pill's BREAK face ("BREAK · 6 MIN LEFT", body tap returns to
the Deck at the END BREAK tablet, D §8) gives the break a
face in other apps and a way home. All of that is right.

**The tax — the one number the repose exists to give is the
one number its Deck face states worst.** On the Deck, the
break's line is "BREAK · N MIN" housed on the base course
(D §7). The spec never says whether N is the break's *length*
or its *remaining* — and the two readings diverge by the
minute as the break runs. Everywhere else Temple is precise
about this distinction: the pill prints remaining, the door
prints remaining, the clock's banner prints remaining
(F §2.3). A man glancing at the Deck mid-break, in a doorway,
with water in the other hand, will read N as *time left* —
because that is what every other Temple surface means by a
number next to BREAK. If N is the set length, the Deck is
quietly lying in the one sentence the repose owns; if N is
remaining, the spec should say so, because the builder will
choose one and the file currently lets him choose wrong. This
is a one-word defect with a daily blast radius: nine times a
day, at the exact glance the surface exists for. There is a
second, smaller tax beside it: **END BREAK is a bronze tablet
in the threshold course** (D §7) — the right place (where the
bolt would be) — but it inherits the Held Door's problem in
miniature: no stated touch height, met one-handed in motion,
and its verb is *new machinery that does not yet exist in the
tree* (D §7, §13: one VM verb, register-priced small, Ren to
rule). Until that verb lands, the repose's Deck face promises
a door the building doesn't have — the pill's BREAK tap
already routes to it (D §8), so a missing verb strands the
pill's promise too.

**Smallest cure (flips TAXES → FITS):** (a) one word in D §7 —
the line prints **"BREAK · N MIN LEFT"**, N = remaining,
derived from the same row fields the clock's banner already
reads (F §2.3: print-layer, no machinery); if the set length
must also show, it belongs on the break-length tablet beside
the set stones (D §9.1), which already prints "BREAK 10 MIN"
as a setting — the two numbers then live in two named places
and can never be confused. (b) END BREAK's tablet takes the
same 56dp touch-height floor as the Held Door cure above —
one geometry law for every tablet a moving thumb must hit.
The verb itself is already priced and queued for Ren's
ruling; this seat adds only that the pill and the repose
ship together or the pill's BREAK face waits — never a tap
that lands on a verb that isn't there.

## 19:30 — the finish: receipt + the keystone moment · FITS

The day's crown, met with tired eyes and a finished man.
Two surfaces in sequence: the receipt of the last block (and
the day's finishes behind it), and — on the evenings he sets
tomorrow up — the Shield wall, where the loadout's keystone
travels (D §4).

**The receipt fits tired eyes by construction.** Temple does
not redesign the loved slip (his ❤️ on the batch-7 render is
this world's founding evidence, P §5); it *materials* it —
parchment strip under a brass rule — and then fences its
print by Nacht's sheet law: fact rows in primary ink only,
the one quiet line (the WITHIN window) at the glyph tier,
**8.24:1 on sheet** (P §5, §10). Temple's secondary tier
measures 3.49:1 on its sheet and is *forbidden there by
Temple's own filing* — the slip a tired man reads at arm's
length contains no tier that cannot carry it. Witnessed vs
reconstructed is printed, never blended (P §5): a recovered
finish wears a brass-outline unstruck seal, a witnessed one
the vermilion outline. At 19:30, after a day the machine
mostly witnessed, that distinction is the difference between
a record and a story, and Temple prints it on the one surface
he keeps. FLOW slips omit PLANNED (P §5) — no invented plan
on a stopwatch day. Motion: the slip arrives downward, once,
no flourish (P §9) — a tired man's last surface does not
perform. **Standing debt, not a tax:** the in-world receipt
render (with the seal at the №) is owed (P §5, §13); the
verdict judges the filed print law, which is complete and
falsifiable — if the render breaks its own fence, this
verdict falls with it.

**The keystone at 19:30.** Loadout selection on the Shield
wall: three stones on the ground course, the chosen one set
into the wall, and the bronze keystone that travels the
groove and seats over the chosen inscription (D §4) — his
sliding-cap verdict housed in masonry, *bronze chooses, gold
commits*, gold never entering this wall (P §2). Day-fit for
a tired hand: a groove is a *constrained* slide — the cap
cannot leave its channel, so the gesture tolerates the
imprecision of a tired thumb in a way a free sled never did;
the seat is confirmed by the seat knock's family and by the
chosen stone's shadow gap, two channels, neither of them
eyesight. One caution, recorded as a watch item rather than
a tax: the keystone's travel is the evening's one *deliberate*
gesture in Temple, and it must keep the press law (P §9) —
answer in 90 ms, travel may be slow, the seat must not lag.
A keystone that arrives late to its stone at 19:30 will feel
like the app doubting his choice at the exact moment he is
deciding tomorrow. If the merge finds the travel cannot tap
as well as slide (the Deck's bolt carries his tap verdict;
the Shield's cap historically slides), that asymmetry should
be named to Ren — not charged here, because the groove's
constraint makes the slide honest work, and his sliding-cap
verdict was given for this selector by name.

**The Confession, if the day broke** (P §8): a 6dp break in
the stele's double rule, the fact in primary ink — "DAY
BROKEN — SLEEP DID NOT START · 20:00" — one filled vermilion
square before it, the pigment's one lawful home. At 19:30
this is the surface that keeps him trusting the app on its
worst days: no score, no shame copy, no red wash. A temple
that prints its broken days plainly is one he will arm again
