## 0. The confession, and the claim

Phase 1 was paint. Phase 2 was grammar — LIT / UNLIT / ABSENT
applied to a composition I did not question. Read my own spec
with Fremd's eyes and he is right: it is the skeleton wearing
signs. A header band, an eyebrow, a hero in a card, a wheel
object, a strip of cells, a rail at the foot — the old app's
furniture, re-lit. The grammar was mine; the room it was spoken
in was borrowed.

The deepening rebuilds the room from the grammar. The question
it answers, element by element: **if light is what a fact does,
what does the screen's arrangement itself say?** In NOIR the
arrangement is a street, and a street is not a grid with neon on
it. A street is read by walking it, and what you meet is signs —
each one either burning, formed-but-dark, or not there. Three
compositional laws generate everything below:

1. **Brightness is address.** Hierarchy is carried by luminance
   step and sign scale, not by position on a borrowed grid. The
   brightest standing object is where the eye goes first; the
   composition choreographs that path on purpose — hero sign,
   then the hand's one lit verb, then print. If two things burn
   at the same tier, the composition is lying about which
   matters. (Phase 2's "one light per decision" survives — as a
   consequence of this law, not as a rule pasted on top.)
2. **The ground is the street; light falls on it, nothing sits
   on it.** There are no cards. A sign does not stand on a plate;
   it burns against the street, and its light pools *below* it
   on the ground. Every "raised surface" in the skeleton is a
   plate pretending to hold a sign up. Cut the plates and the
   signs still stand — that is the test of whether the world was
   ever in the composition or only in the colours.
3. **The street has states, not styles.** Idle, arming, running,
   break, peek, door, night — the street's light changes because
   its facts change, never because a screen changed. The same
   objects persist through every state; what changes is which
   of them burn. A man who learns the street once can read any
   state of it — that is what "feel in its soul" means at the
   level of use: not that each screen looks different, but that
   no state is a different app.

The soul test, self-applied before it is applied to me:
greyscale, names covered, the six finalist frames. What a
stranger should see is not "the app, darker" but a framed field
of great letterforms that can be *dark while composed and lit
once committed*, small signs standing along a kerb, three loose
plates, paper nailed under the big sign, and — at idle — a
street that is mostly *off*. If any frame still reads as a
dashboard, the deepening has failed on that frame and §7 says
where I know it still might.

## 1. The Deck — the marquee

The skeleton's Deck is a stack: band, eyebrow, card, wheel,
strip, line, track, slide, rail. The deepened Deck is one
object with a street around it: **the marquee, standing at the
head of the street.**

### 1.1 What dies

- **The hero card.** The Deck's one raised surface (kept
  deliberately in phase 2, §7.9 — re-tried here and cut). A
  card is a plate; a marquee needs none. The hero numerals burn
  directly on the street. The wet gradient — phase 1's plate
  falloff — **moves**: it is no longer the card's background but
  the sign's *light-spill on the pavement beneath it*, present
  only in proportion to how much the sign burns. Idle: no spill
  (an unlit sign spills nothing). Running: the pool under the
  numerals. The gradient was always light looking for its
  source; the deepening gives it one.
- **The wheel as a depicted object.** The dial's machinery —
  the (θ,ω) integrator, the detents, the seat — is machinery
  and stands untouched. Its *depiction* as a skeuomorphic
  wheel, hub and spokes in a card, dies. (The 8-dot hub dies
  with it — Fremd's confusion was correct: a hub that displays
  nothing is furniture.)
- **The progress track.** A bar with a fill is dashboard
  furniture, and phase 2 already killed its twin (the `%`
  print) for saying one fact twice. The bar itself now follows.
- **The eyebrow row.** A label strip above a card is form
  furniture. Its three facts (tense, loadout, birth) do not
  die — they move onto the marquee (§1.2).
- **The proof strip as a strip.** Three cells in one bar is a
  spreadsheet wearing a uniform. The bar dies; the plates
  survive (§1.3).
- **The slide track as a track.** A groove with a cap is
  switch-room furniture (§1.4 naturalises it).
- **The header band.** App chrome. Its two facts move: the
  wordmark becomes the street's name sign; the Guard line
  becomes the lack sign (§1.5).

### 1.2 The marquee — what replaces the stack

One standing sign, three lines, all signage:

- **The top line** (the theatre's NOW SHOWING line): the tense
  — TOTAL / SESSION RUNNING / BREAK · N MIN / ROUTINE ·
  \<NAME\> — set as the marquee's changeable line, print-tier
  letterspaced mono. While a peek lease burns and he is back
  inside Monolith, the top line carries the peek as a printed
  sub-line: `PEEK · 1:23` (§5.3). The tense was always a sign's
  headline; it just lived in a form row.
- **The great numerals**: the span, unchanged in machinery,
  changed in state-law: **while composing, the marquee is
  UNLIT** — letterforms at the muted tier, fully readable as
  form (the unlit tier measures 5.14:1 off the ground; §9 N5),
  silent as light. The wheel still turns under the thumb; the
  register still moves; but the number he is composing does not
  burn yet, because he has not committed to it. **The slide is
  the ignition** (§1.4): at the seal, the numerals step to full
  gas burn in the seal shot's flare (Haut's lawful flare, A8 —
  it finally has its true object: not a decoration on the hero,
  the hero *lighting*). Running, the sign burns steady at the
  running tier — and the accent is gone from it: gas-white is
  the burn, magenta has left the screen (§3.2).
- **The sponsor line**: beneath the numerals, print tier — the
  loadout's name (A1: the wiring that presents this show) and,
  for routine-born sessions, the birth (A4). Print, because
  these are facts of record, not states begging attention.
- **The frame.** Around the numerals, the marquee's border —
  and here the wheel and the track, two circular gauges doing
  related work, become one object. The frame's teeth are the
  detent ring: composing, the teeth are unlit bulbs and the one
  **register tooth burns** where the hand is — turning the
  wheel walks the single lit tooth around the frame while the
  numeral changes. The hand reads the frame; the eye reads the
  number. Running, the same frame is the session's gauge:
  teeth burn in sequence as the span elapses — the marquee's
  border bulbs lit for the elapsed portion, unlit ahead of it.
  No bar, no `%`: the sign's own frame keeps the time. One
  object, two tenses, both native: a marquee frame is *supposed*
  to be a ring of bulbs; the skeleton needed a wheel *and* a
  track because it was drawing instruments. The city draws a
  sign.

### 1.3 The plates

BLOCK · APPS · TIME leave their shared bar and stand as three
loose plates under the marquee — print on small standing
plates, hairline-separated, no container. Their grammar was
already sign grammar (a cell can print UNKNOWN; a stamp can
print nothing — ABSENT); the deepening only removes the bar
that made them a strip. A failed bind lights its word **amber**
on the BLOCK plate (the refusal family, §11.4 of phase 2) —
the plate carries its own lamp; there is no banner. TIME keeps
ENDS HH:mm, derived, never stored. The plates' address never
moves across states — a street's fixtures don't migrate.

### 1.4 The slide — the ignition lever

The slide's physics and law are machinery and stand. Its
depiction changes: the track is drawn as the marquee's
**conduit** — the cable run along the pavement at the sign's
foot — and the cap is the connector the hand carries along it.
The trail behind the cap is the street lighting up behind the
lever (phase 2 already said the words; the deepening makes the
composition pay for them: the trail's light is real light in
the scene, pooling on the ground, not a progress fill in a
groove). At commit the lever seats, the proving holds its
breath (nothing burns but the frame's register tooth — the
machine working reads as a street holding still), and at t=0
the marquee ignites. Selection and ignition are one event:
*you were composing a dark sign; the slide is what lights it.*
Tap-to-commit stands (his ruling) — the same lever flipped.

### 1.5 The street's head and kerb

- **The name sign.** MONOLITH stands at the street's head as
  formed letters — unlit by day, half-tier by night; the city's
  name is always present and never the hero. At its right, in
  print tier, **the time of day** (§5.5) — a street clock is a
  fixture, and print is its tier.
- **The lack sign.** `GUARD n/4 — <first lack>` becomes a sign
  that obeys the grammar it prints: guard whole → the sign is
  UNLIT (form present, tappable, silent); a lack exists → the
  sign **lights** — a lack is the machine reporting a refusal-
  adjacent fact, and the lit sign is the door (tap → the fix,
  the PARK detent as shipped; HB-1/HB-2's doorLocked predicate
  governs its behaviour during detention and proving, exactly
  as the truth car wrote it). The header stops being a band
  and becomes two fixtures with light states.
- **The kerb.** The bottom rail dies as a bar: no container, no
  fill, no indicator pill. What stands at the foot of every
  screen is the street's kerb — the ground line itself — with
  the district signs standing on it: DECK · SHIELD · TASKS ·
  LEDGER as four standing signs, the current district's sign
  **lit**, the others formed and unlit. Argued head-on, because
  a row at the bottom is the skeleton's most recognisable
  furniture: in a grid-world a bottom row is a toolbar; on a
  street, signs *stand in a row along it* — the row is the
  world's own geometry, not Material's. The test that separates
  the two: the kerb carries light states and no container; a
  toolbar carries icons and a bar. PARK is ABSENT from the kerb
  — it is the fuse box, reached through the lack sign and the
  street's end, as shipped; a utility detent was never a
  district.
- **The paper.** Under the marquee, nailed to the street: the
  NOW bill (current task, DONE tear tab, QUEUE count in its
  corner) — print on paper, the city's second native material
  (§6.1). A city has bills posted under its signs; paper is not
  borrowed furniture here, it is what streets are also made of.

### 1.6 Sheets become doorways

The digest sheet and the disarm sheet lose their card
containers: a sheet in NOIR is a **doorway** — black opening,
a lit sign over it naming the room (HELD / DISARM), the room's
contents in print. The Held Door (§4) is this grammar standing
outside the app; the in-app doors rhyme with it because they
are the same object at a different address.

## 2. The other districts, composed

**Shield — the street walked.** The switch-room stops being a
settings list and reads top-down as the street itself, walked
awake (its moment stands — completeness over glance): the
**master signs** first (the loadout: three standing signs,
STUDY / WORK / SLEEP — and the sliding cap's *frame* dies
here: there is no window travelling over names; **the burn
travels** — the selected sign burns, the other two stand
unlit, the slide gesture his tap chose moves the light, §6.2),
then the **timed signs** (routines: each a sign with its hours
printed beneath it, ON = lit / OFF = unlit, its editor the
sign's back panel opening as a doorway), then the
**shopfronts** (the app rows: the name *is* the sign, A2's
name-light now the row's whole identity — SHUT unlit, 2m
mid-burn with its stop named from the live setting (§5.4),
OPEN lit; the sled at the sign's foot is its switch), then the
**standing orders** (A5's pair, two small signs that never
move), then the **addresses** (blocked websites — print, a
directory nailed by the door). The census lines are print
throughout: the street's directory, not its signage. A man
re-wiring the city reads it as light first, print second —
which streets are dark, which burn, at one walk.

**Tasks — the noticeboard wall.** The board is a wall on the
street; notices pin in a column (queue order is the wall's
reading order). The current task's notice is the one *lit
paper* — a notice under the wall's lamp. DONE tears it off
(the tear's grammar stands; the booking lands at the tap).
The composer is a blank notice at the wall's foot. Nothing
else on the wall glows; a wall of lit notices is a wall of
demands, and the queue is not a demand — it is paper waiting.

**Drawer — the archive of dark signs.** The basement deepens
its own idea: every stored task is a **switched-off sign filed
in the dark** — its name in unlit letterforms on black stock.
Search is the archivist's lamp: **matches light** — a found
name steps to half burn while it matches, and only while it
matches; the drawer you didn't ask for stays dark. Months are
corridors (EARLIER = deeper in, same darkness). The cabinet
keeps its shelf of objects, each labelled with its one true
fact (FORGED, and when). The receipt lives here as what phase
2 said it was: the one sign the city switches off and keeps —
bone print on black stock, facts in a column, the № its
serial plate. Its layout is print discipline, and print is
substrate, not world (§6.3).

**PARK — the fuse box.** Not a district, never on the kerb: a
panel at the street's end, reached through the lack sign. Its
contents are labelled breakers and printed sentences — the
audited sentences keep their exact words (Truth Car 3 owns
their truth); composition gives them a panel, not a personality.

**The desk clock — the night-watchman's sign.** Phase 2
rebuilt the face (§11.2: it never burns — no gas white, no
magenta; numerals at the half tier; the fill a deep-violet
length; ground true black; the burn-in patrol walks it). The
deepening states the district law that generates that face, so
it is composition and not a colour exception: **the clock is
the street in its quiet state** — the one sign left standing
when the street sleeps, built entirely from the tiers that
don't hold the eye because its job is to be glanced at and
forgotten. It contains nothing else: no banner fragment, no
progress-as-light. And idle — no session, no study facts in
existence — the quiet sign does what street clocks do: it
prints **the time of day** at its dim tier (§5.5). Running, it
prints the study numbers, by his kept ruling. The patrol
continues either way: a dim sign can afford to move; a burning
one would be stared at.

## 3. Ruth — answered in composition

Ruth's verdict is FIGHTS, and her counts are counts about
*where light lives in his day*. Tokens can't answer them; the
deepening answers by moving the light.

### 3.1 03:30 — the meditation hour

Her image: a full-burn magenta cap across the room from a
meditating man; the phone become signage. The image conflates
two surfaces, and the deepened composition separates them by
state. Across the room during meditation, the phone shows the
**clock** — the quiet sign (§2): dim, still, no accent, built
to be forgotten. The Deck is not across the room; the Deck is
in his hand for the twenty seconds of arming, and in the
deepened street what his hand meets is a **dark marquee**: the
numerals unlit while he composes, the frame's register tooth
the only moving light, the cap the only burn — small, in his
palm, at arm's length. There is no full-burn signage to face
the room because the sign he is setting has not been lit yet.
**Ignition happens at the seal, after the choice** — and by
then the phone is going face-down or back on the desk. The
compositional law, stated once: **brightness follows presence
— hand, then desk, then across-the-room, in that order, and
the tiers are assigned by the surface's distance from him,
not by the screen's importance.** The clock (farthest) gets
the quietest tiers; the cap (in the hand) may burn; the
marquee (at the desk, glanced) burns at the running tier only
once it is a fact, not a temptation.

### 3.2 05:00 — the fasted arm, and calm

Phase 1's designer — me — wrote that NOIR argues the opposite
of calm at 5 AM. That sentence described a storefront already
lit, daring him to look. The deepened street argues the
opposite: **darkness is the default; light is the commitment.**
At the 05:00 arm the street is in its pre-opening state —
signs formed, street dark, the composing done by form and by
the frame in the hand. One ignition, once, when he commits;
then the desk tier for the block he is now inside and mostly
not looking at. A world whose default state is *off* cannot be
spending his calm; it spends light only where he has already
chosen the dark. Magenta's full burn exists at exactly one
moment in the whole system — the slide's travel and commit,
in his hand, for seconds. Running: no accent anywhere. The
colour Ruth indicted is, in the deepened composition, the
colour of a decision being taken — and of nothing else, ever.

### 3.3 Daylight, and the day dial

Her daylight counts stand where they stand: the day face was
the safety valve, and its dial was the weakest-reading wheel
of the four. The deepening's answer is compositional identity:
day is not a second face but **the same street with its signs
off** — and unlit signs in daylight are flat by physics, not by
retreat. The marquee frame, the kerb signs, the plates all
persist *as forms*; the numerals are ink (violet-black at full
print contrast — the day hero was never the weak reader; the
dial was). The frame's teeth read by their shadow side (the
knurl cure, in code, §14) and the register tooth prints in ink.
What I concede without varnish: day remains this world's
plainer state, because the world's material is light and noon
is not its hour. What the deepening removes is the *split* —
one street, two states of its lamps — which was the true
substance under her "safety valve" sentence.

### 3.4 Saturday evening

Test day asks the app to be a closed door. The deepened idle
street *is* one: first glance after the paper lands on a dark
marquee, unlit kerb signs, a quiet name sign. The street's
only lit door is the one he knocks on (the Held Door) or lights
himself (the arm). Nothing on the idle face glows at a tired
man — glow is now something the app does *after* he acts, not
something it wears while waiting.

### 3.5 The receipt

One line, because phase 2 already carried it and she credited
the discipline elsewhere: the slip is print — its soul is
print — and NOIR's version keeps every virtue of the loved
slip and changes only its stock (§2, Drawer). Neon never
touches it. The kept sign is a sign *switched off*; its
character is the character of paper.

## 4. The Held Door — the third sign (baseline item 2 closed)

Phase 2's door (A9) carries two signs: the app's name unlit,
Monolith's answer lit (`BACK AT <HH:mm>`), the peek key the
only lit verb. Klar's pass and the baseline agree on the hole:
**the cooldown prints nowhere** — the door offers a peek and
cannot say when the last one lets it offer again. The
deepening closes it in grammar, because the grammar already
owns exactly the state the cooldown is:

**The door carries a third sign: the peek sign itself, in
three states.**
- **Lit** — a lease can be honoured on this door and the app
  is off cooldown: the peek burns, its price printed (the
  setting's live length, §5.4), one tap takes it.
- **Unlit, with its return printed** — the app is cooling:
  the sign is present as form and cannot burn; beneath it,
  print tier: `BACK IN <mm:ss>` — and when a peek is refused
  *by* the cooldown, the refusal sentence prints the reason in
  the same print (a withheld peek that vanishes silently is a
  lie of silence; a sign that stands dark with its hour
  written under it is the truth wearing the grammar). The
  cooldown is, literally, a lamp that is warming — the city
  already knows how to draw a thing that exists but does not
  burn yet.
- **Absent** — on a door that cannot honour a lease (the
  baseline's item 3), there is no peek sign at all. Not unlit:
  absent. An unlit sign promises a future burn; a door that
  can never honour the lease has no future to promise.

The door's other laws stand from A9: the ignition flicker
once at switch-on (the refusal event's own physics), the
amber lamp declined on the door's clock (amber is refusal's
family; the door's facts print gas-white at print tier), the
finish time the first fact on the door. Counts, if the door
ever prints them, print the honest split (baseline item 4).

## 5. Klar's pass — answered item by item

Klar read all five worlds for the small things and found NOIR
wanting on five counts. Each is answered here — designed, or
argued out by name. (The Small Things register itself is
answered at §5.6, where my surfaces touch it.)

### 5.1 The pill — ABSENT → designed: the sign over the other street

Freya's machinery stands as specced (Route A overlay; the
derivation table in PILL-AND-TIMES §2.1 — pure derivation from
stored ends, BREAK outranking PEEK, the ✕ that hands a peek
back). What this file owes is the pill's *face* in the grammar,
and the grammar finds it native: when he is inside another
app, the city hangs **one small sign over that street** — a
miniature marquee: a state word and a number, nothing else.

- **SESSION** — the marquee in miniature: remaining, gas tier,
  steady burn. The sign he knows, at pocket size.
- **BREAK** — the sign at **half burn**: the city between
  shows. BREAK + remaining. (Freya's priority is also the
  grammar's: during a break the peek doors stand open
  lease-free, so a peek fuse on the pill would be a lit sign
  lying about urgency — the break's sign burns lower and
  tells the truth.)
- **PEEK** — the sign burns its fuse: the app's initial and
  the remaining, and at its right edge the **✕ — the sign's
  own switch**, the first reachable hand-back of a peek
  (recordPeekEnded, machinery Freya's §2.1). At 15 seconds
  remaining the sign **steps its burn down one tier** — the
  end announced by light, in the surface he is already
  looking at (§5.2). At expiry the sign goes ABSENT — not
  dark-with-a-message: gone, because the fact is gone.

The pill is dismissable only by its state's end, never by
accident (the order's own words); it never appears over
Monolith's own surfaces or over the Held Door (Freya's
never-list, adopted whole). It is the Held Door's sibling, as
the order said: the surfaces he lives in, outside the Deck.

### 5.2 Warnings before things end — the chime refusal, re-tried

Phase 2 refused a break chime by name and let the refusal
stand wider than its argument. Klar's separation is correct
and I adopt it: the refusal argues about announcing a break to
a man *inside* his session — phone present, state visible, he
chose to be there. It says nothing about a peek ending under a
man inside another app who cannot see it end. Silence there is
not calm; it is a trapdoor. **Designed, for the peek:** the
pill's tier-step at 15 s (§5.1) and the expiry's return of the
door itself (the intercept is the event). No sound added —
NOIR's sound law is untouched, and the warning lives in the
surface he is looking at, which is where warnings belong in a
city of light. **For the break and the block:** I narrow my
§8 rejection on the record — it stands against *announcements
and ambience* (a chime as a feature, a supervisor's voice) and
**is withdrawn** against the register's single knocks at state
flips (ST-03 / ST-06, Ren-ruled IN): a knock is the machine's
own voice class — the seat-knock family this world's feel law
already speaks (knocks, never vibrations; events, never
alarms). The rejection is amended here, not silently dropped.

### 5.3 A running peek counts down — designed, two homes

Outside the app: the pill's PEEK face (§5.1) — the countdown
in the only place he is. Inside the app: if he returns to
Monolith mid-peek, the marquee's top line carries the peek's
remaining as print (§1.2) — the state exists, the derivation
is Freya's, the home is the tense line because a live peek *is*
part of the session's tense. Between the two homes there is no
minute of a peek that no surface counts.

### 5.4 Editable peek and break durations — stance taken

Klar: no world has a stance. NOIR's stance: **both lengths are
his, and they are set where their facts are read — there is no
settings ghetto in a street.** The machinery is Freya's §3
(GuardSettings columns + the break length stamped onto the
session at creation, so a running session finishes on the
numbers it was armed with — adopted whole; mid-flight
immovability is a truth law, not a convenience). The
presentation:
- **The peek's length** lives in the switch-room, on the
  peek's own street: every sled stop that offers a peek names
  it from the live setting — `PEEK <n>M`, derived, never a
  frozen "2m" (a label that prints a number must derive it;
  the frozen label becomes a lie the day the setting exists).
  Setting it: the standing-orders district gains the peek's
  length sign — the numeral itself is the control, tapped to
  the direct-set clock of §4.6 (touch the time, never step
  against it). The Held Door's price and the pill's fuse read
  the same derivation — one fact, every surface.
- **The break's length** lives where the span is composed:
  the Deck prints the span's anatomy in the sponsor-line
  district — `+ BREAK <n> MIN` between blocks, print tier —
  and the same direct-set touch edits it. A man composing
  three blocks sees the breaks he is buying in the same line
  of print that names his wiring.

### 5.5 The current time — ABSENT → designed

The street has a clock: the name sign at the street's head
carries the time of day in print tier at its right (§1.5) —
on the Deck and Shield heads; a fixture, not a state, so it
prints and never burns. The desk clock idle face (§2) prints
the time at its dim tier when no session exists — ST-01's
complaint was that idle renders the composer's dial setting
dressed as a time, and the grammar's answer is the ABSENT
law: print what exists. Idle, the time exists and the session
doesn't; running, the study numbers exist and are the clock's
law. (Flagged for Ren: the study-display ruling governs
sessions; the idle face is ungoverned ground, and I argue —
not smuggle — the time onto it.)
