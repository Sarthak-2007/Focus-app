## §1 · THE FIRST TEN SECONDS — as the stranger

> I opened something called **MONOLITH ML-01**. I don't know if
> ML-01 is the app, a model, or a version of my phone. Under it:
> SETUP + INTRODUCTION. So this is a form. Forms at 4 in the
> morning mean work before I'm allowed in.
>
> First real sentence, and it's a good one: while a session runs,
> it shuts the apps I ruled shut and the sites on my list, and
> backs me out of Shorts and Reels. So: a study lock. It closes
> the apps that eat me, while I work. That I understood in one
> read, and it's the only thing in the first ten seconds I
> understood in one read. But — *ruled* shut? *My* list? I haven't
> ruled anything. I haven't listed anything. Either it already
> has a list for me and isn't saying, or I'm about to be handed
> homework the sentence pretends I already did.
>
> Next line, in capitals: **YOUR STUDY HOURS LIVE IN YPT.**
> I don't know what YPT is. Nobody on this sheet ever tells me.
> It reads like a place my hours already live without my knowing,
> which is a strange thing to be told by a stranger app at 04:00.
> The second half lands — it keeps no score and grades nothing —
> good, I don't want another report card. But the first half is
> a door slammed in a language I don't speak.
>
> Then: THE FOUR DOORS. Four permissions, and two of them scare
> me in the way Android has trained me to be scared. One of them
> watches which app is in front. One of them makes Android
> *refuse to uninstall the app*. I have been on a phone long
> enough to know that "you can't uninstall me" is what the bad
> apps say. The sheet does tell me how to take that one back —
> that line is the single best thing on the page, it is the
> moment I decide this thing is probably honest — and it tells
> me twice that nothing leaves this phone. I believe it a little
> more each time. I also notice it never tells me what these
> doors *see* while they're on my phone. Only where it doesn't go.
>
> What does it want from me? Four switches flipped in four
> different Android lists, each one a trip out and a trip back,
> at 04:00, half asleep. That's the want. It's a big want and
> the sheet knows it — it warns me about Android's warnings
> before I meet them, which is the second-honest thing it does.
>
> What happens if I refuse? **In the first ten seconds: I have
> no idea.** Nothing near the top says I can refuse, skip, leave,
> or press on half-done. The top of the sheet reads like a form
> that must be completed. I only learn I'm allowed to walk away
> near the very bottom — past all four doors, past how a session
> runs, past the list button — where it finally says LEAVE WHEN
> YOU LIKE and that DONE may be pressed with doors unset. The
> permission to refuse is printed at the end of the thing I'm
> refusing to read. A man with less patience than me doesn't
> refuse this sheet. He just closes the app, and the sheet never
> learns the difference.

That is the ten-second truth: **the app is clear, the ask is
clear, the exit is invisible.** Everything below is what the
next minutes cost.

## §3 · THE CURED TOTAL AT THE DONE PRESS — does it make the sheet trustworthy for a man who skipped everything?

**Short answer: it makes the press *honest*. It does not make
it *trusting*. Those are different, and the skipper needs both.**

What the cure genuinely fixes, and I won't dilute it: S-1 was
the sheet's one self-contradiction, and the branched total
kills the mechanical lie. A missing door is now priced as
missing, an aged door as aged, an unread sheet prints no count
at all rather than inventing 0/4. The per-lack sentences
(§5.6) mean the man who reads **nothing but the foot** still
meets every price before he pays it: GUARD 0/4, then
"SESSIONS WILL NOT SEAL" for the two doors that gate
everything, "CAN BE UNINSTALLED" for the anchor, "NOTHING IS
HELD" for the listener. At the decision moment, for the
doors-as-missing case, the foot no longer deceives him. That
is real, and Blockit — per the audit's own §7 — never does it.

But test it against the actual skipper, the man who scrolled
past everything to find the exit:

1. **The total prices function. It never prices safety.** The
   two lines that answer a stranger's actual fear — "NOTHING
   LEAVES THIS PHONE" (Door 1) and "THEN IT UNINSTALLS LIKE
   ANY APP" (Door 3) — live mid-sheet, inside job lines, in
   the section a skipper by definition skipped. He arrives at
   DONE having been told exactly what won't *work* and nothing
   about what won't *harm*. A price list is not a trust
   argument. For the man who read everything, the sheet earns
   trust cumulatively and the total seals it; for the man who
   read only the foot, the total is four warnings under a
   button — which reads less like honesty and more like a
   disclaimer.
2. **The four prices arrive flat.** Door order does the ranking
   by accident, but nothing at the foot says the first two
   sentences are the fatal ones and the last two are comforts.
   The rank line said it — a screen earlier, in the part he
   skipped. So the skipper's takeaway is "everything is broken
   equally," which is not the sheet's own truth.
3. **The act-line spends what the total just earned.** See
   M-26: "THIS FINISHES THE SETUP" ten lines under "GUARD
   0/4." The tear sits between them, and the tear *helps* —
   total above, act below, different pieces of paper — but a
   skipper doesn't read paper metaphysics. He reads: nothing
   is set / setup finished. Pick one. The act-line's other two
   clauses ("IT STARTS NOTHING, SEALS NOTHING") are exactly
   right and they carry the line; "FINISHES" is the word that
   wobbles.
4. **The unread case is the impatient man's actual case.** If
   he scrolls straight down before the sweep lands, the total
   prints no count — just "THE DOORS ARE STILL BEING READ." —
   and the DONE key is live under it anyway. The sheet's most
   honest state is the one where its decision moment contains
   *no information at all*, and the act-line still invites the
   press in the same words. Trustworthy means the foot can't
   be empty at the moment of decision. As cured, it can.
5. **The stale case still shows two verdicts** (M-24). Rare
   at first open, common on a PARK reprint — and the reprint
   reader is the one deciding whether to trust the sheet
   *again*.

**Verdict:** for the 0/4 skipper, the cured total converts a
lying foot into a truthful price list, and the DONE press that
follows it is an *informed* press about capability. It is not
yet a *trusting* press, because everything that makes this
app safe to refuse-with — the privacy negations, the way back,
the rank — is spent in the body he didn't read, and the foot
repeats none of it. The total redeems the sheet's truth. It
does not redeem its trust. One line would change that (N-03).
