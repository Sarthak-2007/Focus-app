# FEEL-5 — The Queueing Ritual (Issue #12 cell)

*Seat: Feel-5, the ritual. Sense assigned: the ritual as a whole — the
night-before act that F-1 finally makes true. Paper only. Sources:
AUDIT-tasks.md (Amt's verdict, incl. Muede's 1 AM errand and Plan's
recomposition) and PLAN-issue-12-tasks-f1.md (Amt, cell lead). No code
read beyond what those two cite; no compile, no tests run.*

## Thesis, in one line

This is not a to-do list with a start button. It is a vow made the
night before, in his own words, that the morning honours without
asking him to make it again. Every beat below is judged by one test:
does it feel like *setting tomorrow in place*, or does it feel like
*doing admin on himself?* The first is Monolith. The second is every
tracker he has ever abandoned.

The ritual has three acts across roughly eight hours, and the screen
is only act one:

1. **Night — the writing.** He opens Tasks, writes tomorrow's first
   task on the blank leaf at the pad's foot (or FIRSTs it to the
   head), closes the phone.
2. **05:00 — the keeping.** The FASTED block seals wearing that
   task's name. He did not touch the phone. The promise kept itself.
3. **After — the proof.** At the block's end the receipt, and later
   the Drawer, carry the same words he wrote at night — not a
   paraphrase, not a routine name, not a category. His words.

F-1 (PLAN-issue-12-tasks-f1.md) is what makes act two true at all:
before it, RoutineDerive.kt:169 seals `intention = routine.name`
and the routine path never reads the queue, so act one was theatre —
a perfect night's queueing still woke up as "FASTED BLOCK". F-1's
shape is ritually correct in three specific ways this seat wants on
record: the intention is a **snapshot at seal instant** (the morning
cannot rewrite the night's words), sealing **never consumes the
head leaf** (the block borrows the name; the leaf still has to be
torn by his hand, so finishing stays an act, not an automation),
and the **empty-queue fallback is the routine name** — a fact, not
a scolding. Amt's overruling of "No task queued" as a headline is
a ritual ruling as much as a data one: that string in the Drawer
would have archived a shame label as if it were an intention.

## The walk — beat by beat

Each beat's feel is given in one breath — touch, sound, motion,
light together, as they actually arrive together.

### Beat 1 — Opening Tasks at night

**Feel (one breath):** thumb lands on a quiet screen in a dark room,
no chime, no badge count in his face, the pad already there at rest
— paper tones low, the head leaf faintly raised like the top sheet
of a pad left open on a desk, nothing pulsing for his attention.

This is the threshold beat and it must be *boring in the right way*.
He is tired. Muede walked this at 1 AM; the real hour is after
sadhana-and-dinner tired, closer to sleep than to planning. If the
screen greets him with totals, progress, or yesterday's verdict,
the ritual starts as an inspection and he is the inspected. The
audit's kill of the HORIZON header and its minute total is, in feel
terms, the removal of a greeting that graded him. What should greet
him is only the pad and its one header.

### Beat 2 — Seeing the queue as it stands

**Feel (one breath):** a short scroll of paper under the thumb,
leaves separated by a hair of ground, the stack lines at the right
edge brushing past like page edges, #01 at the top in bold — his
own handle, read in half a second — and below it, quieter leaves
in the order he left them.

Order here must read as *place*, not *rank*. First is next, not
best; last is later, not worst. The audit killing the ACTIVE /
QUEUE plate words serves exactly this: a printed ACTIVE on the
head leaf while nothing runs is a status, and statuses invite
judgement. Raised paper, bold title, #01 and the one accent verb
say "you are here" the way a bookmark does. A bookmark never
congratulates or accuses. Keep it that way.

### Beat 3 — Writing on the blank leaf at the foot

**Feel (one breath):** the thumb travels down the pad — past work
already named, which asks nothing of him — and finds ruled blank
paper waiting as the pad's own last leaf, keyboard up, cursor
blinking on the line, keys clicking soft under his thumbs as the
words appear *on the leaf itself*, in the leaf's type, at the
leaf's size.

This is Plan's recomposition, and in feel terms it is the audit's
single best move. The old composer was a form bolted above the
list it fed: field up top, leaf appearing at the bottom, cause and
effect in different rooms, a full-width ink ADD out-shouting START.
That is clerical — fill the form, submit the form, the system files
it somewhere. The recomposed composer *is* the next leaf: he does
not submit a task, he writes on the pad, and the writing is already
sitting where it will live. Spatial honesty is the whole ceremony
here. The composer asking a name and nothing else — MINUTES DIE,
duration hard-coded 0, acquitted by all six audit seats — is the
other half: a leaf that asks "how long will this take you?" is an
estimate demanded at midnight, and an estimate is a promise he can
fail by breakfast. The blank leaf asks only: *what is it called?*

### Beat 4 — ADD at the foot, or FIRST to the head

**Feel (one breath):** one small key at the leaf's end under his
thumb — a soft tick as the leaf settles into the stack with the
faintest downward ease, the pad one leaf thicker at its edge — or,
for the dawn task, the quieter FIRST key, and the new leaf is
simply *there* at the head when his eye travels back up, raised,
bold, #01, the old head stepped down without argument.

Two different intentions, two different weights, and the weights
matter (see Fights, §2 below). ADD is the ordinary case: tomorrow
joins the line. FIRST is the errand Muede walked — *this one opens
the fasted block* — and it must feel like placing a leaf on top of
a pad, not like filing a priority ticket. No dialog, no confirm, no
"are you sure you want to preempt". CUE's old behaviour — promote
*and* teleport to the Deck — was a small betrayal at this beat: he
came to arrange tomorrow, not to be taken somewhere. The audit's
split (CUE = make head, STAY on Tasks; START = head only, arms via
Deck) restores the geography: arranging happens here, arming
happens there, and tonight he is only arranging.

### Beat 5 — Closing the phone

**Feel (one breath):** screen down to black in his hand, the room
dark again, no summary offered, no "3 tasks queued for tomorrow"
toast, no streak, no goodnight statistic — just the physical click
of the phone set face-down, and the head leaf's words are the last
thing he read.

The ritual's most important beat is the one the app does least in.
Clerical software closes with a receipt of what you entered;
ceremonial software closes with silence, because the act is done
and both parties know it. Anything printed here — a count framed
as readiness, a tomorrow-preview, an encouragement — reopens the
negotiation he just finished. He should go to sleep carrying one
sentence (the head leaf's title), not a dashboard. This is also
where the single ceremonial detail proposed below belongs: at
this beat, and nowhere else.

### Beat 6 — 05:00, the FASTED block seals wearing his words

**Feel (one breath):** he wakes to a phone that already knows —
the block sealed at its hour with no tap from him, and where
"FASTED BLOCK" used to sit, the title line carries the exact words
he typed in the dark, same capitals, same spelling, unedited, the
way a name written on a door is the same name in the morning.

This is F-1's beat and it is the ritual's hinge. Everything before
it is intention; this is the other side answering. Two properties
carry the feeling and both are already in the plan, worth naming
as ritual requirements so they are never traded away in a refactor:
**verbatim** (the snapshot string, byte for byte — a corrected,
truncated, or re-cased title would feel like the app tidied him,
and tidying is a tracker's instinct) and **unasked** (the seal
reads the queue at the seal instant without waking him, prompting
him, or needing the app opened — a ritual that requires attendance
at 05:00 is just a chore with better typography). The plan's
seal-instant read also heals a quiet anxiety in this beat: if he
reordered or deleted at midnight after second thoughts, the
morning honours the *last* version. The vow is what he finally
left on the pad, not the first draft of it.

Note what sealing does *not* do, per the plan, and why it feels
right: it does not tear, complete, or consume the head leaf. The
block wears the name; the leaf waits. When the block ends, the
work itself is still his to finish and tear by hand. An app that
auto-completed his task because a timer ran would be claiming his
work — the claim-token and taskId-bond variants were killed in
the cell for machinery reasons; this seat records they were also
wrong in feel, for the same reason.

### Beat 7 — Inside the block, the sealed leaf

**Feel (one breath):** if he glances at Tasks mid-block, the leaf
sits behind its solid perforation overprint, the QUEUE header
carrying one printed word — SEALED — every verb simply absent
rather than greyed, the title fully legible, the paper still, and
his thumb finds nothing to fiddle with, so the glance ends.

Absence-not-greying, acquitted in the audit from the tear's
existing behaviour, is the difference between a sealed envelope
and a disabled form. Greyed verbs say "you may not"; absent verbs
say "this is a record now". The audit's F-5 gating (DEL, CUE/START,
SPLIT, sub-step toggles all absent under seal) completes a state
the trunk only half-kept — today only the tear withdraws, and a
sealed leaf you can still delete or re-split is a vow with an edit
button. Mid-block is when resolve is thinnest; the ritual protects
the night's decision from the morning's mood by making the leaf
un-negotiable, politely, by simply not offering the negotiation.

### Beat 8 — The block ends: the tear

**Feel (one breath):** his thumb takes the 48dp TEAR zone on the
head leaf — the word short now, the perforation already teaching
the gesture — one press, the tick, and the leaf goes: 30dp of
accelerating travel, alpha held so he can *see* it leave, dashes
lifted out of the fade, booking already honest at the tap, the
next leaf rising into the head's raised place behind it.

The tear is the ritual's only celebration and it must stay
physical, brief, and unaccompanied. No confetti logic, no "task
complete" banner, no count of today's tears. The audit's respin
(F-7: one 300ms clock, accelerating cubic, perforation outside the
fading layer, ghost 260→300ms) fixes a real felt lie — the trunk's
tear is a fade wearing a tear's name, its second half invisible by
construction — and a misnamed motion at this exact beat would
poison the whole rite: finishing tomorrow's first work should feel
like tearing a leaf off a pad, an ending with an edge to it.
ZIP-TEAR remains the house standard for the next tear build per
the audit; this seat agrees with the cell that the respin ships
now, because the ritual needs one true tear more than it needs a
perfect one later.

### Beat 9 — The receipt and the Drawer, after

**Feel (one breath):** days later, in the quiet drawer he opens
rarely, the finished session sits in plain print — and the
intention line still reads exactly what he wrote that night,
word for word, next to a block that would once have been filed
under "FASTED BLOCK", the two halves of the ritual finally on
the same page.

The Drawer's job in this ritual is *witness*, not scoreboard. The
audit's acquittal stands and this seat underlines it: nothing on
this screen or in its archive grades, streaks, or totals finished
work. The receipt header nuance the Critic flagged — the receipt
prints session title in its header and intention as a sub-line —
is the one place act three could still short-change him: if his
words sit small under a generic header, the morning-after proof
reads as "a FASTED BLOCK happened, footnote: his task". Wherever
the typographic hierarchy is settled, his title should hold the
highest line the session's own record offers it. The name he gave
the work is the work's name. Everything else is metadata.

## Where the ritual can still break emotionally

Named plainly, in the order he would meet them:

1. **The guilt pile (Beats 2–3).** A queue is one bad week away
   from reading as a list of things he already failed to do.
   Leaves that survive day after day migrate, in feel, from
   "queued" to "owed". Monolith is a blocker, not a tracker —
   nothing scores him — but a long, untouched queue scores him
   *visually* without printing a single number. The recomposition
   can worsen this: the composer now sits at the foot, so on a
   long queue the night's writing requires travelling the whole
   pile first. Mitigation is tonal, not structural: leaves carry
   no age, no "waiting N days", no overdue language, ever; #NN
   stays a handle, never a debt counter; and the "N TASKS" count
   in the QUEUE header must stay a flat address ("here is the
   pad's thickness"), set quiet, never coloured, never celebrated
   at low numbers or alarmed at high ones. If N ever gets a tone,
   the count becomes a score.
2. **The 1 AM fumble (Beats 3–4).** The undo banner's 5,000 ms
   window, already charged by Muede and Dens in the audit, is an
   emotional hazard, not just a usability one: a tired thumb
   deletes the dawn task, the banner expires mid-reach, and the
   ritual ends that night in a small panic and a retype. A broken
   closing beat is worse than a broken opening one — he goes to
   sleep with the failure, not the intention. The strike-tick /
   narrowed-target cure for sub-step toggles (audit item 10) is
   the same class: silent full-width toggles at 1 AM are traps
   laid in a ceremony.
3. **The renegotiation urge (Beat 7).** If any verb survives the
   seal, the block becomes a suggestion he argues with at his
   weakest hour. F-5 is therefore ritual-critical, not tidiness.
4. **The unanswered vow (Beat 6, pre-F-1 state).** The deepest
   break, and the one this issue exists to fix: he does
   everything right at night and the morning answers with a
   routine name. One occurrence teaches him the queue is
   decorative; two teach him to stop queueing. F-1 is not a bug
   fix in feel terms — it is the restoration of cause and effect
   across sleep.
5. **The fallback that feels like failure (Beat 6, empty queue).**
   On a night he queues nothing, the block seals as FASTED BLOCK
   and that must feel *neutral* — a block with no named first
   work, not a ritual skipped. Amt's ruling protects this: no
   "No task queued" headline, nothing archived that reads as an
   absence. This seat adds only: no empty-queue prompting the
   night before, either. A nudge to queue is a tracker asking
   for its data.
6. **The vanishing deferral.** Deferred tasks are invisible on
   this screen (audit escalation 5, Fremd). Emotionally, a task
   he deliberately postponed disappearing entirely reads two bad
   ways: as relief that curdles ("did I lose it?") or as the app
   hiding his avoidance from him. The ritual needs deferral to
   feel like putting a leaf at the back of the pad — findable,
   unjudged — not like dropping it behind the desk. Cross-screen
   question, flagged here because the night ritual is where
   deferral decisions are made.
7. **Repetition at the second seal.** Two routines in one morning
   both read the same head (plan-ruled CORRECT — the head names
   the morning's first work). Felt risk is minor but real: if he
   notices the identical intention twice before the leaf is torn,
   it can read as the app repeating itself rather than the name
   holding. This seat accepts the ruling — the alternative,
   consuming the leaf at seal, is far worse (Beat 6) — and notes
   the tear is the natural divider: once torn, the next seal
   reads the new head, and the sequence feels authored again.

## The single detail that would make it ceremonial

**The echo.**

One sound-and-touch signature, used exactly twice in the app's
life each day, nowhere else, never reused for any other event:

- **At night (Beat 5):** when he leaves Tasks with a head leaf in
  place — or, if a trigger must be a tap, when a leaf lands at
  the head via FIRST or settles as head via ADD — the phone gives
  one low paper tick with a single short haptic underneath it:
  not a notification ping, not a success chime; the sound of a
  stamp meeting paper, felt as much as heard. The head leaf's
  perforation holds its printed state; nothing else changes.
  The ceremony is: *it is set.*
- **At 05:00 (Beat 6):** when the FASTED block seals wearing that
  leaf's title, the identical tick-haptic fires once, before any
  other morning signal. He does not need to look. The same sound
  he made setting it returns, made by the morning keeping it:
  *it held.*

Why this one detail and not another screen, animation, or phrase:
ceremony is a clerical act plus a *mark that the act crossed
time*. F-1 already moves the words across the night invisibly —
correctly, but invisibly. Data syncing is silent; a vow kept
should be *perceptible without being checked*, because checking
at 05:00 means opening the app, and opening the app at 05:00 is
the exact behaviour the block exists to replace. The echo costs
no new surface, prints nothing, scores nothing, survives silent
mode as haptic-only, and cannot become a streak — it fires on
empty-queue mornings too (the routine seals either way; only the
words differ), so it never becomes a reward for queueing. It is
the difference between *the system stored my input* and *the
night and the morning shook hands*. Everything else in this
document is making the ritual honest; the echo is what makes it
*felt*.

## Judgement — the audit's recomposition against the walk

**What serves the ritual:**

- **Composer → the pad's next blank leaf at its foot, ADD shrunk
  to an end-key (item 2 / F-2).** The strongest single ruling in
  the audit, ritually. Writing becomes continuous with the
  object written on; the form disappears into the pad. (Beat 3.)
- **FIRST key (item 2 / F-2, Muede's errand).** Gives the dawn
  task a one-gesture road to the head inside the writing act
  itself, replacing preempt-by-teleport. The errand and the
  ordinary case now live on the same leaf, distinguished only by
  which end of the pad receives it. (Beat 4.)
- **Horizon bar + HORIZON header killed, minutes dead, count
  moved to QUEUE (items 1, 4 / F-3).** Removes the scoreboard
  from the threshold beat. A proportional chart of a floored
  phantom quantity was not merely a lie; it was a *grade*,
  printed above the queue, at the exact moment he came to make
  a promise rather than be measured. (Beats 1–2.)
- **QUEUE as the one header, with the SEALED state word
  (item 4 / F-3).** One printed sentence for the seal, in the
  header he already reads, instead of a silent darkening he has
  to interpret at his weakest hour. (Beat 7.)
- **ACTIVE / QUEUE plate words killed (item 7 / F-4).** Position
  speaks; labels judge. The bookmark principle. (Beat 2.)
- **All verbs gated under seal, absent not greyed (item 12 /
  F-5).** Makes the sealed leaf a record. The ritual's
  protection clause. (Beat 7.)
- **HAND-SPLIT — blank ruled lines, his own steps (item 11 /
  F-6).** The fabricated physics triple on a biology leaf was
  the app putting words in his mouth; blank lines keep every
  word on the pad his. Authorship is the ritual's whole
  substance. (Beat 3, deepened.)
- **TEAR rename, 48dp target, the respin (item 13 / F-7).** The
  finishing act gets a true physical motion and a target a
  tired or triumphant thumb cannot miss. (Beat 8.)
- **The held blank leaf, kept (item 6).** Emptiness rendered as
  an addressed place — "NOTHING QUEUED" as a held position, not
  an error — means a night with nothing to queue is a valid
  night. (Beat 1; break 5.)

**What fights the ritual, or leaves it undefended:**

1. **The foot composer's long-pile problem (item 2, unpriced
   cost).** Plan's recomposition is spatially honest and
   ritually right for queues of any sane length — but it makes
   the night's entry point the *end of the accumulation*. On a
   bad week, Beat 3 requires scrolling the guilt pile (break 1)
   before he can write. The audit does not address how the
   blank leaf is *reached* — first paint position, a jump, the
   pad opening at its foot when empty-headed? This seat does not
   overrule the recomposition; it flags that the reach is part
   of the ritual and deserves one ruled line in the build,
   because a ceremony you must wade to is a ceremony skipped
   on exactly the nights it matters most.
2. **FIRST and ADD at equal visual weight would clericalise
   Beat 4.** The audit specifies two keys on the blank leaf but
   not their hierarchy. If both shout, the tired writer faces a
   form's decision ("which submit do I mean?") at the moment of
   writing. Ruling requested in feel terms: ADD is the leaf's
   end-key, full stop — the natural full-stop of finishing a
   line. FIRST is the quieter, secondary key — present, legible,
   unmistakable, but visually subordinate, the way turning a
   leaf over is quieter than finishing it. The dawn intention is
   the exception, not the default, and the keys should say so.
3. **The audit is screen-bound; the ritual is time-bound.** The
   recomposition perfects act one and F-1 wires act two's data,
   but no beat in the audit covers the *crossing* — closing the
   phone (Beat 5) or the dawn's arrival (Beat 6) as felt events.
   Left as-is, a flawless build still delivers a silent sync:
   true, and unfelt, and therefore — over weeks — unbelieved.
   The echo (above) is this seat's one proposed bridge. It is
   small, it is priced as a sound/haptic pairing plus a seal-time
   trigger, and it is the only addition this filing requests.
4. **The 5-second undo window stands unrepaired (item 3).**
   Noted in the audit as a machinery price, not ruled. In the
   walk it is the single most likely cause of a *night that ends
   badly* (break 2). This seat asks that it not be allowed to
   drift as a footnote: for a ritual performed half-asleep, the
   forgiveness window is part of the ceremony's mercy, and 5
   seconds is not merciful.
5. **LAST-FINISHED's placement is acquitted, its shadow is not
   discussed.** Placement stays (Amt ruled; a finished record
   does not belong between the man and his next task — agreed).
   But the line sits inside the night screen, and on the night
   after a bad day it is the first biography he reads. The
   audit's own cure — printing the day when it isn't today —
   helps more than it may know: "yesterday" is a mercy word; it
   files the finish in the past where it belongs. Ship that
   cure with F-4, not after it.
6. **"N TASKS" inherits the bar's old chair.** Moving the count
   to the QUEUE header is right, but the count is the horizon
   total's survivor and it will be read in the dead bar's
   spirit unless its rendering is deliberately flat (break 1).
   One line in the build spec — quiet tone, no colour states,
   never paired with any second number — keeps the survivor
   from becoming the scoreboard's heir.

None of the six overturns a ruling. The recomposition serves the
ritual at nearly every joint; the fights are at its edges —
reach, hierarchy, mercy windows, and the one missing bridge
across the night.

## Close

The screen's promise, before F-1, was a lie told kindly: queue
tonight, and one arm path remembered while the other forgot. With
F-1 and the recomposition, the promise becomes mechanical truth.
This seat's whole filing reduces to one sentence: **make the
truth perceptible at exactly two moments — the setting and the
keeping — and let every other moment stay paper-quiet.** A pad
he writes on at night, a name the morning wears, a tear at the
end, a drawer that remembers his words without grading them.
That is a ritual. Anything louder is an app asking to be fed.

**DECLARED** — paper only; nothing compiled, nothing run.
