# FEEL — THE DRAWER ACROSS TIME (issue #8)

Feel Specialist 5 — Time & the lived moment, LADE's cell.
Sources of law: `studio/plan/AUDIT-drawer.md` (Lade's verdict,
esp. §2, §4, §6), `designer-drawer.md` and `innovator-drawer.md`
in this folder, and the critic's verified catches in
`critic-drawer.md` (S1, S2, S5, M1, M2, M9) where they touch how
a moment *feels*, not just how it is built.

My brief is only this: how the finished Drawer feels **across
time** — at 01:00, on a Saturday night, at year five, the
morning after, and on the first ever open. The floor (filing
zone, inline MONTHS shelf, cabinet evicted, search as an entry
line) is the Designer's and is not re-argued here. What follows
walks five lived moments against that floor. For each: what he
feels, what the screen does to earn it, and any priced delta —
work the floor does **not** already contain, priced so LADE can
rule it in or out. Where the floor already earns the feel, the
delta says so plainly: none. Restraint priced at zero is still
a spec.

Standing law for every moment (binding, not re-opened): Lade's
sentence — *"finished work rests — findable in seconds,
readable without being judged"*; no totals, counts, streaks or
percentages in print **or by arrangement**, at any age of the
archive; the only verbs are BACK, EARLIER, the filing words
until declared, and tap-to-read; day words print in one voice
with no semantic colour (ILL is not red — Designer §1); the
filing day begins at 03:30 (DRW-05) and every date this screen
prints is a filing date. No cabinet home is proposed, hinted
at, or implied anywhere in this file — issue #21 owns that.

## MOMENT 1 · 01:00, SICK — FILE TOMORROW ILL AND SLEEP

**The walk.** He has been awake inside one filing day since
03:30 yesterday — nearly twenty-two hours by the house clock.
He is ill, half-asleep, phone brightness already low. He opens
the Drawer, taps ILL on tomorrow's line, puts the phone down.
Two taps. That is the whole errand, and the audit prices it as
the screen's worst current failure (impossible at any tap
count) and its cleanest cure (§2, §4). My job is the feel of
those two taps.

**What he feels.** *The app is awake in the same day I am.*
No jolt: no light blast, no sound, no greeting, no question.
The filing is done before he has finished deciding to do it,
and the screen goes still again. He does not have to think
about dates — thinking is exactly what he cannot do at 01:00.

**What the screen does to earn it.**

- **It keeps his clock, visibly.** At 01:00 the filing zone's
  TODAY line still carries yesterday's calendar date — the day
  that began 03:30 — and TOMORROW carries the date that begins
  at the coming 03:30. Nothing rolled over at midnight while he
  wasn't looking. This is the whole "feels like it knows" test:
  if TODAY had flipped to the calendar date at 00:00, he would
  file the wrong day *confidently*, which is worse than being
  unable to file at all. The printed dates are filing dates,
  derived live (DRW-05 ticker, acquitted), and the two lines
  sit directly under the search line, before any stored work —
  zero scroll, zero hunting, at the top of the first screen he
  lands on.
- **Two taps, no third anything.** Drawer → ILL on the
  TOMORROW line → sleep. No confirmation dialog ("file ILL for
  Saturday?" is a question asked of a sick man at 01:00 — it is
  a third tap and a small cruelty; the floor has none and none
  may be added). No success toast, no animation of the word
  flying anywhere. The line simply retires: ILL stays beside
  its date in full ink `#34323B`, the other two words vanish,
  violet leaves the line (Designer §4). The retirement *is*
  the confirmation — readable in one unfocused glance: the
  word is in ink, the choice is closed, he can stop looking.
- **Mis-tap defence is geometry, not forgiveness.** A declared
  line is read-only forever (audit §2), so there is no undo to
  rescue a thumb. The floor's defence is the right one and is
  a feel requirement, not a nicety: each line ≥44dp tall, the
  three words spaced so a half-asleep thumb cannot land on a
  neighbour. At 01:00 this spacing is the difference between
  filing ILL and waking up having filed FAMILY.
- **Dark-room manners.** The Drawer never raises brightness,
  never forces a theme, never flashes a white interstitial on
  open — it composes at the brightness and theme he already
  has, instantly, on the landing. It makes **no sound**: no
  filing chime, no tap sonification, nothing. Silence at 01:00
  is not an absence of polish; it is the polish.
- **A wrong key does not eject him.** The audit rules system
  back is consumed inside the Drawer, one step per press,
  before any global ejection may fire — and the critic (M1)
  shows that today one press throws him to the Deck from any
  depth. At 01:00, with a thumb that misses, that ruling is a
  feel law: a fumbled back press steps him back one shelf, it
  does not throw him out of the room and make him re-enter it.
  Likewise the saveable-state ruling (audit §2; critic M2 —
  state is lost per *visit* today, not only on process death):
  if the phone or the app dies mid-errand, he reopens the
  Drawer standing where he stood, not re-walking from a cold
  landing with the filing still undone and no memory of
  whether he did it.
- **Sealed, it is never a painted door.** If the Drawer is
  under seal at 01:00, the filing words either file or do not
  appear as an affordance at all (Designer §4 leaves the
  doctrine open and lays down exactly this rule). A sick man
  tapping violet words that silently refuse is the one feel
  this moment cannot survive. Which branch of the doctrine is
  Ren/LADE's; the feel requirement — never show him a door
  that won't open, at 01:00 above all — is mine to state.

**Priced deltas.**

- *Floor-priced already:* the filing zone itself, tomorrow's
  line, the 2-tap path — audit §2 / Designer §4. The plumbing
  the critic prices in M3 (a date-carrying declare callback
  through DrawerScreen → MainActivity → ViewModel; the
  repository verb is already generic) is on this moment's
  critical path and is the Builder's floor work, not a feel
  extra. Without it this moment does not exist.
- *One soft haptic tick on the filing tap only* — a single
  muted pulse as the line retires, so the confirmation can be
  felt with eyes barely open, and no haptic anywhere else on
  this screen in this moment. **Price: ~10–20 LOC, under an
  hour of builder time; the real cost is proof, not code —
  haptics are unproven until Sarthak's phone, and a tick that
  arrives late or doubles reads as a machine stutter at 01:00.
  If it cannot be made single and immediate, ship without it:
  the visual retirement already confirms.**
- *Everything else in this moment: no delta.* Dimness and
  silence are not features to build; they are features to
  refuse to build over. Any future proposal that adds a sound,
  a dialog, or an entry animation to this path should be read
  as a charge against this moment, not an enhancement of it.

## MOMENT 2 · SATURDAY NIGHT, AFTER A TEST — WATCHING THE WEEK REST

**The walk.** The test is over. He is not looking for anything.
He opens the Drawer the way you open a drawer you tidied —
to see the week's finished work sitting where he left it —
and closes it again. There is no errand. This is the only
moment of the five where he comes to the Drawer with no task,
which makes it the purest test of Lade's sentence: judged by
nothing, found without searching.

**What he feels.** *Quiet pride that nobody hands him.* The
week is visibly there — day after day of it, in his own
titles — and the room says nothing about it. The calm is not
a reward the app gives; it is what is left when the app
refuses to take the moment over. He closes the Drawer feeling
that the work is *kept*, not that he has been *scored*.

**What the screen does to earn it.**

- **The week is simply present, in order.** Landing opens on
  the current month: this week's day groups under their
  headings (10.5sp bold caps, the 18dp shelf break making each
  day read as its own shelf, not another row — Designer §2),
  StoredRows with title, category, finish clock, SPENT. SPENT
  stays per-row as the receipt grammar (acquitted, audit §2):
  on Saturday night it reads as *what each thing took*, one
  slip at a time — the moment anyone sums it, aligns it into
  a week column, or prints a weekly line above it, Dens's
  standing watch (§2) revives and the moment dies. The feel
  of this evening is precisely the discipline the audit
  already verified: not one printed total exists in the file.
- **Nothing congratulates him.** No "week complete," no
  summary card, no streak kept or broken, no badge, no new
  anything. No element animates in, counts up, or unfolds on
  open — the room is already composed when he arrives, the
  way a real drawer is already full when you pull it. A
  resurfacing card, an anniversary line, a "from deep in the
  drawer" slip (Innovator P4's rider) — none of it may greet
  him *unasked* on this visit; anything that wants to show him
  old work must wait until he is standing over a specific task
  reading it, inside a visit, where he can walk past it. The
  Drawer never summons (acquitted); Saturday night is when a
  summons would feel most like praise and do the most damage.
- **EARLIER grows only at his tap.** If he drifts further
  back than the week, the archive extends because he pulled
  it, page by page, and stops when he stops. Nothing
  auto-loads the month to impress him with volume. Depth on
  this screen is always his gesture, never the app's display.
- **Leaving costs nothing and loses nothing.** He closes the
  Drawer mid-scroll without consequence; the saveable-state
  ruling means a later visit can find him near where he stood
  (scroll restoration, Designer §3.6 — priced as a delta
  under Moment 3, where it is load-bearing; here it is a
  grace note). A room you can leave without ceremony is a
  room that was never performing for you.

**Priced deltas.**

- **None. Delta: zero, and that is the deliverable.** Every
  element of this feel is already bought by the floor and the
  audit's acquittals; the work is a *never-add list*, recorded
  here so a future "engagement" idea meets a written refusal:
  no weekly summary, no congratulatory print of any tense, no
  on-open animation, no resurfacing on the landing, no totals
  by arrangement. If a proposal claims this moment needs a
  feature, the proposal is the problem.

## MOMENT 3 · FIVE YEARS IN — SIXTY SHELF ROWS

**The walk.** NEET is long over; the archive is not. Sixty
stored months — the Designer's own ceiling case (§3.5) — sit
on the shelf. He opens the Drawer to find one slip from two,
three, four years back. He does not remember its month. He
may barely remember its title. The archive must feel like a
*deep* drawer, not a *long* corridor: depth is something you
reach into; length is something you walk.

**What he feels.** *Weight without fatigue.* The scroll has
mass — years of finished work pass under his thumb — but the
shelf is patient: it does not hurry him, paginate him, or ask
him to declare what he's looking for before it will move.
And when he has a word, he doesn't scroll at all: search is
the front door at this scale, the shelf is the browse.

**What the screen does to earn it.**

- **The shelf never changes its manners as it grows.** One
  ruled row per stored month, 48dp, name *with year on every
  row* — `SEPTEMBER 2026`, never a bare month leaning on its
  neighbour (Designer §3.1) — reverse-chronological, one
  unpaginated list, no EARLIER, no "show more," no year
  sub-headings, no collapsing. Rows do not shrink, compress,
  or speed up to "fit" five years: the sixtieth month gets
  the same 48dp and the same full ink as the first. That
  refusal to economise is what patience feels like from
  inside a scroll — the archive is in no hurry, so he doesn't
  have to be either.
- **Depth is communicated by the names, not by machinery.**
  As he scrolls, the years in the row names walk backwards —
  2029, 2028, 2027 — and position in the list *is* age (the
  floor's ordering does this for free). No position counter,
  no scrollbar percentage, no "you are here in your archive"
  print: a number for depth would be a count wearing a
  compass's clothes. The bottom of the shelf, when he finally
  reaches it, is the oldest month's row, then the 100dp
  bottom padding, then nothing. The end of five years is
  quiet, not a footer.
- **Search is the primary route, and it feels primary.** At
  sixty rows the honest first move is the entry line under
  STORED: type a word, get day groups with year headings,
  the substep reason printed on the row when the match lives
  in a step (audit §2). The shelf is for the days he has no
  word — browsing, rummaging, recognising. The floor already
  contains both routes and §3.5's test is the right one:
  nothing new is needed at year five that wasn't needed at
  month two. What must *not* happen at scale: search results
  re-ranked by "relevance" (an oracle he can't predict, and
  it destroys the chronological place his spatial memory is
  using — Innovator's rejection, adopted), and the shelf
  acquiring counts to "help" him choose a month (a count per
  month is a score per month, at sixty rows as at six).
- **Honest risk, named: the shelf's position (critic S1).**
  The floor puts the shelf at band 5, *below* the entire
  current month with its in-place EARLIER paging. In a heavy
  current month, reaching even last month means scrolling
  past all of it — the shelf inherits the current month's
  length before it can show its own depth. At year five the
  current month is no heavier than today's, so the risk is
  bounded — but "findable in seconds" is measured in thumb-
  travel, not taps, and S1 stands as the one place this
  moment can quietly turn *deep* into *long*. The deltas
  below are priced against exactly this risk; the floor as
  specified ships without them and this moment still passes —
  slower at the top of the shelf than it should feel.

**Priced deltas.**

- *Scroll restoration at shelf scale (Designer §3.6, critic
  S3).* Returning from a MONTH lands him at the shelf row he
  tapped, not at the landing's top. At six rows this is a
  courtesy; at sixty it is the difference between browsing
  three candidate months in one visit and re-walking the
  whole current month three times. The critic is right that
  it is an unbuilt mechanism wearing a design detail's
  clothes: it needs a saved list state, stable shelf keys,
  and a ruling on what survives a destination switch.
  **Price: ~80–150 LOC, half to one builder-day, plus the
  ruling. Recommended: rule it IN for this moment's sake —
  it is the cheapest depth-feel on this page.**
- *Ghost search (Innovator P2).* Typing narrows the room in
  place — non-matches recede to hairline ghosts, shelf rows
  light or recede by presence (a boolean, never a count) —
  so at sixty rows he sees *where in time* his word lives
  before tapping anything: three lit months among forty is a
  map no result list gives. This is the fullest form of
  "search as the primary route." **Price as filed by the
  Innovator: ~300–450 LOC, 1.5–2.5 builder-days; the critic
  (M7) notes the per-month contains-hit query does not exist
  yet, so treat the top of that range as the honest one.**
  Optional; the floor's search already serves the errand.
- *The thumb rail (Innovator P5).* A section-scoped rail that
  rifles the shelf with a soft detent per month crossed. It
  is the only proposal that gives the sixty-row scroll speed
  *in the gesture itself*. **Price as filed: ~200–300 LOC,
  1.5–2 builder-days, and it needs the density seat's hearing
  the audit reserved for gesture layers on a storage screen —
  not shippable on this cell's say-so.** Filed here so the
  year-five scroll has a priced answer if LADE wants one;
  the shelf's patience must never depend on it.
- *Anchor titles (Innovator P3) — a depth-feel note.* A real
  stored title under each month name would make sixty rows
  scannable by recognition instead of by reading sixty names.
  It changes the shelf row's feel materially (48dp → ~56dp,
  a second voice on every row) and is the Designer's call to
  weigh against the shelf's name-only restraint. **Price as
  filed: ~60–100 LOC, half a builder-day (critic M7: the
  grouped query must reproduce the 03:30 filing rule — real
  work inside that price).** Recorded, not recommended from
  this seat: depth that needs a label on every shelf is one
  step from summary, and Moment 3's feel is that it doesn't.

## MOMENT 4 · THE MORNING AFTER A FILED ILL DAY — THE WORD JUST STANDS THERE

**The walk.** He filed ILL for today sometime last night
(Moment 1), slept, and wakes — still ill, or better; the app
neither knows nor asks. He opens the Drawer during the day.
This moment is the audit's Dens ruling lived out: a declared
word retires to *pure print — the word beside its date,
forever, read-only*. My brief names the core of it: **the
absence of follow-up is a feel decision. Specify it.**

**What he feels.** *It is written down, and nobody is making
anything of it.* The ILL stands in the record exactly like a
finished task's title stands: a fact he authored, kept. Not a
stain, not a storyline, not a debt. He doesn't brace when he
opens the Drawer, because the Drawer has never once had a
follow-up in it — and he knows it never will.

**What the screen does to earn it — the specification of an absence.**

The absence is specified as four concrete nevers, each one a
place a follow-up would naturally grow if nobody forbade it:

1. **No question.** Nothing on this screen — on this open, on
   any later open, in any state — asks how he is. No "feeling
   better?", no recovery check, no wellness line, no prompt
   attached to the day, the word, or the filing. The Drawer
   has no reply field for ILL because ILL was never a
   conversation; it was a filing. A question would also be a
   summons — the room that asks nothing (audit §2, Dens) does
   not start asking because the word was ILL rather than
   REST or FAMILY. All three words are beneath notice in
   exactly the same way: same ink, same voice, no semantic
   colour, no special handling for the sick one. Singling ILL
   out for tenderness would grade it as surely as singling it
   out for shame.
2. **No echo.** The filing generates nothing that arrives
   later: no notification that day or the next, no reminder
   that a day was declared, no surface anywhere in the Drawer
   that re-presents the word to him out of its place. The word
   lives in exactly two kinds of place, both of which he goes
   to: beside its date in the day's heading print, and on its
   month's shelf address (see delta below). He will only ever
   meet it by opening the room and standing at that date —
   the word never comes to him.
3. **No arithmetic.** The word is never counted, totalled, or
   placed in a series. No tally of ILL days exists to be
   shown, and none may be arranged into view — a run of ILL
   headings in a bad month prints as separate days' facts,
   never as a pattern the screen points at. The same law that
   kills per-month task counts (Designer §3.1) covers this
   with room to spare: his authored word, never a number
   (acquitted, audit §6).
4. **No editing, ever.** The morning after offers no amend,
   no retract, no "actually I studied" correction — the line
   retired when he tapped it (Moment 1) and retirement is
   permanent. This permanence is part of the comfort, not a
   harshness: because the word can never be revised, it also
   never needs to be defended, explained, or managed. It is
   print. Print doesn't ask to be revisited.

**And what the screen *does* do.** The word prints — that is
the whole positive spec. In the current month's groups, the
day's heading carries it beside the date; the day may hold no
finished tasks at all, and the heading with its word still
stands, because the word *is* that day's stored fact. The
filing zone the next morning shows the new today and the new
tomorrow, undeclared, unremarkable — yesterday's declaration
is not carried up into the zone as a status. The morning
after an ILL day looks, on this screen, like any other
morning. That ordinariness is the feel being specified:
the app files his worst days with the same clerical calm as
his best ones, and calm, repeated, is what lets him file the
next one without flinching.

**Priced deltas.**

- *The word must survive in a word-only month (critic S2) —
  REQUIRED, not optional.* The repository deliberately
  treats a month holding only a declared word as still an
  address in the index, "or the word would be filed
  nowhere." The Designer's shelf as written lists only
  months containing stored *tasks* (§3.3) — adopted
  literally, an ILL-only month drops off the shelf and the
  word becomes unreachable print: filed, stored, shown
  nowhere. Forever-print that cannot be visited is a broken
  promise, and this moment is where it breaks — a sick month
  with no finished work is precisely the month whose words
  matter most to have kept. **Spec from this seat: the shelf
  lists months holding stored work *or* a declared word —
  one row, name and year, identical voice, no mark
  distinguishing a word-only month (a distinguishing mark
  would print *why* the month is thin, which is judgement
  by arrangement). Price: the months set already unions
  word-months in the repository; the cost is a spec sentence
  and a test, ~10–30 LOC at most. Rule it in LADE's merge.**
- *The word must print the same way through every door
  (critic M9).* Today, search results build their day groups
  with an empty day-words map: the same ILL day found by
  search prints under a bare date, while the landing prints
  the word beside it. Same record, two prints — and the
  thinner print lands exactly when he comes looking for that
  period later. A record that changes its face by the door
  he entered through does not feel kept. **Price: small —
  pass the existing day-words into the search grouping,
  ~10–20 LOC. Rule it in with the above; together they are
  this moment's entire build cost.**
- *The four nevers above: no delta.* Like Moment 2, the
  absence is the deliverable. Any future "care" feature —
  recovery prompt, gentle check-in, declared-day follow-up
  of any tense — meets this section as its written refusal.

## MOMENT 5 · FIRST EVER OPEN — THE EMPTY DRAWER

**The walk.** Before any work has finished, before any word
has been filed, he opens the Drawer for the first time. It is
empty. Every other empty screen in his life has meant one of
two things: *broken* or *begging* (a tutorial, a "create your
first…" plea). This one must mean a third thing: **a clean
desk before the work starts.**

**What he feels.** *Nothing is here yet, and nothing is
wrong.* Not a flicker of "did it load?", not a flicker of
"what does it want from me?" The room is named, furnished,
and waiting — the way a real new drawer is not broken for
being empty. He closes it without doing anything and without
having been asked to do anything. That un-asked close is the
moment passing.

**What the screen does to earn it.**

- **The room introduces itself by being complete, not by
  explaining itself.** Everything the finished Drawer has is
  already here on day one, in its final form: STORED at full
  weight (with the cabinet row and capsule gone, the header
  is again the heaviest print on the screen — Aug's cure,
  Designer §2), the search entry line composed and working,
  the filing zone standing with today's and tomorrow's lines
  undeclared, and below them the one acquitted line:
  `NOTHING STORED YET` (§6, by name). A screen with its real
  furniture in place reads as a room; a screen stripped to a
  spinner-and-apology reads as a loading error. There is no
  spinner that outstays its welcome, no skeleton shimmer, no
  error costume anywhere in this state — the empty state is
  a *state*, composed like any other, not a failure branch.
- **The filing zone is what saves the silence.** The audit
  acquits empty-month silence (Dens, Plan) over the 1 AM
  charge that silence reads broken — and on the *first* open
  the acquittal holds for a structural reason the later
  moments don't need: the screen is not actually silent. Two
  live filing lines stand on it, offering the only action
  this room ever offers, in the room's real voice. He may
  file nothing. The offer's presence is what makes the
  emptiness underneath it read as *not yet* rather than
  *nothing works*. (On later opens the MONTHS shelf does
  this job — named months prove the archive exists beside a
  bare current month, audit §4. On the first open there is
  no shelf, §3.4 — the filing zone carries the proof alone,
  and it is enough.)
- **Nothing begs.** No "finish your first task" instruction,
  no arrow pointing at another screen, no onboarding copy
  explaining what a Drawer is for, no illustration of an
  empty drawer, no mascot of absence. A create-here plea
  would also be a small lie: this room has no verb that
  creates work — finished tasks arrive here from the rest of
  the app, they are not made here — and print that implies
  otherwise fails the truth standard on his very first visit.
  The screen trusts that a named, furnished, silent room
  explains itself. It does.
- **It is already the size it will stay.** The 100dp bottom
  padding stands under the emptiness exactly as it will stand
  under five years of archive; the page padding, the
  hairlines, the type — nothing is in a "starter" costume
  that later gets replaced by the real one. The first open
  and the six-hundredth open are the same room at different
  fullness. That continuity is the seed of every later
  moment in this file: the 01:00 errand, the Saturday calm,
  and the sixty-row shelf all feel the way they do because
  the room never once performed being new.

**Priced deltas.**

- **None. Delta: zero.** The floor's empty landing is already
  this moment, complete — header, search line, filing zone,
  `NOTHING STORED YET`, no shelf over an empty shelf. The
  risk is purely additive: any future first-run treatment
  (tour, hint, welcome line, "get started") proposed for this
  screen should be charged against this section. The clean
  desk is finished. Do not furnish it.

## SUMMARY FOR LADE — THE FIVE TESTS, ONE LINE EACH

1. **01:00, sick:** two taps in the dark, on his 03:30 clock,
   in silence — the line retiring to ink is the only reply.
2. **Saturday night:** the week rests in his own titles and
   the room says nothing about it; the calm is the reward.
3. **Five years:** sixty patient rows and search as the front
   door — depth he reaches into, never length he walks.
4. **Morning after ILL:** the word stands in print beside its
   date forever — no question, no echo, no arithmetic, no
   editing; the absence of follow-up is specified, priced at
   two small consistency fixes (S2, M9), and non-negotiable.
5. **First open:** a named, furnished, silent room — a clean
   desk, not a broken app and not a begging one.

Deltas priced above, totalled: haptic tick (<1 h, ship only
if provably single — Moment 1); scroll restoration (0.5–1 d,
recommended — Moment 3); ghost search (1.5–2.5 d, optional —
Moment 3); thumb rail (1.5–2 d + density hearing, parked —
Moment 3); word-only shelf months + search word-print
(~30–50 LOC combined, required — Moment 4). Moments 2 and 5
price at zero and are defended as refusals, not gaps.

— Feel 5 — Time (issue #8 cell)
