## 2 · THE FUSION (RAHMEN) — TWO JOINTS TO SETTLE BEFORE IT IS BUILT

Rahmen's composition is strong where it counts: returned dp is
spent on air, the pinned collapse (≈216.5 → ≈120dp) comes with
the law that fault may *grow* the stack while health may not,
and Δ-address = 0 across the seal is stated as law, not taste.
Two points where the drawing and the physics disagree:

1. **Δ-address = 0 vs burn-in shift — an unresolved contradiction
   in the same document.** §2.1 rules wheel centre, hub centre
   and slide address never move; the same section requires the
   ticking hub to inherit the AOD clock's shift discipline —
   which works by moving. Both rules are right; as written, a
   builder must break one silently. Craft resolution, offered
   for Rost's ruling: the shift must move wheel, rim and hub as
   **one rigid body**, by at most a few dp, slowly — Δ-address
   governs *state changes* (the eye never re-finds its clock
   because the app changed its mind), while burn-in drift is a
   material property of the panel, beneath notice, applied to
   the whole instrument at once. Write that sentence into the
   design and the contradiction dies before it becomes a bug
   someone "fixes" by deleting the shift.
2. **Rim ink quantises, and the design should say so.** Progress
   as ink on a tick ring is discrete: the leading edge can only
   stand on a tick. At the wheel's tick count that granularity
   is fine — position read at a glance wants steps, not decimals
   — but it means rim ink must never be asked to agree with a
   numeric readout to the second; the hub carries precision, the
   rim carries position (Rahmen's own §2.3 says exactly this —
   hold him to it at build). The joint that photographs well
   here would be a smooth arc pretending to analogue truth; the
   ticked ring is the honest one. Keep the ticks.

## 3 · THE STOP CHAIR — ONE PRICE-LABEL WARNING

One chair, one verb, whole session (audit §3, Muede adopted) is
the right ruling and it will age well. One wear point in the
form-to-beat — `STOP — USES YOUR 1 BREAK THIS WEEK`: that
sentence is true only while the week's token is unspent. The
token is a derived fact (the spend ledger), and the chair's
words must be *derived from the same ledger at print time* —
if the break is already spent, the chair must say what a tap
actually does then, not repeat the price of a token he no
longer holds. A label carrying a price must read the till.
Flagged for whoever builds F6: derive, never hard-code.

## 4 · THE PINNED COLLAPSE — GUARD THE AIR

The single most perishable line in Rahmen's design is the
spending order for the ≈120dp returned: wheel keeps 288, idle
fits, then *silence*. Every future element will arrive with a
good reason to take 20dp of that air permanently. The filing
requirement Rahmen attaches (name which of 1–3 you take the dp
from) is the only thing that will still be protecting the Deck
in three years. Keep it verbatim in whatever survives of the
design doc; it is worth more than any single element in it.
