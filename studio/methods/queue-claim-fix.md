## §1 ITEM 15, BUILT — the write-then-rename claim

**The failure it kills** (Auslese's diagnosis, Oct 29 ~21:32, verified from the flag file and the v2.0 source): v2's claim writes the record *into the flag name* — the noclobber redirect creates `compile-<lane>.flag` first, the five record-writes land second. A write refused between the two (depcull's claim, 21:14:32, free RAM 171 MB) mints a **0-byte phantom**: the lane's own acquire can never claim over an existing file, so it polls against its own corpse until the corpse ages past LEGACY_STALE (1,200s) and a reap clears it. Dawn's twin phantom (born 21:03) was kept young past even that line by a wrapper re-touching it.

**The candidate's changes** (diff: `methods/queue-sim/v2.0-to-v2.1.diff`, 160 lines; everything not listed here is byte-identical to v2.0 — constants, subcommands, exit codes, youngest-yields):

1. **Write-then-rename claim.** The record is written to a temp name in the same directory (`.claim-<lane>.<wrapperpid>.<shellpid>.tmp`); only a complete, non-empty record is **hardlinked** onto the flag name — `link(2)` fails if the flag already exists, so the claim stays atomic, and the flag name only ever exists carrying its full record. A refused or killed write mints *nothing at the flag name*; the temp litter it can leave is invisible to the flag glob, counts as no slot, blocks no lane, and is reaped as debris (change 3).
2. **Slot-check vanish guard.** v2.0 stats every other flag blind; a flag released between the glob and the stat errors (`stat: cannot statx …` + `[: : integer expression expected` — captured live in warrant's own log, Oct 29 23:07:23) and is silently uncounted. v2.1 skips a vanished flag explicitly. (Proof status: hygiene, not a proven cure — see the churn test below.)
3. **Reaper: notes + litter.** `reap-stale` now (a) leaves `reaped-<lane>.note` (lane, class, wrapper PID, age, reap time) beside the flags for every stale flag it takes — item 15's reaped-flag note, the Wartung seam from mail/wartung/1028: reaping frees the slot, the note lets the daemon sweep hunt the run's Gradle daemon, which is what frees the RAM; and (b) clears `.claim-*.tmp` litter older than DEAD_PID_GRACE (60s — a live claim's write+link is a single breath; a 60s-old temp is a dead claimant's remains). Younger temps are never touched.
4. **Status tells the truth about corpses.** v2.0 counts files: 1 live claim + 2 corpses prints `LAW 3 BREACH IN PROGRESS`. v2.1 counts live records and record-less flags separately and only cries breach over *live claims* (reproduction in Sim D2 below — and §2 is why this matters).
5. **An inherited false print, fixed.** v2.0's reap summary uses `${dry:+ [dry-run]}`, which expands whenever `dry` is *set* — including `dry=0` — so real reaps were labelled `[dry-run]`. (Visible in tonight's sim logs against the baseline; warrant's 23:07 log shows the same label.) v2.1 sets the suffix only for a true dry run. Disclosed here because it is the one change outside the approved fix's letter: it corrects a print, changes no behaviour.

**What was deliberately NOT changed — the slot arithmetic still counts corpses.** The slot check counts every flag with mtime ≤ the claim's, corpses included, so an inherited corpse still eats a slot until the reaper takes it at 1,200s. Excluding record-less flags from the count was considered and rejected: a v1-dialect lane's *live* flag is record-less (routine-edit's species, §2), and excluding it would let two v2.1 lanes claim over a running compile — a real breach to cure a bookkeeping one. A corpse and a live v1 flag are undecidable from the file; v2's conservatism is the safe side. The cure for corpses is prevention (change 1: v2.1 claims mint none) plus the reaper.

## §3 COMPILE-ONCE — what the new regime does to the queue

**The old regime's demand** (methods/queue-waits.md, today): 92 timed runs, ≈264 compiler-minutes consumed, median 133s / p90 264s / max 960s; lanes waited ≥150s in the morning audit window and one lane budgeted a 25-minute wait as normal; the day's one proven concurrency breach (10:13–10:19, three runs × ~360s) came from racy v1 claims. Demand was a *steady flow*: every lane compiled at every proof, all day. Tonight's machinery lanes still run that way — routine-edit alone filed 11 proof runs today (proof-9: 217s; proof-10: 718s), warrant's completion build ran 1,108s.

**The new regime's demand.** Under compile-once (Sarthak's order for Truth Car 3, 23:06: lanes write whole and compile *never*; one compile + full suite lands on the merged tree), a six-lane car produces **one** queue event instead of six lane suites plus six transplant runs. Queue demand collapses from steady flow to **merge-window bursts**: nothing for hours, then the merge train, the proof session, and the finishing machinery lanes converging at once — the 23:06–23:07 four-lane pile-up in §2 *is* the burst shape, arriving early. Two consequences: waits stop being a tax on every lane and become a property of merge windows (predictable, Ren-controlled); and the queue's centre of gravity moves from *throughput* to *correctness at the burst* — which is precisely where corpses and false verdicts do their damage, and why §1 lands at a merge window (§4).

**The cap of 2 — KEEP, unchanged.** The cap was never really a queue number; it is a RAM law in a queue's clothes. The box has 7,935 MB; Waage's repriced budget is ≈2,000 MB per Gradle daemon with a ratified 1,000 MB OS reserve; two concurrent runs (daemon + workers + the test JVMs) sit at the edge of that envelope, and tonight's free-RAM readings (171 MB at 21:14; 163 MB at 23:34) show the envelope already tearing at two. A third concurrent run is not a policy breach, it is an OOM/write-refusal event — §2's proof that none existed at 22:47 is what makes that event a non-event. Under compile-once the pressure to raise the cap also falls (bursts are short and schedulable), so there is no compensating gain. **Recommendation: cap stays 2.**

**The 1,200s reaper — the price has thinned; reprice at the swap.** LEGACY_STALE was priced off the morning's max observed run: 960s + 25% margin = 1,200s. Tonight warrant's completion build ran **1,108s** — the max moved, and the margin over the longest *lawful* run is now 92s (8%). That matters because a record-less flag is undecidable (§1): a v1-dialect lane's flag for a 1,150s run and a phantom are the same file, and at 1,200s the reaper eats whichever it is. Under compile-once the runs get longer still — a merged tree's full suite is the biggest compile the studio runs (batch-8's full run carried 186 tests; Car 3's merged suite is larger), and its first timing does not exist yet. **Recommendation: at the §4 swap, Ren sets LEGACY_STALE to 1,800** (= HARD_CAP; 1,108s max + 62% margin) — the candidate ships the constant at 1,200 (the approved fix's scope) with the line marked, so the swap is the ruling point. If the first merged Car 3 suite lands beyond ~1,500s, reprice again from that datum before the second car. HARD_CAP (1,800s, the HUNG report) stays as is; note it and the repriced LEGACY_STALE would then coincide, which is coherent: past 1,800s, a record-less flag is reapable and a recorded one is reportable — both are "older than any lawful run."

**One line for the merge train:** under compile-once, Pruefer's per-lane proof runs are replaced by one merged-tree session — the queue sees the car *once*, and the discrimination runs against the proofbase ride in the same window. Book the merge window's queue the way the queue was used at 23:06 tonight — first-come, two at a time — and the burst costs minutes, not the ≥150s-per-lane tax the morning audit measured.
