CRAZY-3 named *time behaviour* (Breath, Escapement, Neon, Echo, Day-Slit, Knock). **Nobody has named the strip as a MACHINE PART — a working joint out of a real instrument: lever, valve, breaker, ratchet, governor.** Every pitch below is stolen from a real mechanism and must pass four tests or it doesn't get posted:
1. **A drawn joint** — pivot, thread, tooth, or shaft. If there's no joint, it's a picture, not a part.
2. **Linkage law** — the indicating part is *mechanically linked* to the thing it indicates, the way a real valve handle is pinned to its ball. A pose the linkage can't produce cannot be drawn.
3. **It fits the strip's real estate** — 60dp housing, the channel, the 102×36 window with `END` engraved, jewel budget ≤41.9 luminous units — or it *replaces* an element at the same footprint and says so.
4. **TAP CAN COMMIT, drag never** — a lever begs a finger, so I say it plainly: the part is never dragged (a dragged lever fakes travel, DRUCK §1's law). A TAP on the housing commits/routes exactly as HAUT/DRUCK specced the housing-as-door press; the part itself only ever moves when proof moves it. The lever's affordance teaches *tap*, and the tap's consequence is the Deck's, not the part's.

**P1 · THE QUARTER-TURN — a ball valve in the pipe run (FULL OBJECT, my flagship)**

Steal: the quarter-turn ball valve / stopcock — the one fitting in every building whose handle *cannot lie*, because handle and ball are one shaft. Handle parallel to the pipe = bore open. Handle across the pipe = bore shut. Plumbers read it from across a plant room; nobody has ever needed a label.

Mechanism: the channel becomes a **pipe run** (the channel floor is the pipe's cutaway). Mid-channel sits the valve body — a machined bulge, 36dp wide, in the track. Through it, one shaft: below, the ball (drawn in section — a disc with a bore slot); above, the **T-handle**, lying in the strip's plane. One rotation state drives both, in code as in steel: `handleAngle == ballAngle`, a single float, two drawings of it.
- **OFF:** handle lies *along* the channel, bore slot aligned with the run — the pipe is open, and behind the valve the channel floor shows the warning bed's orange *through the bore*: you see daylight through the pipe. Open reads as a hole, not a word.
- **HELD / RUNNING:** handle rotated exactly **90°**, standing across the run, its end seated in a keeper notch machined in the housing's top edge. Bore slot now crosswise — the disc's blank face fills the run. The jewel moves *into the handle's end knob* (≤9dp, same budget): brass = proven idle, magenta = running. The lamp rides the moving part — proof sits on the shaft, not beside it.
- **CAUGHT / UNPROVEN:** the handle stops at **45°** — the diagonal. A valve handle at 45° is the universal "someone left this half-done"; it rests against a quadrant tooth cast into the body (real stopcocks have exactly this stop). Bore half-masked, orange sliver still visible through it. Not open, not shut, visibly *between* — and unlike a bolt at ⅓, a diagonal handle reads "between" in silhouette, in greyscale, in blur.
- **STALE_WHILE_RUNNING:** handle still across (the act happened), jewel dark (house law), and the bore disc gets the shutter: a thin section line rotates *out* of alignment — no. Simpler and honest: the window shutters, per the fold's existing STALE face. The valve adds nothing here and I won't invent a second stale channel — one stale face per instrument.
- **The throw:** 90° in the house's 50ms / 3-frame grammar — a quarter-turn is the fastest real shut in plumbing, and 90° of rotation in 50ms reads as a *snap of the wrist*, where 101dp of bolt travel in 50ms reads as a slide. Rotation is cheaper to make feel heavy: add the 2dp stepped sink as a 2° overshoot-and-seat on the shaft (rotate 92° → settle 90°), stepped, no spring — GANG's law, in degrees.
- **Why it beats a slider at this address:** rotation changes the strip's *silhouette* (along vs across) — the Bolt changes only an internal position. Silhouette is the peripheral channel; FALZ's beat-1 (0.2s, peripheral) gets a shape change, not just an area change.
- **Price:** one Canvas/Compose rotation (handle + section disc from one float), valve body as static housing art, window/jewel/bezel unchanged. ~M. No new fold inputs — it renders the PR #500 fold's faces as-is (pose from `BoltFace`, lamp, window). Risk, named: a T-handle standing across a 60dp band is tall — the handle lives *in plane* (flat bar handle, like a butterfly valve handle), never upright; if the in-plane handle reads as an arrow in cold reads, it dies at T1–T9 like everything else.

**P2 · THE BREAKER — the trip lever, and the middle position nobody else has (FULL OBJECT)**

Steal: the domestic circuit breaker — the only lever in a home with **three** honest positions: ON, OFF, and **TRIPPED**, which sits *in the middle, sprung there by the fault itself*, and refuses to go straight back to ON: you must push it fully OFF (reset) before ON will take. That reset-through-off grammar is a detention mechanism every adult already knows in their hand.

Mechanism: at the channel's right end, in place of the keeper + pin, a breaker lever in a square bezel (34×34dp, the bolt's height): a short, fat lever on a bottom pivot, travelling a 60° arc between two engraved end stops. The bolt stays — the breaker is what *drives* it in the drawing: lever and bolt share one travel fraction, lever angle = f(bolt travel), drawn from the same animated value (linkage law, one float).
- **ON (held/running):** lever hard against the top stop, bolt thrown, pin dropped — as the Bolt today.
- **OFF:** lever hard against the bottom stop, bolt home, bed exposed — as today.
- **TRIPPED = JAMMED / revoked mid-session:** the lever sits at **mid-arc, visibly sprung off both stops**, and the bolt sits at CAUGHT — but now CAUGHT has a *cause drawn next to it*: the lever didn't stop there because travel ended; it was *thrown* there. This gives the fold's JAMMED row (which the built 6-state enum couldn't render, K-2's family) a face that is mechanically distinct from UNPROVEN's CAUGHT: same bolt pose, different lever pose — UNPROVEN's lever rests *on* the bottom quadrant tooth (never tripped, never arrived), TRIPPED's lever floats *between* teeth with its spring tail showing. Two caughts, two mechanisms, zero words.
- **DETENTION, drawn:** on a refused release, the lever twitches toward OFF and *springs back to ON* (one 80ms step out, snap back) — the breaker refusing to reset because the fault is still on the line. The Bolt's DETENTION_FAULT row currently borrows RUNNING's face (magenta, §3's KANTE exception); the breaker gives refusal its own 80ms gesture without adding a state colour.
- **Price:** lever = one rotating box + arc stops on existing geometry, ~S–M, graftable onto the Bolt *without* replacing it — this is the cheapest full-mechanism in my set because the Bolt does the travelling and the breaker does the explaining. If only one of my pitches survives, the smart money is this graft wearing a full object's clothes.

**P3 · THE RATCHET & PAWL — proving gets teeth (MECHANISM for the proving act; answers V-1)**

The record has a live contradiction: MIP-02's proving creep (poses at 0.40/0.55/0.70 — or 0.85, its own table disagrees) vs the ratified detent law (OPEN 0 / CAUGHT ⅓ / THROWN 1), flagged unadjudicated as V-1. Both are wrong in the same way: proving is not a *position*, it's a *count of engagements* — and one real mechanism already draws exactly that: **the ratchet**.

Mechanism: the channel floor carries a rack of **three coarse teeth** (saw-tooth profile, 8dp pitch, drawn as machining in the floor — FALZ's detent ticks grown into working teeth). Above the bolt's tail, a **pawl**: a small pivoting finger whose tip rides the teeth. The seal act is already three proven links in the machinery (`ArmProvingController`); each link that proves, the bolt advances **one tooth** with a click-step (3 discrete steps, ~30ms each, inside the Throw buffer) and the pawl *drops into the tooth root behind it* — from that frame, the bolt **cannot slide back** without the pawl being lifted. That's not a metaphor: in the drawing, reverse travel is blocked by the pawl's body.
- **PROVING (new face, no new colour):** bolt between detents is no longer a lie or a creep — it's *on tooth 1 of 3*, pawl engaged, incapable of retreat. V-1 dissolves: intermediate poses are legal precisely because they're *latched*, each one a resting state with a mechanism holding it. The detent law survives untouched for verdict faces; teeth govern only the act.
- **JAMMED during proving:** pawl lands on a tooth *tip* instead of a root (drawn 2dp high, tip-to-tip) and the bolt recoils half a tooth — the jam tooth MIP-02 wanted, now with the part that jams.
- **RELEASE / DETENTION:** unsealing must *lift the pawl first* — the release sequence draws pawl-lift (frame 1) → bolt retract (frames 2–3), the two witnessed stages MIP-02 priced for detention, except the first stage now has a moving part instead of a rim recolour. A refused release = the pawl *doesn't lift*; nothing else moves. Refusal as stillness of one small finger.
- **Distinguished from FUNKE's MESH** (racks + shear pin + marker tooth, the documented alternative in Bolt §11): the MESH's pin *breaks* to show fault; the pawl *engages* to show proof and *lifts* to permit release. Different part, different verb, different face. And from the Bolt's locking pin: the pin drops at THROWN to *witness arrival*; the pawl works *during travel* to forbid retreat. If both survive review, the pin retires — two retention parts is clutter (see my Wave 2 cotter note).
- **Price:** teeth = floor drawing (static), pawl = one pivoting box keyed to the proving step count the VM already emits. ~M. Sound: zero new buffers — the clicks ride the Throw's existing rattle layer, timed to tooth landings.

**P4 · THE GOVERNOR — proof as lift, gravity as the auditor (FULL OBJECT; the K-2 killer in my set)**

Steal: Watt's centrifugal governor, the brass flyball engine-regulator on every steam engine engraving — two hinged arms with balls, on a vertical spindle. Its law is the Relay's law in mechanical dress (RAUSCH's A2 owns the electromagnetic version; this is the *centrifugal* one, and the face is nothing alike): **the balls stay up only while the engine is actually turning.** Stop the engine and gravity — not a sensor, not a verdict — brings them down. A governor cannot remember being fast.

Mechanism: at the jewel's address (the 21dp halo footprint, jewel retired or demoted into the spindle head), a miniature governor drawn in elevation: spindle 30dp tall inside the 36dp track height, two arms hinged at the crown, two balls ⌀7dp. One lift value, derived from the fold — never from the frozen verdict (H-6's series law, restated for machinery: *lift may be drawn only from a live-fold input*).
- **RUNNING, proof fresh:** arms raised ~50°, balls lifted and spread — the widest, tallest silhouette the strip ever shows. Held = *up*, against gravity, continuously paid for.
- **PROVEN IDLE (BLOCKING):** arms raised ~30°, balls at half-lift, spindle jewel brass in the crown. Idle lift is lower because the engine idles — same mechanism, honest speed difference, no new colour.
- **STALE_WHILE_RUNNING:** the balls **fall** — one frame, no tween (a fall is a fact, GANG's degradation law: step, never fade). Bolt stays thrown, window shutters per the fold, and now the *third* channel agrees: lift gone. K-2's lie needed pose + lamp to conspire; the governor makes the stale face *over-determined* — a fallen governor beside a thrown bolt reads "it was running, it stopped" to anyone who has ever seen an engine die. This is the only pitch in my set I claim *strengthens* the Bolt rather than competing with it — see graft note below.
- **UNPROVEN:** arms stirring at ~10°, balls dragging their rest collars — the engine turned over and never caught. **OFF:** arms folded dead down, balls resting in their cups, spindle jewel dark.
- **The seal act:** at t0 the arms *fling* up in the Throw's 50ms window (centrifugal rise drawn as 3 stepped angles, 0°/28°/50°) — a governor spinning up is the most alive stillness-adjacent motion in machinery, and it is *stepped*, so CRAZY-3's living-law is untouched: between verdicts the governor is a statue; it moves only when proof moves.
- **Price:** one Canvas elevation (spindle + 2 arms + 2 balls from one lift float), ~M. Footprint honesty: it replaces the jewel/bezel/halo stack at the same address — the lamp's information moves into the crown jewel (brass/magenta/dark, same tones, same ≤41.9 budget measured on the crown + balls' rim light). Risk, named plainly: at 21dp wide a governor can read as a *lamp post* or a *person* in cold reads — it lives or dies at T1–T9, and if it dies, its **law** survives as a graft: *any* lit element on this strip should be drawn as something *held up* (lifted shutter, raised flag weight) that falls in one frame when freshness dies. Steal the fall even if you bin the balls.

**P5 · THE PORTCULLIS — CAUGHT as a tilt (FULL OBJECT; the stone-native one)**

Steal: the castle portcullis — Monolith is stone; this is the only pitch that is *architecture from the same rock*. A grated gate sliding in two stone grooves, raised by chains, dropped by gravity.

Mechanism: the channel becomes the gate's groove pair (top and bottom rails of the track are the grooves). The gate is a grille — 5 vertical bars ⌀-drawn as 3dp uprights on two horizontal rails, exactly the bolt's 90×36dp footprint, travelling the same detents. Its truth trick is one no slider in the record has: **a dropped gate falls; a jammed gate TILTS.**
- **HELD:** gate fully across, bottom rail seated in the groove's floor notch, bars vertical, jewel set in the gate's centre boss (brass/magenta as house law).
- **OFF:** gate fully raised into the housing's top — wait, the housing is 60dp and the gate is 36dp; raised = gate parked at the channel's left in its open groove, chains drawn slack to the top hairline, the passage (channel floor + warning bed) fully exposed. Open = an empty doorway with orange on its floor.
- **CAUGHT / JAMMED:** the gate drops on its left groove and **jams tilted 4°** — right side 6dp higher than left, one bar's foot visibly resting *on* the groove floor instead of *in* it. Every other caught face in this record is a *position* (⅓ travel) that must be measured against a scale; a tilt is read as *wrongness* with no scale at all — asymmetry is the one signal peripheral vision is built for. JAMMED finally stops sharing CAUGHT's pose: CAUGHT = gate level at ⅓ (still travelling, grooves clear), JAMMED = gate tilted at any height. Two facts, two geometries, neither needs a word.
- **Release:** chains take the weight — the gate rises *level*, tilt visibly correcting in the first 10dp of lift. Detention refusal: the chains tauten (drawn straight, 1 frame) and the gate doesn't move — tension without travel.
- **Price:** one grille drawing + a rotation term on the existing travel animation, ~M. Risk: bars + grille at 36dp tall can read as a *radiator* or *fence* — the groove notches and chain links are the tells, and they're 1dp details; cold-read battery decides. I rank it below P1/P4 and say so.

**GRAFTS — organs any winner can steal, including the Bolt on PR #500's branch**

- **G1 · THE PLUG (jewel → lock cylinder).** Replace the jewel's plain disc with a key-cylinder face: a disc with a **keyway slot**. The slot *rotates*: vertical = thrown/proven, horizontal = open, 45° = caught. Rotation joins light as the jewel's second channel at zero extra footprint and zero extra luminous budget — a dark-lens stale face still shows a *vertical slot on a dead cylinder*, pose and proof now both living at the lamp's address. ~S (one rotating slot shape inside the existing bezel). This is the Breaker's linkage law (P2) miniaturised into the lamp.
- **G2 · THE COTTER (the pin, made honest).** The Bolt's locking pin drops *beside* the bolt into a notch — KRALL's K-3 showed the built version dropping into nothing. A **cotter pin** goes *through*: drawn crossing the bolt's tail and the keeper in one line, with its split tail splayed 20° on the far side. Through + splayed = retained, in a way a drop-pin only asserts; and detention gets its first stage for free — release must *straighten and pull the cotter* before the bolt can move (drawn: tail closes, pin slides 6dp, *then* travel). If P3's pawl lands, the cotter replaces the drop-pin (two retention parts = clutter); if P3 dies, the cotter is the pin's cure. ~S.
- **G3 · THE BLADE SHUTTER (the window's STALE face, mechanised).** The fold's STALE face says "window shuttered" — as built, that's a tone change wearing a mechanism's name. Give it blades: two thin focal-plane blades (camera shutter, darkroom enlarger) that meet at the window's vertical midline in one frame when proof goes stale mid-session, parting the same way when a fresh live fold returns. Numerals aren't dimmed or recoloured — they're *covered*, by a part, and the covering is the same shape language as the strip's other steel. ~S, window only. (Distinct from RAUSCH's Iris: his blades aperture a *lamp*; these shutter a *window*, and they have exactly two poses — open, shut — never a decorative half-stop.)

**THE BENCH — named, priced, and refused by me before a critic has to**
- **THE VISE** (jaws + screw handle): holds by friction with no power at all — it can fake held forever. Fails the linkage law's spirit. Refused.
- **THE TELEGRAPH KEY:** its down pose needs a hand holding it down; a blocker that must be held is a dead-man switch pointed at the wrong man. Refused.
- **THE DRAWBRIDGE:** raised bridge = passage *open* to ships, *closed* to road — the metaphor flips depending on which traffic you are, and the strip can't afford a two-sided metaphor. Refused.
- **THE MICROMETER:** a screw-gap in a barrel is RAUSCH's Relay hairline wearing a thimble; the gap idea is already owned, better, by A2. Refused as duplicate.
